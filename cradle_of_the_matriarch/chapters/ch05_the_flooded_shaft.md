# Chapter 5: The Flooded Shaft

The water was black as ink and cold enough to steal the breath from my lungs before my boots even touched the bottom.

I dropped four yards into the dark, submerging to my chin in freezing, oily brine. The shock made my chest clamp shut, an involuntary gasp sucking the bitter taste of chemical coolant, mineral lime, and wet rust into my mouth. When my boots found purchase on the slime-coated flagstones of the vault floor, the current pushed violently against my thighs, dragging me toward an open drainage culvert in the eastern wall where the runoff roared into the subterranean depths.

I reached out with my right hand, catching an iron rung bolted into the dripping masonry, hauling my head above the churning surface.

The darkness was near absolute. Only the pale, sickly azure glow of my left forearm illuminated the shaft, casting shifting ripples of blue light across the arched brick ceiling. The vault was thirty yards wide, bisected by massive cast-iron conduits four feet in diameter that hummed with terrifying internal pressure. Every three seconds, a colossal steam piston in the pumping station above released a pneumatic stroke, sending a dull, shuddering concussion through the water that vibrated straight through my breastbone and set my teeth rattling against one another.

I clung to the rung, coughing up mouthfuls of bitter brine, my teeth clattering together like dice in a leather cup.

My clothes were dead weight. The wool cloak, heavy with water and river muck, dragged at my shoulders like lead chains, threatening to pull my center of gravity into the undertow. I unclasped the brass brooch with numb, trembling fingers, letting the sodden fabric slide into the current to be swallowed by the drainage grates. The cold bit through my soaked leather tunic, sinking into my ribs, into my marrow, until the shivering turned from violent tremors into a dull, terrifying ache that slowed my thoughts into sludge.

Worse than the cold was the crystallization.

Submerged in the conductive brine, the azure glass in my left forearm began to feed on the ambient magical discharge of the cooling pumps. The petrification was creeping upward with cruel, unyielding momentum. A sharp, prickling heat—like hundreds of red-hot needles driving into the muscle fibers—crawled past my elbow, over the triceps, hardening the flesh into translucent, sapphire-colored quartz. 

When I tried to flex my fingers, the movement was stiff and flinty. A thin film of frost formed over the crystal skin the moment I raised the limb from the water, smoking faintly in the damp air. The boundary where the living skin met the petrified tissue burned with an intense, caustic itch that I could not scratch.

*Keep moving,* I told myself, feeling the hard metal of the triangular key-wrench in my belt. *If you stop in this water, you freeze. If you freeze, you drown.*

I waded upstream against the current, bracing my shoulder against the slippery iron skin of the central conduit.

The water rose to my collarbones in the middle of the chamber. Submerged debris bumped against my legs in the dark—rotted timber ties from old construction scaffolds, discarded iron turnbuckles, and occasionally the soft, sodden bulk of a dead stray animal washed down from the street gutters above. 

With every yard I dragged myself forward through the muck, the silence between the piston strokes was filled with voices.

They were not real. I knew they were not real. The data degradation in my neural pathways was leaking ghost-code from previous cycles, projecting sensory phantom echoes directly onto my auditory nerve as my core strained against the chill.

*Lord Yuuki... please take some soup...* Kokkoro’s gentle, piping voice murmured right beside my ear, so vivid I could almost smell the steam of boiled parsnips, wild leeks, and dried bay leaves. *You must not neglect your health. The morning patrol through the marketplace was long, and your cloak is damp...*

I shook my head violently, spraying cold droplets against the conduit, grinding my jaw until the phantom whisper receded. "Not now," I rasped into the dark, my breath pluming grey in the chill. "Stay in the memory. Don't come out."

*Yuuki! Look what I caught!* Pecorine’s boisterous laugh echoed from the dripping masonry ahead, bright and warm and heavy with unspilled tears. *It's huge! If we roast it with wild garlic and river salt, there'll be enough for everyone in the quarter! Come on, smile for me! Why do you always look like the sky is about to fall?*

The ghost of her laughter cut deeper than the freezing brine. 

In the eighty-fourth cycle, she had starved to death in a cellar three streets from here, having given her last royal ration bar to a stray street urchin while her Royal Equipment slowly consumed her muscle tissue to fuel its wards. I remembered holding her gaunt, shivering hands in the dark, watching the light fade from her violet eyes while she apologized for having an appetite. I remembered the coldness of her fingers when the pulse finally stopped, and how the simulation’s auto-cleanup routines had dissolved her body into blue static before I could even dig a grave.

I slammed the heel of my right hand against the iron conduit to shock my nervous system back to reality. The sharp, dull pain cleared the haze from my skull.

Ahead, the conduit terminated in a massive vertical intake bulkhead. 

A cast-iron sluice gate, eight feet square, barred the passage into the northern pumping station. The gate was lowered, its heavy teeth sunk into the silt at the bottom of the pool. To the right of the frame, half-submerged in the swirling foam, was the emergency bypass valve Jun had described—a heavy, three-sided brass spindle covered in green verdigris and calcified lime.

I waded to the spindle, the water swirling around my chin, lapsing over my lips with each surging stroke of the distant pumps. 

I drew the triangular key-wrench from my belt, my right fingers stiff and clumsy from hypothermia. I slotted the socket over the brass spindle, bracing my boots against the slime-slicked masonry wall, and pulled with both arms.

The valve didn't budge. Decades of calcium deposits and lime-scale had welded the gears together into an unbroken lump of oxidized metal.

"Turn, you bastard," I growled, planting my heels deeper into the silt, tasting blood where I had bitten through the inside of my cheek.

I brought my crystallized left hand to the lever. The petrified limb had no nerve endings left to feel pain, no soft tissue to tear. I locked the azure glass fingers around the iron shaft of the wrench and channeled a single, sharp burst of Princess Knight resonance directly into the metal.

The blue flare illuminated the flooded chamber in blinding, lightning-bright starkness.

The lime-scale cracked with a dry, ceramic snap that echoed off the vaulted brickwork like a pistol shot. The spindle groaned, screaming with metal-on-metal agony, and began to turn in greasy, jerky revolutions. 

With a deep hydraulic clunk that shook the water around my chest, the cast-iron sluice gate hoisted upward six inches, then a foot, then two. A vortex of black water rushed beneath the rising teeth, creating a powerful undertow that pulled at my boots, dragging mud and stones along the floor.

I didn't wait for the gate to open fully. I ducked beneath the iron teeth, sliding belly-down through the churning gap.

The current seized me, hurtling me through a narrow brick-lined flume into the secondary drainage pit. I tumble-turned in the dark, my shoulder crashing into a wooden baffle that splintered under my weight, before my hands caught the rusted rungs of an iron service ladder mounted to the vertical wall.

I hauled myself out of the water, coughing violently, vomiting up black bile and salty brine onto the iron grating of the maintenance catwalk.

I lay on my hands and knees for several minutes, gasping for air, shivering so hard my teeth ached in my jaw. The air here was drier, warmer, smelling not of sewer rot, but of heated solder, ionized ozone, and high-frequency electrical hum.

I looked down at my left arm.

The azure crystal now coated my shoulder, creeping over the clavicle toward my throat. The petrified veins glowed with a steady, eerie luminescence, casting pale blue light across my soaked leather armor. Every breath felt shallow, restricted by the stiffening tissue across my ribs, but the core inside my chest beat with stubborn, unyielding rhythm.

Above me, through an iron service grate in the ceiling, flickered a strange, rhythmic luminescence—not the dull violet of Kaiser's tethers, but the sharp, emerald-green wireframe geometry of an artificial reality.

Lines of digital code and glowing coordinates cascaded across the walls of the chamber above, accompanied by the clatter of keyboard relays and the sharp twang of an enchanted bowstring.

Labyrinth was right above my head, fighting for their lives in the wireframe rift.

I reached down, confirmed that my notched blade was still firm in its scabbard, wiped the greasy brine from my eyes with the back of my hand, and climbed the iron ladder toward the glowing grate.
