# Chapter 8: The Iron Revolt

The western descent down the Mid-Spire buttress was a steep, bone-jarring scramble through drifting banks of artillery smoke and freezing mountain drizzle.

Every step down the narrow granite stairs rattled the loose scree into the foggy abyss below. By the time my boots slammed onto the flagstones of the Citadel’s outer courtyard, the air was thick with the suffocating reek of scorched pitch, vaporized machine oil, burning horse manure, and sulfur fumes. The grand stone stables on the western perimeter were completely engulfed in flames, their timber roofs collapsing inward with muffled, thunderous crunches that sent showers of glowing cinders swirling into the freezing fog. 

Ahead, through the drifting soot, the black banner of Nightmare snapped in the gale above a hasty barricade of overturned supply wagons, shattered iron caissons, and heavy sandbags.

"Hold the redoubt!" Jun’s deep, metallic voice bellowed through the din.

Her visored helm was dented across the crown, her breastplate scorched black from proximity mortar blasts, but she stood like an iron pillar at the center of the breach. In her gauntlets, she held a two-handed heavy halberd, swinging the five-foot blade in wide, concussive cleaves that shattered the ceramic shields of the advancing Porcelain Paladins with deafening cracks.

Beside her, Christina was a blur of crimson and cold steel. 

Her rapier struck with supersonic speed, the point puncturing the optical eye-seams of the automatons with sharp, pneumatic pops that sprayed black coolant over the wet stones. Yet her breathing was ragged, whistling through her clenched teeth; her crimson cape was shredded by shrapnel, and her boots slipped on the bloody grease coating the cobblestones. Around them, two dozen city watchmen—common soldiers who had torn the imperial lion crest from their tunics—fired repeating crossbows into the smoke, their hands shaking with exhaustion as they reloaded.

"Six more on the colonnade!" Christina yelled, lunging forward to run a paladin through its throat before kicking the falling carcass into the ditch. "Jun, our crossbow bolts are running dry! If Matsuri doesn't crack that armory vault, we’re down to throwing paving stones and spitting curses!"

A heavy ceramic centurion lunged from the smoke behind her, its rotary saw screaming with high-pitched fury as it aimed a horizontal stroke at her exposed waist.

I didn't shout. I hit the flagstones in a low sprint, drawing my notched arming sword with my right hand while raising my crystallized left forearm like a buckler.

The howling teeth of the rotary saw met my sapphire wrist with an ear-splitting screech that showered the courtyard in white sparks. The ceramic teeth fractured against the petrified crystal, sending jagged shards whistling into the brickwork like shrapnel. Before the centurion could recover its balance, I stepped inside its guard, driving my steel blade straight through the brass intake manifold beneath its breastplate.

High-pressure steam hissed from the wound in a scalding cloud. The machine shuddered, its optical sensor flashing yellow static, and toppled heavily onto the cobbles with a hollow clang.

Christina spun on her heel, her rapier hovering inches from my collar before she recognized my face through the smoke. A breathless, blood-stained laugh broke from her lips, her golden hair clinging in wet strands to her flushed temples.

"Well, look at the hero," she panted, wiping soot from her nose with her leather gauntlet. "Turned your back on the high throne just to wallow in the mud with us, did you? You always had terrible taste in company, Yuuki."

Jun brought the butt of her halberd down onto the stones with a heavy, ringing thud, turning her iron visor toward me. The single glowing slit behind her faceplate narrowed with fierce, solemn respect.

"You answered the call," Jun said, her voice dropping into a low, rumbling cadence that cut through the thunder of distant cannons. "I told Christina you would not abandon the rear guard. The civilian wagons have reached the fourth ravine, but the imperial cavalry is massing on the Grand Causeway."

"How much time do they need?" I asked, wiping black grease from my blade against my leather tunic. 

The azure crystal across my neck and collarbone was pulsing with cold energy, but inside my chest, the Princess Knight core beat with steady, focused adrenaline. Every breath tasted of ozone and soot, but my footing felt planted, solid against the stones.

"Two hours," Jun replied, pointing with her steel gauntlet toward the southern pass that wound into the karst hills. "The mountain road is narrow, choked with mud, ruts, and thousands of terrified refugees. If the porcelain cavalry catches them in the gorge, it will be a slaughter. Ten thousand people, Yuuki. Weavers from the lower wards, wounded city watchmen, children who don't even know what an imperial reset means. They were born in this cradle. They deserve to see an honest morning."

From the arched entrance of the lower magazine bunker, Tomo sprinted across the yard, ducking beneath a burst of shrapnel from an incoming mortar shell that chewed into the stable masonry. Her short hair was plastered with sweat, her lightweight scout armor covered in powdered white lime and dust.

"The vault doors are open, Captain!" Tomo shouted, skidding to a halt beside Jun, her boots splashing through a puddle of black machine grease. "Matsuri bypassed the hydraulic lock with an explosive charge! The armory is intact—twenty crates of heavy siege powder, sixty repeating crossbows, and three cases of concentrated alchemical fire-flasks!"

"Good," Jun said, her stance tightening with grim, tactical resolve. "Christina, take the watchmen and haul the powder kegs up to the central viaduct arch. We rig the bridge to blow. If the imperial vanguard tries to force the gorge, we drop fifty thousand tons of granite into the river."

Christina flashed a fierce, predatory grin, her exhaustion vanishing behind her natural thirst for the fray. "Now that sounds like proper military engineering. Tomo, grab the fuses! Let's give these hollow dolls a fireworks display they won't forget!"

Christina, Tomo, and Matsuri turned, rallying twenty bruised watchmen into the magazine bunker. The men moved with renewed energy, shouldering heavy barrels of black powder and crates of iron spikes, their heavy boots splashing through the water and grease as they sprinted toward the bridge piers.

Jun remained beside me at the barricade, her halberd resting across her shoulder, her iron visor looking out over the burning courtyard toward the Grand Causeway.

Through the yellow smoke, the rhythmic, ground-shaking thunder of heavy mechanical hooves began to roll across the valley like an earthquake.

The Imperial Shock Cavalry was deploying. 

Fifty mechanized centaurs—towering war-machines of lacquered white ceramic and blackened iron, each mounting a twelve-foot heavy lance tipped with crackling violet lightning—were forming into a massive, unstoppable wedge at the mouth of the bridge. Above their heads, the silver tethers descending from the leaden clouds swayed in the freezing gale, feeding cold, algorithmic targeting vectors directly into their central processors.

"They won't give us two hours," I said quietly, feeling the steel hilt of my sword cold in my palm.

"Then we take it from their throats," Jun replied, rolling her massive shoulders against her dented breastplate. 

She turned her visor toward me, placing her heavy steel gauntlet on my right shoulder. The metal was cold, scratched by a hundred blades across a dozen lost campaigns, but the grip was steady as bedrock.

"My oath was sworn to protect the people of Landosol," Jun murmured, her voice steady beneath her helm. "Not the walls, not the crown, and not the hollow god who sits upon the throne. If this is where the Royal Guard makes its stand, then let it be a stand worthy of a knight's honor."

She tightened her two-handed grip on her halberd, stepping out from behind the sandbags onto the open causeway.

"Stay with me in the breach, Yuuki," Jun said. "When the time comes to strike the flint, we hold the line together."

The roar of the charging cavalry grew deafening, the iron hooves of fifty mechanized centaurs striking the granite causeway like a rolling drumfire of thunder. The wind whipped between the canyon cliffs, tearing the smoke into shredded banners of black and grey. In the distance, high above on the mountain pass, the rear wagons of the refugee train were already vanishing into the shelter of the karst gorge, their oil lanterns flickering like fragile, brave fireflies in the dark.

I looked at the massive stone piers of the bridge beneath our feet. Fifty feet of solid granite, weathered by centuries of rain, anchoring the gorge against the wild river below. Christina was already descending into the inspection galleries with the powder kegs, her wild laugh echoing from the darkness as she hammered the wooden fuses home.

I drew my notched steel arming sword with my right hand, brought my crystallized left arm up into a tight defensive guard, and stepped out onto the granite roadway beside Jun. The sapphire crystal along my forearm hummed with cold, defiant light, reflecting the violet glare of the charging lances.

The line would hold. Whatever the cost in iron or blood, the line would hold.
