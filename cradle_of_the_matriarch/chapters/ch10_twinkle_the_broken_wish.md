# Chapter 10: The Broken Wish (Twinkle Wish Route)

The bronze gates of the High Cathedral had not been opened by human hands in three years. 

When Hiyori drove her boots into the central seam, the ten-inch locks sheared with a dry, hollow crack that echoed down the empty esplanade like a gunshot. The heavy leaves swung inward on rusted hinges, groaning against the stone sill and releasing a blast of stale, frigid air that reeked of tallow, embalming fluid, and scorched copper wire.

We stepped into the cavernous darkness of the central nave.

The cathedral had once been the architectural jewel of the kingdom, a soaring monument of white limestone built to celebrate the harmony of Astraea. Its ribbed vaults rose eighty cubits above the marble floor, supported by double rows of clustered columns that resembled petrified palm trunks. Stained-glass windows had once filtered the dawn light into mosaics of sapphire, rose, and gold, telling the ancient legends of the sky-maidens who guided lost travelers home.

Now, every pane of stained glass had been systematically hammered out and replaced with sheets of lead-backed mercury glass.

The mirrored panels did not reflect the interior of the church. Instead, they flickered with endless cascading lines of green diagnostics and flickering monochrome video feeds. Across those cold glass surfaces, hundreds of sleeping faces drifted past—weavers in their barracks, miners curled in their straw bunks, old women in the hospice wards of Sector 4. Their features were motionless, their eyes locked behind slack lids, their respiratory rhythms kept in synchronization by the humming cables that ran down the cathedral walls like petrified vines.

Suspended fifty feet above the high altar, in the central crossing where the transept intersected the nave, hung the Heart of the First Desire.

It was a gargantuan, pear-shaped chrysalis constructed from dark violet crystal and beaten iron hoops. Thick hydraulic conduits, wrapped in black insulation tape, fed into its apex from the ceiling vaults, pulsing like arterial veins with every heartbeat of the city. Inside the translucent purple shell, a swirling vortex of condensed starlight writhed in agony, its golden luminescence clouded by thick, oily veins of black miasma.

Whenever the chrysalis pulsed, the entire cathedral shuddered. 

A high, keening sound reverberated through the stone floor—not a mechanical whine, but the amplified, distorted sound of Yui’s own childhood voice, repeating a single sentence across a million recursive loops:

*I wish... I wish everyone could just be happy... I wish no one ever had to hurt again...*

Yui dropped to her knees on the threshold flags, her hands clutching her ears as tears streamed down her cheeks. 

"Make it stop..." she whispered, her voice choking on bile. "Please... that's the day I met you, Yuuki... the day behind the guild hall when the cherry blossoms were falling... she took that memory... she took that exact moment and made it into a noose!"

"She won't use it anymore," Rei said, stepping forward onto the central carpet with her rapier drawn, her knuckles pale against the wire-wrapped hilt. "We are going to cut that thing down and burn the pieces."

Before Rei could take a third step, the shadows beneath the altar dais surged upward.

The darkness coalesced into a massive figure that rose from the floor with horrifying fluidity. 

It was the Crown Arbiter: Omnia—Kaiser's high executioner for the religious districts. The construct stood nine feet tall, encased in overlapping plates of mirror-polished platinum armor that reflected our distorted images in grotesque funhouse shapes. It bore no human face; in place of a helmet, a massive golden balance scale hovered above its armored shoulders, its twin pans filled with burning violet coals that showered sparks onto the marble flags. In its right hand, it held a massive executioner’s broadsword forged from blackened titanite, its blade jagged as a meat saw.

"Intruders identified," a synthetic voice intoned from the empty collar of the armor, flat, hollow, and devoid of inflection. "Cognitive variance detected in Subject Kusano Yui. Diagnostic status: terminal corruption. In accordance with Matriarchal Decree 402, purification protocols are engaged."

The Arbiter swung its blackened broadsword with terrifying, effortless speed.

The blade cut through the air with a supersonic boom, generating a concussive shockwave that ripped up the limestone paving stones along the center aisle, hurling thirty-pound chunks of rock toward us like cannon fire.

"Scatter!" Rei barked, diving to the right behind a clustered pillar.

Hiyori leaped to the left, rolling across the stone floor just as the shockwave pulverized the cathedral’s first two rows of heavy oak pews into toothpicks. 

I scooped Yui into my arms, throwing myself behind the massive marble base of the baptismal font. The concussive wave slammed against the font, splashing consecrated water across our hair and showering our shoulders with pulverized stone chips.

"Yuuki!" Hiyori shouted from across the aisle, ducking behind the pulpit as the Arbiter’s heavy boots began to advance down the nave, each step ringing like a sledgehammer on an anvil. "That blade carries a nullification enchantment! If that steel touches our mana, the link will blow backward into your chest!"

"Rei! Can you get behind its neck?" I called out, my voice cutting through the ringing in my ears.

"The armor is sealed at the gorget," Rei called back, leaning out from behind her pillar to study the construct’s movements with icy calculation. "The only vulnerable mechanical joint is the counterweight linkage beneath the balance pans! If we sever the left pan, the construct's kinetic balance will collapse!"

"Then you take the pan," Hiyori yelled, her eyes flashing with wild, desperate fury. "I'll take the teeth!"

Hiyori broke cover.

She charged straight down the center aisle, her red boots pounding against the broken marble with the rhythm of a war drum. She didn't weave. She didn't seek cover behind the columns. She ran with her head lowered, her twin gauntlets cocked at her hips, screaming a raw, furious battle cry that echoed off the high ribbed vaults.

The Arbiter tracked her instantly. Its platinum arm rose, bringing the blackened titanite saw-blade down in a vertical executioner's chop aimed straight at Hiyori’s skull.

"Hiyori, no!" Yui screamed from behind the font.

"Yuuki! Ignite me!" Hiyori bellowed.

I stepped out from behind the baptismal font, thrusting my crystallized left arm forward. 

The Princess Knight core in my chest flared with a blinding blast of sapphire fire. The heat was immense, ripping through my pulmonary arteries with the sensation of swallowing boiling lead. A web of sharp azure needles sprouted along the skin of my left cheek, hardening into crystal scales that stole my breath. 

The power hit Hiyori like a thunderbolt.

Her entire body became a silhouette of blinding solar brilliance. When the Arbiter’s executioner blade descended, Hiyori didn't parry. She caught the jagged black titanite edge between the palms of her bare hands.

The kinetic collision shook the entire cathedral to its foundations. 

A circular shockwave blasted outward in all directions, blowing out forty sheets of leaded mercury glass in a deafening cascade of singing shards. The marble flagstones beneath Hiyori’s boots pulverized into white talc, her heel-irons sinking four inches into the concrete subfloor. Blood sprayed from Hiyori’s nose and ears from the sheer concussive pressure, and the steel plates of her gauntlets began to warp under the crushing weight.

"Rei!" Hiyori roared through clenched, bloody teeth, her muscles tearing as she held the blade suspended inches above her brow. "Cut it! Cut it now!"

Rei appeared above the Arbiter like an avenging angel of blue frost.

She had sprinted up the spiral stairs of the choir loft, leaped from the wooden balustrade, and descended through the air with her rapier pointed straight down like a spear of starlight. The azure boost I fed into her blade had turned the thin steel into a needle of absolute, diamond-hard severance.

Her rapier struck the left balance pan’s bronze swivel arm with surgical precision.

*Snap!*

The bronze sheared clean through. The heavy pan, loaded with burning violet coals, tumbled to the marble floor, shattering into molten fragments that hissed against the stone.

The Arbiter’s kinetic gyro failed instantly. 

The nine-foot construct lurched violently to the right, its balance overthrown by the sudden loss of counterweight. The massive titanite broadsword slipped from Hiyori’s grip, its point burying two feet into the concrete floor.

"My turn!" Hiyori snarled, wiping the blood from her chin with a swollen forearm.

She pivoted on her left heel, cocked her right fist, and drove a full-force solar haymaker directly into the construct’s faceless golden breastplate.

The concussive force sounded like a church bell being struck by a siege ram. The platinum breastplate caved in three inches, the internal clockwork gears groaning and snapping like dry twigs. The nine-foot giant was lifted clean off its boots, hurled twenty paces backward down the aisle, and slammed through the stone steps of the high altar in an explosion of white marble dust and twisted brass piping.

The construct lay motionless in the rubble of the altar steps, its limbs twitching feebly as its purple power conduits severed, drowning its golden chassis in inert hydraulic sludge.

"It's down..." Rei landed lightly on the floor beside Hiyori, leaning heavily against her rapier, her chest heaving as she fought for air.

"Did we... did we win?" Hiyori asked, sinking down onto the edge of a shattered pew, her bruised knuckles trembling violently as the solar adrenaline faded from her bloodstream.

"No," a small, trembling voice answered.

Yui was standing in the center aisle, her white staff slipping from her fingers and clattering against the stones. 

Her amber eyes were fixed upon the massive chrysalis suspended fifty feet above the ruined altar.

The destruction of the Arbiter had not severed the Heart of the First Desire. It had triggered the failsafe.

High in the vaults, the iron hoops surrounding the violet crystal began to rotate at dizzying, terrifying speed. The black miasma inside the shell boiled, expanding violently against the crystal walls with the muffled roar of an approaching tidal wave. The diagnostic feeds lining the cathedral walls turned from green to screaming crimson, flashing a single emergency directive across fifty screens:

**[CRITICAL CAPACITY EXCEEDED — WISH OVERFLOW DETONATION IN 120 SECONDS]**
**[ESTIMATED CASUALTY RADIUS: ENTIRE CENTRAL BASIN — 850,000 SOULS]**

"The backpressure..." Yui whispered, her knees giving out beneath her as she collapsed onto the rubble of the steps. "The arbiter was the pressure release valve... with it dead, the wish has nowhere to vent... it's going to blow... it's going to wipe everyone's minds completely clean!"

Rei hurried to Yui’s side, her sharp face pale beneath the grime of battle. "Can we sever the ceiling cables?"

"No!" Yui sobbed, clutching her hair in despair. "If you cut the cables, the core drops and detonates on impact! There's no mechanical switch! It's my soul... it's my voice holding that bomb together! The only way to stop it is to absorb the entire miasmic load back into the spirit that made it... but my body can't take that much poison... it will disintegrate me in five seconds!"

I walked up the marble steps, my boots crunching softly through the pulverized fragments of the altar. 

My left hand was burning with a quiet, icy luminescence. The azure crystal had climbed past my jawline now, numbing the left side of my tongue and coating my collarbone in an unbroken carapace of sapphire stone. My mortal body was already halfway into the grave; my biological life was an expired ledger that had only a few pages left to turn.

I knelt down in the dust before Yui.

I reached out and gently cupped her tear-streaked face in both my hands—my warm, bleeding right hand and my cool, crystalline left.

"You aren't absorbing it alone, Yui," I said softly, looking directly into her wide, terrified eyes. "You never had to."

"Yuuki... what are you talking about?" she whispered, her breath catching in her throat.

"I am the Princess Knight," I said, a faint, gentle smile touching the right corner of my lips. "My power reaches far beyond simple blade strikes or heavier blows. My power is resonance. I connect hearts. If the poison belongs to your wish, then through my link, your wish belongs to me."

"No!" Rei snapped, stepping forward, her hand reaching out to grab my shoulder with desperate, furious strength. "Yuuki, that's suicide! Look at the volume of that core! It's the collected despair of hundreds of thousands of people! If you draw that much miasma into your vessel, your internal organs will be burned to ash!"

"And if he doesn't," Hiyori whispered from behind Rei, her voice thick and trembling, her eyes wide with agonizing clarity, "eight hundred thousand people die right now. And Yui burns with them."

The silence in the ruined cathedral was absolute, broken only by the screaming rotation of the iron hoops overhead.

Yui looked up at me, her hands rising to press against the back of mine. 

Her fingers were small, cold, and trembling, but as she looked into my eyes, the panic in her gaze slowly dissolved into something vast, quiet, and profoundly sacred. She knew what was coming. She knew the price that Astraea demanded from the boy who had chosen to love them all.

"We stand together at the altar," Yui said softly, her tears flowing freely, washing clean streaks through the dust on her cheeks. "If we have to burn... we burn as Twinkle Wish."

I stood up, pulling Yui gently to her feet, and turned to face the high altar dais beneath the screaming chrysalis.

Rei stood at my right side, sheathing her rapier and taking my right arm. Hiyori stood at my left, her bruised, iron-bound hand resting firmly against my waist. Yui stepped between us, taking my crystallized hand in both of hers, holding it tight against her beating heart.

Four souls. One covenant. 

We climbed the remaining three steps of the altar to meet the storm.
