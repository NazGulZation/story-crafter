# Chapter 4: The Barracks Mutiny

The granite courtyard of the Grand Barracks smelled of scorched pitch, shattered bone, and the sharp chemical sting of exploded gunpowder. 

Smoke from burning grain wagons hung low under the leaden sky, stinging my eyes and turning the morning light into a greasy, ochre haze. The cobbles were littered with shattered shields, sheared ballista cables, and piles of white ceramic armor plates that hissed as cold rain splashed against their heated fractures. In the gutters, thick black machine oil pooled alongside crimson smears, sluggishly draining into the iron storm-grates.

Above the central bailey, the white and gold banner of the Royal Guard had been hacked down. In its place, tied to the broken iron railing of the command balcony, hung a tattered black cloak stained with soot—the emblem of Nightmare.

I ducked behind the overturned iron hull of an armored transport wagon, my back pressing against the cold steel plates. 

Twenty yards ahead, the main portcullis had become a butcher's gate.

A phalanx of sixteen Porcelain Paladins was advancing in a tight wedge, their crescent poleaxes locking into a continuous wall of white enamel and sharp steel. Behind them, two heavy siege automatons—massive, six-legged iron crustaceans mounting hydraulic ram-cannons—were methodically pulverizing the defensive barricades thrown up by the mutineering soldiers. Every discharge shook the ground with an earthquake shudder that loosened the slate tiles from the stable roofs.

Holding the breach alone at the foot of the portcullis was Captain Jun.

Her full plate harness, once immaculate silver and blue, was blackened by fire and gouged deep by ballista bolts. Her great tower shield was split down the center, held together only by emergency copper rivets, yet she stood with her boots planted in the bloodied slurry of the ditch, absorbing the rhythmic hammer-blows of the vanguard without yielding an inch of ground. 

"Form on the inner arch!" Jun’s voice roared from inside her visored helm, deep, metallic, and steady despite the exhaustion cracking its edges. "Keep the wagons moving through the western sally port! Matsuri, Tomo, secure the refugee line! If the gatehouse falls, ten thousand people in the lower cantonments will be caught in the sweep!"

Down the lane behind her, regular city watchmen with bandaged limbs and blood-streaked tunics were hauling hand-litters bearing wounded conscripts and children. The soldiers had torn the royal crests from their surcoats. Their faces were hollow with horror, but they worked with desperate discipline, passing waterskins and dry biscuits to the fleeing weavers and tanners who had broken their tethers during the night.

From the rampart above the portcullis, a blur of crimson and flashing steel descended like a falling star.

Christina hit the center of the paladin wedge with a concussive shockwave that buckled the granite flags. Her crimson cape billowed in the draft of her descent, her rapier moving with terrifying, reality-bending speed. The blade did not simply pierce armor; it struck the resonant fault-lines of the ceramic plates, shattering three paladins into whistling fragments of porcelain and black oil in the space of a single breath.

"Oh, come now, you hollow dolls!" Christina laughed, a wild, breathless sound that carried the edge of genuine desperation. Her golden hair was plastered to her sweat-soaked forehead, her cheeks flushed with the dangerous, feverish heat of an overtaxed body. "Is this the best the Matriarch can muster for her loyal guards? I expected at least a proper executioner!"

A heavy siege crustacean swiveled its iron chassis, its hydraulic ram whining as it locked onto Christina’s position. A steel spike, four inches thick and six feet long, punched forward with the force of a steam cannon.

Christina twisted mid-air, deflecting the bolt with the flat of her rapier, but the kinetic recoil blew her off balance. She stumbled across the slick cobbles, her footing slipping in the mud.

A second paladin lunged from the smoke, its poleaxe raised for a downward cleave straight toward her unprotected neck.

I didn't think. I kicked off the iron wagon and sprinted across the open yard.

My crystallized left arm moved on pure instinct, rising to intercept the falling blade. The steel crescent of the poleaxe struck my azure forearm with a deafening, flinty crack. Pale blue sparks rained over my tunic, but the petrified bone and crystal veins held firm, biting two inches into the forged steel of the axe.

The paladin's featureless white head jerked downward, its internal sensors registering the anomalous structural hardness of my limb.

I drew my notched steel arming sword in an upward rising strike, driving the point straight through the automaton's neck-joint, severing the fiber-optic neural bundle. The machine went rigid, sparks sputtering from its chest seam, and collapsed into the muck.

Christina dropped onto one knee beside me, leaning heavily on her rapier, her chest heaving with ragged, whistling gasps. When she wiped the sweat from her eyes and looked up at my face, her sharp smile twisted into something between disbelief and ferocious amusement.

"Well, look who finally crawled out of the gutter," she rasped, wiping a smear of black machine grease from her lip with the back of her leather gauntlet. "The little Princess Knight himself. Look at that arm. What did you do, dip your sleeve in diamond?"

"Save the banter, Christina," I said, offering her my right hand to haul her upright. "You're burning through your code. Your hands are shaking."

"Occupational hazard," she muttered, gripping my palm with iron strength, though the muscle spasms in her forearm were obvious through her glove. "Our dear Kaiser decided our contract had expired. Issued a decree at midnight: all non-tethered populations in Sector 4 are classified as biological memory leaks. We were ordered to herd them into the foundries to be processed into fuel."

Captain Jun stepped back from the barricade, her heavy shield dripping with black coolant, her visor turning toward me. The single glowing slit behind her iron faceplate widened in recognition.

"Yuuki," Jun said, her voice dropping an octave in solemn recognition. "You made it through the southern perimeter. We received Kasumi’s flare ten minutes ago, but we assumed you were pinned down at the Grand Oak."

"Caon is holding the hollow," I replied, keeping my eyes on the smoking breach where the second siege crustacean was reloading its pneumatic ram. "The southern pylons are damaged. But Kaiser’s automated response is routing the entire central garrison down the main viaducts. This citadel will be surrounded within an hour."

"We know," Jun said grimly. She unbuckled her dented tower shield, tossing the broken slab of iron aside to draw her broadsword with two hands. "We never intended to hold this stone. Our only duty is to keep the northern drawbridge open until the last civilian transport clears the ravine. Once they reach the karst caverns, Caon can guide them into the deep hills. My oath was sworn to the people of Landosol, not to a porcelain god who feeds on their memories."

"And what about you?" I asked, looking at the cracks in her armor. "When the gate falls, Nightmare has no exit."

Jun looked toward the upper terraces of the palace that loomed directly behind the barracks, its soaring white towers piercing the leaden clouds like jagged icicles.

"We stay," Jun said, her voice entirely devoid of fear. "A knight does not run while the baggage train is in the pass. But you cannot stay here, Yuuki. The main gates to the upper royal district are sealed beneath twelve feet of solid cast iron. Kaiser has dropped the portcullises and electrified the ramparts."

"Then how do I get to the Spire?"

Christina gave a dry, rattling chuckle, sheathing her rapier with a sharp click. "Through the sewers, little prince. Where all honest rats do their business."

Jun reached to her utility belt and detached a heavy, triangular brass key-wrench, pressing the cold metal into my right palm.

"Beneath the central stable blocks lie the primary hydraulic pumping vaults," Jun explained, pointing with her steel gauntlet toward a heavy stone bulkhead set into the courtyard floor. "Those vaults circulate the freezing brine used to cool the root engine of the Tower. Follow the high-pressure conduits northward. They bypass the palace walls entirely, discharging into the subterranean maintenance corridors beneath the Mirror Archives."

"Labyrinth," I said, recognizing the layout.

"Akira is down there," Christina added, her voice tightening with rare solemnity. "Her frequency is fluctuating wildly. If she loses control of the wireframe rift before you reach her, the entire lower city collapses into null-space. Don't waste your time playing hero here with us. Go down into the dark and break that white witch's neck."

A heavy mortar shell shrieked through the smoke, exploding against the parapet above our heads. A shower of burning masonry and iron splinters rained down across the flags, peppering our leather coats.

Down in the courtyard, Tomo’s whistle sounded three sharp blasts: the final wagon had cleared the sally port.

Jun took a deep, steady breath behind her iron visor, rolling her shoulders to reset her stance, and planted her boots firmly before the breach.

"Move, Yuuki," she commanded, raising her great broadsword with both gauntlets. "The bulkhead is unlatched. Don't look back."

I gripped the cold brass key-wrench in my fist, nodded to the two knights, and sprinted toward the stone bulkhead in the stable yard.

Behind me, the clash of steel and the roar of Christina’s battle cry erupted anew as the next wave of porcelain soldiers crashed into the gate.

I hauled open the heavy iron hatch, dropped into the damp, echoing darkness of the flooded shaft, and pulled the lid shut behind me.
