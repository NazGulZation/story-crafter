# Chapter 11: The Starlit Covenant (Twinkle Wish Route)

The air around the high altar tasted of ozone, copper, and salt. 

Sixty seconds remained on the emergency diagnostics. Across the fifty mercury panels lining the shattered walls of the cathedral, the warning glyphs flashed in violent, pulsing crimson, casting rhythmic flashes of blood-red light over the ruins. Overhead, the iron hoops surrounding the Heart of the First Desire spun with a deafening, supersonic shriek, whipping the rising miasma into a swirling funnel that clawed at the ribbed vaulting sixty cubits above.

We took our positions upon the white marble flags of the altar dais.

Rei knelt at my right side, driving the point of her rapier three inches into the seam between two stone pavers. She placed both her gloved hands over the crossguard, bowing her head as the blue aura of her soul flared into a steady, razor-sharp perimeter of protective force.

Hiyori knelt at my left side. She planted both her iron-shod gauntlets flat against the marble, bowing her broad shoulders until her forehead nearly touched the stone. The golden solar fire in her veins roared to life, casting an amber dome of radiant heat over our heads that held the falling debris and toxic cinder at bay.

Between them stood Yui, her white staff raised toward the screaming chrysalis. Her small hands were trembling violently, but her jaw was set with quiet, unwavering resolve.

"Yuuki..." Yui whispered, her amber eyes reflecting the spinning violet vortex above. "I'm ready. Take my hands."

I reached out and took her fingers in mine. 

My right hand was flesh, warm and sticky with sweat and grime; my left hand was solid sapphire crystal, cold and hard as a glacial moraine. When our skin touched, a sharp, electric chime rang through the cathedral, vibrating through our teeth and into the marrow of our bones.

The Princess Knight core in my chest ignited with everything I had left.

There was no holding back, no rationing of reserves, no calculation of tomorrow’s strength. I opened the valve completely, tearing open the spiritual conduit that bound my soul to Twinkle Wish.

The connection slammed into us with the concussive weight of a falling portcullis.

My vision flared white. The physical cathedral dissolved around me, replaced by the vast, luminous expanse of our shared subconscious. 

I saw Yui’s memories unfolding like illuminated parchment: the small courtyard behind the guildhall where we had gathered fallen plums, the smell of sweet wheat bread baking in her mother's brick oven, the warm afternoon sun slanting across her face as she shyly offered me a homemade bento wrapped in checked cloth. I saw the pure, fragile dawn of her original prayer—a prayer that had asked for nothing more than a world where the people she loved could smile together beneath the open sky.

And then, roaring like a black avalanche down the conduit, came Kaiser’s corruption.

The inverted wish poured into my chest.

It was an ocean of liquid despair. It carried the suffocating apathy of eight hundred thousand sleeping citizens—the weight of exhausted farmers who had surrendered their plows, the silence of mothers who had watched their children taken to the neural towers, the bitter, cynical calculation of Kaiser Insight, who believed that humanity could only be saved by stripping away their will to feel.

The miasma tore into my lungs like inhaled lye.

My physical flesh rebelled instantly. Blood erupted from my nose and mouth, splattering across Yui’s white robes in dark crimson blossoms. The sapphire crystal on my left arm began to groan under the sheer thermodynamic pressure, hairline fractures spiderwebbing across the polished stone as it fought to contain the torrent. The blue luminescence turned a sickly, bruised violet, the crystalline growth spreading violently across my collarbone, creeping up my jaw, encasing my left ear in cold mineral slag.

"Yuuki!" Hiyori cried out, her voice raw with agony. 

She poured her solar fire into my left side, the golden heat washing over my ribs like molten bronze to keep my heart from freezing under the miasma’s icy grip. The strain tore through her own muscles, her shoulders trembling, the leather straps of her armor groaning as she anchored my physical frame to the stone.

"Hold the line, Hiyori!" Rei shouted, her voice ringing like a struck anvil through the howling gale.

Rei’s rapier sang with pure, diamond-hard focus. She channeled her piercing discipline directly into my nervous system, acting as an external spine, steadying the erratic tremors in my right arm and keeping the mana conduits from rupturing under the catastrophic pressure.

"Yui!" I forced the words past my swollen, bloody tongue, looking straight into the girl’s tear-filled eyes. "Sing it! The song of the spring maidens! Let them hear the real wish!"

Yui drew a shuddering breath, her fingers tightening around mine with all the strength in her body.

She began to sing.

Her voice was cracked at first, raspy from smoke and tears, but as she opened her throat, the ancient melody of Astraea’s dawn hymns swelled through the ruined nave. It was a simple, rustic tune—the song the market women sang when the first melon carts arrived from the south, the lullaby fathers hummed to put their infants to sleep when the autumn gales rattled the window shutters.

As the notes rose, the golden starlight dormant within her soul responded.

Inside my chest, the Princess Knight core acted as a massive catalytic furnace. 

The black miasma did not destroy me; it encountered the stubborn, irrational heat of four souls bound together in absolute love. The despair was compressed, filtered through the agony of my burning marrow, and distilled through Yui’s voice. 

The violet bile burning my veins began to clear.

Where the poison had coursed through my arteries, liquid starlight took its place. The sapphire crystal encasing my left arm ceased its erratic groaning; instead, it began to shine with an unbearable, blinding golden radiance that outshone the sun.

Overhead, the Heart of the First Desire reached its tipping point.

The iron hoops surrounding the chrysalis seized with a catastrophic screech of sheared brass. The translucent violet crystal shell buckled inward, unable to maintain its inverted geometry against the pure, undiluted resonance pouring upward from the altar.

"Now!" Yui wept, raising her staff high above her head. "Yuuki! Release it!"

I lifted my crystallized left arm toward the ceiling vaults.

"Break the cradle!" I roared, throwing every remaining fragment of my life, my memories, and my spirit into the sky.

A pillar of pure golden starlight erupted from my palm.

The beam struck the Heart of the First Desire dead center. 

The chrysalis did not detonate with concussive destruction. It shattered into a billion glittering embers of golden starlight, each spark singing with the sweet, clear pitch of a crystal chime. The heavy iron hoops sheared their rivets, tumbling harmlessly into the transept where they clattered against the stone like empty hoops from an old wine barrel.

The shockwave of golden light expanded outward from the cathedral nave.

It tore through the shattered roof, blowing the leaded sheets of mercury glass out into the square in a swirling vortex of harmless diamond dust. It surged down the royal avenue, sweeping through the burning petal drifts and washing over the ruined library. Wherever the golden wave touched, the toxic violet smog was incinerated, replaced by a warm, fragrant wind that smelled of wild clover, blossoming lilacs, and fresh rain.

High above Landosol, the starlight struck the artificial sky.

The heavy leaden dome that Kaiser had hung over the city began to crack. A circular fracture, five hundred paces across, opened directly above the cathedral spires. The dull grey clouds rolled back like an unfastened curtain, dissolving into thin air to reveal what had been hidden for three long years.

It was night.

A deep, velvet-blue sky stretched into infinity, dusted with millions of cold, sparkling silver stars. And in the east, resting on the sharp silhouette of the royal mountains, the faint, golden rim of the rising sun began to cast its first long rays of morning light across the rooftops of Landosol.

Down in the cathedral, the light faded.

The high vaults settled into deep, echoing silence. The only sound was the gentle patter of cooling mortar falling from the ceiling, and the ragged, shallow breathing of four figures kneeling upon the altar steps.

Hiyori’s gauntlets were cold, smoking faintly in the morning air. She sat back on her heels, wiping her soot-stained forehead with a trembling hand, staring up through the shattered roof at the stars in stunned, breathless awe.

"The sky..." Hiyori whispered, her voice barely a breath. "Rei... look up... it's the real sky..."

Rei pulled her rapier from the marble flags, sheathing it with hands that shook so violently the scabbard clicked three times against her belt. She lifted her face toward the opening in the clouds, her sharp blue eyes shining with quiet, unspilled tears as the first rays of morning sun touched her brow.

"We did it," Rei murmured softly. "The wish is free."

Then she looked down.

"Yuuki!"

My grip on Yui’s hand had loosened. 

The golden radiance had extinguished from my crystal arm, leaving it dull, opaque, and cold as a gravestone. Inside my chest, the frantic pounding had stopped, replaced by a slow, erratic flutter that grew fainter with every passing second. My knees slipped from beneath me, my body pitching forward onto the cold marble flags of the altar.

Yui caught me before my head hit the stone.

She fell to her knees, pulling my head into her lap, her arms wrapping around my shoulders with desperate, frantic terror. 

"Yuuki! No, no, no... please, look at me!" Yui cried, pressing her hands against my cheeks, trying in vain to warm the cold, waxy skin of my face. "Open your eyes! We broke the core! The sky is open! We can go back to the guildhall now! We can make clover bread! Yuuki!"

Hiyori and Rei scrambled across the dais, throwing themselves down beside us. 

Rei caught my limp right wrist, her fingers pressing frantically against my radial artery. Her face drained of all color, her lips parting in silent, horrified realization.

"His core..." Rei whispered, her voice cracking. "The mana conduit... it's completely burned out. He absorbed the entire reserve..."

"No!" Hiyori roared, slamming her bare fist into the marble floor, her knuckles splitting against the stone. "He can't! We promised! We promised we'd see the sunrise together!"

I tried to speak, but only a thin breath escaped my lips. 

My vision was narrowing, the cathedral vaults dissolving into a soft, warm blur of amber morning light. Yet beneath the cold creeping through my chest, there was no pain anymore. The suffocating weight of the cradle had lifted. The screams in the dark had ended.

Above us, through the jagged opening in the roof, the morning star shone bright and steady against the blue.
