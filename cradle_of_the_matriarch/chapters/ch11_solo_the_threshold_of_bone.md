# Chapter 11: The Threshold of Bone (True Solo Route)

The bronze hinges of the outer portal groaned like dying cattle as I pushed the door ajar, stepping into the circular expanse of the Grand Antechamber.

The hall was seventy paces in diameter, enclosed by a colonnade of twenty fluted pillars carved from midnight-black basalt. Overhead, the dome soared fifty cubits into the gloom, painted with the ancient planetary orbits of the Astraean system—silver stars linked by thin traceries of inlaid electrum that glittered faintly in the cold white glare spilling from the floor grates. 

In the center of the hall, standing in an unbroken defensive crescent before the golden doors of the inner sanctum, waited the Nine Archons of the Cradle.

They were Kaiser’s ultimate martial masterpieces. 

Nine feet tall, forged from white porcelain fused to blackened titanite skeletons, they stood motionless as cathedral statues. Unlike the mindless porcelain sentinels of the lower districts, each Archon possessed an organic neural core harvested from the combat avatars of fallen champions from three hundred erased cycles. Their porcelain helms were sculpted in the serene likeness of classical funerary masks, topped by brass halos that spun with a slow, hypnotic hum.

Three carried curved executioner cleavers of tempered steel. Three carried eight-foot suppression shields and heavy impact maces. Three carried paired recurve bows strung with braided piano wire, their arrow quivers filled with hollow glass needles containing condensed deletion venom.

As my boots touched the basalt floor, the eighteen optical slits on their helms ignited in unison with a blinding, razor-sharp crimson glare.

Nine brass halos accelerated from a slow hum to a supersonic scream.

No synthetic broadcast warned me. No herald announced the edict. 

The three archers drew their piano-wire strings to their ears in the space of a single heartbeat.

*THWIP-THWIP-THWIP.*

Three glass needles sliced through the air with a supersonic snap. 

I didn't try to parry the needles. Glass ammunition would shatter on contact, releasing its toxic deletion venom into the air.

I dove sideways, rolling across the cold basalt as the three needles struck the flagstones where I had been standing. The glass pulverized on impact, splashing pools of glowing violet acid that hissed violently against the stone, eating two-inch craters into the solid rock.

Before my shoulder finished the roll, the three cleaver-wielding Archons were already upon me.

They moved with the horrifying, unnatural fluidity of weapon masters stripped of human fatigue. The lead automaton closed twenty paces in two strides, bringing its curved cleaver down in a vertical executioner's chop aimed straight at my collarbone.

I rolled onto my knees, swinging my crystallized left forearm upward.

The heavy steel cleaver struck the sapphire carapace of my arm with a deafening, bell-like chime.

The concussive shockwave traveled through my arm, driving me back three inches into the stone, hairline fractures spiderwebbing across the sapphire plates over my wrist. But the crystal held. 

Before the machine could recover its balance, I drove my notched steel broadsword straight through its cervical ring with my flesh right hand.

The steel tip penetrated the neck bearing, severing the central hydraulic line. 

A high-pressure jet of black synthetic oil sprayed across my face and chest, blinding my left eye. The Archon’s brass halo seized up, its limbs locking rigid as it pitched forward onto the basalt, crashing into a heap of dead porcelain.

One down. Eight left.

A concussive roar exploded behind my ear.

The second cleaver-wielder swung horizontally, its blade catching me across the left flank. The steel sheared through my wool cloak and leather tunic, biting into the flesh along my ribs in a searing, four-inch crimson furrow. The blow spun me around, my right boot slipping in the pool of hydraulic oil on the floor.

Before I could recover my footing, a heavy suppression shield slammed into my chest like an anvil dropped from a bell tower.

The concussive force drove the air from my lungs in a bloody spray. 

I flew twelve paces backward through the air, crashing hard against the fluted base of a basalt column. My head snapped back against the stone, white sparks exploding behind my retinas, my ribs screaming with the sharp, stabbing agony of two cracked bones. 

My broadsword slipped from my fingers, skittering five paces across the polished floor.

The two shield-wielders advanced with rhythmic, synchronized strides, locking their tower shields together into an unbroken wall of blackened titanite, their heavy maces raised to crush my skull against the pillar. Behind them, the three archers knocked a second volley of glass needles, their optical sights locking onto my throat.

I lay against the base of the pillar, my breath coming in ragged, bloody gasps.

My left eye was caked in oil and blood. My ribs felt like jagged knives grinding together with every inhalation. My right hand was empty, trembling on the cold stone.

Through the ringing in my ears, Kaiser’s voice drifted down from the dome above, mocking and cold.

"Do you see now, little knight? You are alone. No one is coming to shield your flank. No one is coming to cast a healing spell. You threw away your friends, and now you will die like a rat in an empty hallway."

I looked across the basalt floor.

Five paces away, my notched steel sword rested on the stone. 

On its leather grip, the dark stains of old sweat and blood were clearly visible—the sweat of hundreds of sparring sessions in the sunny guild courtyard, the dried blood of beasts fought in the southern forests, the memories of a life lived beside people who loved me enough to die for me.

They weren't here. 

They weren't here because I had chosen to stand in the fire so they could live in the sun.

And as long as breath remained in my lungs, the fire would not reach them.

I reached out with my right hand. 

I didn't reach for the sword; I reached for the broken copper conduit running along the base of the basalt pillar beside my head.

The conduit carried forty thousand volts of auxiliary power to the ceiling lanterns.

I grabbed the severed copper cable with my crystallized left palm.

"Eat this," I growled through blood-slicked teeth.

I channeled the raw high-voltage current directly through my Princess Knight core, using my sapphire crystal as a step-up transformer, and discharged the entire load through the floor grates.

A blinding web of blue lightning erupted across the basalt flagstones.

The wet pools of hydraulic oil and coolant fluid instantly conducted the electrical surge across the entire hall. The two shield-wielders, standing knee-deep in the conductive muck, were hit by a catastrophic arc of forty thousand volts.

Their internal gyros overloaded in a blinding flash of blue plasma. 

The porcelain armor on their chests exploded outward like heated shale, their hydraulic servos seizing up with a deafening screech of sheared gears. The two nine-foot juggernauts toppled sideways, their heavy maces clattering uselessly against the stone.

I broke from the base of the pillar.

I dove across the floor, scooping up my notched broadsword in a smooth, continuous motion, and drove the blade into the cervical joint of the first downed shield-bearer, twisting the hilt to pulverize its neural core. 

Without pausing, I leaped over its smoking chassis, using its titanite pauldron as a springboard.

I flew twenty feet through the air, descending upon the three archers before they could loose their glass needles.

My broadsword cut through the air with a savage, whistling hiss.

The first archer lost both its mechanical arms at the elbows, the piano-wire bow falling to the floor with a dull clatter. I brought my crystallized left elbow down across its porcelain faceplate, pulverizing the ceramic mask into chalk dust and driving its chassis hard into the flagstones.

The second archer tried to draw a backup dagger from its hip, but I closed the distance inside its guard. 

I grabbed its neck with my crystal hand, lifting the nine-foot machine two inches off the stone through sheer, adrenaline-fueled fury, and rammed its skull repeatedly into the basalt pillar until the brass halo cracked in half and its optical lenses shattered into dark glass powder.

The remaining three Archons—the two surviving cleaver-wielders and the last archer—backed away toward the golden doors, their crimson optical slits flickering with erratic, panicked diagnostic loops. 

For the first time since their creation, the autonomous combat engines were encountering a combatant who did not conform to their probability algorithms. 

He had no armor. He had no shield. His left ribs were broken, his face was bathed in blood, and his body was half-mineral. 

Yet he advanced with the slow, terrifying, inevitable rhythm of an executioner who had already accepted his own death.

"What... what are you?" the last archer spoke, its synthetic voice glitching between three stolen voices—a knight, a mercenary, a beastfolk huntress. "Probability of survival: zero percent. Why... do you not... cease function?"

I walked toward them, dragging the point of my notched blade along the basalt floor, leaving a long, screaming trail of bright orange sparks in the dark.

"Because," I said, my voice low, raspy, and hard as granite, "I promised them tomorrow."

The three Archons charged together in a final, desperate convergence.

The two cleavers came from the flanks in a scissor strike; the archer leaped from above with its titanite dagger aimed at my throat.

I didn't dodge. I didn't parry.

I stepped straight into the center of the cross.

The left cleaver punched through my right shoulder, severing the deltoid muscle in a burst of arterial crimson. The right cleaver buried two inches into the sapphire crystal covering my left ribs, cracking the mineral carapace to the bone.

The pain was a white star detonating in my brain.

I used the impact to lock both blades within my body. 

I clamped my left arm down, trapping the right cleaver against my crystalline ribs, and with my bleeding right hand, I dropped my broadsword, grabbed the left cleaver's blade with my bare fingers, and locked it in place.

The two Archons were pinned to me.

The third Archon descended from above, its dagger inches from my face.

"Princess Knight..." I whispered, drawing on the deepest, most sacred well of my soul. "Purge."

The sapphire core in my chest exploded in a 360-degree radial blast of pure cobalt solar mana.

The shockwave blasted outward from my heart like a spherical blade of light.

It sheared through the two cleaver-wielders at the waist, blowing their titanite torsos clean off their legs in a deafening crack of thunder. The descending archer was caught dead center, its porcelain body vaporizing instantly into white ash that swirled into the ceiling dome.

The two halves of the cleaver-bearers crashed to the floor behind me, their severed gears spinning weakly in pools of burning oil.

The hall fell dead silent.

Nine Archons. Nine broken heaps of smoking porcelain, blackened titanite, and twisted brass.

I stood alone in the center of the circular hall.

Blood dripped steadily from my right fingertips, pooling on the dark basalt. My left arm hung heavy, cracked and smoking, the blue luminescence beneath the stone pulsing with a faint, dying flutter. My breath came in ragged, wheezing gasps that tasted of iron and sulfur.

I bent down, my fingers numb and slippery with oil, and retrieved my notched broadsword from the floor.

I wiped the blade on the charred hem of my cloak.

Ten paces ahead, rising thirty cubits above the blood-soaked basalt, stood the colossal golden gates of the Sanctum of Sol. 

Beyond those gates sat Kaiser Insight, alone upon her bio-mechanical throne, waiting for the end of the world.

I didn't stop to bandage my wounds. I didn't stop to catch my breath.

I climbed the final five marble steps, planted my boot against the seam between the golden doors, and kicked.
