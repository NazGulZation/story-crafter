# Chapter 8: The Outer Climb (True Solo Route)

The iron ladder was bolted directly into the sheer exterior glass of the Tower of Sol, three thousand cubits above the roofs of Landosol.

When I stepped out onto the open maintenance gantry, the wind hit me with the concussive force of a battering ram. The gale was freezing, driven down from the high troposphere where the leaden dome of Kaiser’s cradle met the edge of the void. Sleet whipped horizontally across the black iron rungs, encasing the metal in a half-inch crust of clear rime ice that cracked like glass beneath the tread of my boots.

I reached up with my right hand, my worn leather glove gripping the freezing rung. 

The cold bit through the wet cowhide instantly, numbing the pads of my fingers until they felt like blunt sticks of wood. My left arm, encased in its heavy carapace of sapphire crystal, dragged against my shoulder like an anvil. The weight pulled relentlessly at my frozen collarbone, threatening to peel my grip from the ladder with every violent gust that roared around the curve of the spire.

Beneath my heels, the city of Landosol spread out into the freezing fog like an anatomical drawing.

I could see the five smoking battlefields below. To the south, the faint green luminescence of the animal quarter flickered where Caon fought the harvesters; to the west, the orange flashes of artillery marked the citadel where Nightmare held the portcullis; beneath the center, the blue digital static of Labyrinth pulsed through the fissures; in the grand square, the violet smog of Twinkle Wish’s altar rolled over the colonnades; and at the foot of the palace, the royal kitchens stood silent and cold.

They were waiting for their Princess Knight. 

Every single one of them was holding the line, pouring out their blood and their tears, believing that if they just held out long enough, their knight would arrive to turn the tide.

I looked down at the empty plinth on the rotunda below, where my communications prism lay silent under its lead rivet.

"No," I whispered into the howling storm, the words freezing instantly against my cracked lips. "Not this time."

In every cycle I had lived, I had answered their call. I had joined their charges, ignited their strikes, and stood beside them as they burned away their lives to buy me an inch of ground. And every single time, Kaiser had simply turned their love into fuel, harvesting their broken bodies to feed the eternal cradle while I died in their arms, leaving them to wake up in a reset world with their memories hollowed out.

If the bill for this world had to be paid, it would be paid in one coin.

Mine.

I kicked the ice from the next rung and hauled myself upward into the blinding grey mist.

Ten rungs. Twenty rungs. Fifty rungs.

The climb was a grueling, rhythmic test of physical endurance. My boots slipped on the glaze, my knees banging hard against the iron stanchions, leaving dark bruises through my woolen trousers. My right shoulder burned with the deep, sickening ache of torn muscle fibers as it bore the double burden of my torso and my mineral arm. Every breath was a razor drawn across my throat, the thin, sulfur-choked atmosphere searing my bronchial tubes until I tasted the familiar coppery tang of internal bleeding.

A mechanical hum cut through the shriek of the blizzard.

Fifty cubits above me, an automated security pod detached from the glass wall of the tower.

It was an Imperial Sentry Node—a four-foot sphere of burnished brass and optical lenses, suspended in the howling air by three counter-rotating magnetic gyros. A high-intensity magnesium searchlight flared to life in its center, its blinding white beam cutting through the driving sleet like a physical spear, sweeping across the glass facade until it locked directly onto my silhouette.

A harsh, synthetic klaxon began to pulse through the hull:
**[UNAUTHORIZED EXTERNAL SCALER DETECTED — LETHAL PURGE AUTHORIZED]**

Beneath the sphere's brass belly, a twin rotary repeater swung downward, its six steel barrels spinning with a high-pitched pneumatic whine.

I didn't try to climb backward. On a frozen ladder three thousand cubits in the air, retreat was suicide.

I unhitched the leather retaining strap from my scabbard with my thumb.

As the sentry's barrels aligned, I let go of the ladder with my right hand. 

I hooked my crystallized left elbow around the vertical iron riser, locking the heavy sapphire forearm behind the steel bar. The sudden shock of my entire falling weight wrenched violently through my left shoulder joint, the sapphire plates groaning as they bit into my collarbone, but the stone held fast.

With my free right hand, I drew my notched steel broadsword.

The repeater opened fire.

A storm of lead shot chewed through the air, sparking violently against the glass facade and severing the two iron rungs directly above my head. The concussive blasts deafened my right ear, showering my face with fragments of pulverized cast iron and smoking lead.

I waited for the rhythm. 

Every automated repeater had a three-quarter-second indexing pause between six-round drums to allow the pneumatic feeder to cycle. 

The fifth shot screamed past my cheek, slicing a clean crimson furrow across my earlobe. The sixth shot punched through the tail of my wool cloak.

*Click-whir.*

The feeder paused.

I lunged upward with my right arm, throwing my broadsword like a javelin.

The notched blade cut through the howling gale, spinning end-over-end across fifteen feet of empty space. The steel tip struck the sentry’s central gyro housing with pinpoint accuracy, burying three inches into the brass gear-casing.

The internal clockwork gears sheared with a catastrophic, screeching shriek. 

The magnetic stabilization field collapsed instantly. The heavy brass sphere wobbled violently, its counter-rotating rings seizing up as smoke poured from its vents. It tipped forward, broke away from its magnetic track, and plummeted three thousand cubits into the freezing fog below, trailing a spiral of orange sparks until it vanished into the abyss.

My sword remained pinned in the shattered mounting bracket bolted to the tower wall, twelve feet above my head.

I reached up with both hands, my boots finding traction on the twisted remains of the severed rungs, and climbed.

When I reached the mounting bracket, I wrenched my blade free with a sharp twist of my wrist. The steel was cold as an icicle, coated in black hydraulic grease, but the edge was unbroken. I wiped the grease on my cloak, slid the sword back into its worn leather scabbard, and looked up into the storm.

We were entering the cloud ceiling.

Here, at four thousand cubits, the Tower of Sol changed its architecture. 

The smooth glass exterior gave way to massive ribbed rings of dull, riveted iron that circled the circumference of the spire like the vertebrae of a colossal leviathan. Every thirty paces, gigantic exhaust ports vented superheated steam from the city’s lower converters, producing violent, unpredictable thermal drafts that clashed against the freezing mountain gales. 

One moment, the air was thirty degrees below freezing, encrusting my eyelashes with ice; the next moment, a blast of five-hundred-degree sulfur steam washed across the ladder, scalding the skin of my cheeks and turning the ice on my cloak into boiling water.

My lungs were burning. 

The sapphire crystal along my ribs flared with every thermal transition, expanding and contracting against my flesh, sending sharp, stabbing spasms of agony through my ribcage that forced me to stop every ten rungs to dry-heave against the iron bars. 

Yet beneath the physical torment, my mind was absolute, crystalline stillness.

There was no fear left. There was no hesitation.

When you walk into the dark alone, you leave behind the terrible, paralyzing anxiety of protecting others. You don't have to look over your shoulder to see if Kokkoro is out of breath. You don't have to calculate whether Pecorine has enough mana to survive the next blow. You don't have to watch Karyl scream as a collar digs into her neck, or listen to Yui weep over a broken wish.

The battlefield becomes pure, simple geometry: the distance between my blade and Kaiser's throat.

At five thousand cubits, the vertical ladder ended at a cantilevered maintenance catwalk that wrapped around the northern face of the spire.

I hauled myself over the rusted iron railing, collapsing onto my hands and knees upon the steel mesh flooring. My breath came in ragged, wheezing gasps that rattled in my chest like gravel in a dry gourd. I pulled off my right glove; my fingers were mottled purple and white from frostbite, the nails cracked and oozing pale lymph. 

I clenched my fist three times, forcing the blood back into the stiff joints through sheer force of will, until the pain returned in a searing, throbbing wave that told me the nerves were still alive.

Ahead of me on the catwalk, recessed into the dark iron flank of the tower, stood the primary coolant intake hatch: **Airlock 0-Zero**.

It was a heavy, circular hatch of three-inch cast steel, secured by an eight-spoke manual dogging wheel. Above the wheel, an embossed brass indicator plate glowed with a single amber diagnostic rune:
**[PRIMARY HELIUM CONDUIT — INNER CORE TEMPERATURE: -180°C — MANUAL OVERRIDE ENGAGED]**

This was the pressurized vein. 

The internal circulation route that carried cryogenic refrigerant from the atmospheric scrubbers directly into the central biological processor where Kaiser Insight was entombed. No human soldiers guarded this passage; the temperature and pressure inside were lethal to organic flesh within ten minutes of exposure.

I stepped up to the hatch.

I planted my boots wide on the steel grating, grabbed the eight-spoke wheel with both hands—my frostbitten flesh fingers locking around the right spoke, my sapphire crystal palm gripping the left—and threw my entire body weight into the turn.

The three-inch dogs groaned against their brass seats. 

For three agonizing seconds, the wheel refused to move, rusted shut by decades of ice and neglected seals. I ground my teeth together until I heard the enamel creak, digging deep into the Princess Knight core in my chest, drawing a single thread of cold azure energy into my crystallized arm.

The sapphire plate flared blue.

*CLANG!*

The seal broke with the sound of a cannon shot. 

The heavy wheel spun two full rotations, releasing a deafening hiss of pressurized cryogenic nitrogen that blasted past the hatch edges in swirling white plumes. The steel door swung outward on its counterbalanced hinges, groaning against the howling mountain gale.

Beyond the threshold lay a pitch-black tunnel of ribbed titanium piping, sloping upward at a forty-five-degree angle into the freezing bowels of the machine.

I drew my notched steel sword, checked the buckle of my scabbard, and stepped across the sill into the dark.

The heavy steel hatch slammed shut behind me, locked by the automated pressure systems. 

The howling of the blizzard died instantly, swallowed by the terrifying, deafening roar of the machine's beating heart.
