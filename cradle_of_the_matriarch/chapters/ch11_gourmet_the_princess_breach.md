# Chapter 11: The Princess Breach (Gourmet Guild Route)

The iron platform of the freight lift ascended into the heart of the palace, its chains rattling against the vertical shaft with the slow, deliberate cadence of a funeral march. 

Fifty feet above us, the heavy bronze floor-hatches slid back with a hydraulic hiss, revealing a blinding glare of harsh, unblinking white light. The warm, cedar-scented air of the sub-vault vanished instantly, replaced by a draft so frigid it frosted the breath in our throats. The air reeked of liquid nitrogen, dry ozone, and the chemical sweetness of neural preservative fluid.

The platform locked into the floor with a concussive clang.

We stepped out onto the polished white marble flags of the Grand Throne Room of Astraea.

It was a cavernous hall, three hundred paces long, flanked by colossal fluted columns of white granite that supported a soaring barrel-vaulted ceiling sixty cubits overhead. Once, fifty silk tapestries depicting the bountiful harvests of the realm had hung from those columns, and the throne dais had been draped in royal crimson velvet. 

Kaiser Insight had transformed the seat of kings into the control nexus of her eternal machine.

The silk tapestries had been pulled down, replaced by towering vertical arrays of black glass diagnostic screens that flickered with cascading green hex code. Hundreds of thick, translucent neural cables cascaded down from the ceiling vaults like petrified waterfalls, converging upon the high dais at the far end of the hall. 

There, perched thirty feet above the marble floor upon a throne carved from petrified bone and brass piping, sat Kaiser Insight.

Her slender frame was encased in an extravagant gown of woven silver wire and white ermine, though her lower limbs were completely submerged into the bio-mechanical roots of the throne. Her golden hair fell in immaculate waves over her shoulders, framing a face of exquisite, terrifying perfection. Atop her brow rested the stolen Royal Crown of Astraea—a circlet of beaten gold set with a massive sun-ruby that burned with a sickening, pulsing crimson glare.

Her mismatched eyes—one gold, one emerald—drifted down the long expanse of the center carpet, taking in our battered armor, our frayed cloaks, and the weapons gripped in our hands.

A slow, mocking smile parted her red lips.

"The royal scullery girl returns to her dining room," Kaiser’s voice echoed through the vast chamber, amplified by the ceiling conduits until it reverberated inside our skulls with dizzying acoustic pressure. "Accompanied by a disgraced elven guide, a cowardly stray cat who traded imperial glory for cold scraps of fish, and my broken knight, whose body looks like a shattered glass lantern."

Kaiser rested her chin upon her palm, her manicured fingers tapping rhythmically against the armrest of the bone throne.

"Did you enjoy your little supper in the cellar, Eustiana?" Kaiser asked, her tone dripping with amused contempt. "Did the salted trout remind you of the father who looked into your face and saw an empty doorway? Did it warm the chill of knowing that in the memories of eight hundred thousand people, you do not exist?"

Pecorine stepped forward onto the central carpet.

Her golden armor, battered and wired together, caught the harsh white light with proud, unyielding brilliance. Her Royal Claymore rested across her shoulder, its steel gleaming like morning frost. Her chin was held high, her bright blue eyes blazing with a fierce, royal wrath that filled the cavernous hall like thunder.

"My name is Eustiana von Astraea," Pecorine declared, her voice ringing off the high granite columns with the majesty of a true queen. "You took my crown. You poisoned my city. You locked my people in a cradle of cold sleep because you were too cowardly to face the tomorrow that belongs to them. But I am still standing here. My family is standing beside me. And we have come to take back our home."

Kaiser’s amused smile vanished. Her mismatched eyes narrowed into slits of venomous fury.

"Insolent vermin," Kaiser hissed, her voice dropping into a harsh, metallic rasp. "You are an obsolete remnant of a failed timeline, Eustiana! You cling to messy feelings, to hunger, to grief, to the chaotic filth of human weakness! In my cradle, there is no hunger! There is no loss! There is only eternal, undisturbed perfection!"

Kaiser raised her right hand, her fingers snapping with the crack of a pistol shot.

"Purge the anomalies," she commanded. "Leave the knight’s core intact for harvesting. Pulverize the rest."

From the shadowed alcoves between the granite columns, twenty Imperial Paladins strode onto the marble. 

They were Kaiser’s personal guard—ten-foot-tall juggernauts forged from solid platinum armor, their faceless helms crowned with brass halos that crackled with arcs of white lightning. In their four mechanical hands, they held paired tower shields and eight-foot power lances that hummed with enough kinetic charge to shatter a fortress gate.

"They're deploying high-voltage barrier fields!" Karyl shouted, her staff raised, her green eyes flashing. "If we hit those shields head-on, the feedback will fry our nervous systems!"

"Then we don't hit them head-on," I said, stepping up between Pecorine and Karyl.

I placed my flesh right hand on Pecorine’s pauldron, my crystalline left hand on Karyl’s shoulder, and looked down at Kokkoro, who immediately placed her small palm against my waist.

"One strike, Gourmet Guild," I said softly, my voice hoarse from the crystal spreading through my vocal cords. "Give her everything we have."

The Princess Knight core in my chest detonated.

It was not a controlled release. It was the complete, catastrophic ignition of my remaining life force. 

The sapphire crystal on my chest fractured with a sound like breaking ice, the roots of the mineral biting clean through my pericardium. A spray of hot blood coated the inside of my teeth, running freely down my chin, but the wave of power that flooded into my family was absolute, infinite, and overwhelming.

The royal mana surged through them like a roaring sun.

Kokkoro’s sickle staff ignited with an emerald tempest of divine wind that ripped the lead cables from the walls, whipping up a gale so fierce it threw the twenty ten-foot paladins off their balance. 

Karyl leaped into the slipstream of Kokkoro's wind, her staff unleashing a cataclysmic barrage of dark matter spheres that drilled through the platinum tower shields like hot lead through butter, shattering their electrical halos in blinding explosions of purple plasma.

"Pecorine! The path is open!" Karyl screamed through the storm.

Pecorine charged.

She moved with the velocity of an imperial siege bolt, her boots obliterating the marble flagstones beneath her, trailing a tail of blinding golden solar fire. She reached the throne dais in the space of three heartbeats, leaping thirty feet into the air with her heavy claymore raised above her head.

"Princess Strike!" Pecorine roared, bringing the golden blade down in an earth-shattering vertical cleave.

The blow struck the bio-mechanical roots of Kaiser’s throne.

The shockwave pulverized the marble dais into chalk dust. The thick neural cables severed with explosive pops, spewing gallons of purple coolant fluid across the floor. The bone throne split in half, hurling Kaiser backward onto the upper terrace, her silver gown torn, her golden hair tangled with shredded cables.

Kaiser scrambled back against the ruined wall, her face contorted in manic, hysterical terror as she saw the crown slip slightly upon her brow.

"No... no! I am the Matriarch! I am the sovereign of this world!" Kaiser shrieked. 

Her hands clawed at the air, channeling the entire remaining power reserve of the Tower of Sol directly into her palms. 

The white light in the throne room died, swallowed by a terrifying, pitch-black vortex of concentrated deletion mana that coalesced between Kaiser's hands. It shaped itself into the Lance of the Matriarch—a nine-foot spear of crackling, violet-black antimatter that hummed with the sound of a thousand screaming souls.

It was an attack designed to permanently erase Eustiana’s soul code from the world’s memory registers.

Pecorine had just landed on the terrace flags, her claymore buried two feet into the concrete floor, her muscles momentarily locked by the sheer kinetic recoil of her own strike. She looked up, her blue eyes widening as she saw the black lance aimed directly at her exposed chest.

"Die, Eustiana!" Kaiser screamed, hurling the lance with all the fury of her dying empire.

The spear tore through the air at supersonic speed.

Pecorine could not dodge. She could not pull her blade free in time.

I didn't think. I didn't calculate.

My boots left the shattered floor of the dais. 

I threw myself across the thirty paces of empty air, moving on instinct, on love, on the simple, absolute truth that a knight exists to stand between the monster and the girl who smiled at him in the sunlight.

I slammed into Pecorine’s shoulder, shoving her violently sideways off the terrace.

The Lance of the Matriarch struck me dead center.

The black spear drove through the left side of my chest. 

It punched straight through the sapphire crystal carapace, shattering the mineral into a thousand singing fragments, and buried itself three feet into my heart. 

The agony was beyond human comprehension. It was the sensation of being erased—every memory, every nerve ending, every atom of my blood being boiled into black static. The force of the blow lifted me into the air, pinning me against the wall above the ruined throne.

"Yuuki!" Pecorine’s scream tore through the hall, a sound of raw, shattered heartbreak that echoed off the high vaults.

"Ahahaha! It's over!" Kaiser laughed hysterically, clutching her bruised ribs as she pushed herself up from the rubble. "You sacrificed yourself for a starving ghost! You died for nothing, little knight!"

Kaiser took a step toward me, reaching out to rip the remaining crystal from my throat.

She never saw my right hand move.

My body was dead. My heart had stopped. But the soul of the Princess Knight did not belong to flesh and blood; it belonged to the promise I had made to the three girls who had given me a home.

With my dying breath, I pulled my notched steel sword from its scabbard with my flesh right hand.

I threw my entire weight forward, pulling my impaled chest off the black lance, and drove the steel blade straight down into the center of Kaiser's forehead.

The notched point struck the stolen Royal Crown of Astraea.

*CRACK!*

The golden sun-ruby shattered into a million scarlet shards. The beaten gold circlet snapped in two, flying from Kaiser’s brow to clatter across the broken stones.

Kaiser’s laughter choked in her throat. 

Her mismatched eyes widened in absolute, paralyzing horror as the tip of my blade bit into the bone between her brows. The royal mana dormant within the crown backfired instantly, flooding into her neural circuits in a catastrophic surge of pure, golden fire.

"Impossible..." Kaiser choked out, her voice dissolving into crackling electronic static. "How can you... still move...?"

"We... share the pot..." I forced the words past my bloody lips, a faint, bloody smile touching my face. "Down to the last bean."

I twisted the hilt.

A blinding blast of golden light erupted from Kaiser's skull. 

Her body convulsed violently, her flesh and wire disintegrating into ash and sparks as the bio-mechanical link to the Tower of Sol violently severed. With a final, synthetic shriek that died into the wind, Kaiser Insight vanished into a cloud of drifting grey cinders, extinguished forever from the kingdom she had tried to enslave.

My steel sword slipped from my numb fingers, clattering against the marble steps.

The strength left my legs. 

I pitched forward, falling from the terrace wall into the cold, empty air.

Strong, armored arms caught me before I hit the stone.

Pecorine pulled me against her chest, collapsing to her knees upon the pulverized dais. Her golden armor was drenched in my blood, her tears pouring down her face in an unstoppable flood as she pressed her bare hands against the gaping wound in my chest, desperately trying to hold the torn flesh and shattered crystal together.

"Yuuki! No! Hold on! Please, hold on!" Pecorine sobbed, her voice breaking into hysterical, frantic pleas. "Kokkoro! Karyl! Heal him! Use everything! Fix him! Please, fix him!"

Kokkoro and Karyl fell to their knees beside us, their hands glowing with green healing wind and purple restoration spells, pressing frantically against my chest. But the magic washed over my wound without taking hold; there was no vessel left to mend, no marrow left to knit.

The black lance had taken everything.

"Yuuki..." Karyl wept, clutching my limp right hand against her cheek, her tears wetting my calloused fingers. "You idiot... why did you jump? Why do you always have to jump...?"

Kokkoro could not speak. She buried her forehead against my neck, her small frame convulsing with silent, agonizing sobs that shook the stones beneath us.

I looked up at Pecorine.

The white artificial light in the throne room was dying. High above our heads, the ceiling conduits were shutting down, their hum fading into a deep, gentle stillness. Through the high, arched clerestory windows, the first rays of genuine morning sun began to pierce the darkness, painting the white marble columns in warm, golden amber.

"Eustiana..." I whispered, my voice barely a rustle of dry leaves.

"I'm here, Yuuki! I'm right here!" she cried, leaning down until her tear-streaked face was inches from mine. "You're going to be okay! We're going to the restaurant! We're going to have the corner table! You promised me!"

I lifted my right hand, my bloody fingers touching the soft skin of her cheek, tracing the line of her trembling jaw.

"The throne is yours again, Princess," I whispered, smiling with the last warmth left in my soul. "Keep the hearth warm... for me."

My hand slipped from her cheek, falling limp against the bloody marble.

My eyes drifted toward the high windows, watching the golden sunbeams illuminate the free city of Landosol. 

The table was set. The hunger was gone.
