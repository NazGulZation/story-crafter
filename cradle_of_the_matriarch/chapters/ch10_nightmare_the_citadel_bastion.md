# Chapter 10: The Citadel Bastion

The vaulted granite walls of the gatehouse shook with every concussive strike of the battering ram outside. 

Dust and powdered lime drifted down from the ceiling like cold snow, settling over our shoulders and coating our eyelashes with a fine, chalky film that stung our eyes. The six-inch oak timber of the main doors was bowing inward with terrifying, groaning strain; deep, splintered fractures ran along the grain where a heavy pneumatic siege drill was chewing into the center seam, sending showers of hot sawdust, iron rivets, and sparks bouncing across the wet floor.

In the center of the hall, illuminated by the flicker of a single iron storm lantern, sat the demolition manifold.

Thirty iron-hooped kegs of military-grade black powder were stacked three high against the load-bearing granite pillars that supported the gatehouse arch and the southern bridge pier. Bundles of hemp fuse-cord, thick as ship’s ropes and soaked in saltpeter, ran from each barrel into a central iron junction box mounted to the floor. The air inside the room was thick with the scent of sulfur, dry sawdust, cold rain, and the bitter copper tang of fresh blood.

Matsuri was on her knees beside the junction box, her grease-stained fingers frantically stripping the insulation from a bundle of copper ignition wires with a pair of rusty iron shears. Her short blonde pigtails were singed by sparks, her breathing coming in shallow, terrified gasps that rattled in her chest.

"The primary line is dead!" Matsuri cried, looking up with wide, frantic eyes that glistened with tears. "The mortar blast on the outer lintel severed the galvanic circuit! The spark-plugs won't fire from the rear trenches! The copper core is melted solid inside the masonry!"

Jun stepped over the powder kegs, her heavy steel boots clanking against the granite flags with heavy, measured deliberation. She knelt beside Matsuri, inspecting the severed wires with a calm, stoic eye behind her visored helm, testing the frayed ends with her steel gauntlet.

"Can you splice a slow-match?" Jun asked, her voice low, even, and devoid of panic.

"No, Captain!" Tomo shouted from the murder-holes above, where she was peering through the narrow arrow slits into the burning fog. "The entire bridge is packed with porcelain paladins! Two full regiments! They're shoulder-to-shoulder on the span, pushing forward behind heavy ceramic mantlets! If we wait five minutes for a slow-match to burn down, the siege drill will punch through the doors and their shock troops will overrun this room before the spark ever reaches the powder!"

Christina slowly pushed herself up from the floor, leaning against the cold stone of the wall. She drew her rapier, her thumb testing the razor-sharp tip against her leather gauntlet. 

"Then it's a manual strike," Christina said, her voice dropping all its usual flippant bravado, turning as sharp and cold as a winter morning. "Someone stays in the chamber. When the doors give way and their vanguard crowds into the hall, someone drives an alchemical fire-flask straight into the central powder keg."

The silence inside the gatehouse became heavier than the stone walls.

Outside, the siege drill shrieked, its hardened teeth grinding through the inner iron reinforcement plates with a deafening, metallic screech that vibrated through our teeth. A fissure of blinding white light broke through the center of the oak door, illuminating the dust-motes swirling around our heads.

Jun stood up, her armor creaking with strain. She unclasped her heavy black Nightmare cloak, folding the torn, soot-stained wool with deliberate, ceremonial care, and laid it across the top of the central powder keg like a pall over a coffin.

"Christina," Jun commanded, her voice ringing with the absolute, unquestionable authority of the Royal Guard's supreme captain. "Take Tomo, Matsuri, and the remaining watchmen. Fall back through the southern escape tunnel. The refugee convoy is entering the karst defiles. They have no cavalry screen. You are now the commander of the vanguard."

Christina went rigid. Her eyes widened, then narrowed into slits of dangerous, furious disbelief.

"Are you insane, Jun?" Christina snarled, taking a furious step forward, her rapier rattling against her thigh. "You're ordering me to run? To scurry through a rat hole like a frightened baker while you play the noble martyr on a powder keg?!"

"It is not a request, Christina," Jun replied, her iron visor locking onto Christina’s face with unyielding resolve. "It is my final order as your captain."

"Damn your orders!" Christina shouted, her voice echoing off the vaulted stone ceiling, cracked with raw, furious grief. "I am a Seven Crown! I am the strongest blade in this rotten world! If anyone is holding this bridge, it’s me! You think you get all the glory of an epitaph while I babysit a baggage train of screaming weavers and orphans?!"

"A knight does not die for glory, Christina!" Jun roared back, her voice shaking the stone walls with thunderous conviction. "A knight dies so that those who cannot hold a sword may live to see tomorrow! You are the deadliest fighter in Astraea! If Kaiser sends her aerial hunters or her shadow hounds into those karst hills, none of those ten thousand people will survive without you! They need a protector who can slaughter gods in the dark! That is your duty! That is your oath!"

Christina stared at her, her chest heaving, her knuckles white around her sword hilt. Tears broke from her eyes, cutting clean tracks through the soot on her flushed cheeks, her breath catching in her throat as she realized the cruel, inescapable truth of Jun’s words.

"You stubborn, thick-headed iron fool," Christina wept, her voice dropping into a broken rasp. "I hate you. I swear to the heavens, Jun, I hate you."

"I know," Jun said softly, reaching out with her steel gauntlet to gently touch Christina’s shoulder. "Now go. Protect them."

"Wait," I said, stepping between the two knights.

Both of them turned toward me.

I raised my left arm. The azure crystal was glowing with a fierce, blinding intensity, the blue luminescence pulsating in rhythm with the desperate pounding of my heart. The petrification had crept over the left half of my face, cold and unyielding, but my right hand was steady as I rested my palm upon the iron casing of the central powder barrel.

"Neither of you is dying here," I said, my voice cutting through the noise of the grinding drill. "Jun, you lead the refugees. Christina, you hold their flank."

"Yuuki, no," Jun said, reaching to pull me away from the barrels with her heavy gauntlet. "This is a knight’s burden. You are the Princess Knight. You must reach the summit—"

"Look at me, Jun," I interrupted, looking directly into the glowing slit of her visor. 

I pulled aside the torn, scorched leather of my collar. 

The azure crystal had engulfed my entire upper chest. The blue veins beneath the skin hummed as solid, incandescent filaments of raw, unstable Princess Knight resonance. My avatar code was on the absolute brink of thermodynamic collapse.

"I have hours left at best," I told them quietly, my voice calm in the shaking room. "My code is already burning out. But my core has the heat to detonate this powder instantaneously with pure mana-shock. If you stay, you die for a delay. If I stay, I buy you an entire dawn. Jun, lead your people into the hills. Don't let their sacrifice be for nothing."

Christina looked at my chest, then at the glowing crystal crawling up my neck, her breath trembling in her chest as the harsh reality sank into her sharp mind. She looked down at her rapier, her fingers loosening on the hilt.

"Yuuki..." she whispered, her voice stripped of all pride. "You... you really never planned on coming back down, did you?"

"No," I answered honestly. "I promised myself that no more of you would bleed for me. This is where the debt ends."

Jun stood motionless for three heartbeats. Her iron visor tilted down toward my hand resting on the powder keg. Then, slowly, with formal, reverent precision, she brought her right fist to her breastplate in a supreme knight’s salute.

"Tomo, Matsuri," Jun ordered softly, her voice thick with swallowed tears. "Into the tunnel. Now."

The oak doors gave a final, catastrophic shriek. 

The central drill punched completely through the wood, followed by the concussive slam of three heavy ceramic rams. The iron locking bar sheared in two with a sound like a cannon shot, and the heavy oak doors burst inward off their hinges in a blinding cloud of splintered timber, dust, and sparks, revealing the smoke-choked bridge packed with hundreds of advancing Porcelain Paladins, their white crescent poleaxes lowered for the kill.

The cold autumn wind howled through the gaping breach, carrying the bitter reek of burning sulfur and ozone. Behind me, the heavy stone slab of the escape tunnel slammed shut as Jun shoved Christina and the watchmen into the subterranean dark, sealing their passage to safety. 

I was alone with the powder kegs.

I drew my notched steel arming sword with my right hand, planted my boots on the blood-spattered flagstones of the threshold, and faced the roaring tide of porcelain white.
