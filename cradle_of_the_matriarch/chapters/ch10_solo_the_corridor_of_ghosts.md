# Chapter 10: The Corridor of Ghosts (True Solo Route)

The Corridor of Ghosts stretched eighty paces into the gloom, flanked on both sides by towering mirrors of polished black obsidian.

The floor was laid with slabs of purple porphyry stone, smooth as ice, reflecting the pale, sickly light of amber lanterns suspended from the vaulted ceiling by rusted brass chains. The air was uncomfortably warm, thick with the heavy perfume of dried nightshades, myrrh, and warm copper oil. 

Every breath tasted of dust and old honey.

I walked down the center of the porphyry aisle, my boots clicking softly against the polished stone. My right hand was locked around the hilt of my notched steel broadsword, held in a relaxed, low guard. My left arm, encased in its heavy carapace of sapphire crystal, dragged against my shoulder like an iron bar, its blue mana veins flickering with slow, deliberate pulses.

A soft, feminine laugh echoed from the walls.

It did not come from an iron speaker horn. It resonated directly inside my skull, sliding through my auditory nerves like warm silk.

"You really climbed the outside of my tower, little knight," Kaiser’s voice purred, filled with mocking, patronizing amusement. "Through five thousand cubits of freezing sleet, with one arm turned to stone and blood pooling in your boots. How delightfully dramatic. How utterly, predictably sentimental."

The first pair of obsidian mirrors flared to life.

Across the dark glass surfaces, ripples of emerald light coalesced into three-dimensional projections so vivid they seemed to breathe the same air.

On the left mirror, the ruined kitchen of the palace appeared. 

Pecorine sat alone on a broken flour barrel, her golden armor stripped of its brilliance, her face hollowed by starvation. In her trembling hands, she held an empty wooden trencher, her blue eyes wide and brimming with tears as she stared at the dark scullery doorway.

"Yuuki...?" the projection of Pecorine whispered, her voice cracking with agonizing despair. "Where are you...? We waited... Kokkoro made the fire, but it went out... my stomach hurts so much, Yuuki... why didn't you come down to the kitchen? Did you get tired of us? Did you leave us to starve in the dark...?"

Beside her, Kokkoro knelt in the dust, her delicate sickle staff broken across her lap, her small hands pressed over her face as she wept. 

"I guided you faithfully across three hundred cycles, my Lord..." Kokkoro sobbed softly into the silence. "Was my service so worthless to you? Why did you cast your humble guide aside to die alone on the heights?"

On the right mirror, Karyl stood against a brick wall, the brass collar around her throat flaring with blinding purple agony. Her fingers clawed frantically at the metal band until her nails tore, her emerald eyes wild with betrayed terror.

"You liar!" Karyl screamed, her voice echoing off the porphyry floor with deafening force. "You promised you'd protect me! You swore we were family! You left me here so Kaiser could turn me into a puppet again! You're just like all the rest of them! You run away and leave us to rot!"

The holographic projections reached out, their hands pressing against the obsidian glass, their tear-streaked faces distorted by desperate, pleading misery.

I stopped in the center of the aisle.

My heart gave a heavy, painful thud against my ribs. The sapphire crystal on my chest pinched my collarbone, sending a sharp wave of heat through my collar. 

I looked at the projections. I looked at the tears on Pecorine’s cheeks, at the sorrow in Kokkoro’s eyes, at the terror twisting Karyl’s features.

And then I kept walking.

My boots clicked rhythmically against the porphyry stone, measuring the distance without a tremor.

"Do you feel that, Yuuki?" Kaiser’s voice purred again, drifting from the ceiling vaults like incense smoke. "That is the mathematical reality of your failure. In every timeline you tried to save them, you only prolonged their agony. You dragged them into muddy trenches, forced them to swing rusted blades, and made them watch you die while their cities burned to cinder."

A second pair of mirrors ignited.

Now it was Twinkle Wish.

Yui was kneeling in the center of the weeping plaza, surrounded by knee-deep drifts of smoldering purple petals. Her white robes were torn, caked in blood, her white staff burning to ash in her hands. She looked up at the ceiling, her amber eyes hollow, dead, stripped of all light.

"You let the cradle take me, Yuuki..." Yui whispered, a single bloody tear tracing down her cheek. "I wished for everyone to smile... but you weren't there to hold my hand. The poison burned my lungs... and now everyone is asleep forever..."

Behind her, Rei lay motionless among the broken columns, her rapier snapped in two, her body half-submerged in violet sludge. Hiyori lay beside her, her iron gauntlets shattered, her red tunic scorched black by plasma fire.

"Look at them, Yuuki," Kaiser said, her voice softening into a tone of gentle, seductive reason. "Why must you fight? Why must you insist on the clumsy, brutal agony of physical struggle? Surrender your core to me. Step into the cradle."

The third pair of mirrors lit up, projecting a vision of breathtaking, golden splendor.

It was a grand dining hall bathed in warm afternoon sunlight, its high arched windows open to green meadows and clear blue skies. At the center of the room sat a massive table of polished oak, laden with golden roast meats, steaming loaves of sweet clover bread, platters of ripe fruits, and sparkling decanters of spiced cider.

Around that table sat everyone.

Pecorine was laughing, heaping roasted potatoes onto Karyl’s trencher while Karyl pretended to complain with flushed, happy cheeks. Kokkoro was pouring mint tea with a serene smile. Yui, Hiyori, and Rei were sitting together by the open window, weaving crowns of wild daisies. Makoto and Maho were sharing sweet melons with the animal quarter children; Jun and Christina were raising flagons in a joyful toast to peace.

At the head of that table sat an empty walnut chair, carved with a royal crest.

"I can give you this, Yuuki," Kaiser whispered, her voice warm, intimate, promising an eternity of unblemished joy. "In my network, this dream is real. Their consciousnesses will be preserved forever in this paradise. No one will ever starve. No one will ever age. No one will ever have to bleed on a cold stone floor or mourn a lost companion. All you have to do is drop that rusted sword, lay down upon my altar, and let your core merge with mine. You can sit at that table with them... forever."

I stood before the vision of the sunlit banquet.

The aroma of warm bread and sweet clover seemed to drift from the glass, so rich, so vivid, so achingly familiar that for a single heartbeat, the agony in my lungs seemed to quiet. It was everything I had ever fought for. Everything I had ever dreamed of across hundreds of cycles of blood and ash.

It was a perfect peace.

I lifted my right hand. 

I rested the flat of my notched steel broadsword against my shoulder, looking directly into the projected eyes of the smiling Pecorine in the glass.

"It looks delicious," I said softly, my voice raspy and dry in the quiet hall.

"Doesn't it?" Kaiser answered, a note of triumphant satisfaction creeping into her tone. "It is the perfection of Astraea."

"Except it's a lie," I said.

The smile on the projected Pecorine's face froze.

"In the real kitchen," I continued, stepping toward the glass, "Pecorine burns the toast twice a week because she gets excited and starts talking with her hands. In the real guildhall, Karyl complains about the dust on the windows and threatens to hex the scullery boy, but she secretly mends his socks when he falls asleep. In the real world, Yui's knees shake when she faces a monster, but she steps forward anyway because she loves her friends."

I looked up at the ceiling where Kaiser’s presence hummed through the copper wires.

"You don't understand them, Kaiser," I said, my voice hardening into cold, unyielding iron. "You think human beings want to be preserved like dried figs in an empty jar. You think love is an error in the code that needs to be scrubbed clean. But life isn't about avoiding pain. Life is about sitting at a table with the people you love, eating burnt bread, and choosing to wake up together tomorrow."

I drew my sword back.

"They aren't your museum pieces," I said. "And I am not your guest."

I swung my blade.

The steel struck the obsidian mirror with the concussive force of a thunderclap. 

The glass shattered into ten thousand black shards that rained down upon the porphyry floor in a musical, singing cascade. Behind the mirror, the brass projection terminal sparked violently, its copper coils burning out in a puff of white smoke that reeked of ozone.

The sunlit banquet dissolved into thin air.

I didn't stop.

I moved down the corridor with swift, rhythmic brutality. 

*Strike. Shatter. Spark.*

My broadsword cut through the second mirror, pulverizing the false projection of Yui into harmless cinders. 

*Strike. Shatter. Spark.*

The third mirror cracked down its center, the weeping phantoms of Kokkoro and Karyl blowing away like smoke before a storm.

One by one, the eighty paces of Kaiser’s psychological mausoleum were smashed into rubble. The obsidian glass pulverized beneath my steel; the brass conduits ripped from their plaster mountings, spewing showers of dying orange embers across the polished purple stone.

Above my head, Kaiser’s voice lost all its velvet sweetness. 

The mocking purr disintegrated into a shriek of raw, hysterical fury that rattled the ceiling lanterns until the glass globes cracked.

"Insolent, wretched dog!" Kaiser screamed, her voice distorting into harsh digital static. "You throw away eternity for grease and dirt?! You choose to die in a heap of broken stone?! Then die! Die alone, Princess Knight! Die on the threshold where no one will ever hear you scream!"

The final pair of mirrors shattered into dust beneath my boots.

The corridor ended.

Before me, at the head of a flight of twelve marble steps, stood the colossal iron portal of the inner sanctum: **The Threshold of Bone**.

The double doors were forty cubits high, forged from blackened titanite and banded with plates of petrified leviathan-bone salvaged from the ancient world. Through the seam between the massive doors, a blinding white light spilled onto the marble steps, accompanied by the deep, terrifying thrum of the Primal Matriarch’s true engine.

I wiped a fleck of obsidian dust from my cheek with the back of my hand.

My breathing was heavy, my lungs burning, but the crystal core in my chest was steady, cold, and ready.

I ascended the twelve steps to face the guardians of the crown.
