# Chapter 8: The Ruined Kitchen (Gourmet Guild Route)

The grease-encrusted spiral staircase plunged four hundred feet below the palace terraces, terminating in the damp, vaulted cellars of the Royal Butteries. 

In the years before Kaiser turned the kingdom into her suffocating nursery, these corridors had hummed with the rich, chaotic industry of imperial feasting. A hundred royal cooks had worked these flags: rolling barrels of spiced cider from the southern orchards, butchering salted oxen for autumn coronation banquets, and kneading mountain flour into sweet clover buns that filled the stone flues with the aroma of yeast, caramelized butter, and cloves.

Now, the grand kitchens were a graveyard of rusted iron and sour mildew.

Giant copper stockpots, six feet across, lay overturned in the drainage gutters, their verdigris hulls dented by crossbow bolts. The great brick hearths stood cold and black, choked with soot and broken roof slates. Overhead, the heavy oak beams dripped with condensated grease and sulfur fog that seeped through the ventilation grates from the city above. Across the floor, shattered porcelain platters and iron roasting spits lay tangled with severed brass cables, discarded by Kaiser’s automaton servants when the royal pantries were stripped to feed the central converters.

I moved with a low, cautious crouch across the greasy flagstones, my right boot stepping softly over the rusted hoops of burst pickle casks. 

The air smelled of damp stone, stagnant vinegar, and the sharp, metallic tang of unshielded mana. In my left shoulder, the sapphire crystal thrummed with a steady, dull throb, warning me of proximity to royal bloodlines. The resonance was faint, flickering like a tallow candle guttering in an open cellar draft, but its frequency was unmistakable.

It was Eustiana’s frequency.

Behind a barricade of overturned oak flour bins and stacked cast-iron cauldrons at the far end of the scullery, a faint orange glow flickered.

I stepped through the shattered archway of the pastry larder.

"Who's there?" a voice hissed from the shadows.

A bolt of crackling violet electricity hissed through the darkness, striking the flagstones three inches before my left boot with a sharp, ozone-scented snap. 

Karyl leaned out from behind an iron bake-oven, her black staff gripped in knuckles so white the tendons strained against her skin. Her robes were ripped at the shoulder, caked with dry flour and dark grease, and her twin black tails twitched with ragged, frantic hostility. Around her neck, the brass band of Kaiser’s imperial control collar was glowing with a sickening crimson pulse, its sharp contact prongs biting into the tender flesh beneath her jaw. Fresh trickles of dark blood ran down her throat, staining her frayed collar.

Her emerald eyes were dilated, bloodshot with the agony of a perpetual migraine, but as the dim light of my crystal arm caught my face, the staff wavered in her grip.

"Yuuki...?" she breathed, her voice cracking. "No... that's... that's another illusion. She's inside my head again... she's sending ghosts to make me drop my guard..."

"It isn't an illusion, Karyl," I said quietly, sheathing my steel sword and stepping slowly into the light of their small hearth fire.

From behind the barricade, a small figure rose with a gasp that was half a sob.

Kokkoro stumbled into view. 

Her pale green tunic hung loose on her small frame, the linen stained with soot and herbal poultice paste. Her delicate staff was chipped, its silver sickle bound to the shaft with frayed twine. In her thin hands she carried a chipped stoneware bowl containing a watery, grey broth made from boiled wild clover roots and crushed acorn meal. 

When she saw my face, the bowl slipped from her fingers. 

It hit the flagstones with a dull clatter, the thin soup spilling across the cold stone, but she didn't look down at the food. She ran across the scullery, her small boots slapping against the grease, and threw herself against my chest with a force that drove the breath from my lungs.

"My Lord!" Kokkoro cried, burying her face into the rough wool of my cloak, her small hands clutching my tunic with desperate, trembling ferocity. "My Lord... you are alive... you found us... the stars did not lie to your humble guide..."

I dropped to my knees on the cold floor, wrapping my right arm around her shoulders, holding her thin, shivering body against me. Her hair smelled of woodsmoke and dried lavender, the familiar, comforting scent of home that cut through the rotting stench of the ruined castle like a warm sunbeam.

"I found you, Kokkoro," I whispered into her hair, my own throat tightening until I could barely force the words past my teeth. "I'm here."

From the shadows behind the flour barrels, a heavy iron gauntlet rested on the rim of a broken kneading trough.

Pecorine stepped out into the firelight.

My breath caught in my throat.

The Princess of Landosol had always been a creature of sunlit laughter, boundless vitality, and an appetite that could empty a market district in an afternoon. Now, her golden Royal Armor was battered, scratched, and held together by lengths of rusted baling wire. Her cheeks were hollow, her collarbones sharp as knife edges beneath her dented breastplate. Her stomach gave a deep, agonizing growl—a hollow, cavernous rumble of a body running on empty reserves for three desperate months.

Yet when her bright blue eyes met mine, the weariness vanished behind the widest, most stubborn smile I had ever seen.

"Yuuki!" Pecorine cried, her voice cracking with pure, unvarnished joy. 

She took two bounding steps toward me, her heavy claymore clattering against her armored hip, before her knees gave out beneath the sheer weight of her armor. She stumbled, pitching forward onto the greasy flags.

I sprang up, catching her beneath the shoulders with both arms—my flesh right hand gripping her dented pauldron, my sapphire left arm locking under her waist.

"Whoa... careful, Princess," I said, straining to take her weight as she leaned heavily against me. 

She was light. Far too light for a girl wearing sixty pounds of enchanted royal plate. Her skin was cold through her linen gambeson, and when she laid her forehead against my shoulder, she was trembling from the deep, marrow-deep chill of chronic starvation.

"Hehehe... clumsy me," Pecorine whispered, a wet, breathless laugh shaking her chest as tears began to spill down her hollow cheeks. "My legs are just... a tiny bit stubborn today. But look! Look at you! You're whole! Kokkoro said you were climbing the lower shafts, but Karyl kept saying you'd been converted, and I told her she was a silly cat who didn't know anything about our knight!"

"I told you that because looking at reality is better than starving on empty hope, you idiot!" Karyl snapped from the stove, though her voice broke halfway through the sentence. She slid down the brick wall, pulling her knees up to her chest, hiding her face in her arms as her shoulders shook with violent sobs. "You idiot... both of you... we're trapped four hundred feet under the throne room... we have no food, my head is splitting open, and now he walks in with an arm made of glass..."

I supported Pecorine over to the low wooden bench by the hearth, easing her down onto the rough pine boards. Kokkoro immediately hurried over, picking up a clean scrap of linen to wipe a streak of chimney soot from Pecorine’s brow.

"Lady Eustiana has not eaten a full portion in seventeen days," Kokkoro said softly, her large pink eyes brimming with tears. "She gives every scrap of bread and salted fish we forage to Karyl and me. Her Royal Equipment requires immense caloric intake to channel the royal mana. Without food, the armor is eating her own life force to keep its seals intact."

"It's fine! Really, it's totally fine!" Pecorine insisted, waving her gauntleted hand with a bright, sunny grin that couldn't hide the grey shadows beneath her eyes. "A princess has to keep her figure, right? Besides, when we kick Kaiser out of my dining room, we're going to roast an entire ox! With rosemary potatoes! And honeyed ham! And three kinds of apple pie!"

Her stomach let out another long, hollow groan that contradicted every word she spoke.

A sharp, electric whine suddenly cut through the cold air.

Karyl screamed, arching her back against the brick stove as the brass collar around her neck flared with blinding crimson light. Sharp needle-probes extended from the inner ring, biting deeper into her cervical vertebrae. The smell of singed hair and scorched skin filled the kitchen as a synthetic broadcast hummed from the collar's miniature diaphragm:

*Cognitive leash recalibrating. Subject Kiruya Momochi: execute defectors in Sector Sub-Four. Execute. Execute.*

"Karyl!" Pecorine cried, trying to lurch to her feet, but her starved muscles failed her again, sending her back onto the bench.

"Get... away from me..." Karyl choked out, her fingernails clawing at the brass band until blood poured over her knuckles. "She's... she's overriding my motor cortex... my hands... my hands are moving on their own..."

Karyl's staff lifted, its violet orb aiming directly at Kokkoro’s chest.

I moved without thinking.

I dropped before Karyl, reaching out with my left hand and clamping my sapphire crystal palm directly over the brass collar.

"Stop!" I commanded.

The Princess Knight core in my chest flared. 

The azure crystal on my arm ignited with brilliant cobalt luminescence, flooding into the imperial collar with the force of an electrical surge. The alien software in the brass housing shrieked, its internal capacitors overloading and popping like tiny firecrackers. The crimson light turned blue, then fractured into dull grey sparks as my mana burned out the receiver circuits.

The needles retracted with a soft hiss of escaping steam.

Karyl slumped forward into my arms, gasping for breath, her forehead resting against my neck as the crushing migraine finally released its grip. Her small, trembling hands reached up, clutching the wool of my cloak with desperate, exhausted relief.

"You... you really burned it out..." she whispered, shivering against my chest.

"I told you I'd protect you, Karyl," I said softly, resting my flesh hand on the back of her head, smoothing her dark hair.

Before we could draw another breath, the iron grates above the buttery shuddered.

A heavy, metallic thud rattled the ceiling beams, showering the scullery with dust and flakes of dry rust. From the drainage conduits leading down from the palace, a chorus of guttural, clockwork growls began to echo. 

"Palace Purge Hounds," Kokkoro said, rising to her feet and raising her sickle staff, her posture stiffening with protective resolve. "Kaiser’s automated scavengers. They detected the mana discharge from the collar."

Four massive silhouettes dropped through the ceiling shaft into the far end of the kitchen.

They were horrific amalgams of clockwork butchery and porcelain armor—six-legged mechanical beasts with rotating meat-cleavers for jaws, their brass ribs housing whirring circular saws that threw orange sparks against the stone floor. They scent-tracked the blood from Karyl's collar, their optical sensors flashing yellow as they locked onto our position.

"They're coming for the hearth!" Pecorine gritted her teeth, reaching for her claymore.

"Pecorine, wait," I said, stepping between them and the advancing machines.

I reached out with both hands. My right hand caught Pecorine’s gauntlet; my left hand caught Karyl’s staff, while Kokkoro stepped close, laying her palm against my back.

"Yuuki?" Pecorine looked at me, her blue eyes wide.

"You need food, Pecorine," I said, looking back at her with a gentle, fierce smile. "Until we reach the larder, take my mana. Eat every drop."

The Princess Knight connection opened wide.

The sapphire crystal along my ribs bit deep into my flesh, extracting its toll in a wave of icy agony that numbed my left lung. But the power that flowed through my hands was pure, concentrated royal sustenance.

It flooded into Pecorine’s veins like rich, hot wine.

The hollow, starving grey in her cheeks vanished, replaced by a radiant, blooming flush of health. Her Royal Armor flared into blinding gold, the dented plates smoothing out under a torrent of imperial mana that made the entire kitchen vibrate. The cavernous ache in her belly was silenced, replaced by the boundless, roaring vitality of the true heir of Astraea.

Beside her, Karyl’s violet eyes ignited with brilliant, clear sorcery, her staff humming with a high, lethal pitch. Kokkoro’s sickle glowed with emerald wind, ready to sweep the stone clean.

"Princess Strike!" Pecorine roared, her voice ringing off the kitchen vaults with the joyous, earth-shaking power of old.

She charged.

Her boots tore up the flagstones, throwing shattered pavers behind her like cannon shot. Her golden claymore swung in a magnificent, soaring arc of blinding light that cut the first two purge hounds clean in half, sending their brass cogs and whirring saw-blades clattering across the floor in burning heaps.

Karyl followed with a sweep of her staff, unleashing a storm of dark lightning that pulverized the third machine into twisted scrap, while Kokkoro’s gale-force spirits pinned the fourth hound to the wall until its boiler burst with a harmless hiss of steam.

The kitchen fell silent once more.

Pecorine stood among the smoking brass wreckage, her claymore resting on her shoulder, breathing hard but smiling with radiant, triumphant vitality. She turned to us, raising her thumb in the air with her classic, wide grin.

"Yummy!" she cheered, her voice echoing joyfully through the ruins. "That was the most delicious mana boost I've ever tasted! I feel like I could eat ten wild boars and still have room for strawberry tart!"

Kokkoro wiped a tear of relief from her eye, while Karyl crossed her arms, turning her face away with a faint blush on her soot-stained cheeks.

"Show-off," Karyl muttered, though the small smile on her lips couldn't be hidden.

I walked up to them, leaning slightly on my steel sword as the exhaustion of the boost caught up to my legs. 

Ahead of us, beyond the ruined buttery, lay the heavy iron bulkhead leading into the palace sub-vaults—the direct path to the grand throne room where Kaiser sat upon her high seat.

The family was back together.
