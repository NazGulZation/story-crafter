# Chapter 9: Starving Hearts (Gourmet Guild Route)

The service corridors ascending from the butteries were narrow, steep, and cut from raw granite that sweated alkaline brine. 

Two centuries ago, these passages had been the private thoroughfares of the castle retainers—maids carrying baskets of fresh linen to the guest wings, pages running silver pitchers of mulled claret to the royal library, and guards changing the watch on the lower battlements. 

Now, every thirty paces along the damp walls, Kaiser had mounted brass sensor conduits that pulsed with a dull, rhythmic crimson glow. Thick bundles of lead-sheathed cables lay across the floor like tangled black serpents, trailing upward toward the palace foundations where the weight of the Tower of Sol rested upon the earth.

I climbed the stone stairs behind Pecorine, my left hand pressed against the sweating wall to steady my footing. 

The sapphire crystal had crept higher along my neck, its hard, unyielding plates locking the muscles along the left side of my jaw. Every breath felt like drawing cold river silt into my lungs. A sharp, burning ache radiated through my left shoulder blade, but I kept my pace even, hiding the slight drag in my left boot whenever Kokkoro glanced back over her shoulder.

At the head of the third flight of stairs, the passage opened into the Hall of Dynastic Memory.

It had once been a gallery of high marble arches, displaying oil portraits of the royal house of Astraea across seven generations. Kings in ermine-trimmed mantles holding ceremonial sceptres; queens in velvet gowns surrounded by hunting hounds; young princes on horseback carrying hawking gloves.

Kaiser had turned the gallery into a cognitive processing abattoir.

The oil paintings had been slashed down their diagonal axes, their linen canvases peeling from gilded frames like dried skin. Through the rents in the portraits, Kaiser’s engineers had driven iron mounting brackets directly into the brickwork, hanging rows of cylindrical glass tanks filled with pale green fluid. Inside the tanks, submerged human brains salvaged from defecting palace staff floated in suspended animation, their optical nerves wired into brass manifolds that fed data upward into the throne room.

Pecorine stopped in the center of the gallery.

Her golden claymore lowered until its steel point clicked against the marble flagstones. Her eyes were fixed upon the largest painting at the center of the wall.

The canvas had been stripped completely of its varnish. The faces of the King and Queen of Astraea were visible beneath a spiderweb of scorch marks, their royal robes still bearing the proud golden sun of the realm. But between them, where a young princess in a ruffled summer dress had once stood holding a white kitten, the canvas had been burned away with acid. 

Only a blackened, empty silhouette remained.

Beside the ruined portrait, an imperial brass plaque had been bolted into the marble:
**[ERASURE COMPLETE: SUBJECT EUSTIANA RECORD TERMINATED — NAME VACATED BY IMPERIAL EDICT]**

Pecorine’s hand tightened around the leather wrap of her hilt until the metal rings groaned.

"They didn't remember me," she whispered, her voice trembling with a quiet, hollow grief that cut deeper than any wound she had taken in battle. "When Kaiser sat on the throne and activated the crown... my mother looked right through me. I was standing three feet in front of her, crying, holding her hand... and she asked me what sector I was assigned to sweep. She asked me if I was the new scullery girl."

Kokkoro stepped up beside her, reaching out to gently touch the edge of Pecorine’s battered gauntlet. 

"They were under the spell of the crown, Lady Eustiana," Kokkoro murmured softly, her large eyes filled with deep, unwavering loyalty. "The false queen stole their sight, but she could never erase who you are. The spirits of the wind know your true name. We know your true name."

"I know," Pecorine said, taking a deep, shuddering breath and blinking back the hot tears that threatened to spill down her cheeks. She forced a small, courageous smile to her lips. "I know they didn't mean it. But when your own mother looks at you and sees a ghost... it leaves a hunger inside you that no amount of roast beef can ever fill. You feel like... if you just stop eating, if you just stop smiling, you'll disappear completely."

Karyl stood five paces back, leaning against the cold stone frame of an arched doorway, her black staff held loosely in her hands. Her ears were flattened against her dark hair, her gaze fixed stubbornly upon the floor tiles.

"I helped her," Karyl muttered, her voice raw with self-loathing. "Back when I was blind... when I thought Kaiser was the only person who cared whether I lived or died. I carried the resonance stones into these galleries. I helped the technicians calibrate the dampening fields that wiped the royal family's memories. Every time you smile at me, Pecorine... every time you share your bread with me... I feel like a filthy traitor who should have been hung from the city walls."

Pecorine turned around.

She didn't hesitate. She closed the distance in two broad strides, dropped her claymore to the floor with a loud clang, and threw her arms around Karyl, pulling the smaller girl into a fierce, bone-crushing hug.

"Stop that, you silly cat!" Pecorine declared, her voice ringing with stubborn warmth. "You're my friend! You're Gourmet Guild! If Kaiser lied to you, that's her crime, not yours! You broke her collar, you stayed with us in the freezing cellars, and you shared every scrap of wild clover with Kokkoro and me! That makes you my family, Karyl! And Eustiana von Astraea never turns her back on her family!"

Karyl froze in Pecorine’s grasp, her eyes widening in stunned disbelief. She tried to pull away, her cheeks burning crimson, but the strength in Pecorine’s arms was absolute. Slowly, the rigidity in Karyl’s shoulders collapsed. She buried her face into the cold steel of Pecorine’s breastplate, letting out a small, muffled sob as her fingers clutched the back of Pecorine’s leather cape.

"You're... you're an idiot, Pecorine..." Karyl wept softly into the steel. "A complete and total idiot..."

"Hehehe, I know," Pecorine laughed, resting her chin on Karyl’s head. "That's why I need you to keep me from eating strange mushrooms!"

A sudden, sharp spasm seized my chest.

I doubled over, dropping to one knee as a violent wave of coughing tore through my throat. I clamped my right hand over my mouth, the taste of hot, coppery bile flooding my tongue. When I pulled my hand away, my palm was smeared with dark, thick blood mixed with tiny, glittering flecks of sapphire grit.

"Yuuki!" Kokkoro cried out, rushing to my side.

She dropped to her knees, supporting my shoulder with both her hands, her small face draining of all color as she saw the blood on the stone floor. She pulled a clean linen square from her sleeve, desperately wiping the crimson from my lips and chin.

Pecorine and Karyl broke apart, rushing over to kneel beside us.

Pecorine’s eyes fell upon my chest. 

During the fight in the scullery, my cloak had been torn open. Now, through the ripped linen of my shirt, the full extent of the crystalline corruption was laid bare. 

The sapphire stone now covered the entire left half of my chest in an unbroken plate of jagged, translucent mineral, its roots sinking deep into my sternum and winding around my ribs. Beneath the surface of the crystal, my heart burned as an incandescent, pulsing knot of cobalt mana that throbbed with irregular, frantic tremors.

"Yuuki..." Pecorine whispered, her hand hovering over the stone carapace, trembling with horror. "Your chest... what did you do to yourself?"

"The Princess Knight core..." Karyl’s voice shook as she pointed her staff’s sensor at my ribs. The diagnostic crystal on her staff flickered with violent, alarming pulses. "Every time he boosts our mana... every time he feeds us his strength... the core consumes his living tissue to generate the output. His body is petrifying from the inside out!"

"My Lord..." Kokkoro wept, pressing her forehead against my arm, her tears soaking into the rough wool of my sleeve. "Why did you not speak? Why did you carry this terrible burden in silence? Lady Ameth commanded me to guide you, to preserve your life... and I have failed you so completely..."

I reached out with my right hand, lifting Kokkoro’s chin so she had to look into my eyes. 

"You haven't failed anyone, Kokkoro," I said, forcing my voice to remain calm and steady despite the fire raging in my lungs. "You kept them alive. You kept the hearth warm. That was the mission, wasn't it?"

I looked up at Pecorine and Karyl, offering them the same quiet smile I had worn across a hundred different mornings in Landosol.

"I didn't do this to die," I said softly. "I did it so we could reach the throne room together. Kaiser thinks she can starve the soul of this kingdom until everyone forgets how to love each other. But we didn't forget. We're standing in her house, and we're going to take back your dining room, Pecorine."

Pecorine looked down at my crystallized chest, her jaw clenching with fierce, protective resolve. She wiped the tears from her cheeks with the back of her iron gauntlet, her blue eyes blazing with royal authority.

"We take it back," Pecorine declared, her voice ringing clear through the ruined gallery. "And when we do, we sit at the table together. All four of us. No one gets left behind."

A heavy pneumatic clang echoed from the end of the corridor.

At the far end of the Hall of Dynastic Memory, the heavy iron security bulkhead slid upward, revealing the entrance to the Royal Sub-Vault. 

Beyond the threshold lay the central freight lift—a massive platform of riveted iron and brass gears, suspended by four giant chains that ascended directly into the floor of the Grand Throne Room above. But guarding the lift stood three Kaiser Porcelain Praetorians: ten-foot-tall elite automatons encased in fluted gold and white ceramic, their four arms wielding paired halberds and heavy suppression shields.

"They're guarding the lift shaft," Karyl said, standing up and sweeping her staff forward, her emerald eyes narrowing with lethal focus. "If we trip the alarms, the lift cable detonates and we're trapped down here forever."

"Then we strike fast and quiet," Pecorine said, picking up her golden claymore and resting the heavy flat against her shoulder. "Kokkoro, blind their optical sensors with the wind! Karyl, crack the shield mounts! Yuuki... stay behind us. Save your breath for the top."

"I've got your backs," I said, pushing myself up from the stone floor and gripping the hilt of my notched blade.

The battle for the sub-vault was swift, brutal, and coordinated with the absolute perfection of four souls who knew each other's rhythms down to the fraction of a heartbeat.

Kokkoro darted across the marble floor like a swallow riding a storm, her sickle staff whipping up blinding vortexes of jade wind that shattered the praetorians' glass sensor clusters with showers of sharp gravel. 

Karyl followed in her wake, firing concentrated bolts of dark matter that drilled clean through the brass hinges of the heavy suppression shields, ripping them from the automatons' ceramic grips.

And Pecorine delivered the decisive stroke.

She charged down the center aisle, her golden armor flaring with the warm, rich mana I had fed her in the kitchen. She swung her claymore in three massive, overlapping cleaves that sheared through the automatons' porcelain necks, hip pivots, and hydraulic cores. The three ten-foot giants collapsed into heaps of smoking white shards and spinning brass cogs that rattled across the floor and fell silent.

The sub-vault was ours.

Behind the destroyed security station, we found a small, reinforced storeroom that had remained untouched by Kaiser's scavengers. 

Inside, resting on clean pine shelves, sat three sealed ceramic crocks of preserved river trout in salted butter, a bushel of wrinkled winter apples that still smelled sweet, a tin canister of dried mountain tea leaves, and a sack of coarse grey salt.

Pecorine looked at the food, and then looked at the four of us standing in the dim light of the sub-vault.

"Before we take that lift," Pecorine said softly, her voice filled with quiet, reverent warmth, "we eat. Together. As the Gourmet Guild."
