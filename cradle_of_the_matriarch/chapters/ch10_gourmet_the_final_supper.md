# Chapter 10: The Final Supper (Gourmet Guild Route)

The storeroom behind the sub-vault had thick walls of dry limestone that shut out the cold drafts of the drainage shafts. 

In the center of the flagstones, Kokkoro cleared away the chalky mortar dust with the hem of her cloak, setting down a heavy cast-iron brazier she had salvaged from the watchman's lodge. With small, patient movements, she laid sticks of dry cedar kindling across a bed of grey charcoal, struck an iron flint against a piece of quartz, and blew gently into the sparks until a clean, golden flame caught the wood.

A thin, fragrant curl of white smoke rose toward the ceiling vent, carrying the resinous scent of cedar and warm embers.

Around that small circle of fire, the four of us sat on upturned wooden salt tubs.

Outside in the corridors, Kaiser's kingdom of porcelain and lead hummed with its suffocating, mechanical cadence. Five miles above our heads, the Tower of Sol pierced the poisoned sky; four hundred feet below, the flooded shafts of the undercity groaned under the weight of the lake. But here, within the dry stone walls of the pantry, the world shrank to the width of our hearth.

"I found an iron skillet," Karyl said, emerging from the dark corners of the pantry with a heavy black pan and a roll of linen towels. She set it on the brazier grate, wiping the rim with a corner of her sleeve. "It had a layer of lard on it to keep the rust off. I scrubbed it clean with salt and sand. It'll hold heat."

Kokkoro opened the earthenware crock of preserved river trout. 

The fish had been caught in the upper reaches of the Astraean falls two autumns ago, packed tightly in cold churned butter and coarse bay salt. With her slender horn-handled knife, Kokkoro lifted the pale pink fillets from the pale grease, trimming the silvery skin with practiced, delicate precision.

"We have three fillets," Kokkoro murmured, her brow furrowing slightly as she counted. "I will take only the broth from the kettle. My Lord and Lady Eustiana must have the meat to restore their mana."

"You will do no such thing, Kokkoro," Pecorine said firmly, though her stomach let out a thunderous rumble that made her cheeks turn bright pink. She took the knife gently from Kokkoro's hand, placed the three trout fillets on a clean board, and with four clean diagonal cuts, divided the meat into four perfectly equal portions. "In the Gourmet Guild, we share the pot down to the last bean. That is the rule. That has always been the rule."

Karyl set the iron skillet onto the red-hot coals. 

She dropped a generous dollop of the salted butter into the pan. It sizzled with a sharp, ecstatic hiss, foaming into golden bubbles that filled the small stone room with the rich, nutty aroma of roasted cream. She laid the trout fillets into the pan. The fish crisped at the edges, turning a glorious, toasted amber, while Kokkoro added thin crescents of sliced winter apple that caramelized in the butter, releasing the tart, sweet perfume of late autumn orchards.

From a copper kettle boiling at the edge of the grate, Kokkoro steeped the dried mountain tea leaves. The steam rose thick and green, smelling of wild mint, pine needles, and high alpine meadows.

Pecorine set out four wooden trenchers on a rough pine plank between our knees. 

With solemn, joyful ceremony, she lifted each fillet from the skillet with the tip of her hunting dagger, placing the golden fish alongside the sweet apple slices, and ladled a spoonful of sizzling brown butter over the top. Kokkoro poured the hot tea into four chipped horn cups, the amber liquid steaming in the chilly air.

We looked at the food, and then we looked at each other.

"It smells..." Karyl’s voice caught in her throat. She stared at the plate in her lap, her eyes shining in the firelight. "It smells like our kitchen in Landosol. The morning after we fought that giant cabbage monster in the southern fields."

"Hehehe! I remember that!" Pecorine laughed, her eyes crinkling at the corners as she leaned her elbows on her knees. "Karyl got covered in purple cabbage slime, and Kokkoro spent three hours washing her hair with chamomile water! And Yuuki accidentally put salt in the tea instead of sugar!"

"It was rock salt," I smiled, holding my warm horn cup between my hands. "I thought it was sugar crystal."

"You drank the whole cup anyway without making a face," Kokkoro recalled softly, her gaze resting upon me with a love so pure and deep it made my chest ache. "You said that as long as your family sat at the table, even salt tea was a banquet."

"Well, this tea has no salt," Kokkoro added, bowing her head. "It was harvested from the high cliffs of Mount Sol by the wild goats. It brings clarity to the mind and strength to the blood."

Pecorine clasped her hands together, closing her eyes. 

"For the food before us," Pecorine said, her voice clear, resonant, and filled with the ancient, simple prayer of her people. "For the earth that gave the trout, for the trees that bore the fruit, and for the companions who walk beside us into the storm. May this meal warm our hearts, and may our guild never know hunger again."

"Let's eat!" we said together.

I lifted a piece of the trout with my carved wooden spoon.

The fish flaked apart on my tongue, rich with the savory butter and seasoned with the clean mineral crunch of the sea salt. The tart sweetness of the roasted apple cut through the richness, filling my mouth with the vivid, overwhelming memory of autumn sunlight breaking through the royal gardens. 

It was the most delicious thing I had ever tasted in my life.

Beside me, Pecorine took her first bite. 

A soft, strangled gasp escaped her throat. She chewed slowly, her eyes wide, and then she buried her face in her free hand, weeping into her sleeve as she swallowed.

"Pecorine?" Karyl asked gently, resting her hand on Pecorine’s armored knee.

"It's just... it's so good..." Pecorine sobbed softly, laughing through her tears as she wiped her eyes with her gauntlet. "It tastes like home. It tastes like the days before the sky went black. I had forgotten... I was so hungry for so long, I thought I had forgotten what real food tasted like. But my tongue remembered. My heart remembered."

Karyl took a bite of her apple slice, her ears twitching with quiet pleasure. She chewed thoughtfully, looking across the fire at me, her sharp emerald eyes softening into a gaze of profound, tender gratitude.

"You really brought us back together, you know," Karyl said quietly, looking down into her tea. "When Kaiser took the city, I was ready to throw myself off the battlements. I thought I was cursed... that everywhere I went, I brought ruin to the people who took me in. But you kept looking for me. You kept believing I was worth saving."

She looked up, meeting my eyes with fierce, unyielding sincerity.

"If we die up there today," Karyl said, "I don't regret a single step. Meeting the three of you was the only good thing that ever happened to me in this world."

"No one is dying up there, Karyl!" Pecorine declared, sitting up straight, her golden armor catching the firelight with royal splendor. "We're going to march into that throne room, we're going to smash Kaiser's porcelain crown, and then we're going to open a restaurant!"

"A restaurant?" Kokkoro tilted her head, blinking.

"Yes!" Pecorine smiled broadly, her eyes shining with radiant certainty. "Right in the middle of the artisan quarter! A grand, bustling tavern with ten ovens, twenty long tables, and an open hearth that never goes out! Anyone who walks through the door hungry gets a bowl of hot stew and a loaf of warm bread, no questions asked, no coins required! And right at the front, by the big bay window looking out over the city gardens, we'll keep a round oak table reserved for the Gourmet Guild."

Pecorine pointed her wooden spoon at each of us in turn.

"Kokkoro will manage the supplies and make sure we don't buy bruised apples," Pecorine said. "Karyl will handle the books and scare away any rowdy mercenaries who try to skip on their bill. Yuuki will greet the guests and sit at the head of the table. And I will do the cooking and the eating!"

"You'll eat all the inventory before the customers arrive," Karyl muttered, but her lips curved into a wide, happy smile that lit up her entire face.

"I will exercise royal restraint!" Pecorine laughed, leaning over to bump her shoulder against mine. "Right, Yuuki? You'll keep me in line, won't you?"

"I will," I said softly, looking into her bright blue eyes.

My chest was cold. 

Beneath the rough wool of my tunic, the sapphire stone had crept across my collarbone, numbing the pulse in the left side of my neck. I knew, with the cold mathematical certainty of my Princess Knight connection, that my physical body had hours left at most. The next time I ignited my core to empower their royal strikes, the crystal would claim the rest of my heart. There would be no restaurant for me. There would be no corner table by the sunny window.

Yet as I looked around the small circle of fire—at Pecorine’s radiant smile, at Kokkoro’s gentle, devoted eyes, at Karyl’s flushed, laughing cheeks—there was no sorrow in my heart. 

I had been given a family. I had been given a home. I had been given bread and tea, laughter and love, in a world that had tried to turn everything into cold, unfeeling glass. 

That was more than enough. That was worth every ounce of blood, every shard of bone, and every beat of my dying heart.

We finished the fish. We scraped the skillet clean of every drop of golden butter with our crusts of apple, and drank the mint tea until only the dark leaves remained at the bottom of the cups.

Kokkoro washed the wooden trenchers in the remaining warm water, drying them carefully with a clean cloth and packing them back into her leather satchel. Karyl smothered the red embers of the brazier with a shovel of grey sand, plunging the room into darkness save for the pale, steady blue luminescence radiating from my left arm.

Pecorine picked up her heavy claymore, resting the golden blade against her shoulder. Her face was calm, resolute, and filled with the majestic authority of the rightful sovereign of Astraea.

"Time to go home," Pecorine said.

We stepped out of the storeroom and walked onto the iron platform of the central freight lift.

Kokkoro stepped up to the control housing on the wall, took hold of the heavy brass lever, and pulled it down with both hands.

The massive iron gears engaged with a deep, concussive groan. The four giant chains groaned under the sudden tension, vibrating with a low, baritone hum that shook the platform beneath our boots. 

Slowly, smoothly, the lift began its long ascent through the dark vertical shaft, carrying the four of us upward toward the light, toward the throne, and toward the reckoning that awaited at the summit of the world.
