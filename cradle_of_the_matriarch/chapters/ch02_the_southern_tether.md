# Chapter 2: The Southern Tether

The mud of the orchard track gave way to damp leaf-litter as I crossed the southern boundary ditch. The stench of wet peat mixed with the sharp, chemical reek of scorched ozone and hot iron.

Above the black canopy of the pines, the violet pulse had quickened. Every few seconds, a rhythmic ripple passed through the low cloud ceiling, illuminating the needle-covered ground in cold, electric lavender. With each pulse, the tiny hairs on the back of my neck stood up, and the crystallized veins of my left arm began to hum—a high, dry pitch like a wet finger circling the rim of a crystal goblet.

I scrambled up the mossy slope of a granite outcrop, keeping low beneath the dripping branches of a dwarf cedar, and looked down into the hollow of the Sacred Grove.

The grove was an ancient bowl of limestone and primeval timber, sacred to the beastfolk clans long before the first stone of Landosol had been squared. At its center rose the Grand Oak, its trunk five fathoms thick, its gnarled roots anchoring deep into the subterranean aquifers. 

Three massive siphon-pylons had been driven directly through those roots.

The pylons were octagonal pillars of cold-rolled steel, thirty feet tall, anchored into the damp earth by hydraulic spikes. Their upper sections housed spinning magnetic rings that whined with terrifying torque, driving thick steel needles four feet into the living timber of the tree. Wherever the metal bit into the bark, the sap hissed and boiled into pale grey steam, glowing with sickly fluorescent purple. Translucent cables, thick as an ox's flank, looped upward from the pylon heads, trailing straight into the belly of the clouds.

Around the base of the oak, huddled inside the hollow root vaults, were dozens of non-combatant beastfolk. Elderly sheep-kin in tattered homespun shawls, rabbit-eared children clinging to their mothers' skirts, their eyes wide with uncomprehending dread.

Between the pylons and the refugees stood Caon.

Makoto was at the front. Her two-handed greatsword was chipped along half its edge, the dark steel spattered with milky white coolant and black puppet-blood. A deep gash crossed her left collarbone, soaking the ragged wolf-fur trim of her leather jerkin in dark crimson, but her footing remained planted in the churning mud. Her lips were pulled back in a feral snarl, exposing her sharp canines as she braced against an incoming rush.

"Hold the line!" she roared, her voice shredded and hoarse. "Don't give them an inch! If they plant the fourth stake, we lose the hollow!"

Beside her, Kaori moved in a blur of spinning footwork and savage knuckle-strikes. Her canvas hand-wraps were shredded, the skin beneath split and raw, but her strikes hammered against the ceramic chest plates of the Porcelain Paladins with concussive cracks that echoed off the granite cliffs. Each blow sent spider-web fractures across the white enamel, but the automatons felt no pain, advancing steadily through the spray of their own broken casing.

Behind the vanguard, kneeling in the moss before the refugee children, was Maho.

The fox matron looked ghastly pale. Her ornate pink and gold robes were dragged through black muck, the hem soaked in bilge water. In her hands, she clutched her ancestral parasol, its ivory ribs strained to the verge of snapping as she channeled a shimmering, rose-tinted fairy dome over the hollow. The dome was under constant bombardment. Every time a siphon-pylon pulsed, a lance of violet lightning struck the fairy barrier, sending violent tremors through Maho's wrists. Blood seeped steadily from her nose, dripping down her chin to stain the silk of her collar, yet her eyes remained fixed on the sky with desperate defiance.

Further up the slope, concealed in a bramble thicket, Kasumi’s voice cut through the clash of metal and the grinding hum of the turbines.

"Pylon Three is reaching terminal synchronization!" the detective shouted, adjusting the brass lens of her magnifying wand with trembling, mud-caked fingers. "In two minutes, its frequency locks to the central grid! If that happens, the feedback loop will cook the brainstems of everyone under the dome! Makoto, we have to break the transformer ring now!"

"I hear you!" Makoto yelled back, ducking beneath a sweeping poleaxe that sheared three inches off her wolf-tail. She swung her broken greatsword upward in a two-handed arc, the flat of the heavy blade catching the paladin beneath its chin, popping the ceramic helm off its mounting in a burst of black wires and yellow sparks.

The headless machine did not fall. It simply swung its crescent axe backhand, the butt-spike catching Makoto across the ribs with a dull, heavy thump that lifted her off her feet and threw her five yards into the churning mud.

She hit the ground hard, coughing up a bright spray of blood that splattered across the white roots.

Three Porcelain Paladins advanced on her immediately, their poleaxes raised in absolute unison, their featureless white heads tilting down with algorithmic dispassion.

I could not leave her in that mud. 

The Princess Knight core in my chest ignited with a savage, burning jolt. The heat tore through my ribcage like molten copper, coursing down my left arm until the azure crystal in my forearm glowed with blinding brilliance. 

I did not jump into the middle of the melee to be surrounded. I vaulted from the granite ledge directly onto the gantry of Pylon Three.

My boots slammed onto the vibrating steel grating with a ringing crash. The ceramic sentinels below swiveled their featureless helms upward in mechanical unison, but their reaction cycle was a fraction of a second too slow. I drew my notched steel arming sword with my right hand and rammed the blade straight into the transformer housing, right between the spinning copper induction rings.

"Overload," I snarled through clenched teeth.

I channeled the Princess Knight’s raw resonance directly into the circuit. The blue light from my crystallized left palm surged into the steel casing. 

The reaction was immediate and violent. The high-voltage capacitors erupted in a concussive blast of white sparks and boiling mineral oil. The heavy induction ring shattered into three jagged arcs of red-hot brass that went spinning into the trees, slicing through the hydraulic lines. Deprived of its power source, Pylon Three let out a dying, pneumatic groan and slumped sideways, its steel needles ripping free from the oak’s root in a shower of hissing steam.

The violet tether connecting the pylon to the leaden sky snapped with a sound like a broken harp string, whipping wildly through the clouds before dissolving into harmless lavender ash.

The shockwave blew me backward off the gantry. I landed in the churned leaf-muck on my feet, rolling onto my right shoulder to absorb the impact, my boots digging deep furrows into the wet loam.

Makoto scrambled backward through the mud, wiping the blood from her chin with her forearms, her amber eyes widening in sheer shock as she took in my tattered cloak and the glowing crystal of my arm.

"Yuuki...?" she gasped, her voice cracking. "You... you're alive? We thought—in the capital, they said—"

"Save your breath, Makoto," I said, rising to my feet, keeping my blade pointed low toward the remaining paladins. "Pylon Three is dead. The synchronization is broken."

From the bramble thicket, Kasumi darted forward, her monocle swinging wildly on its brass chain. Her gaze flicked from my face to the smoking ruin of the transformer, then up to the thinning tether web above the canopy.

"The resonance pressure dropped forty percent," Kasumi reported, her words tumbling out in rapid, analytical bursts. "The paladins are falling back into defensive formation around Pylon One and Two. But this is a temporary respite, Yuuki. Kaiser's automated routing will detect the severed node within minutes. Heavy reinforcements will deploy from the Citadel."

Maho lowered her parasol by an inch, gasping for breath as the rose-tinted fairy dome stabilized. Her fox ears twitched with exhaustion, her fingers trembling against the ivory handle. "Knight... you came for us. You truly came."

"I can't stay," I told them, my voice flat and urgent. The Princess Knight core inside my chest was throbbing violently, each heartbeat shooting sharp, needle-like spasms of pain through the azure veins in my wrist. My mouth tasted of salt and hot copper. "If I linger in this clearing, Kaiser will trace my signature and drop the entire southern garrison on this hollow. You need to pull your people back into the deep karst caverns beneath the limestone ridge."

"And what about you?" Makoto demanded, hauling herself upright with the help of her broken greatsword. She stepped toward me, her wolf-tail stiff with stubborn fury. "You think you're just going to run off alone toward the Spire? Look at your arm! You're turning to glass, you idiot! We fight together. Caon owes you our lives ten times over."

"You fight here, Makoto," I said, looking her straight in the eyes. "Keep your people breathing. If Kaiser takes the grove, there is no south left to save. I have to push north toward the industrial foundries and find the others. We need the Royal Guard broken, and we need Labyrinth's terminal online before anyone can touch the Tower."

Kasumi nodded grimly, her sharp mind immediately grasping the tactical reality. She reached into her leather satchel and pulled out a small, brass-encased tuning prism wrapped in oiled velvet, pressing it into my right hand.

"Take this," Kasumi said quietly. "It's keyed to Labyrista's encrypted frequency. If you reach the mid-spire viaduct above the foundries, the prism will resonate with our communications network. When the time comes to strike the root engine, we will be ready. But only if you survive the climb."

"Understood," I said, tucking the cold brass prism into my belt pouch.

Behind us, the remaining two Porcelain Paladins raised their shields, sounding a low, brassy alarm horn that echoed ominously through the foggy pines. Across the northern ditch, the heavy iron gears of the slag-foundry gates were already grinding in response.

I turned toward the northern embankment without looking back.

The sleet fell in thick, stinging sheets now, plastering my hair to my forehead and washing the soot from my leather coat. Ahead of me, the massive, soot-blackened stone walls of the Industrial Redoubt loomed against the leaden sky, belching plumes of yellow coal smoke into the freezing air.

I tightened the straps of my scabbard against my hip, wiped the greasy transformer oil from my steel blade, and vaulted over the ditch toward the northern aqueduct.
