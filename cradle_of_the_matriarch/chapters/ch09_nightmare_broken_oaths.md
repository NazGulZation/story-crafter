# Chapter 9: Broken Oaths

The impact of the cavalry charge struck the barricade like a falling cliff of iron and bone.

The lead centaur hit the overturned timber caisson at full gallop, its twelve-foot lightning lance discharging with a deafening, blinding arc of violet electricity that vaporized three sandbags and hurled two watchmen ten yards into the ditch. The air instantly filled with the sharp, bitter stench of ozone, scorched wool, and splintered oak.

Jun met the beast head-on.

She did not flinch; she did not take a single step backward into the retreat. She dropped her weight low into the mud, planting the butt-spike of her halberd into the deep seam between two granite paving stones, angling the five-foot steel blade directly into the centaur’s charging chest at a forty-five-degree angle. 

The collision sent a concussive shockwave through the flagstones that rattled the bones in my feet and chipped the mortar from the parapets. The forged steel blade of the halberd punched straight through the centaur’s ceramic breastplate, shearing its internal steam manifold and severing its central hydraulic conduits in a spraying rupture of boiling oil. The twenty-ton machine screamed—a high, pneumatic shriek of dying machinery—and pitched forward, its iron hooves flailing wildly in the freezing air as it crashed across the parapet, toppling into the mist-choked river gorge four hundred feet below with a distant, thunderous crash.

"Second rank! Brace!" Jun’s command tore through the thunder of charging hooves, her visored helm spattered with black machine grease and river silt.

Behind the fallen leader, four more centaurs charged in echelon, their heavy lances lowered, their violet arcs overlapping into a blinding curtain of high-voltage death that scorched the air and left trails of ozone in our mouths.

I lunged forward onto the bridge roadway, my boots skidding across the wet, blood-stained stones.

I channeled the Princess Knight core in my chest. The blue flame flared behind my ribs, sending a scalding wave of mana through my veins that made my teeth ache. The azure crystal on my left arm blazed with sapphire fire, extending a shimmering, translucent wall of resonance five yards across the road, shimmering like hardened ice against the darkness of the gorge.

The lances hit my barrier in unison.

The impact felt like being struck in the chest by four flying battering rams. The kinetic recoil drove my boots six inches backward through the gravel, tearing the leather soles and straining the ligaments in my knees, sending searing bolts of diverted lightning crackling through the crystal of my forearm. Blood rose in my throat, hot and salty, spraying from my lips across my chin, but the barrier held. The violet energy dissipated into the air in a hissing cloud of harmless steam.

"Now, Jun!" I yelled through clenched, bleeding teeth. "Take their flanks!"

Jun roared, tearing her halberd free with titan-like fury, her blade moving in a sweeping horizontal cleave that sheared through the front leg-joints of two centaurs simultaneously, dropping them to their iron bellies. 

From the inspection hatch beneath the bridge road, Christina burst onto the flags like a crimson tempest. 

Her rapier whistled through the smoke in four blinding thrusts. Each strike found the unarmored hydraulic neck-seams of the machines, puncturing the control cables with surgical, reality-bending precision. Black oil geysered into the freezing air, drenching her red cape as three more centaurs collapsed in tangled heaps of iron and ceramic, their wheels and gears spinning out in dying, spasming fits.

"First line of powder kegs is anchored!" Christina shouted, wiping a spray of black coolant from her eyes with her sleeve, her chest heaving with wild, ragged breaths. "Thirty barrels under the southern pier! But the fuses are wet, Jun! The river mist is soaking the cord! We need dry match-cord and a continuous fire-source, or the powder won't take!"

Before Jun could answer, a high, whistling scream ripped through the cloud deck above our heads.

"Inbound! Down!" Tomo’s voice shrieked from the rampart.

We threw ourselves flat against the granite flags as the first salvo from the palace terraces arrived.

Three heavy incendiary mortar shells—massive globes of cast iron filled with compressed alchemical phosphorus—crashed into the roadway fifty yards behind us. The explosions were concussive, deafening thuds that blew craters four feet deep into the granite, throwing geysers of liquid fire into the freezing air. The phosphorus adhered to stone, iron, and wood, burning with a ferocious, blinding white heat that water could not extinguish, sending thick plumes of toxic grey smoke billowing across the gorge.

The heat was blistering, scorching the hair on the back of my neck even through the wet drizzle. Around us, two watchmen rolled on the stones, desperately trying to scrape the burning chemicals from their leather sleeves before the fire ate into their flesh.

Through the smoke of the mortar barrage, the second imperial wave became visible.

Kaiser had committed her heavy palace reserves. Two battalions of Porcelain Paladins were marching onto the northern approaches of the causeway in rigid, unbroken squares, their crescent poleaxes held high, their white breastplates gleaming like tombstones in the burning phosphorus light. Behind them, four heavy siege crawlers were rolling their pneumatic ram-cannons into firing positions, their hydraulic pumps grinding with rhythmic, terrifying finality.

They were not trying to capture the bridge. They were preparing to carpet the entire causeway with artillery fire, vaporizing the barricades and everyone behind them.

Jun pushed herself up from the stones, her armor blackened, small tongues of phosphorus fire licking at the rivets of her left greave before she stamped them out in the mud. She looked down the long, burning span of the bridge, then back toward the mouth of the mountain pass where the refugee train was struggling through the mud.

"They're going to level the bridge from range," Jun said, her voice dropping into a flat, deadly calm. "If we stay on this open span, their mortars will chew us into red grease before we can light the fuses. And if the bridge falls prematurely, our retreat is cut."

"We pull back to the Citadel Bastion," I said, rising to my knees, leaning heavily against my sword as the crystal at my neck throbbed with cold fire. "The gatehouse has three-foot granite walls and an iron portcullis. It will shield us from the mortar barrage and funnel their infantry onto the central arch. We let them think they’ve won the crossing."

Christina sheathed her rapier, her golden hair singed by phosphorus sparks, her eyes burning with grim, sharp calculation. "And once their main force crowds onto the bridge to breach the gatehouse... we detonate the piers beneath their boots. Burying forty battalions of imperial guard in the riverbed."

"Yes," Jun agreed, her heavy visor turning toward the burning bastion. "Tomo, Matsuri, fall back to the inner portcullis! Help the wounded through the arch! Rig the secondary fuses to the gatehouse crank! We give them the causeway, and we take their army with us."

Matsuri hauled a wounded archer over her shoulders, scurrying through the hail of cinders toward the gatehouse door, while Tomo laid down covering fire with a repeating crossbow, her bolts whistling into the advancing ceramic ranks.

A second volley of mortar shells shrieked overhead, tearing into the battlements of the gatehouse, showering the bridge in burning stone, shrapnel, and molten metal.

We turned and sprinted through the smoke toward the heavy archway of the Citadel Bastion, the roar of the advancing porcelain legions closing like an iron vise behind our heels. 

We threw ourselves through the entrance just as an explosive shell detonated against the outer lintel, blasting the granite arch into jagged fragments that rained over our shoulders. Jun slammed her bulk against the left leaf of the colossal iron-banded oak door, while Christina and I shoved the right leaf shut with everything we had. 

The six-inch-thick oak slabs slammed together with a deafening, vault-like boom that cut off the immediate blast of the heat. Jun dropped the five-hundred-pound forged iron locking bar into its stone brackets with a brutal heave of her shoulders, her gauntlets sparking against the cold metal. 

Outside, the first wave of porcelain poleaxes began hammering against the exterior iron plates with rhythmic, terrifying percussion—a drumfire of blades and hydraulic rams that set the ancient timber groaning against the wall brackets. 

Christina slid down against the locked door, gasping for air, her rapier clattering against the stones beside her knee. Blood dripped from a gash along her hairline, running into her left eyebrow, but her lips curled into that same unyielding, reckless grin. 

"Well," Christina laughed between ragged wheezes, "we certainly have their undivided attention now. Every tin soldier in Landosol is queuing up on our front porch."

Jun turned from the door, wiping soot and blood from the visor-slit of her helm, and looked at the heavy iron winch in the center of the bastion floor where the demolition lines converged.

"Tomo, report on the fuses," Jun commanded, her voice dropping into the solemn register of a commander facing the final ledger. "How much time do we have before that timber gives way?"
