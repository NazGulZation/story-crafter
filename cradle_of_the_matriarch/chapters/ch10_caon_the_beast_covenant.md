# Chapter 10: The Beast Covenant

The iron blast doors groaned on counterweighted chains as we heaved them open with pry-bars, venting a blast of hot, sweet air that smelled of ancient pine sap, wild honey, and scorched copper wire.

We stepped into the Heart Chamber.

The chamber was a cathedral of living wood and brutalist iron. Sixty feet across, its circular walls were formed entirely by the intertwined taproots of the Grand Oak, descending into the deep bedrock like the ribbed vaulting of a sunken temple. The moisture from the subterranean aquifers wept down the moss-covered bark in steady, glistening rivulets. In the center of the room, suspended over an abyssal fissure of dark turquoise spring water, hung the Heart of the Oak—a massive, fibrous sphere of amber resin six feet in diameter, pulsating with a slow, hypnotic green luminescence that bathed our battered armor in emerald warmth.

Kaiser’s engineers had encased the sphere in a spherical cage of heavy copper induction rings. Twelve hydraulic needles, thick as battering rams, were embedded in the living resin, draining glowing chartreuse lymph into overhead conduits that disappeared into the northern rock toward the Tower of Sol.

Every time the Heart pulsed, a low, grieving groan echoed through the timber, vibrating through the soles of our boots like the dying heartbeat of an ancient god.

Kasumi rushed to the primary control console bolted to a limestone ledge, her fingers flying over the brass levers and telemetry dials. Her monocle reflected cascading columns of warning runes that flashed angry, strobe-like crimson against the damp stone. The brass housing of the console was hot to the touch, venting thin plumes of acrid steam that smelled of boiling mineral acid.

"The telemetry is worse than I projected," Kasumi reported, her voice tightening with cold panic. "The destruction of the cistern sentinels tripped the emergency dead-man switch. In twenty-five minutes, the siphon array enters reverse-synchronization. It will drain the entire moisture and mana reserve of the southern province in one catastrophic surge to patch the Tower's upper cooling manifolds."

Makoto stepped to the edge of the chasm, looking down into the churning turquoise depths beneath the root-heart. Her jaw was set so hard the tendons in her neck stood out like cords beneath her sun-browned skin.

"How do we stop it?" Makoto demanded, her hand tightening around the rawhide grip of her broken greatsword. "Can we smash the induction rings?"

"If we cut the rings without establishing an anchor," Kasumi explained, slamming her hand against the iron casing of the terminal, "the stored pressure will detonate. The blast will vaporize the tree, the hollow, and the karst caves. The entire southern province will be reduced to a crater of crushed limestone. The only way to decouple the siphon is to execute the Twilight Covenant right now. Maho, how long does the chant take?"

Maho stepped onto the edge of the chasm, her torn silk skirt fluttering in the warm, fragrant updraft of the sacred spring. In her small hands, she held her ancestral ivory parasol, its tip resting gently against the pulsating amber surface of the root-heart.

"The ancestral words require twelve cycles of breath," Maho whispered, her fox ears drooping with heavy, heartbreaking sorrow. "The ritual is ready. The ancient wards remember our blood. But the keystone... the keystone must enter the resin before the sixth breath, or the boundary will collapse under its own weight."

Makoto took two rapid strides forward, her heavy leather boots ringing against the iron grating.

"Then I'm going in," Makoto snapped, her voice rough and aggressive, masking the tremor in her throat. She reached for the buckles of her sword harness, unhooking the heavy scabbard and letting it drop to the floor with a metallic crash. "I'm the captain of the vanguard. I swore to protect this clan when the old elders were dragged off to the foundries. It's my blood that belongs in this wood. Not his."

She turned toward me, her amber eyes burning with desperate, angry tears, pointing a calloused, trembling finger directly at my chest.

"Do you hear me, Yuuki?" she snarled, her breath coming in ragged hitches that shook her shoulders. "You've bled for this city in three hundred iterations! I remember the loop where you died in my arms in the orchard mud, choking on your own blood while the sky burned! I remember when your chest gave out on the castle bridge! You don't get to die for us again! You walk out of here, you take the survivors, and you live! Let me hold this damn door!"

She stepped toward the amber sphere, her hand reaching for the resin.

"Makoto, stop!" Kasumi yelled, lunging from the console to grab Makoto’s arm with both hands. "You can't! Look at the spectral analysis!"

Kasumi thrust her magnifying wand between Makoto’s eyes, projecting a glowing holographic waveform of the siphon’s lock into the damp air.

"The induction rings aren't keyed to beastfolk mana," Kasumi said, her voice shaking with bitter, scientific despair. "Kaiser anticipated a rebellion from Caon centuries ago. The root-lock is encrypted with Minerva's administrative protocol. A beastman soul doesn't have the bandwidth to override the command hierarchy. If you dive into that resin, Makoto, your avatar code will be incinerated into grey slag in three seconds, and the manifold will still detonate."

Makoto went rigid. She stared at the projection, her pupils contracting into pinpricks, her breathing stopping entirely. The greatsword slipped from her fingers, clattering against the grating.

"No," she choked out, dropping her head, her wolf ears folding flat against her white hair in complete, agonizing defeat. "No... that's a lie. That's a sick, rotten lie..."

"It isn't a lie, Makoto," I said quietly, stepping up beside her on the narrow ledge.

I looked down at my left arm. 

The azure crystal had reached my jaw now. A thin, cold line of sapphire had crept over my cheekbone, numb and flinty, shining in the green light of the root-heart. Inside my chest, the Princess Knight core was spinning with terrifying speed, humming with the exact harmonic frequency that Kaiser’s induction rings were fighting to suppress. The cold ache in my marrow had ceased to be an enemy; it had become an engine.

I was the only key. I had always been the only key.

"Yuuki..." Kaori whispered from the archway, her hands dropping to her sides, her tough, brawler’s bravado completely crumbling into silent, horrified grief. "No... please... there has to be another way. We can smash the foundries, we can storm the palace..."

"Listen to me, all of you," I said, my voice steady, cutting through the hum of the turbines and the low groan of the dying tree. "Eighty people are hiding in those caves. Families with children who haven't seen a patch of green grass or smelled sweet bread in three years. If we do this, the southern forest disappears from Kaiser’s maps forever. You get your twilight. You get your home. No machines, no tethers, no resets. An unbroken life."

Makoto grabbed both lapels of my tunic, shaking me with wild, desperate fury, her forehead pressing against my chest as sobs finally broke from her throat.

"I don't care about the damn trees!" she wept, her tears soaking through the linen of my collar. "I want you! We promised we'd eat roast boar together at the tavern! We promised we'd drink summer ale until the sun came up! How am I supposed to sit in a peaceful grove and look at a stone with your face on it?!"

I raised my right hand—the only hand of flesh and blood I had left—and gently rested my palm against the side of her face, wiping a tear from her cheek with my thumb. Her skin was warm, vibrant, alive with the fierce pulse of life that made this world worth defending.

"You remember me, Makoto," I told her softly. "You keep the fire burning. You teach those children how to run in the grass. That’s how you pay the debt."

Above our heads, the ceiling erupted in a deafening concussive explosion.

A massive shower of shattered limestone and twisted tree roots crashed into the cistern pool below, throwing waves of scalding water across the chamber. High above in the smoke, the four-legged iron hull of the first siege walker descended through the breach on hydraulic pistons, its twin rotary cannons whining as they spun up to maximum velocity, spewing sulfurous smoke into the sacred air.

Behind it, a second walker dropped onto the rim of the chasm, its heavy searchlight bathing the root-heart in harsh, blinding white glare.

Maho raised her ancestral parasol, her face set in majestic, tear-stained defiance, and began to sing the first notes of the ancient Twilight Chant. Her voice rose pure and clear above the din of grinding gears, resonating with the living timber of the oak.

Makoto scooped up her greatsword from the flags, her tears drying in the heat of the rotary cannons, her amber eyes burning with an unbroken, lethal promise. Kaori stepped up beside her, wrapping her bruised knuckles around her iron knuckles, while Kasumi loaded her last flash-cartridge into her wand.

I drew my notched steel arming sword with my right hand, planted my boots on the edge of the abyss, and faced the blinding glare of the siege engines.
