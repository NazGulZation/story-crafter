# Chapter 9: Petals in the Ash (Twinkle Wish Route)

The avenue connecting the Grand Plaza to the municipal cathedral was choked with knee-deep drifts of smoldering ash. 

Before the cradle enveloped the sky, the royal avenue had been lined with double rows of Astraean thorn-roses, famous throughout the southern provinces for their deep crimson petals and rich, clove-scented perfume. Now, the flower beds were carbonized trenches of brittle charcoal. The thermal vents running beneath the street had superheated the soil, cooking the roots until they cracked into cinder. Every gust of wind sweeping down from the high roofs whipped up swirling funnels of black dust mixed with burning purple petals that settled like hot coals upon our clothes.

I walked with my right arm wrapped firmly around Yui’s waist, taking more than half her weight as her boots dragged through the ash. 

Her body felt light as a bundle of dried willow boughs. Her fever had turned her skin waxen, and the veins along her wrists stood out in dark, branching traceries of violet ink. Every fifty paces, she doubled over in coughing spasms that sprayed fine grey dust across the front of my cloak.

"Just a little further, Yui," I murmured, tightening my grip as her knees buckled over a shattered stone curb. "Keep your eyes on the cathedral spires."

"The noise... Yuuki, do you hear it?" she whispered, her fingers clutching my sleeve with frantic strength. Her gaze darted between the dark, ruined facades of the merchant guildhalls lining the avenue. "It isn't wind. It's everyone who ever sat on the fountain steps... they're crying out for sleep. They think if they just stop thinking, the hunger will stop. They think if they surrender their names, the cold won't hurt anymore."

Beside us, Rei walked with her rapier drawn, her leather boots crunching deliberately through the cinder drifts. Her blade was held low, the steel tip catching the dull purple glow of the street like a needle of ice. Her sharp jaw was set in rigid lines, her eyes scanning the rooftops for the telltale glint of brass rangefinders.

"Kaiser isn't giving them sleep," Rei said coldly, her voice cutting through the hiss of the smoldering ash. "She is harvesting their cognitive drift to stabilize her own life-support matrices. The people think they are finding peace, but they are being processed into cooling oil for her converters."

"We're going to smash those converters," Hiyori said from the point of our wedge. 

She walked five paces ahead, her broad fists swinging at her sides. The golden solar enchantments on her gauntlets hummed with a quiet, steady thrum, leaving small trails of smoking embers in the soot behind her heel-irons. Her red tunic was torn along the ribcage where shrapnel had grazed her during the plaza fight, but her posture remained stubborn, broad-shouldered, and defiant.

"Look at that building ahead," Hiyori pointed with a blunt iron knuckle. "The old library. There's a resonance anchor bolted straight through the pediment."

The municipal library was an ancient three-story structure of grey granite, built during the early reigns of the Astraean dynasty. Its classical portico had been brutally modified by Kaiser’s engineers. Six thick cables of braided lead and copper descended from the roof, driving into the center of a black iron obelisk anchored into the library steps. 

The obelisk was twelve feet tall, forged from dull basalt and banded with pulsing brass plates that hummed with a deep, sickening baritone vibration. Around its base, hundreds of thin glass vials hung from brass manifolds, collecting a thick, glowing violet distilled from the cognitive arrays inside the archives. 

Whenever the obelisk pulsed, the air in the avenue rippled with heavy psychic turbulence. 

A sharp, nauseating wave of despair washed over my temples, forcing me to halt in my tracks. It felt like walking into an invisible wall of ice water—a sudden, suffocating certainty that every effort was futile, that our boots were merely dragging us toward a predetermined grave, that the world would be better off if we simply laid down in the warm ash and closed our eyes.

Yui cried out, falling to her knees in the cinder drift, her hands pressed over her ears as blood welled from her nostrils.

"Yui!" Hiyori turned, rushing back to kneel in the dirt beside her. 

"The anchor is siphoning the memories stored in the archive," Rei said, her teeth grinding together as she fought the psychic backpressure. She leaned heavily against a stone hitching post, her rapier trembling in her hand. "The birth records, the wedding contracts, the poetry collections... Kaiser is burning them to generate the negative resonance that holds Yui's wish in inversion."

From the dark windows of the library, the defenders revealed themselves.

They were not human soldiers. They were Kaiser's Porcelain Sentinels—graceful, eight-foot-tall automatons sculpted from white glazed ceramic, their faces blank and featureless save for a single vertical slit of crimson glass. They carried long, curved halberds of tempered steel, leaping down from the second-story balconies with terrifying, silent agility that sent clouds of ash billowing twenty feet into the air.

Six of them formed a defensive wall across the library steps, their halberd blades locking together into an unbroken hedge of steel.

Behind them, atop the library roof, a heavy hydraulic mortar rotated its brass barrel toward our position, its firing mechanism clicking into place with a hollow, metallic rat-tat-tat.

"They're guarding the manifold," Rei said, stepping between the sentinels and Yui. "If that mortar fires into these petal drifts, the fuel will vaporize us in an instant."

"Then we don't give it time to fire," Hiyori growled. She drove her fists together, the steel plates ringing like church bells. "Yuuki! Give me the spark!"

I didn't hesitate. 

I knelt beside Yui with my left hand resting flat against Hiyori’s backplate, while my right hand caught the pommel of Rei’s rapier.

The Princess Knight link surged through my chest like a torrent of molten copper. 

The azure crystal along my throat expanded, its razor-sharp edges biting through my skin in three thin, bleeding lines that ran down my neck into my collar. The agony was blinding, white and absolute, threatening to drown my vision in pure static. Yet behind the pain lay the raw, unyielding connection to the girls who had fought beside me through hundreds of forgotten timelines.

I poured that connection outward, holding nothing back.

Hiyori’s solar aura erupted with the blinding intensity of a noonday sun. The crimson and gold flames around her fists swelled into roaring wings of radiant heat that instantly turned the surrounding ash drifts into white incandescent slag. 

Rei’s rapier sang with a pitch so pure and razor-sharp that the glass vials hanging from the distant obelisk began to ring in sympathetic resonance. Her blue aura sharpened into a needle of absolute piercing force, enveloping her body in a slipstream of silent speed.

"Now, Rei!" Hiyori bellowed, launching herself forward like an artillery shell.

The lead Sentinel swung its heavy halberd in a sweeping horizontal arc designed to sever a horse at the shoulders. 

Hiyori didn't duck. She drove her right fist directly into the flat of the ceramic blade.

The impact produced a concussive shockwave that blasted the ash drifts forty paces back into the street. The ceramic halberd shattered into a cloud of chalky dust, and Hiyori’s fist continued through the sentinel's porcelain chest plate, blowing out its internal clockwork and hydraulic gears in a shower of screaming metal.

Before the remaining five sentinels could adjust their formation, Rei was already among them.

She was a ghost of blue steel moving through the cloud of white dust. Her rapier struck four times in the space of a single breath—the cervical joint, the right shoulder bearing, the hip swivel, the optical slit. Each thrust was executed with clinical, geometric perfection, severing the high-pressure hydraulic tendons before the automatons could complete their swings. 

The sentinels collapsed like marionettes with severed strings, their heavy porcelain limbs clattering uselessly against the stone steps.

Above them, the rooftop mortar finished its charging cycle with a high-pitched pneumatic whine. Its brass barrel dropped, targeting Hiyori’s back.

"Hiyori! Above you!" Rei shouted, recovering from her thrusts.

Hiyori didn't look up. She leaped twenty feet straight into the air, using the shattered shoulder of a sentinel as a springboard. 

Mid-air, she tucked her knees, ignited her solar thrusters, and slammed both fists down upon the mortar’s recoil mechanism like twin meteorites. The iron casing sheared clean off its mountings, the high-pressure steam reservoir detonating with a deafening crack that tore the library roof in half. Hiyori rode the falling wreckage down onto the granite steps, rolling through the debris and coming to a halt before the humming basalt obelisk.

"Rei! The manifold base!" Hiyori shouted, planting her boots wide. "Drive the spike!"

Rei sprang up the steps, her rapier glowing with the full concentrated power of my azure mana. She drove the thin blade into the junction where the lead cables met the basalt core.

"Break!" Rei cried out, twisting the hilt with all the strength in her shoulders.

The azure power inside the steel discharged with the force of a lightning strike. 

The basalt obelisk split down its vertical seam, cracking open like a hollow egg. The brass manifolds ruptured, spewing their stores of violet cognitive fluid across the stone, where the boiling liquid evaporated instantly into harmless, odorless white mist. The heavy cables ripped free from the ceiling, thrashing like dying serpents across the steps before burning out in a shower of harmless orange sparks.

The oppressive psychic fog in the avenue lifted in an instant.

The suffocating baritone hum died, replaced by the clean, quiet whisper of the wind rustling through the blackened branches of the dead rose trees.

On the street below, Yui drew her first clean breath in hours. 

She lifted her head from the ash, her amber eyes widening as the violent purple fever in her cheeks subsided, replaced by the faint, natural color of living blood. She pressed her hand to her throat, her chest rising and falling in smooth, steady rhythms.

"The voices..." she murmured, looking up at the sky where the violet smog had thinned enough to reveal the dark, leaden vault above. "They're quiet... the people aren't screaming anymore."

I walked up to her, sinking to one knee in the cinder. I took her hands in mine, rubbing the cold out of her slender fingers with my calloused flesh palm.

"One anchor down," I said softly, offering her a tired, reassuring smile. "Two to go, Yui. We're cutting her strings."

Rei and Hiyori walked down the library steps, their boots crunching through the shattered porcelain of the sentinels. Rei wiped a smudge of black grease from her cheek with her cuff, while Hiyori swung her bruised arms, blowing warm breath onto her reddened knuckles.

"The cathedral is right through this colonnade," Rei said, pointing her scabbard toward the end of the avenue. 

Beyond the library lay the Grand Arch of Astraea, leading directly onto the elevated stone esplanade of the High Cathedral. 

Rising above the rooflines, the twin gothic spires of the cathedral stood silhouetted against the dark sky. From its high rose window, a massive pillar of dark, inverted wish-mana roared upward into the clouds, feeding the central nexus of Kaiser’s cradle. The two remaining resonance anchors were anchored directly into the cathedral’s foundation piers, pulsing with violent, erratic bursts of power that shook the very ground beneath our feet.

"Kaiser knows we're here now," Hiyori said, stepping up beside Yui and slinging an arm over her shoulder with an easy, fearless grin. "That means she's going to throw everything left in the toy box at us. Good. I'd hate for her to say we didn't give her a fair fight."

Yui looked from Hiyori to Rei, and then turned her gaze to me. 

The fear in her eyes had not vanished—the terrible weight of what her wish had created still hung heavy in her heart. But behind the fear, a small, stubborn spark of the girl who had once baked clover bread in the warm sunshine of Landosol had returned. 

She gripped her white staff, leaning on it as she stood up straight on her own two feet.

"Let's go," Yui said, her voice steady and clear. "I want to see the stars again."

We stepped past the ruined library, advancing through the last drifts of ash toward the great bronze gates of the cathedral.
