# Chapter 11: The Sacred Siege

The rotary cannons opened fire with a continuous, tearing roar that sounded like canvas being ripped across the heavens by an iron hand.

Twenty-millimeter explosive shells chewed through the granite vaulting of the ceiling, spraying the Heart Chamber with clouds of pulverized rock, hot jagged shrapnel, and splintered oak timber. The concussive overpressure blew out our eardrums in a single instant, leaving our skulls ringing with high, shrill static while the air filled with the suffocating reek of burning cordite, scorched insulation, and boiling resin. Every bullet that struck the ancient roots tore deep, ragged gashes into the living wood, causing glowing green sap to bleed across the stone flags.

"Hold the perimeter!" Makoto’s roar cut through the static, a feral, soundless shape against the blinding muzzle-flash.

She lunged straight into the teeth of the gunfire, her greatsword trailing pale blue fire as she threw herself across the narrow iron gantry. The air around her whined with ricochets, lead fragments gouging her leather armor, but her wolf-instinct carried her forward in low, weaving bounds that gave the tracking sensors no static target.

Beside her, Kaori moved like a shadow through the smoke. She dipped beneath a volley of explosive tracer rounds, the blistering heat scorching the fur on her bare arms, and vaulted onto the lower chassis of the lead walker. Her iron-wrapped knuckles hammered into the rotary cannon's brass feed chute with rapid, concussive blows that cracked the housing, battering the ammunition belt until the cartridges jammed in the feed mechanism with a violent metallic screech.

The walker’s right hydraulic arm snapped downward, attempting to crush her like an insect against the stone wall.

I moved on pure instinct, leaping across the chasm. 

My crystallized left arm intercepted the hydraulic piston with a deafening impact that sent vibrations through my jaw and teeth. The petrified sapphire of my forearm gouged three inches into the hardened tool-steel of the ram, halting its descent in a shower of screaming sparks and burning oil. The azure crystal along my collarbone flared with blinding light, drawing the kinetic shockwave into my own ribcage, cracking two ribs on my right side with a sickening crunch that drove the breath from my lungs.

I didn't let go. I drove my notched steel arming sword upward into the walker's exposed oil manifold, twisting the blade with both hands to rip the copper hoses free.

Boiling hydraulic fluid sprayed across my chest, hissing violently against my leather brigandine. Deprived of pressure, the walker’s left front leg buckled with a groaning shriek of failing steel, tipping the twenty-ton machine sideways over the lip of the chasm.

"Makoto! Cleave the mounting!" I yelled through the spray of black oil.

Makoto was already in the air. 

Empowered by the Princess Knight’s resonance coursing through her blood, she descended like a thunderbolt, her greatsword glowing with brilliant azure fire. The heavy steel blade sheared straight through the walker's central pivot-ring, cleaving the chassis in two. With a thunderous splash that sent boiling turquoise water fifty feet into the air, the first siege walker plunged into the abyssal depths below the roots, swallowed by the subterranean dark.

Behind us, on the sacred ledge, Maho’s voice rose higher, clearer, cutting through the thunder of battle with ethereal, heart-stopping beauty.

*"Breath of the elder oak, seal the threshold,"* Maho sang, her fourth breath completing as glowing green runes cascaded from her ivory parasol. *"Let the sky forget our names. Let the iron sleep in the stone. Weave the twilight fold where no sorrow may enter... where the beast may run unchained beneath the moon..."*

The second siege walker swiveled its heavy turret, its red searchlight locking directly onto Maho’s unprotected silhouette.

A warning siren screamed from its chassis—a shrill, algorithmic klaxon that meant a lethal payload had armed. The dorsal missile bay slid open with a sharp pneumatic hiss, venting cold nitrogen as a six-foot heavy thermal bunker-buster rocket locked onto the target—a thermite lance designed to incinerate underground redoubts and vaporize organic matter.

"Maho, look out!" Kasumi screamed, firing her magnifying wand point-blank into the launcher, but the brass flash-cartridge only scorched the outer paint.

The missile launched.

The rocket motor ignited with a deafening, blinding flare of white-hot phosphorus, rocketing across the twenty yards of open air straight toward Maho’s chest.

There was no time to pull her away. There was no time to deflect it with steel.

I threw my entire body between the rocket and the fox princess.

I met the thermal warhead with my chest, my crystallized left arm locking around the nose cone, my right hand bracing against the missile's steel fins as we collided mid-air.

The world vanished in a roar of white fire.

The thermal blast incinerated the wool and leather of my tunic in a split second, scorching the skin of my right shoulder, my ribs, and my neck down to the raw meat. But the Princess Knight core in the center of my breastbone did not shatter. It opened wide—a bottomless thermodynamic abyss—swallowing the phosphorus fury, the concussive pressure, and the kinetic violence directly into my avatar code.

The heat was agonizing, beyond anything I had endured in three hundred cycles of war. It felt as if my blood had turned into molten lead, boiling inside my veins, searing my throat, blinding my eyes with flashes of sapphire light. My nervous system screamed as code burned and regenerated in microscopic loops of raw torment.

When the smoke cleared, the missile was gone.

The shockwave had shredded the copper induction rings surrounding the Heart of the Oak. The heavy needles were torn free from the living timber, their severed ends spitting showers of yellow sparks into the empty air. The siphon was broken. The ancestral mana was surging back into the roots of the tree, causing the amber sphere to pulse with brilliant, radiant emerald life that filled the cavern with the scent of wild elderflowers.

The second walker, caught in the backlash of the severed manifold, shuddered violently, its clockwork core detonating from within as its ammunition cooked off, collapsing into a heap of burning scrap against the far wall.

I hit the stone flags on my knees.

I couldn't feel my legs. I couldn't feel my left arm. The azure crystal had climbed over my chin, hardening the left side of my jaw, my cheek, and the edge of my left eye into solid, cold glass. Through my right eye, the world was dim, rimmed in gold and violet static. My breath came in shallow, rattling gasps that tasted of charred flesh and mineral lime.

Makoto fell to her knees beside me, her greatsword dropping with a dull clatter, her trembling hands hovering over my scorched, crystallized chest.

"Yuuki..." she whispered, her voice broken, tears streaming down her soot-streaked face. "Yuuki... look at me... please... don't close your eyes..."

Beside her, Maho completed the fifth breath.

The cavern began to hum with an ancient, primeval vibration. The walls of limestone were softening, dissolving into swirling clouds of soft, lavender twilight mist. The smell of ozone was gone, replaced by the scent of night-blooming jasmine, damp pine needles, and sweet moss.

*"The sixth breath..."* Maho whispered, her eyes brimming with tears as she looked down at me from the rim of the amber heart. Her voice trembled with ancient, sacred agony. *"The keystone must take the root, Sir Knight... or the twilight will collapse, and the fire will take the children... the boundary cannot hold without an anchor..."*

I looked at Makoto. I reached out with my right hand—the last scrap of living flesh I possessed—catching her trembling, bloodied fingers in mine, squeezing them with the fading warmth of my human heart.

"Take care of them, Makoto," I whispered through the stone at my jaw. "Give them a home where they never have to fear the sky."

Makoto choked on a sob, pressing her forehead against my hand, her tears hot against my knuckles. "I promise... I swear on my life, Yuuki... we will never forget you. Not in ten thousand seasons."

Beside her, Kaori knelt in the dust, her bruised forehead touching the stone flags in a warrior’s deepest salute, her voice thick with swallowed sorrow. "Thank you, big brother. You fought like an ancient titan. We'll tell every pup in the hollow who gave them their morning."

Kasumi took off her monocle, wiping the fog and tears from the glass with a trembling sleeve, bowing her head toward the dais. "The calculations are complete, Yuuki. The sanctuary will hold for an eternity. Your name will be written into the bedrock of this forest."

I pulled myself upright, step by agonizing step, my knees trembling beneath the dead weight of my petrified body. The heat from the amber sphere washed over my burned skin like a summer hearth fire, carrying the sweet, clean scent of blooming oak leaves and fresh rain. Through the swirling lavender mist, I could hear the distant, joyous cries of the refugee children in the karst caves as the suffocating leaden sky began to tear away above the forest, replaced by an eternal canopy of gentle stars and twilight indigo.

I reached the lip of the pool, laid my notched steel sword upon the mossy stone for Makoto to keep, and turned toward the glowing amber sphere of the Grand Oak.
