# Chapter 8: The Weeping Plaza (Twinkle Wish Route)

The descent from the stone rotunda plunged into the heart of Landosol's ancient commercial center. 

In the old days, before the sky curdled into the leaden dome of Kaiser’s cradle, the Grand Plaza of Astraea had been the living lung of the city. A thousand market stalls had crowded its concentric flagstones: spice merchants shouting over wicker hampers of southern cloves, bakers setting out golden loaves of sunflower bread, children darting between the legs of floral carters with paper pinwheels clutched in their fists. 

Now, the square was submerged beneath three feet of sluggish, clinging violet smog.

The miasma seeped directly from the seams between the marble paving stones, venting in steady, greasy plumes that smelled of charred honey and oxidized blood. Overhead, the great brass bell-clock of the municipal exchange hung half-crushed in its collapsed belfry, its giant pendulum frozen mid-swing like a gallows weight. Across the broad avenue, marble colonnades lay shattered into chalky rubble, their fluted shafts blackened by thermal backwash from Kaiser's neural converters.

I drew my dark wool cloak tight across my mouth and nose, coughing as the acidic smoke rasped against my throat. 

Every step downward along the terrace stairs sent cold tremors through my boots. The azure crystal coating my left arm and shoulder pulsed with rapid, stinging needles of heat, reacting to the ambient field of raw, inverted wish-magic flooding the basin. The energy in the air was thick, heavy with the phantom sound of weeping—a chorus of a hundred thousand overlapping whispers murmuring childhood regrets, forgotten promises, and the quiet despair of citizens who had traded their will to live for an endless, dreamless slumber.

At the center of the vast plaza, surrounded by concentric rings of shattered limestone fountain basins, rose the Altar of the Wish.

It had once been a white marble fountain carved in the likeness of an open lily, dedicated to the ancient spirits of spring. Now, its stone petals were split down the grain, weeping viscous violet sludge into the dry pools below. In the center of the cracked lily knelt Yui.

Her pale pink robes were torn at the hem, caked with greasy soot and dried mud. Her white staff lay across her knees, its golden crest bent and dripping with purple embers. Her hands were pressed flat against her chest, her fingers clawing at the linen of her collar as though suffocating. Every breath she drew produced a wet, shuddering wheeze, her shoulders trembling violently beneath the weight of the dark pillar of light erupting from the fountain floor directly into the smog above.

"Stand clear of the flank, Hiyori!" a sharp voice cracked through the fog.

Rei’s rapier whistled through the violet air. 

The blade struck a lunging silhouette made of coalesced ash and static, severing its formless torso with a crisp, shearing snap. The shadow creature collapsed into a puddle of inert grey sludge, but two more took its place, rising like tar from the cracks in the flagstones. Rei stumbled as her right boot found an oily puddle, her breathing ragged, her blue tunic drenched in dark sweat. The leather guard on her sword-grip had split; fresh blood seeped from her split palm, running down the steel ricasso in thin crimson rivulets.

Five paces ahead of her, Hiyori drove both fists into the flagstones.

Her iron-plated knuckles hit the marble with the concussive boom of an anvil drop. A shockwave of golden solar force tore through the smog, disintegrating a semicircle of creeping shadows into glowing dust. But the effort extracted an immediate price. Hiyori collapsed to one knee, coughing violently into the dirt, her chest heaving as crimson specks flecked her lips. The solar enchantments woven into her gauntlets flickered, sputtered, and died, leaving the metal cold and smoking.

"They just... keep crawling out of the pavement..." Hiyori gasped, wiping her mouth with a blistered forearm. "Rei... how many more are down there?"

"The entire municipal grid is feeding into the altar," Rei replied, her voice strained as she stepped back to cover Hiyori’s blind spot, her rapier held in a low guard. "Every soul Kaiser puts under in the eastern districts gets routed straight through Yui's frequency. Unless we sever the conduits, the pressure will rupture her heart."

A low, mechanical drone cut through the haze.

From the northern avenue, three Kaiser Vanguard Harvesters crawled into view. 

They were massive, crab-like engines forged from white porcelain and brass gears, their jointed limbs clattering against the marble flags with rhythmic, concussive weight. At the apex of each machine, a circular cage of tempered glass glowed with pulsing purple fluid, holding rows of submerged human brains harvested from Sector 4 defectors. Steel needle-arrays extruded from their undersides, seeking the perimeter of the fountain to siphon the remaining pure mana from the altar.

"Hiyori! Fall back to the fountain basin!" Rei barked, lunging forward to parry an automated harpoon fired from the lead chassis. 

The heavy iron spike glanced off Rei’s rapier with a shower of orange sparks, throwing her sideways against a shattered stone dolphin. The concussive blow drove the wind from her lungs, her rapier skittering across the flagstones.

The lead Harvester surged forward, its brass claws screeching against the stone as it raised a hydraulic crushing ram directly above Rei’s shoulder.

I kicked off the marble stairs.

The azure crystal covering my left arm caught the ambient mana in the air, roaring into bright cobalt fire. The sudden surge tore through my nerve endings, driving an agonizing spike of heat through my collarbone, but I ignored the ache. I threw my entire weight into the charge, drawing my notched steel sword with my right hand while thrusting my crystallized left palm forward.

"Get away from her!"

The blast of royal mana struck the porcelain harvester square in its brass chest plate.

The concussive shockwave ripped through the chassis, blowing its tempered glass cylinders into thousands of singing shards. The purple nutrient fluid splashed across the flags, sizzling like battery acid against the limestone. The massive machine overturned, its brass legs thrashing helplessly in the air before its central boiler detonated with a dull, muffled crump of black powder.

I landed between Rei and the remaining two machines, my boots skidding across the wet pavement. 

The azure light radiating from my left arm illuminated the dense fog, carving a ten-pace circle of pure white radiance that drove back the creeping shadows like vermin fleeing an open torch.

"Yuuki..." Rei whispered, her hand clutching her bruised ribs as she pushed herself up from the rubble. Her sharp blue eyes widened, taking in the crystal spreading across my throat and the ragged edges of my blade. "You came down to the plaza..."

"Yuuki!" Hiyori cried out, her battered face breaking into a wild, tearful grin. She tried to scramble to her feet, but her knees buckled from exhaustion, forcing her to lean heavily against the fountain rim. "You're here! You're really here!"

From the center of the cracked lily, Yui’s head lifted slowly.

Her face was translucent, pale as skimmed milk, her lips cracked and bleeding from the internal fever raging through her spirit. When her amber eyes met mine, a soft, strangled sob tore from her throat.

"No..." Yui wept, her fingers clawing at the stone flagstones as she tried to crawl toward me. "Yuuki... run... please, you have to run! This whole place... it's my fault! The cradle... Kaiser made it out of my wish! She took my voice and turned it into a cage for everyone!"

The remaining two Harvesters recalibrated their sensor clusters, their brass limbs screeching as they brought twin rotary repeaters to bear on the fountain.

"Talk later," I said, my voice low and raspy from the sulfur fumes. 

I reached out with my left hand, catching Rei by the shoulder and dragging her behind the fountain's granite pedestal just as a hail of lead shot chewed through the masonry where she had been lying. Stone chips showered our heads like hail.

I placed my right hand flat against the hilt of Rei’s rapier, and with my left, I gripped Hiyori’s cold iron gauntlet.

The Princess Knight core in my chest ignited.

It was an instantaneous, brutal expenditure of my remaining biological reserve. The blue crystalline growth along my ribs flared violently, biting deeper into my marrow. My lungs seized with an icy spasm, and I felt two hairline fractures spiderweb across the sapphire plate covering my left forearm. 

The raw, undiluted power flooded into Rei and Hiyori like liquid sunlight.

The split skin on Rei’s sword palm knit together under a surge of clean golden light. The dented steel of her rapier resonated with a high, bell-like hum, its balance restored to razor perfection. Beside her, Hiyori’s solar gauntlets ignited with a blazing corona of brilliant crimson and gold fire, burning away the soot, the grease, and the exhaustion that had crippled her shoulders.

"Rei! Flank them on the left!" Hiyori roared, her spirit surging back into her voice with the force of a thunderclap. "I'll take the lead core!"

"Understood!" Rei replied, stepping out from behind the pedestal with effortless, lethal grace.

Rei moved like a streak of blue lightning across the broken marble. Her rapier found the joint beneath the first Harvester’s rotating turret, driving clean through the brass steering pin and severing the main steam tendon with a single flick of her wrist. The machine slewed sideways, its guns chewing harmlessly into the rubble of an abandoned wine shop.

Before the second Harvester could bring its crushing ram down upon Rei, Hiyori arrived from above.

She leaped from the edge of the fountain basin, her body trailing a tail of blinding golden fire like a falling comet. Her twin fists slammed down into the machine's armored crown with catastrophic force. The porcelain shell pulverized instantly into chalk powder; the brass gears sheared their teeth; the boiler collapsed under ten tons of concentrated kinetic pressure. The machine pancaked flat against the pavement in a deafening crash of shattered brass and spewing steam.

The plaza fell silent, save for the crackle of burning oil and the heavy, rhythmic hiss of steam escaping the ruined chassis.

Hiyori landed lightly on the flags, shaking the glowing white dust from her fists, while Rei flicked a drop of black grease from the tip of her rapier and slid it back into her scabbard.

I turned back toward the center of the fountain.

Yui was still kneeling in the violet sludge, her hands pressed over her face, weeping into her sleeves. Around her knees, the cracks in the fountain floor continued to bleed dark purple light, rising like thorny briars into the smog above. Every pulse of the altar sent a spasm of pain through her small frame, shaking her until her teeth clicked together.

I stepped across the cracked rim of the basin, walking through the shallow sludge until I stood before her.

I knelt down on the cold stone, ignoring the acidic sting that seeped through my trousers. I reached out with both hands—my calloused flesh right hand and my cold, smooth crystal left hand—and gently pulled her fingers away from her face.

Her skin was burning hot, drenched in cold, greasy sweat. Her eyes were swollen red, filled with a deep, bottomless terror that went far beyond the fear of death.

"Yuuki..." she whispered, her voice barely louder than the steam venting from the wreckage. "In the beginning... all I wanted was for you to smile. I wanted a world where no one had to cry, where we could all just sit together under the trees and eat clover bread. But Kaiser heard it. She took that wish and twisted it into this nightmare. She said a world without tears is a world without choices. She's putting everyone to sleep because of me..."

"It isn't your fault, Yui," I said, wiping a streak of black soot from her cheek with the back of my right thumb.

"It is!" she sobbed, burying her forehead against my chest, her tears soaking into the rough wool of my cloak. "The altar is locked to my soul. If I try to shut it down, the backpressure will detonate the central core. Ten districts will burn in an instant! But if I stay here, the cradle will finish absorbing everyone by sundown!"

Rei and Hiyori climbed into the basin, stepping quietly through the muck to stand at our sides. Rei placed a gentle, gloved hand on Yui’s trembling shoulder; Hiyori knelt down, wrapping both her arms around Yui's waist with fierce, protective warmth.

"We aren't letting you carry this alone, Yui," Hiyori said softly, her forehead resting against Yui’s hair. "We started Twinkle Wish together. We walked the road together. If the road is full of thorns, we step on the thorns together."

"The primary feed line runs directly beneath the central cathedral," Rei said, her sharp eyes scanning the thick smoke drifting from the southern edge of the square. "Kaiser has anchored three heavy resonance anchors to the cathedral nave to amplify the inverted wish. If we advance through the burning petal drifts and shatter those anchors, we can relieve the pressure on Yui's spirit long enough to reach the root."

I looked toward the southern end of the plaza. 

Through the dense purple haze, the black silhouette of the grand cathedral loomed like a fortress of obsidian, its rose window glowing with the sickening, rhythmic pulse of Kaiser’s converters. Between us and the cathedral lay two hundred paces of shattered glass, burning debris, and thick drifts of smoldering purple rose petals blown from the city gardens.

I stood up, helping Yui to her feet, supporting her weight against my shoulder as she clutched her staff.

The road ahead was paved in fire and ash, but the covenant held unbroken.
