# Chapter 11: The Last Cavalcade

The heavy stone slab of the escape tunnel had barely settled into its bedrock recess when the outer gatehouse arch groaned under the impact of twenty-ton pneumatic rams. 

Through the narrow arrow-slits, the freezing rain fell in silver needles against the burning ruins of the stable yards. The three watchmen who had remained behind with me—veteran guardsmen from the Lower District whose surcoats were stained with old soot and dried blood—checked the tension on their heavy windlass crossbows, their knuckles grey with cold and lime-dust.

"Hold your fire until they clear the outer lintel," I told them, my voice rattling slightly from the stiff crystal at my throat. "Make every quarrel count in the choke."

An older guardsman, a man with grey-shot whiskers and a deep sabre scar across his cheek, spit a mouthful of tobacco juice onto the flags. He didn't look terrified; he looked tired, with that grim, patient tiredness of an infantryman who had stood in too many muddy ditches.

"We know the drill, lad," the old guardsman said quietly, leveling his heavy iron-spurred bow at the breach. "Told my girl in the wagons to keep heading south till the pines turned green. She’s safe. That’s enough for me."

The oak doors gave a final, catastrophic shriek. 

The central drill punched completely through the wood, followed by the concussive slam of three heavy ceramic rams. The iron locking bar sheared in two with a sound like a cannon shot, and the heavy oak doors burst inward off their hinges in a blinding cloud of splintered timber, dust, and sparks, revealing the smoke-choked bridge packed with hundreds of advancing Porcelain Paladins, their white crescent poleaxes lowered for the kill.

The watchmen released their strings in unison.

The heavy steel quarrels struck the lead machines at point-blank range, punching through the eye-seams and hydraulic shoulder-collars with bone-shattering force. Two paladins dropped, their internal capacitors shorting out in showers of bright yellow sparks, but the momentum of the imperial vanguard was an avalanche. A volley of high-pressure magnesium darts answered from the smoke, pinning the three guardsmen to the stone wall in flashes of searing white fire.

They died without a cry, their bows clattering to the floor.

I was alone on the threshold.

The first porcelain paladin crossed the broken timber in a blinding burst of hydraulic steam, its crescent poleaxe raised high for a downward cleave that would have split an anvil in two.

I stepped inside the arc of the swing, feeling the cold wind of the blade brush the hair on my temple. I parried the ash-wood haft with the flat of my notched steel blade, throwing the machine’s center of gravity forward, and rammed my crystallized left fist straight into the automaton's optical sensor. The hard azure glass shattered the ceramic faceplate into a shower of whistling white shards, pulping the copper relays and optical fibers beneath in a geyser of boiling black oil. 

The machine went limp, but before its twenty-stone chassis could hit the flagstones, two more surged through the splintered doorway directly over its falling armor.

The threshold of the gatehouse became a butcher's shop of iron, steam, and stone.

I fought with cold, mechanical economy, every instinct honed by hundreds of failed resets. There was no space for grand maneuvers or flourishes in that six-foot breach. When an eight-foot centurion lunged with a heavy boarding pike, I deflected the thrust with my petrified forearm, the hard sapphire bone absorbing the kinetic strike with a dry screech that threw showers of blue sparks across my tunic. I stepped inside the thrust, driving my sword upward through its armpit into the steam governor, twisting the steel to rip the copper feed-tubes from their housing.

High-pressure steam hissed into my face, scalding my right cheek, but I didn't blink. I kicked the automaton's knee with my iron-capped boot, fracturing the bronze hinge, and shoved its dead bulk backward into the charging ranks outside.

The noise inside the stone hall was an unbroken wall of deafening percussion. 

The violent clash of forged steel against ceramic plate, the high-frequency shriek of failing servos, and the continuous roar of the freezing gale blowing through the broken doorway created a chaotic symphony that rattled the teeth in my jaw. The air was thick and nearly unbreathable, choked with the sulfur of burning powder, the bitter chemical stink of vaporized machine oil, scorched wool, and the hot, metallic tang of my own blood leaking through the tears in my leather brigandine.

More came. Dozens. Scores. A relentless, unfeeling tide of white enamel and cold bronze.

Kaiser’s central routing algorithm had detected the prolonged resistance at the gatehouse. Believing the Princess Knight was cornered and attempting a desperate breakthrough toward the mountains, the automated command network funnelled the entire imperial shock reserve onto the Grand Causeway.

Through the narrow murder-holes and arrow-slits set into the three-foot masonry walls, I could see the full scale of the trap. 

The bridge was completely choked with white ceramic armor. Two full regiments—over eight hundred heavy paladins, sixty centurions with whirring saws, and thirty shock centaurs—were packed shoulder-to-shoulder on the fifty-foot-wide granite span. Their white banners snapped violently in the howling gorge wind, their heavy boots and iron hooves striking the stone in an unbroken, rhythmic earthquake that made the mortar dust rain continuously from the ceiling.

They were exactly where we needed them to be. Every unit that crowded onto that bridge was a unit that could not pursue the wagons in the pass.

A heavy poleaxe caught me across the right ribcage.

The forged steel blade sliced through my boiled leather armor and the coarse linen beneath, biting deep into the muscle, cracking the seventh rib with a sickening, wet crunch that drove every scrap of air from my lungs. The force of the blow lifted me clean off my feet, throwing me backward across the wet, oil-slicked flags. I hit the stone hard, skidding five yards into the base of the central powder stack, coughing up a bright, frothy spray of crimson that splattered across the hemp fuse-ropes.

Pain ripped through my chest—sharp, jagged, and blinding, radiating through my collarbone into my spine. My right arm hung limp at my side, the fingers numb, slippery with blood, barely able to keep a desperate grip on the hilt of my sword.

Three Porcelain Paladins advanced through the smoke, their featureless white heads tilting down with algorithmic dispassion, their crescent poleaxes raised in unison for the execution stroke.

I pressed my back against the iron-hooped oak of the central powder keg, breathing in ragged, whistling gasps that tasted of charred flesh and brine. 

I looked past their white shoulders, through the narrow arrow-slit in the southern wall that looked out over the misty mountain gorge.

Four miles down the winding pass, the rear wagons of the refugee train were clearing the narrow bottleneck of the karst defile. The torches and oil lanterns of the ten thousand survivors were winding safely into the deep, pine-covered folds of the limestone hills, beyond the reach of imperial artillery. At the head of the convoy, an unmistakable flash of blue steel and a banner of black wool caught the pale grey light of the horizon—Captain Jun and Christina, their heavy cavalry screen formed in an unbroken, defiant wall between the helpless citizens and the open road.

Jun had kept her oath. Christina was holding the pass. The weavers, the tanners, the wounded watchmen, and the children were across the river.

They were free.

A slow, peaceful smile touched my bloody lips, cracking the crust of dried crimson at the corner of my mouth.

"That's far enough," I rasped into the sulfur-choked smoke. "You don't take another step."

The three paladins swung their axes in unison, the steel whistling through the air on downward execution arcs.

I did not raise my sword to parry. 

I opened my right hand, letting the notched steel arming sword clatter harmlessly against the stone flags. I raised my crystallized left arm, the petrified sapphire veins glowing with blinding, incandescent brilliance, and slammed my open palm flat against the iron hoop of the central powder keg.

The azure crystal along my arm, my shoulder, my neck, and the left side of my skull erupted in pure, unbridled mana.

I opened the Princess Knight core in my chest wide. 

I did not channel a controlled pulse; I did not meter the flow. I dumped the entirety of my remaining avatar code, my life-force, and the accumulated thermodynamic heat of three hundred failed cycles directly into the thirty thousand pounds of military black powder.

The iron hoop began to glow cherry-red in a fraction of a second. The saltpeter, charcoal, and sulfur inside the thirty kegs hissed, expanding into white-hot gas as the alchemical fire-flasks cracked under the heat.

The stone hall vanished in a blinding sun of crimson fire, and the Grand Causeway screamed as the earth gave way.
