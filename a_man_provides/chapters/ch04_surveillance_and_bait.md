# Chapter 4: Surveillance and the Bait

The morning air smelled of wet asphalt and decomposing maple leaves. 

At seven-fifteen on Saturday morning, Gustavo stood in the front entryway of the house, dressed in a pressed charcoal wool three-piece suit, an immaculate white spread-collar shirt, and a burgundy silk tie fastened in a tight Windsor knot. Beside his polished leather oxfords sat a single black ballistic-nylon carry-on suitcase.

Sarah stood at the base of the staircase in a pastel pink silk robe, holding a ceramic mug of steaming French vanilla coffee. Her hair was loose around her shoulders, her face glowing with the dewy sheen of expensive nighttime moisturizer.

"Are you sure you can't push this to next weekend, honey?" she asked. Her voice was laced with a sweet, solicitous pout, delivered with practiced domestic innocence. "It's so cold outside. Traveling all the way to Detroit for an internal compliance summit on forty-eight hours' notice seems completely unfair. Your regional director takes advantage of your good nature."

Gustavo adjusted his cuffs, verifying that precisely one-half inch of white linen showed beyond the sleeve of his tailored jacket.

"Regional inventory adjustments require strict operational oversight, Sarah," he replied, his baritone steady and serene. "When irregularities occur in the logistics chain, an in-person audit is mandatory. It is the responsibility of senior personnel to ensure compliance."

Sarah smiled, leaning upward to press a soft kiss against his cheek. The scent of her vanilla lotion mingled with the clean wool of his lapel. "You're too dedicated for your own good, David. Call me when your flight lands at Metro Wayne, okay? I'll make sure the house is warm when you get back on Monday night."

"I will contact you upon arrival," Gustavo said. 

He lifted his suitcase with his left hand, turned the brass deadbolt, and stepped out onto the porch. The morning fog was dense, clinging to the manicured lawns and silver birch trees lining the suburban crescent. He walked down the aggregate concrete walkway, placed his bag in the trunk of the silver Honda sedan, and started the engine. 

He drove down the winding streets of the subdivision, signaled at the main boulevard, and turned north toward the interstate. 

Two miles from the house, at the edge of an older residential neighborhood bordered by heavy timber, Gustavo turned into a quiet commercial avenue. He parked the Honda behind an all-night laundromat, locking the doors with the remote fob. 

Waiting for him in the adjacent municipal pay lot was a rented dark grey Toyota Camry, reserved three days earlier under a corporate account with prepaid collision coverage. Gustavo transferred his briefcase and an insulated steel thermos to the passenger seat of the rental vehicle. 

He drove back toward the subdivision, avoiding the primary arterial roads. He entered a residential street running parallel to his own cul-de-sac, parking beneath the heavy canopy of an overgrown silver maple four hundred yards from the Ward property line. From this vantage point, framed between two brick garden walls, the intersection leading into his crescent remained clearly visible through the passenger window.

Gustavo shifted the transmission into park and cut the ignition. 

He opened the leather briefcase resting upon his knees. Inside sat the hardened Linux tablet, a pair of noise-canceling studio monitor headphones, and a secondary high-speed cellular data uplink. He plugged the audio lead into the auxiliary jack, secured the padded earcups over his ears, and brought the three-camera surveillance array online.

The interior of the Ward residence appeared on the divided screen in crisp, high-definition four-thousand-pixel resolution. 

In the lower quadrant—the living room camera concealed within the decorative bookend—the front entryway was deserted. Sunlight filtered through the leaded glass sidelights, casting diagonal bars of dust across the hardwood floor. 

In the upper quadrant—the master bedroom camera nestled within the cold-air return vent—the king-sized bed was already stripped of its decorative throw pillows. 

At eight-thirty, Sarah entered the bedroom frame. 

She was humming a pop melody. She had shed the pink robe, wearing instead a sheer crimson silk camisole, high-waisted denim shorts that hugged her hips tightly, and heavy gold hoop earrings. She applied a fresh coat of glossy lip tint before the vanity mirror, tilting her head to admire her reflection with evident, purring satisfaction. 

There was no trace of the nervous homemaker who had flinched over tire tracks twelve hours earlier. Her posture was buoyant, eager, and full of illicit vitality. 

She checked her smartphone, tapped a rapid reply, and danced lightly across the Persian rug toward the adjoining bath to mist herself with peach-blossom perfume.

Gustavo observed her through the glass screen. He poured four ounces of hot black coffee from his thermos into the plastic cup lid, sipping slowly. His pulse remained at sixty-two beats per minute.

For four hours, the house remained quiet. Sarah lounged in the master suite, scrolling through social media feeds, drinking mimosas from a crystal flute, and adjusting the lighting in the bedroom to cast soft, flattering shadows across the bedsheets.

At one-forty-five in the afternoon, a deep, rhythmic rumble vibrated through the Toyota’s closed chassis.

Gustavo looked up from his screen toward the street corner. 

The lifted black Ford F-250 roared down the boulevard, its heavy off-road tires singing against the pavement. The truck turned sharply into the cul-de-sac, tires clipping the curb before accelerating aggressively down the pavement. 

A moment later, the front entryway camera on the tablet flared with movement. The shadow of the massive truck darkened the front windows as Derek Holt parked directly across the Ward driveway, blocking the garage entirely.

Holt did not knock. 

The heavy brass handle turned. The front door swung inward with a dull thud against the baseboard stop.

Holt stepped into the foyer. He wore a tight black thermal compression shirt that showcased his broad chest and vascular forearms, worn blue denim jeans with faint grease stains across the thighs, and heavy steel-toed work boots caked in dried grey mud. A heavy gold curb chain hung over his collar, catching the sunlight.

Sarah came flying down the staircase. Her bare feet slapped rapidly against the hardwood treads. 

"Derek!" she squealed, throwing herself into his arms before he had even closed the door.

Holt caught her by the waist with one thick, tattooed arm, swinging her off her feet with effortless, arrogant strength. His booming laughter crackled through Gustavo’s studio headphones, guttural and triumphant.

"Look at you, little girl," Holt rasped, burying his face into the crook of her neck with an aggressive, smacking kiss. "Look at what you put on for me. You knew Daddy was coming, didn't you?"

"Stop it, you're tracking mud everywhere!" she gasped, laughing, though her hands were already tangled in the short hair at the nape of his neck, pulling him closer. "David spent three hours waxing those floorboards on Sunday."

Holt let out a loud, mocking snort. He deliberately dragged the heavy lug soles of his boots across the polished oak, leaving two broad, greasy smears across the grain.

"Screw your accountant," Holt sneered, slapping her rear with a sharp, resounding crack that made her jump and giggle. "Where is the pencil-pusher, anyway? Still on his knees begging for extra shifts at the freight terminal?"

"He's in Detroit until Monday night," Sarah whispered breathlessly, leaning her forehead against his chest, her eyes sparkling with illicit excitement. "An emergency audit or something completely boring. He packed his little suitcase and left at dawn."

"Good. The guy's a pathetic joke," Holt said, walking into the kitchen while keeping an arm clamped possessively around her waist. "Man works seventy hours a week to buy you this palace, and he can't even keep you satisfied for thirty minutes. You should thank me for keeping your engine running, baby."

Sarah pouted, tapping his broad chest with her manicured fingernails, yet her body leaned eagerly into his frame. "Derek, be nice. He was asking questions yesterday. He actually noticed the tire tracks near the curb. He noticed the two inches missing from his single malt scotch."

Holt paused, throwing his head back in a roar of contemptuous laughter. He pulled open the liquor cabinet above the counter, spotted the bottle of Glenfiddich, twisted the cork free, and took a long, arrogant swallow straight from the glass neck.

"What did the little nerd do?" Holt scoffed, wiping his mouth with the back of his tattooed forearm. "Did he cry? Did he threaten to call the police on his own lawn?"

"I told him my brother stopped by and drank it," Sarah giggled, wrapping her arms around Derek’s waist from behind, resting her cheek against his back. "He believed every single word. He patted my hand and told me not to worry! He's so clueless, Derek. He literally apologized for making me feel interrogated."

Holt turned in her embrace, his grin widening with predatory amusement. He reached out, gripping her jaw firmly between his thumb and forefinger, lifting her face until her hazel eyes locked with his.

"Remember six months ago, Sarah?" Holt murmured, his voice dipping into a low, mocking growl. "Remember when I showed you those pictures from Cabo in the parking lot? Remember how you cried? You were shaking like a leaf. You got on your knees in my truck, begging me not to tell your boring husband. You swore you'd never touch me again."

A sudden flush rose across Sarah’s cheeks. Her lips parted, her breath hitching in her throat. The memory of her initial terror flashed through her eyes, yet it dissolved almost instantly into the dark, intoxicating thrill that had replaced it.

"I hated you," she whispered, her voice husky, trembling with a mixture of shame and arousal. "I thought you were the most disgusting man on earth."

"And look at you now," Holt laughed softly, running his thick thumb over her lower lip. "Now you leave the door unlocked for me. You put on red silk. You count the minutes until hubby gets on an airplane so I can take you in his own bed. You love this, don't you? You love knowing that I own you right under that nerd's roof."

Sarah closed her eyes, letting out a ragged, shuddering breath. She leaned her weight into his chest, her arms wrapping tightly around his neck, surrendering completely to the intoxicating humiliation of the dynamic.

"Yes," she whispered, her voice barely audible over the hum of the refrigerator. "You're a monster, Derek. An absolute monster."

"That's why you keep coming back, baby," Holt grinned, grabbing the waistband of her shorts and hauling her toward the stairs. "Come on. Let's go christen hubby's bed again. I want to see if that headboard still squeaks against the drywall."

Sarah giggled, allowing herself to be dragged up the stairs.

In the master bedroom, the overhead camera captured the subsequent hour with terrifying fidelity. 

Holt kicked off his boots, letting them drop heavily onto David’s wool area rug. He walked over to the mahogany wardrobe, pulled David’s plush navy-blue fleece robe from the hanger, slipped his broad arms into the sleeves, and tied the sash loosely around his waist. He strutted across the room, admiring himself in the full-length dressing mirror, laughing at how the tailored garment strained across his shoulders.

He threw Sarah across the mattress. 

There was no coercion. The bedroom became a stage for base, unbridled indulgence. Sarah actively encouraged his rough handling, wrapping her legs around his waist, whispering degrading comparisons between his strength and David’s physical inadequacy, moaning loudly into the pillows as Holt drove his hips into her with athletic, rhythmic arrogance.

In the front seat of the rented Toyota four hundred yards away, Gustavo Fring sat with his hands resting calmly upon the steering wheel.

The audio feed piped their heavy breathing, vulgar insults, and mattress springs directly into his ears. On the tablet screen, the visual detail was absolute.

Gustavo did not avert his eyes. He did not tighten his jaw. His heart rate did not climb by a single stroke. 

David Ward’s ghost might have broken down in tears, shattered by the horrific betrayal of a partner he had idolized and slaved to support. But Gustavo Fring was not David Ward. 

Gustavo viewed the scene before him with the cold, sterile objectivity of an executive reviewing an inspection report on an irredeemable commercial property. The woman was not a victim of circumstance; she was a vain, morally bankrupt narcissist who traded her husband’s sweat and dignity for the sordid thrill of playing the rebel with a neighborhood bully. And the man was an arrogant, shortsighted parasite whose hubris had convinced him that brute force made him invulnerable to the laws of reality.

Gustavo lifted his hands to the tablet keyboard. 

He did not need to scream. He did not need to engage in an undignified physical scuffle on the front lawn. Violence of that nature was the refuge of weak, undisciplined men.

True destruction was systemic, legal, and total.

Gustavo tapped the screen, opening a split window on his terminal. He initiated the export protocol. 

The system compiled the four-thousand-pixel video stream, embedding synchronized audio tracks, UTC timestamps, and cryptographic checksums verifying that the files were unedited raw forensic captures. He routed the files through an onion-routed proxy network, mirroring the complete archive into three separate offshore cloud storage vaults located in Switzerland, Iceland, and Panama.

Next, Gustavo accessed a secure, encrypted virtual machine partition. 

During his research over the previous forty-eight hours, he had systematically mapped Derek Holt’s real-world operational vulnerabilities. Holt believed he was an untouchable kingpin, yet his empire was built on rotting timber.

Gustavo opened a pre-drafted dossier addressed to the Drug Enforcement Administration’s Regional Field Office and the County District Attorney’s Special Investigations Bureau. 

The document was titled: *Confidential Referral: Unlawful Interstate Distribution of Controlled Substances and Pattern Harassment.*

Gustavo populated the dossier with lethal, incontrovertible specificity:
- Full legal name and tax identification number of Derek Ryan Holt.
- Primary commercial operating hub: Iron Vault Fitness, Suite 104, Westpark Center.
- Secondary commercial asset: Holt Customs & Performance, 880 Railhead Road.
- Specific logistical shipping vectors: Four commercial freight waybills routed through Mid-Valley regional distribution hubs over the previous six weeks, complete with container seal identifiers, shipping manifests, and receiving signatures bearing Holt’s distinctive cursive mark for illicit shipments of grey-market Mexican somatropin and testosterone enanthate.
- Distribution methodology: In-person cash handoffs conducted in the west parking lot of Iron Vault Fitness, including the license plate and registered owner of the black BMW observed on Friday morning.
- Pattern of Predatory Harassment: Detailed documentation of Holt’s history of using unauthorized digital recordings to intimidate female gym clients and suburban acquaintances, establishing an ongoing pattern of criminal coercion under state statutes.

Gustavo read through the document twice, verifying every alphanumeric string for absolute accuracy. 

He opened a secondary complaint addressed to the Board of Managers of Iron Vault Fitness, detailing Holt’s unauthorized distribution of controlled compounds on club premises and documenting systemic civil liability that exposed the facility's majority investors to catastrophic commercial lawsuits.

Gustavo checked his watch. It was three-fifteen in the afternoon.

On the bedroom monitor, the performance was concluding. Holt lay sprawled across David’s side of the mattress, sweating heavily, scrolling through his phone while wearing David's robe. Sarah knelt beside him, running her manicured fingers through the hair on his chest.

"You really own this place, don't you?" Sarah murmured, resting her chin on his sternum with an admiring smile. "David would die if he saw you in his robe."

"Let him die," Holt sneered, slapping her hip casually. "He pays the mortgage, I take the wife. That's the way the world works, baby. Strong men take what they want. Weak men pay the bills."

"You're terrible," she whispered, kissing his jaw. "Stay for dinner. I'll make us something."

"Nah. I got business at the shop," Holt said, swinging his heavy legs off the bed and tossing David's robe carelessly onto the floor. "Keep the bed warm for Tuesday. And leave that front door unlocked."

Gustavo watched Holt pull on his boots, lace them halfway, and grab David’s scotch bottle off the dresser, tucking the remaining liquor into his jacket pocket.

Gustavo turned back to his laptop screen. 

He clicked the primary dispatch icon. 

Through an automated, multi-node encrypted relay service designed to prevent source IP tracking, the comprehensive narcotics and harassment referral was transmitted directly into the priority intake queue of the federal task force and county prosecutors. Simultaneously, the civil liability notice was delivered into the personal inboxes of the gym’s corporate ownership group.

The data packets dissolved into the fiber-optic currents of the state infrastructure.

At three-forty-five, Derek Holt swaggered out of the front door, slamming it shut behind him. The black Ford F-250 roared to life, backed recklessly out of the driveway, and accelerated toward the highway with a cloud of black diesel smoke.

Inside the quiet cabin of the rented Toyota, Gustavo Fring leaned back against the headrest. 

He closed the lid of his laptop with a quiet, satisfying snap. He removed the studio headphones, wound the cord in neat, concentric loops, and placed them into their fitted velvet pouch.

The trap was operational. The bait had been taken. The arrogant predator had stamped his own ticket to oblivion, blind to the machinery turning in the dark.

Gustavo uncapped his thermos, poured the remaining two ounces of lukewarm black coffee into the lid, and drank it in a single, measured swallow. He started the engine, shifted into drive, and pulled smoothly away from the curb, blending seamlessly into the quiet afternoon traffic.
