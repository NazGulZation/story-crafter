# Chapter 12: The Horns of the Karst Horde

When a feudal kingdom faces an invading army of six thousand horsemen, it collapses into religious hysteria.

The priests ring cathedral bells until their knuckles bleed; the nobles gather in gilded chambers to argue over who shall command the vanguard; wealthy merchants hide their gold in wine barrels and flee toward the coast; and the common peasantry is herded into open fields with sharpened bean-poles, told that dying for the Duke’s honor guarantees immediate entry into paradise.

In the *Ordensstaat*, such theatrical panic is categorized as an unexcused operational disruption.

At 0630 hours, beneath a sky the color of frozen slate, the Central Siren of Karst-Hold began its descent into acoustic madness.

*WUUUUU-WUUUUU-WUUUUU.*

It was not a church bell. Church bells ring with erratic, syncopated spiritual vanity. This was a steam-driven rotary siren, powered by high-pressure exhaust from Blast Furnace No. 1, projecting a ninety-decibel, mechanized mechanical wail that echoed off the dolomite bluffs for five square leagues.

Across Sectors Alpha, Beta, and Gamma, the response was mathematically immediate.

There was no shouting. There was no weeping in the streets. Doors did not bang open to reveal panicking mobs.

Instead, two thousand four hundred registered citizens emerged from their standardized timber barracks in silent, double-column files. Men who had spent three months drilling with sixteen-foot pikes walked with synchronized, locked-knee strides toward the outer armory; women of the Agricultural Reserve marched to the granaries to distribute pre-packaged four-day dry biscuit rations; teams of six laborers wheeled two-wheeled carts laden with greased steel quarrels toward the parapets.

Within eighteen minutes of the first siren blast, every defensive sector was manned, every ammunition magazine unlocked, and every drainage sluice opened to flood the lower marshlands.

I stood on the command platform of the North Bastion, wearing my oiled storm-coat and a pair of steel-reinforced leather gauntlets.

At my left stood Master Founder Torvald, clutching an iron sighting quadrant; at my right, Director Weidmann, holding a clipboard with casualty replacement ledgers.

Two miles north, across the frozen peat plains of the Karst Basin, the steppe coalition was assembling.

Through my brass field telescope, the spectacle was magnificent in its biological barbarism. Six thousand nomadic horsemen, allied with three highland mountain clans, stretched across the northern horizon like a swarm of angry black hornets. They beat upon heavy bronze kettledrums that produced a deep, chest-rattling *BOOM... BOOM... BOOM*, their painted leather standards fluttering in the freezing wind.

They had brought horse-archers with double-curved recurve bows, shock cavalry wrapped in layers of felt and boiled leather, and four thousand screaming infantry brandishing spiked morningstars, broad-bladed cleavers, and heavy round shields painted with demon faces.

They had conquered three southern provincial baronies in forty days. They had burned fourteen feudal castles to their foundations, slaughtered the chivalric knights in their glittering armor, and enslaved thirty thousand peasants.

To their chieftain, the "Great Khagan Batur," the grey concrete-faced fortress before them looked like just another frontier outpost—perhaps a bit sturdier than the others, but ultimately doomed to be overwhelmed by a tide of six thousand howling warriors.

Below my command platform, in the secondary support trench behind the main parapet, the *Penal Labor Battalion* was assembling.

Among them was Labor Asset 001-M: Marek of High-Crest.

Marek had spent forty-eight hours in a pitch-black concrete isolation cell on a diet of stale water and cold turnip peelings. His face was a mottled mosaic of yellow and purple contusions from the riot batons; his head had been shaved down to raw stubble; and his six-foot-four muscular frame was swathed in an ill-fitting, oversized grey convict smock stamped with a large black numeral *0*.

On his feet were unpadded wooden clogs that clattered awkwardly in the trench gravel.

Yet, as the deep, thunderous boom of the barbarian war-drums vibrated through the earth, a spark of his old, deluded arrogance flickered back to life in his bruised eyes.

He looked up at the platform where I stood, puffed out his hollow chest, and rattled his iron wrist-chains against the trench wall.

“Lorenz!” Marek bellowed, his voice echoing up the concrete revetment. “Listen to that! That’s the Karst Horde! Batur’s army! I know how these steppe bastards fight! They’ll swarm the ditch! They’ll climb over each other’s corpses like ants!”

I lowered my brass telescope and looked down at him over the iron railing.

“State your operational observation with brevity, Prisoner 001-M,” I said flatly. “You are currently obstructing the transit lane for the mortar ammunition carts.”

“You’re going to be slaughtered, boy!” Marek shouted, a desperate, feral grin stretching across his cracked lips. “You have a bunch of turnip-farmers in grey coats! They don't know how to fight! You need a champion! You need a hero who can hold the breach!”

He stepped forward, raising his chained fists toward the sky.

“Unshackle me! Give me back *Dawn-Sunderer*! Give me a keg of ale and thirty men, and I will lead the sally into their center! I’ll bring you Batur’s head on a spear! I can kill fifty men in the breach before breakfast! You know my strength, Lorenz! Let me fight!”

Behind him, two other penal laborers—former cutpurses—looked at Marek with awe, nodding eagerly.

I looked at Marek for three silent seconds.

“Your proposal has been evaluated,” I said. “And rejected on three distinct administrative grounds.”

Marek blinked, his grin freezing. “What? Are you deaf?! There are six thousand men out there!”

“First,” I stated, tapping my index finger twice against my collar seam. “The weapon you designated as *Dawn-Sunderer* was destroyed forty-eight hours ago under salvage protocol. It was sheared into four billets of scrap iron and melted in Blast Furnace No. 1 to manufacture sixteen standardized mortar carriage bolts. Its metallurgical utility is now vastly superior to its previous incarnation.”

Marek’s mouth fell open in cosmic, horrified disbelief. “You... you melted my sword? You melted *Dawn-Sunderer* into... bolts?!”

“Second,” I continued, completely ignoring his emotional devastation. “Your tactical doctrine—leading a sally into the enemy center—is an act of catastrophic operational idiocy. In an open plain against five-to-one cavalry superiority, a dismounted melee fighter with an oversized blade possesses a survival expectancy of approximately forty-six seconds before being trampled into equine fertilizer.”

“I’m an S-Rank adventurer!” Marek screamed, spitting saliva in pure rage. “I killed a mountain cave-wyrm!”

“Third,” I concluded, my voice dropping into absolute, ice-cold finality, “a champion produces zero collective defensive cohesion. A champion is an unpredictable, narcissistic liability who expends four thousand calories of meat while refusing to carry ammunition. You will not fight in the breach.”

I pointed a gloved finger at a sixty-pound wooden crate sitting on a hand-cart ten feet away. The crate was stenciled in black ink: *PROJECTILES, MORTAR, SULFUR-COMBUSTION, CALIBER 12*.

“You will pick up that crate,” I ordered. “You will carry it up the wooden ramp to Mortar Battery No. 3. You will return. You will repeat this process sixty-four times. If your transit cycle exceeds two minutes and forty seconds per trip, Corporal Brandt will apply twelve inches of seasoned oak to your lower lumbar region.”

Marek trembled violently. His hands shook with murderous, impotent fury. For two seconds, he looked as though he might attempt to lunge up the ramp and tear my throat out with his bare teeth.

*CLICK-CLACK.*

Four Black Cloaks standing along the trench line leveled heavy steel crossbows at his chest. Corporal Brandt stepped up behind him, resting the heavy lead-weighted end of his riot truncheon against the back of Marek's neck.

“Crate in hand, 001-M,” Brandt murmured softly into Marek’s ear. “Or we test whether your skull is harder than seasoned white oak.”

Marek’s breath hitched in his throat. A single, humiliated tear cut through the grime on his cheek.

He dropped his gaze to the gravel. With a low, pathetic groan of defeat, the towering S-Rank hero bent his knees, hoisted the sixty-pound wooden crate of mortar shells onto his broad shoulder, and began shuffling up the ramp, his wooden clogs clicking in the mud:

*Clack. Clack. Clack.*

I did not watch him walk. An administrator does not waste visual focus on compliant cargo handlers.

I turned back to the north.

Across the frozen plain, the horde had begun its advance.

Six thousand men and horses surged forward in a vast, dark crescent, their bronze kettledrums reaching a frantic, deafening crescendo, their battle-cries shaking the frosted pines of the foothills:

*“BATUR! BATUR! BLOOD FOR THE KHAGAN!”*

They came on at a trot, accelerating to a canter, confident that their sheer, overwhelming mass would smash through the wooden gates and turn this grey valley into an ocean of blood and plunder.

Master Founder Torvald leaned over his brass sighting quadrant on the parapet. “Range: fourteen hundred meters. Wind: five knots east. Battery elevation: forty-four degrees.”

“Battery ready, Supreme Commissary,” Torvald said, his hand hovering over an iron lanyard.

I pulled out my brass pocket notebook, noted the time as 0702 hours, and snapped the clasp shut.

“Commence firing sequence,” I said.

The industrial grinder was open.
