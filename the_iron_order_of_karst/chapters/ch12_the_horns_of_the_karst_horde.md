# Chapter 12: The Horns of the Karst Horde & The Allied Host

A barbarian invasion is a problem of kinetic clearance.

The feudal lords of the south write epic poems about the "Great Steppe Host." They depict barbarian warlords as primordial storms, their berserkers as bloodthirsty demigods, and their allied elven spellcasters as immortal masters of the wind and sky. In the mind of a feudal poet, an invading army of eight thousand men is an act of divine wrath that demands prayers, weeping, and desperate heroic sacrifices.

To an administrator of the Directorate, an invading army of eight thousand men is eight hundred metric tons of hostile biological mass advancing into pre-calibrated artillery kill-zones.

At 0600 hours on a crisp June dawn, with the northern mountain defiles cleared of winter avalanches, the alarm sounded across the Karst Basin.

The mechanical, rising and falling howl of electric air-raid sirens mounted atop the concrete watchtowers tore through the morning mist, their piercing acoustic frequency vibrating off the dolomite bluffs.

*WUUUUU-AAAHHH-WUUUUU.*

Within four minutes, the entire civilian population cleared the thoroughfares, moving in silent, orderly files into the subterranean bomb shelters. At the industrial works, the morning shifts continued with clockwork discipline under blackout shutters, stamping rifle ammunition and pouring molten steel.

I stood upon the reinforced concrete command balcony of Bastion 1, my binoculars raised to the northern horizon.

At my side stood Sergeant Major Kester, wearing his steel helmet and holding a field telephone handset connected by heavy copper wire to the battery command pits.

Three miles across the green moorland of the North Basin, the horizon was dark with an ocean of horsemen, war-wagons, and massed foot infantry.

The Steppe Coalition had assembled: eight thousand northern reavers clad in boiled leather and wolf pelts, brandishing curved sabers and recurve bows. At their center strode two dozen highland war-trolls—hulking, nine-foot monstrosities with thick, calcified hides that shrugged off iron arrows like dried grass.

Flanking their vanguard was the elite contingent: three hundred High Elven wind-ward rangers in gleaming emerald mail, led by five High Elven sorcerers draped in shimmering silk robes.

Through the high-magnification lenses of my binoculars, I observed the elven sorcerers raising their carved silver staves.

A translucent, shimmering dome of crystalline arcane energy began to unfurl across the coalition vanguard—a Grand Aeromantic Aegis. The air above the advancing host rippled with pale green light, generating a continuous downward wind vortex designed to deflect arrows, catapult stones, and physical ballistics into the mire.

“They believe their wards make them invincible, Supreme Commander,” Kester murmured, checking his trench chronometer. “The scouts report their warlord has sworn to raze Karst-Hold and offer our severed heads to their sky gods.”

“A predictable rhetorical flourish from an illiterate tribal syndicate,” I replied, lowering the binoculars. “Report the disposition of our combined-arms assets.”

Kester tapped the brass chart board.

“First: The 1st Mechanized Rifle Regiment—two thousand four hundred drilled conscripts drawn from our forty thousand registered basin citizens—occupies the primary concrete trench line across Sector Alpha. Sixty water-cooled MG-08 machine guns are pre-sighted along interlocking grazing-fire corridors.
Second: Ordens-Artillerie Battery Alpha and Battery Beta—twelve 105mm light field howitzers and four 88mm high-velocity dual-purpose guns—are entrenched in the rear limestone redoubts, zeroed onto surveyed fire-grid coordinates.
Third: Panzer-Abteilung 1—six *Sturmwagen Model I* tanks—occupies the hull-down revetments along the central highway.
Fourth: In the subterranean ammo magazines, Penal Unit Omega has completed the distribution of two thousand high-explosive artillery shells.”

Below the command balcony, in the sunken communication trench leading to the forward battery, a line of penal laborers hauled heavy wooden ammunition crates.

At the head of the cart line, sweating profusely in the morning chill, was Prisoner 401-A: Marek the Gilded.

His chestnut curls were completely gone, replaced by a bristling stubble caked with anthracite soot and dry sweat. His gilded plate armor had been replaced by a coarse grey canvas smock and heavy wooden clogs that clattered in the mud. He strained against the harness of a four-wheeled munitions cart, his broad shoulders knotted as he hauled six ninety-pound cases of 105mm high-explosive shells toward the howitzer pits under the watchful eye of a bayonet-wielding guard.

Marek looked up at the northern hills, hearing the distant, terrifying thunder of eight thousand barbarian war-cries and the deep boom of bronze kettledrums.

“They’re coming...” Marek croaked, his voice raw from coal smoke, trembling as he stopped beside the revetment. “The Grand Horde... the mountain trolls... the High Elves... you fools have no idea what they can do! Their magic will tear these walls apart! We have to flee! Let me have my sword!”

The Black Cloak sentry tapped the butt of his *Karst Pattern 1* rifle firmly against Marek’s shoulder blade.

“Keep moving, Prisoner 401-A. Shift your load to Battery Alpha. Four minutes until live-fire commencement.”

Marek grimaced in despair, digging his wooden clogs into the frozen clay, hauling the deadly munitions toward the concrete pits.

Across the valley, the barbarian vanguard reached the two-thousand-meter mark.

The warlord raised his spiked battle-axe, letting out a blood-curdling roar that was echoed by eight thousand screaming warriors. The war-trolls bellowed, beating their stone clubs against their armored chests, while the High Elven wind-ward archers loosed an initial volley of enchanted whistling arrows that curved gracefully through the clouds.

The elven aeromantic dome pulsed with brilliant emerald radiance, shielding their march with an impenetrable wall of compressed kinetic resistance.

I pulled back my left sleeve, tapped my wrist twice against my coat seam, and lifted the field telephone handset.

“Battery Command,” I spoke into the transmitter.

“Battery Command ready, Supreme Commander,” came the crisp reply of Artillery Captain Reiss.

“Target Grid: North Basin Quadrant Four-Zero through Four-Six,” I commanded. “Ammunition: 105mm High-Explosive, Contact Fuze Model 08. Charge: Maximum velocity. Battery... fire for effect.”

I hung up the receiver.

I turned toward the southern runway behind the limestone crag, raising my right hand.

“Signal *Black-Wing Flight Eins*,” I instructed Kester. “Authorize takeoff. Target: Enemy rear command pavilions and high-tier spellcasting circles.”

Kester raised the signal flare pistol and pulled the trigger.

A bright crimson star soared into the grey sky above Karst-Hold, signaling the dawn of mechanized total war.
