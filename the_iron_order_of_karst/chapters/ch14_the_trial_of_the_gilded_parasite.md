# Chapter 14: The Trial of the Gilded Parasite

Public spectacle is an instrument of civic hygiene.

A totalitarian state that operates solely through secret cellars, whispers in the dark, and midnight disappearances creates an atmosphere of pervasive paranoia that stifles economic initiative. Men who fear unannounced arrest do not focus on cutting lathe tolerances or exceeding steel quotas; they focus on survival and concealment.

Learning from the ancient imperial architects of civil stability, the Directorate pairs strict statutory law with the therapeutic release of the amphitheater: *Panem et Circenses*—Food and Entertainment.

On the Saturday following the northern victory, the Arena of the Black Sun was packed to its five-thousand-seat capacity.

The five-tiered stone amphitheater, carved directly into the sheer limestone bluffs of the southern quarry, was bathed in crisp winter sunshine. Large black-and-silver banners bearing the twelve-spoked iron wheel snapped in the mountain breeze.

The atmosphere was electric with communal celebration.

Every citizen entering the gates had received an admission voucher funded through the state recreation tax. In their hands, workers held tin flagons of cold, subsidized 3.5% *Volksbier* and wooden skewers of hot, hickory-smoked pork sausages with spiced cabbage mustard. The rich scents of roasted meat, fermented malt, and fresh pine sawdust filled the arena air.

Brass bugles sounded from the imperial loggia.

Sergeant Major Kester, wearing his dress uniform with polished silver buttons and white gloves, strode to the center of the sand arena. He raised a brass speaking horn.

“Citizens of the Ordensstaat Karst!” Kester’s voice boomed across the amphitheater. “Today we conduct the civic audit of the old world! Bring forth the prisoner!”

The southern iron gate ratcheted open with a heavy mechanical clanking of chains.

Four armed Black Cloak sentries marched into the arena, escorting Prisoner 401-A: Marek of High-Crest.

Marek shuffled across the packed yellow sand in heavy case-hardened leg-irons. His canvas smock was stained with dried coal slurry from his shifts in the ammo trenches; his shaved head was exposed to the biting cold; his shoulders were slumped. He looked up at the five thousand cheering foundry workers, coal miners, and weavers with wide, disoriented eyes.

A massive roar of derisive laughter swept through the stands.

Workers waved their sausage skewers and banged their tin beer flagons against the stone railings. For generations, men like Marek—arrogant wandering champions with gilded breastplates—had treated these common laborers like dirt beneath their riding boots, demanding their daughters, their food, and their deference. Now, the champion stood shackled in the dust before them like a stray mongrel.

I stood at the stone parapet of the central podium, adjusting the acoustic horn.

“Citizens of the Directorate,” I spoke into the horn, my voice carrying cleanly across the quieted amphitheater. “We assemble today to process the balance sheet of an obsolete social class.”

I unrolled the parchment indictment.

“Prisoner 401-A, formerly styling himself Marek the Gilded. You are indicted on three statutory counts:
First: Criminal Sentry Dereliction under Article 04, abandoning strategic border reconnaissance on Raven’s Gap to engage in non-productive biological indulgence.
Second: Armed Sedition and Border Violation under Article 01, brandishing an uncertified weapon against the sentries of Checkpoint 4.
Third: Parasitic Social Vagrancy, consuming twelve hundred metric pounds of surplus frontier provisions over three years without producing a single ounce of grain, iron, or manufactured utility.”

Marek rattled his chains, looking up at my podium with a desperate, wild fury.

“Lorenz!” Marek yelled, his voice cracking against the limestone tiers. “You sit up there in your fine coat, hiding behind your scribes! You know who I am! I am the Gilded Cleaver! I possess the blood of the high kings! My muscle can lift a bullock! If you want to judge me, give me my broadsword and let your finest champion face me on this sand!”

The crowd jeered loudly, throwing crusts of rye bread at his boots.

I held up my hand. The amphitheater fell silent.

“The prisoner appeals to individual biological strength,” I observed calmly. “He believes that because his muscular tissue can generate seven-hundred foot-pounds of torque, he is entitled to rule over productive men who manufacture the future.”

I turned toward the eastern service gate.

“The Directorate respects physical work. We measure work in horsepower. Therefore, we shall test the prisoner’s claim against state engineering.”

I raised my right hand.

“Deploy the challenger.”

The eastern gate rolled open.

A chorus of whistles and cheers erupted from the crowd as six foundrymen hauled a heavy iron machine into the arena on wide steel road wheels: a coal-fired portable steam winch, manufactured in Plant No. 2.

The machine was an assembly of riveted black boiler plates, brass pressure gauges, and a revolving cast-iron cable drum connected to a twin-cylinder steam piston. Thick white steam hissed rhythmically from its relief valves, smelling of hot mineral oil and wet anthracite coal.

Chief Engineer Halder walked beside the machine, holding an ungreased steel tow-cable fitted with a leather harness.

Halder approached Marek, fastening the heavy leather harness around the prisoner’s broad chest, securing the steel cable to the winch drum thirty paces away.

“The conditions of the trial are simple,” I announced to the crowd. “Prisoner Marek claims his heroic physique surpasses the power of modern industry. If the prisoner can hold his ground against sixty atmospheres of state steam pressure for ten seconds, his sentence will be commuted to light agricultural duty. If he fails, he will submit unconditionally to industrial labor reallocation.”

Marek looked at the machine with fierce, wild defiance. He planted his bare feet deep into the packed sand, locked his knees, gripped the steel cable with both calloused hands, and channeled his remaining physical energy into his torso. His back muscles swelled like iron bands.

“Bring on your tea-kettle, clerk!” Marek roared, his teeth bared in a feral grin. “I am Marek of High-Crest! No machine of iron can break—!”

Halder flipped the steam regulator lever.

*PSSSSSH-CLANK.*

The steam valve opened. The twin pistons fired in rapid, alternating strokes.

The iron winch drum engaged with a horrific, metallic shriek.

There was no struggle. There was no heroic contest.

Forty horsepower of mechanical torque hit the steel cable like a runaway locomotive.

Marek’s feet left the sand instantly. The kinetic tension ripped him forward with violent, irresistible acceleration. His face smashed flat into the sand, his body dragged twenty yards across the arena floor in a cloud of dust and flying gravel before Halder touched the brake.

Marek lay sprawled in the dirt, gasping for air, spitting sand, his arrogance pulverized into utter, pathetic absurdity.

The five thousand workers in the stands erupted into thunderous, ecstatic laughter. They stomped their boots, raised their foaming flagons of *Volksbier*, and chanted the name of the Directorate in joyous unison. The myth of the invincible warrior was shattered forever in the minds of the people.

I raised the acoustic horn.

“The diagnostic test is concluded,” I declared. “The machine generated twenty-nine thousand foot-pounds of work in one point eight seconds. The biological asset produced zero effective resistance.”

I signed the judicial register.

“Sentence: Prisoner 401-A is classified as a prime draught-engine. He is remanded to the deep anthracite coking gasifiers of Sector Gamma. He will haul coking coal to fuel the blast furnaces for the next battalion of *Sturmwagen* tanks. Rations are fixed at three thousand five hundred calories, conditioned upon meeting his four-ton daily extraction quota. Remove him.”

Two sentries hauled Marek to his feet.

The former champion of Osterlund did not shout. He did not boast. He hung his head in absolute, broken defeat, allowing himself to be led out through the service tunnel while five thousand well-fed citizens cheered the triumph of the machine.
