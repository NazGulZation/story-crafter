# Chapter 14: The Trial of the Gilded Parasite

The greatest insult you can inflict upon an egomaniac is not execution.

Execution grants him a martyr's climax. It allows him to deliver a dramatic final soliloquy, to spit blood toward the sky, and to imagine that future bards will compose weeping ballads about his defiant end while lute-strings tremble in firelit taverns.

To truly annihilate a narcissist, you must convert his downfall into a public Roman holiday.

At 1400 hours, the **Arena of the Black Sun**—the five-thousand-seat limestone amphitheater carved into the southern quarry bluffs—was packed to maximum statutory capacity. The air smelled of woodsmoke, roasting pork fat, hot mustard, and the rich, yeasty foam of freshly tapped *Volksbier*.

Uniformed vendors from the Municipal Commissary moved along the stone tiers, carrying wicker baskets of hot salt pretzels and brass kettles of smoked pork sausages, exchanging them for single slips of *Arbeitschein*. Families sat on clean wool blankets; ditch-diggers clinked heavy stoneware mugs together in cheerful toasts; children waved miniature black iron wheels on sticks as the twelve-piece Frontier Guard Brass Band played an uptempo regimental march.

The people were warm, well-fed, and thoroughly amused.

In the center of the sand-strewn arena floor, upon an elevated timber platform beneath the iron standard, stood the prisoner's dock.

Standing inside the dock, bound in forty pounds of forged iron chain, was Labor Asset 001-M: Marek of High-Crest.

His face was streaked with soot and dried sweat from hauling mortar canisters. His oversized grey convict smock hung loosely over his massive shoulders, splattered with mud; his wooden clogs were caked in arena sand.

Yet, seeing five thousand people gathered around the tiers, smelling the food and hearing the brass trumpets, the terminal pathology of the fantasy adventurer surged back into his blood.

Marek lifted his chin. He pulled his shoulders back, straining against the chains to make his chest muscles bulge against the coarse hemp of his smock. He shook his shaved scalp, attempting to find the rhythm of his old, seductive tavern swagger.

*A stadium!*

In his fevered, narcissistic imagination, this was his grand gladiatorial comeback! The tyrannical clerk was holding a tournament to test him, and the crowd had gathered to witness their gilded champion earn his freedom!

He took a heavy step forward against the timber rail, his chains clanking loudly.

“Citizens of Karst!” Marek bellowed, projecting his booming, heroic baritone across the roaring amphitheater. “Look at me! I am Marek of High-Crest! S-Rank champion of the Grand Guild! I have slain highland wyrms, broken warlords, and defended the helpless! Look at what this tyrant has done to me! He has put your champion in chains! But I stand unbroken! Hand me a greatsword, and I will fight your greatest beast! I will bleed for your entertainment and lead you to freedom!”

He paused, throwing his chest out, a triumphant, wild grin spreading across his face, waiting for the roar of the crowd.

The response was immediate.

A foundry smith in the third tier, clutching a two-liter tankard of foamy beer, took a bite of a hot pork sausage, wiped mustard from his chin, and shouted:

“Sit down, you loud turnip! We paid two scrip for the Cohort Tug-of-War, not to hear a beggar yell about his sword!”

A wave of thunderous, mocking laughter cascaded down the stone tiers.

A pretzel crust bounced off Marek’s shaved scalp. A washerwoman in the front row chuckled, pointing at his baggy hemp trousers: “Look at his arms! All that meat, and he couldn't even keep the sentry fire lit on the mountain!”

Marek’s grin completely disintegrated.

His eyes darted in frantic, humiliated disbelief across the five thousand laughing faces. Nobody was weeping for his plight. Nobody looked oppressed. They were eating sausages, drinking cold beer, and laughing at him as if he were a drunken circus bear who had tripped over his own paws.

I walked onto the central podium, holding a black leather folder clasped with steel. I wore my tailored charcoal high-collar tunic, my posture unbending.

The twelve-piece brass band ceased playing with a crisp, synchronized rimshot.

Director Weidmann stepped forward to the iron speaking funnel, his spectacles reflecting the pale winter sun.

“Court of Civic Accountability is in session,” Weidmann’s voice boomed through the amphitheater, amplified by the acoustic curve of the limestone bluffs. “Presiding: Supreme Commissary Viktor Vogel. Case File: 001-Alpha-Treason.”

He looked like a loud, unemployed vagrant with an oversized mouth who had arrived without a transit pass, had failed to maintain his weapons, and was currently wasting fourteen minutes of productive municipal labor time with incoherent shouting.

Marek’s grin slowly disintegrated. His eyes darted across the sea of three thousand expressionless, grey-clad faces.

“What... what’s wrong with you?” Marek stammered, his voice dropping an octave, losing its booming resonance. “Didn't you hear me? I’m Marek the Gilded! I’m an S-Rank adventurer!”

I stepped up to the iron podium, opened the black leather folder, and smoothed the top parchment with a gloved palm.

“The accused has concluded his opening statement,” I said into the speaking funnel. “We will now proceed with the formal indictment.”

I looked at Marek. My gaze was completely devoid of personal animosity. It was the gaze of an agricultural inspector reading the bacteria count on an unpasteurized cheese wheel.

“Charge One: Aggravated Dereliction of Sentry Protocol in an Active Operational Zone. Specifically: On the night of Month One, Day 14, at Raven’s Gap, the accused abandoned his designated perimeter guard post to engage in eight consecutive hours of non-productive recreational copulation, while maintaining an unauthorized 300-yard light and acoustic signature in high-threat territory.”

Marek blinked, his jaw dropping. “What?! That... that was three months ago! In a private tent!”

“Private spaces do not exist within a tactical theater,” I replied coldly. “Charge Two: Unauthorized Possession and Transit of an Unlicensed Heavy Kinetic Cleaving Implement exceeding statutory length regulations by thirty-four inches. Addendum: Gross Destruction of Capital Assets through the improper use of said tempered carbon-steel blade as an improvised firewood chopper.”

“That was *Dawn-Sunderer*!” Marek screamed hysterically, rattling his chains. “It was an ancestral blade blessed by the High Patriarch!”

“Its Rockwell hardness was fifty-two,” I corrected him. “Barely sufficient for cutting frozen butter, let alone high-tensile armor. Charge Three: Parasitic Vagrancy and Caloric Embezzlement. Over a period of forty-eight months, the accused consumed approximately four thousand two hundred calories daily while generating zero agricultural yield, zero infrastructural maintenance, and zero recorded tax contributions to any recognized provincial treasury.”

I turned the page with a sharp, dry snap.

“Charge Four: Public Incitement to Mutiny during Active Martial Mobilization. Specifically: Attempting to organize an unauthorized, unregimented civilian charge into the enemy center during the tactical engagement of this morning, thereby risking the loss of thirty combat personnel and four hundred metric tons of state ammunition.”

I looked out across the assembly.

“The evidence is conclusive. The balance sheet is mathematically insolvent. Prisoner Marek, how does the defense plead?”

Marek was trembling now, but it was the trembling of a cornered, humiliated animal whose entire reality had been stripped of its gilded veneer.

“You... you scrawny little worm!” Marek snarled, tears of manic rage leaking from his bruised eyes. “You’re just jealous! That’s what this is! You’re getting revenge because I took your women! Because Clara crawled into my bed! Because Isolde and Brigit begged for my cock while you shivered in the dirt like a dog! You’re doing this because you’re a cuckold!”

A collective, quiet sigh ran through the front ranks of the Frontier Guard.

Several veterans shook their heads in second-hand embarrassment.

I did not raise my voice. I did not flush with anger. I simply made a small, neat pencil mark beside Charge Four.

“The accused’s psychological projection regarding reproductive competition is noted,” I stated into the funnel. “However, biological coupling between non-productive civilian units is an administrative irrelevance. The State does not adjudicate romantic grievances. The State audits resource allocation.”

I closed the black folder and looked Marek directly in the eyes.

“You believe you are being punished because of sentimentality,” I said, my voice cutting through the freezing air like an iron plumb-line. “You are mistaken. You are being processed because you committed **conscious, premeditated betrayal and armed sedition**. You entered a covenant of mutual survival with Lorenz Falk, extracted his resources, took over his party, and deliberately ordered him into a fatal blizzard to die of exposure. Then, when confronted by lawful authority at the gates, you drew steel and incited mutiny. Deliberate treason against collective survival is an unpardonable cancer.”

I raised my right hand.

“Sentence: The accused is permanently stripped of his civilian identity, his family patronymic, and his guild honors. He is officially reclassified as *Perpetual Labor Asset #001-Omega*.”

Marek’s breath hitched. “What... what does that mean? What are you going to do to me?!”

“Execution is rejected,” I announced. “A male of your musculoskeletal volume represents approximately five thousand foot-pounds of daily kinetic pulling power. To destroy that energy in the gallows would be an unpardonable fiscal waste. Nor will you be starved or tortured; cruelty is the resort of an incompetent feudal warlord. A starved engine cannot pull ore.”

I pointed a gloved finger toward the dark, jagged opening of the lower limestone bluffs behind the blast furnaces.

“You are hereby assigned to the **Deep Anthracite Seam, Level 4**. You will be issued a molded leather breast-harness, thick padded knee-guards, and a four-hundred-pound low-clearance coal cart. You will haul coal through the ventilated three-foot seams. Because your labor requires immense kinetic output, your daily sustenance will be three thousand five hundred calories of dense rye bread, salted lard, and boiled bean broth. If you meet your four-ton daily extraction quota, you will receive tobacco and butter incentives. You will be treated with absolute, clinical fairness as a prime draught-engine.”

My voice hardened into cold, unyielding iron.

“*However.* If you slacken your traces, if you attempt violence against an overseer, or if you conspire against the mine’s operations—the stick will fall without a second chance, and your privileges will be permanently revoked. Your life of vanity, boasting, and parasitism is finished. You will earn your bread by the sweat of your back.”

The silence in the yard was deafening.

Marek stared at the dark, smoking hole in the side of the mountain.

He realized with crushing, inescapable finality that he was not being martyred. He was not being granted a tragic, glorious doom. He was being rationally, productively harnessed as a draft-ox, well-fed and well-equipped, completely stripped of his gilded illusions.

“No...” Marek whimpered, his knees buckling, his voice collapsing into a high, thin wail of pure, unadulterated madness. “No! Not the carts! Please! Lorenz! Have mercy! Just fight me! Take a sword and duel me! Let me die like a knight! Don't make me an ox!”

He fell to his knees in the dock, sobbing hysterically, his forehead slamming against the iron bars, blubbering and drooling into the limestone slush.

“A request for chivalric combat,” I said into the funnel, “is denied as a frivolous waste of ammunition. The State does not duel with draught-animals.”

I looked at Corporal Brandt.

“Corporal. Affix the harness. Deliver the asset to Level 4. Shift begins at 1500 hours sharp.”

“Understood, Supreme Commissary!”

Four Black Cloaks stepped into the dock, seized Marek by his iron chains, and hauled him bodily from the timber platform.

He screamed. He wept. He kicked his wooden clogs against the flagstones, begging for the Dawn-Light, begging for his mother, begging for Lorenz to look at him just once with hatred instead of this terrifying, sterile boredom.

Nobody looked at him.

The three thousand workers simply watched in silence as the former S-Rank hero was dragged across the gravel, his desperate shrieks swallowed by the roar of the blast furnaces and the concussive, unyielding thud of the steam triphammers.

Director Weidmann stepped forward, took the black folder from my hands, and blotted the final signature.

“Case closed, Supreme Commissary,” Weidmann murmured obsequiously. “Next item on the docket: The civilian asset allocations for Quarantine Block C.”

I glanced toward the north, where the snow clouds were beginning to unleash the first thick, white flakes of the winter blizzard.

“Bring them in,” I said. “Let us conclude the inventory.”
