# Chapter 11: The Processing Cells & Technical Audit

A human being stripped of vanity is an engineering resource.

In feudal society, a man is judged by the length of his lineage, the luster of his armor, or the theatrical piety of his prayers. Under the Directorate, every biological unit is an equation of caloric input versus mechanical throughput. A hero’s golden biceps produce three hundred watts of sustained physical power; a priestess’s healing touch is a biological catalyst for cellular regeneration; an elementalist’s fire is a thermal source for chemical cracking.

Quarantine Block C operated with clinical, sanitary severity.

The four members of the Silver Dawn were marched through the reinforced steel intake doors into the concrete wash-room. The air inside reeked intensely of carbolic acid, pungent lye soap, and boiling well-water.

Armed orderlies in rubber aprons and oilskin gloves separated them immediately. Marek was shoved into the masculine intake stall, while Clara, Isolde, and Brigit were herded into the female delousing bay.

The procedure followed State Sanitation Protocol 02:
First: Total confiscation of all textiles, jewelry, and personal items. Clara’s torn velvet gown, Isolde’s embroidered silk mantles, and Marek’s rusted plate armor were bagged in coarse burlap, tagged with intake numbers, and consigned to the autoclave sterilizers for recycling into industrial fiber and scrap iron.
Second: High-pressure cold water hoses, followed by scrubbing with sulfur-lye soap. Clara shrieked in horror as the freezing jets blasted away three months of dungeon dirt, lice, and perfumed grease.
Third: Shaving. Heavy iron shears clipped through Clara’s blonde curls and Isolde’s raven locks, dropping them to the wet concrete floor to eliminate typhus vectors. Brigit submitted to the shears with grim, silent stoicism.
Fourth: Uniform issuance. Each was dressed in a coarse, unbleached grey wool smock, matching trousers, and wooden clogs.

At 1830 hours, they were marched into Interrogation Room 1.

The chamber was constructed of reinforced concrete, illuminated by a single electric filament bulb hanging from an insulated iron conduit. The walls were bare except for a framed print of the twelve-spoked Black Sun. A cast-iron steam radiator hissed rhythmically in the corner, radiating dry, metallic warmth.

Behind the central steel desk sat Supreme Commander Viktor Vogel.

I wore a tailored charcoal wool high-collar tunic with polished silver collar tabs. My hair was cropped close to the scalp, my posture straight, my eyes fixed upon four open manila folders. At my right hand stood Director Weidmann, holding a stopwatch and a calibrated slide rule.

The guards shoved the four prisoners onto the bolted steel bench facing the desk.

Clara looked up. Her breath hitched. Her red, swollen eyes widened in absolute, paralyzing recognition.

“Lorenz...?” Clara whispered.

She leaned forward, her voice breaking into a hysterical, sobbing wail.

“Lorenz! Oh, gods of light, Lorenz! You’re alive! You survived the pass!” She lunged toward the desk, her shackled hands reaching for my sleeve, only to be snapped back by the guard’s firm grip on her collar chain. “Lorenz, tell these monsters who we are! Look at what they did to my hair! Look at these rags! Lorenz, please... it’s Clara! Your Clara!”

I did not look up from the folder. I turned the page with a dry rasp of paper, picked up my steel nib, and made a single notation.

“Identify yourself by intake serial number,” I said. My voice was level, monotone, and devoid of emotional inflection.

Clara blinked in shock, tears streaming down her hollow cheeks. “Lorenz... what are you saying? It’s me! We grew up together in the village! We played by the miller’s pond! I know I made mistakes... I know I went with Marek, but I was frightened! I was confused! My heart has always belonged to you, Lorenz!”

I closed the folder with a sharp, flat snap.

I raised my eyes and looked at her face for the first time.

“The individual known as Lorenz Falk is legally deceased under State Certificate of Death 01-A,” I stated. “His biological remains were dissolved into administrative memory eighty-nine days ago. You are addressing Supreme Commander Viktor Vogel of the Karst Security Directorate. Personal sentimentality has zero legal standing in this room.”

Marek, sitting beside her with his shaved head bowed, jerked upright, rattling his heavy wrist-irons against the bench. His eyes burned with bruised, desperate fury.

“You scrawny little worm!” Marek snarled, his voice booming against the concrete walls. “You stole my party! You stole my fortress! You’re hiding behind these machines and guards like a cowardly rat! Take these chains off me! Meet me in an honorable duel, sword to sword, and let the gods decide who rules Karst!”

I glanced at Director Weidmann.

“Weidmann. Record the following psychological evaluation: Subject Marek demonstrates acute delusions of heroic entitlement, combined with severe cognitive resistance to mathematical reality.”

I turned back to Marek.

“An honorable duel is a primitive waste of biological capital,” I said calmly. “You speak of swords. While you were wandering in flooded tombs, this Directorate constructed five blast furnaces, an automated ammunition plant, and forty tons of mechanized armor. A duel resolves nothing except the mortality of an individual. The state operates on the scale of battalions.”

I opened the second folder: Isolde’s dossier.

“Prisoner 402-B. Isolde of the Amber Spire.”

Isolde raised her chin, her lips trembling with defiant academic arrogance. “I am a licensed element-weaver of the Royal Academy! You cannot enslave an archmage! My fire can incinerate your precious factories to cinders!”

“Your fire is a chemical reaction,” I corrected her. “We conducted an energetic analysis of your spellcraft using the intake telemetry from the southern proving grounds. Your maximum thermal output is forty-two thousand British Thermal Units per combustion cycle, with a recharge interval of six minutes.”

Isolde stared at me, her eyes wide with bewilderment. “What... what are British Thermal Units?!”

“A unit of thermodynamic work,” I replied. “Forty-two thousand BTUs is equivalent to twelve pounds of high-grade anthracite coal. You are not a goddess of flame; you are an energetic catalyst with an erratic biological ignition system. We have completed the construction of Plant No. 4—the synthetic fuel cracking facility. We require a sustained thermal source to maintain the distillation retorts at four hundred degrees Celsius to crack coal tar into high-octane aviation fuel.”

I made a notation in her file.

“Reassignment: Thermal Catalyst Specialist, Sector Epsilon Synthetic Fuel Refinery. You will be provided with asbestos overalls, five-minute work rotations, and three thousand calories of high-protein rations. Your compliance will ensure your survival. Sabotage will result in immediate transfer to the gravel-crushing mills.”

Isolde collapsed back against the bench, her face pale, the fire in her spirit extinguished by cold engineering metrics.

I opened the third folder: Brigit.

Brigit sat motionless, her grey eyes meeting mine with direct, unblinking pragmatism.

“Prisoner 402-C. Brigit the Wood-Stalker.”

“I have no magic,” Brigit said quietly. “I track. I shoot. I hunt.”

“Incorrect,” I replied. “You possess exceptional stereoscopic visual acuity and intuitive distance-estimation capabilities. During your intake ballistics test, your baseline optical convergence was measured at eighty-five percent accuracy across eight hundred meters.”

I tapped my pencil against her file.

“The Directorate is mass-producing the *Sturmwagen Model I* tank and the *Black-Wing* ground-attack monoplane. Both systems require precision optical instruments: periscope prisms, telescopic gun-sights, and diving-bomb reticles. The newly established Optical & Ballistics Bureau in Sector Gamma requires technicians capable of grinding quartz lenses and calibrating mathematical aiming reticles.”

Brigit’s eyebrows lifted slightly. “You want me to make glass sights for your iron carriages?”

“I require your eyes and your spatial precision,” I said. “Work diligently, adhere to production quotas, and you will receive double rations, heated quarters in Barracks Block 3, and thirty *Arbeitschein* weekly. Do you accept the contract?”

Brigit did not hesitate. She looked at Marek, then at Clara, and finally at the clean, well-lit room.

“I accept,” Brigit said firmly.

“Good,” I noted. “Weidmann, process her transfer.”

Clara gasped, looking at Brigit in horror. “Brigit! How can you join them?! He’s a tyrant! He’s Lorenz!”

“He feeds his workers, Clara,” Brigit answered flatly. “And unlike Marek, his promises don’t starve us in the mud.”

I turned to the final folder: Clara.

Clara began to weep again, bowing her head, trembling. “Lorenz... please... what of me? I can heal! I have the sacred light of the Dawn! I can bless you... I can heal your wounds as I used to...”

“Your magic is accelerated cellular mitosis,” I stated. “It consumes personal glucose to trigger rapid biological tissue repair. In a fantasy party, you used it to mend minor scratches for an arrogant swordsman. In this state, your ability has real industrial utility.”

I signed the order sheet.

“Reassignment: Orderly Trainee, Munitions Factory Trauma & Chemical Burn Ward. Plant No. 2 experiences occasional acid splashes from the nitration vats and crush injuries from the drop-forges. You will wear rubber gloves, disinfect wounds with carbolic acid, and apply your cellular acceleration to stabilize injured lathe machinists. You will save productive citizens. You will earn your bread through sweat.”

“I... I am a priestess!” Clara cried. “You want me to wash acid burns for peasant laborers?!”

“A peasant laborer produces fifty rifle bolts a day,” I said coldly. “Your status as a priestess produces nothing. Your value begins tomorrow morning at 0600 hours.”

I looked down at Marek.

“And the gilded vanguard,” I said.

Marek glared up, his teeth grinding. “What task do you have for me, clerk? Cleaning latrines? You want to humiliate me!”

“Humiliation is an irrational emotional pursuit,” I replied. “You possess two hundred and ten pounds of dense musculoskeletal mass. Your biological horsepower is measured at zero point seven. In the deep anthracite seam, the high-pressure gasifiers powering the tank armor blast furnaces require thirty tons of coking coal every eight hours. You will haul coal carts.”

“I am an S-Rank adventurer!” Marek bellowed.

“You are a draught-engine,” I answered. “Meet your four-ton quota, and you will receive three thousand five hundred calories of bread, lard, and salt beef. Fail your quota, and your rations will adjust accordingly. Take them away.”

The guards stepped forward, hauling Marek and the weeping Clara to their feet, marching them out into the concrete corridor.

I picked up the canvas bag containing the Wyrm-Heart crystal.

I unsealed the wire, resting the glowing amber stone beneath the electric lamp.

“Weidmann,” I said.

“Sir!”

“Deliver this mineral core to Chief Engineer Halder at Plant No. 3 immediately. Tell him to crush it into three-micron powder and introduce it into the aviation fuel catalytic retorts. *Black-Wing Flight Eins* conducts its maiden live-fire flight test at dawn.”
