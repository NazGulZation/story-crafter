# Chapter 2C: The Quiet Alcove

The shadows pooled deep in the eastern hollow of the elder pine, where the massive buttress roots twisted together to form a secluded alcove far from the crackling hearth. I stepped across the flagstones, carrying the thick, dry wool blanket in both arms. The cedar wood beneath my bare feet was cool here, swept by a faint, damp draft that curled in through the outer boughs. In the gloom, a small silhouette sat curled tightly into a ball, knees drawn up to her chin, her green scout tunic still drenched and dark with rainwater.

"Aoi?" I called softly, keeping my voice low so as not to startle her.

Her pointed elven ears twitched sharply, standing erect before flattening against her short sage-green hair. She flinched, pulling her arms in even tighter, her knuckles white where she gripped the carved wooden form of Companion-kun. In the dim reflection of the distant fire, her emerald eyes were wide, glassy with unshed tears, and her lower lip quivered with an uncontrollable, chattering tremor.

"Y-Yuuki-san...!" she squeaked, her voice trembling like a leaf in an autumn gale. She hastily tried to shove Companion-kun behind her back, only to realize there was nowhere to hide the little doll against the solid cedar wall. A bright, fiery blush washed over her pale cheeks, spreading all the way to the tips of her ears. "I-I'm sorry! Did I do something wrong? Is my wet gear dripping too loud? I-I tried to stay out of the way... so Misato-san and Hatsune-san could have the warm spots near the fire..."

"You didn't do anything wrong, Aoi," I said gently, kneeling beside her on the dry timber floor. The cold air coming off her clothes was palpable, like standing near a melting snowbank. Her lips had taken on a faint bluish tint, and her small shoulders were shaking so hard that her quiver rattled against the wood. "You can't sit back here in sodden wool. Misato warned us that hypothermia will set in if you don't get out of those wet clothes."

"B-But... my tunic is soaked through..." she stammered, looking down at her lap with sheer mortification. The heavy scout wool was plastered to her slender torso, revealing the delicate outline of her collarbones and the small, firm curve of her chest beneath. "I... I only have a thin camisole underneath. I can't... I couldn't possibly take my clothes off in front of you, Yuuki-san! A girl shouldn't... it's not proper, and I'm so clumsy and plain, and—"

"Aoi," I interrupted softly, placing a warm hand gently over her frozen fingers.

The contact made her gasp, a sharp intake of breath that hitching in her chest. Her skin felt like smooth river stones pulled from an icy creek. She stared down at where my hand covered hers, her eyes blinking in stunned bewilderment. The violent shivering in her knuckles seemed to pause for a heartbeat, overwhelmed by the sudden shock of warmth.

"Your health matters more than propriety," I said firmly, but with an open, reassuring smile. "I'm not going to gawk at you. But I am going to make sure my guild's archer doesn't freeze to death in a mountain tree."

I unfolded the thick woolen blanket, holding it up like a protective curtain between us.

"Turn around and slide the tunic off," I instructed quietly. "I'll hold the blanket. The moment it's off, I'll wrap you up."

Aoi swallowed hard, her throat bobbing. For several long seconds, the only sound was the drumming of the thunderstorm outside and the rapid, frightened patter of her breath. Then, with a tiny, submissive nod, she turned her back to me.

Her trembling fingers fumbled at the horn buttons of her drenched scout tunic. The sodden wool peeled away from her skin with a wet, heavy rustle, dropping onto the cedar floorboards with a dull thud.

Beneath it, she wore only a simple, sleeveless white cotton under-camisole. The thin fabric was damp at the shoulders from rainwater that had seeped through the seams, leaving her slender, finely toned archer's arms and pale shoulder blades completely bare. Goosebumps covered every inch of her porcelain skin, making the delicate ridges of her spine stand out sharply in the dim light. Her waist was narrow, athletic, and carved with the subtle musculature of an archer who drew a heavy yew bow every day of her life.

Without hesitation, I brought the heavy woolen blanket around her, draping it over her shoulders and enveloping her small frame in dry, thick fleece.

Aoi let out a long, shuddering sob of pure physical relief. The tension in her spine broke all at once, and she collapsed backward against my chest, her head resting beneath my collarbone as I pulled the folds of the blanket tightly across her front.

"Ahhh... warm..." she whimpered, her hands clutching at the edges of the wool where my arms held them closed. "It's... so warm, Yuuki-san... I thought my bones were going to snap like frozen twigs."

"I told you," I murmured, resting my chin lightly against the crown of her sage-green hair. The scent of rain-washed pine and the sweet, herbal fragrance of her skin rose from the blanket, clean and intoxicating. I wrapped both arms securely around her waist, pulling her flush against my chest and torso to transfer as much of my body heat as possible.

Her slender back pressed directly against my bare chest. Through the thin cotton of her camisole and my woolen wraps, the warmth began to pass between us in steady, rhythmic waves. Aoi's trembling gradually slowed from violent shivers into soft, sporadic twitches. Her small feet, pale and cold, tucked themselves against my shins under the blanket, seeking the heat of my legs.

"Yuuki-san..." she whispered after a long silence, her voice muffled against the fleece. Her fingers crept over the back of my hands, shyly tracing the knuckles that held the blanket closed over her lap.

"Yes, Aoi?"

"Before I joined Forestier... I lived alone in the northern groves for three whole years," she confessed, her words coming in small, hesitant bursts, as if she were uncovering a secret she had buried deep beneath the roots. "Whenever a storm like this came... I would crawl into a hollow log all by myself. I had Companion-kun tucked under my chin, and I would talk to him until my throat was raw, pretending he was answering me. I told myself that I liked being alone. That people were loud, and scary, and that I didn't need anyone."

She paused, taking a shaky, deep breath that expanded her ribs against my forearms.

"But it was a lie," she whispered, her fingers tightening around mine with surprising strength. "Every time the thunder shook the trees, I was terrified. I was so cold... and so lonely that it felt like my chest was hollowed out by rot. When Misato-san invited me into the guild, I couldn't believe it. And when I met you... you listened to me. Even when I stuttered, even when I dropped my arrows, you never laughed. You looked at me like I was a real person."

I tightened my embrace, pressing a gentle, reassuring kiss to the side of her head, right where her soft green hair parted above her pointed ear.

Aoi gasped, a full-body shiver rippling through her that had nothing to do with the mountain storm. Her elven ear flushed a deep, feverish scarlet, twitching wildly against my cheek. She turned her head slightly within the blanket, looking up at me over her shoulder. Her large emerald eyes were shining with tears that caught the distant amber gleam of the hearth fire, reflecting a raw, unvarnished adoration that laid her soul completely bare.

"Yuuki-san..." she breathed, her lips parting as her chest rose and fell in rapid, breathless heaves. Her bare shoulders brushed against my collarbones, smooth as polished ivory and radiating a sweet, blossoming heat that seemed to seep straight into my chest. "When you hold me like this... my heart beats so loud I'm afraid it will wake Misato-san. I don't feel cold anymore. I feel... hot all over. My skin feels like it's tingling... everywhere you touch me."

She raised one small, trembling hand from beneath the blanket and pressed it against my cheek. Her palm was warm now, soft and calloused only at the fingertips from years of plucking bowstrings. Her touch was hesitant, reverent, as if she were touching something sacred that she feared might break under her fingers.

The rain hammered against the massive trunk of the elder pine, a distant, muffled roar that seemed to belong to another world entirely. Inside our quiet hollow among the roots, the air was warm, dense with the scent of cedar heartwood, drying wool, and the sweet, rising heat of our intertwined bodies. Aoi didn't turn away; she leaned back into my chest, her green eyes searching mine with a breathless, desperate hunger that shattered the last remnants of her timid shell.

The cedar roots curved around us like a fortress of living wood, sheltering our warmth against the howling dark.
