# Chapter 1: The Deluge in the Hollow

Cold water poured through the upper boughs of the canopy in roaring gray curtains, battering my shoulders and plastering my soaked tunic flat against my ribs. The mountain thunderstorm had broken over the high ridges with terrifying speed, turning the pine-needle trail into a slick torrent of clay and tumbling gravel. I held my footing against the slope, bracing my boots into the exposed root of an elder pine while reaching back to clasp Misato's trembling hand. Behind her, Hatsune clung to the hem of my traveling cloak with both fists, her teeth clicking together so violently that each breath came as a sharp, ragged shudder. At the rear, Aoi stumbled forward through the stinging sheets of rain, her bow cradled desperately against her chest wrapped in oilcloth, her small frame curled over herself to protect the wooden doll tucked inside her sodden scout pouch.

"Here! Keep your head down, Yuuki-san!" Misato shouted above the drum of thunder, her voice steady despite the gray pallor of her lips. Her long auburn hair hung in heavy, waterlogged strands that lashed her cheeks in the gale. "The eastern roots open into an ancient ranger hollow! Two more steps, quickly!"

She guided us between two colossal, moss-crusted buttress roots that arched outward from the base of the mountain's oldest elder pine like the ribs of a grounded galleon. A thick curtain of twisted roots and hanging mountain ivy blocked the wind. I shoved the dripping brambles aside, holding the gap open with my shoulder as Misato ushered Hatsune inside. Aoi slipped past my chest with a breathless squeak of gratitude, her green eyes wide and dilated from the cold, before I tumbled in behind them and let the root curtain snap shut.

The deafening roar of the deluge instantly muffled into a deep, rhythmic rumble, like waves crashing against the hull of a ship. Inside, the air was still, heavy with the sharp, resinous perfume of seasoned cedar heartwood, dry stone dust, and dried sweetgrass. The root chamber was vast—an ancient emergency refuge carved into the living wood by elven boundary rangers centuries ago. Above us, the smooth curved ceiling of timber rose into shadowy arches, completely bone-dry. In the center of the flagstone floor sat a recessed fire pit filled with pale gray ash and stacked splits of dry, resin-heavy pine heartwood left by past travelers.

"Is everyone unharmed?" Misato breathed, sinking onto her knees beside the stone pit. Her ivory healer's vestments were soaked through, clinging sheer and heavy to her rounded shoulders and the full, heavy swell of her breasts. Even in the shelter's dim light, the violent shivering that wracked her tall frame made the silver clasps of her mantle clatter against stone. "Hatsune? Aoi-chan?"

"I... I c-can't feel my fingers," Hatsune stammered, collapsing onto a dry woven cedar mat with a dull thud. Her bubblegum-pink hair was plastered across her forehead in wet spikes, dripping cold rivulets down the bridge of her nose. Her lips were a faint, alarming shade of lavender. She tried to blow warm breath into her cupped palms, but her jaw trembled so hard that she ended up biting her own tongue with a muffled whimper. "M-Mou... it feels like we were thrown into a frozen lake! My magic feels like ice water sloshing in my stomach!"

Across the chamber, Aoi sat huddled against the curve of an exposed root knuckle, her knees pulled tight against her chin. Her scout tunic was drenched dark green, the sodden wool heavy enough to drag at her shoulders. She held her carved wooden Companion-kun close beneath her collar, shielding the dry pine figurine with her knuckles while her slender elven ears twitched erratically, flushed crimson from the biting wind.

"I-I'm alright... Misato-san," Aoi whispered, though her voice wavered like a frayed bowstring. She looked down at her muddy boots, pulling her sleeves over her hands to hide how violently she was trembling. "M-My bow stayed dry under the waxed wrap... but my arms feel stiff like frozen branches."

"No more talking until we have fire," I said, my own jaw aching from clenching my teeth against the deep, creeping chill settling into my bones. My leather traveling boots squelched as I crossed the flagstones to the dry timber cache carved into the hollow wall.

Beneath an overhang of dry root, I found a sealed birch-bark cylinder containing a dry iron striker, a lump of flint, and a bundle of shredded cedar tinder. My fingers were numb, clumsy as wooden pegs, but I knelt over the hearth pit and struck the flint against the steel with sharp, downward blows. Bright orange sparks rained into the dry cedar fluff. On the third strike, a pale wisp of fragrant smoke curled upward, glowing gold in the dark. I leaned close, blowing steady, gentle breath over the ember until a tongue of yellow flame caught the dry resinous shavings.

Within seconds, the dry pine splits crackled into life. A bright, amber warmth blossomed across the hearth, casting long, dancing shadows up the curved cedar walls of the hollow. The sweet scent of burning pine resin and clean woodsmoke filled the chamber, slowly driving back the bitter damp that had followed us inside.

"Praise the forest elders," Misato murmured softly, closing her eyes as the radiant heat touched her chilled face. She leaned forward, extending her trembling hands toward the licking flames. "Listen to me, all of you. We cannot stay in these clothes. The high mountain wind has dropped the temperature near freezing. Wet wool and sodden leather will draw every ember of heat out of our flesh before the fire can warm our bones. If we do not strip off these waterlogged layers, hypothermia will set in within the hour."

Aoi gasped, her emerald eyes snapping wide as her blush deepened from her ears down to her throat. She instinctively clutched the collar of her soaked tunic tighter, pressing her back against the dry cedar bark of her alcove. "S-Strip...?! H-Here... in front of Yuuki-san?!"

"Aoi-chan, this is a matter of survival, not modesty," Misato replied with a gentle, firm cadence that carried all the calm authority of a guild master. Her fingers worked at the silver brooches of her heavy outer mantle, peeling the drenched, heavy wool from her shoulders with a wet, squelching peel. She set the heavy garment aside, draping it over a horizontal root beam near the hearth where the rising smoke could dry it. Beneath, her thin ivory linen under-shift was damp, sticking to the soft curve of her waist and the deep, heavy valley between her breasts. "Yuuki-san has walked through the storm carrying our heaviest packs and holding the trail for us. He is just as chilled as we are. There is no shame in saving our lives."

"Misato is totally right..." Hatsune mumbled, her voice muffled as she yanked her heavy, water-logged traveling tunic over her head with clumsy, shivering tugs. The sodden wool hit the flagstones with a heavy, wet slap. Beneath it, she wore only a thin, loose travel camisole that left her pale, delicate shoulders and slender waist bare to the warm air. Goosebumps covered her skin, making the tips of her perky breasts strain visibly against the thin fabric. She crawled closer to the hearth on all fours, trembling so hard that a loose iron cup on the hearthstone began to rattle and float an inch off the stone as her psychic energy twitched involuntarily. "Brrr! See? It feels ten times better already just getting that frozen sponge off my back!"

I stripped off my own soaked leather jerkin and drenched linen undershirt, wringing out the cold rainwater into the drainage ditch near the entrance before laying the fabric across the root drying racks. The cold air made my skin pebble with goosebumps, but stepping closer to the hearth sent waves of golden, crackling heat washing across my bare chest and shoulders. The contrast was dizzying; my pulse drummed heavily against my ribs as the warmth began to coax the numbness from my muscles.

From the wall cache, I pulled down four thick, dry woolen blankets kept inside an oiled canvas sack. They smelled faintly of dried lavender and clean sheep's wool, bone-dry and heavy.

I handed the first blanket to Misato, who wrapped it around her shoulders with a soft sigh of gratitude, the thick wool pooling over her full lap. I draped the second over Hatsune, who immediately bundled herself into a tight, shivering cocoon beside the hearthstone, letting out a sleepy, contented whimper as she pressed her forehead against the warm stone rim.

That left two blankets. Across the chamber, Aoi remained in her shadowy corner, still wrapped in her drenched scout tunic. Her lips were trembling, her small fingers clutching Companion-kun so tightly that her knuckles were white. She looked up at the warm light of the fire with an expression of intense, agonized longing, yet her shyness anchored her to the dark root wall, terrified of stepping into the firelight in her sodden clothes.

In the center of the hollow, the cedar fire crackled and popped, throwing sparks toward the high root ceiling. The smell of cedar embers, drying wool, and rain-soaked skin hung thick in the warm, enclosed air. Outside, the storm continued to beat against the living walls of the great pine, sealing us together inside the amber sanctuary of the heartwood.

Misato adjusted the blanket around her lush frame, her blue eyes turning toward me with quiet, maternal understanding. Hatsune peeked out from her woolen fold, her pink hair glowing in the embers as she patted the dry cedar mat beside her. Across the floor, Aoi sat alone in the shadows, her shivering silhouette outlined by the faint reflection of the flames.

The hearth burned bright, pushing back the cold of the mountain storm.
