# Ending 4: The Trapper's Toll

Logan leaned over the parapet and raised his right hand, open-palmed.

"The terms are accepted in principle, Herald!" he called down into the misty gorge. "Commander Aldous's seal is broken, but by authority of the sapper guild, I will surrender the keys to Baron Kestrel’s field captains. Send thirty lances across the span to take ceremonial possession."

The herald bowed in his saddle, visibly relieved. He turned his horse and signaled with his pennant toward the alder line. A trumpet blew two bright notes, and Kestrel’s vanguard detachment—three mounted captains in burnished plate accompanied by thirty elite dismounted men-at-arms—began their advance across the timber approach.

Logan stepped back from the battlement and grabbed Tobias by the collar.

"Two kegs of fine-grain powder," Logan whispered, his words hard and fast. "Rig them directly behind the drawbridge counterweight wells in the gatehouse vault. Lay four feet of sulfur fuse and pack the barrels with iron gravel and chopped horseshoes. Do it now."

Tobias nodded once, his eyes wide, and sprinted down the spiral stair.

Logan descended to the gatehouse gallery. Through the iron observation grate, he watched the mercenary vanguard step onto the central span of the drawbridge. Their armor clanked heavily against the wet oak decking, sixty boots in measured cadence, confident that the fortress was theirs. The lead captain, an arrogant veteran with a boar’s crest welded to his helm, drew his longsword and held it aloft in salute.

Below the archway, Tobias tapped Logan’s forearm. "Charges set. Fuses lit."

Logan pulled a linen kerchief from his collar, spat onto the fabric, and plugged his ears.

Three seconds later, the gatehouse floor erupted.

A blinding orange flash tore through the stone arches, followed by an ear-splitting detonation that blew the heavy oak doors off their iron pintles in a storm of whistling splinters. The two thirty-ton granite counterweights, severed from their retaining chains by the blast, plummeted down their stone shafts and smashed through the bridge piers like cannon shot.

The central span of the drawbridge sheered completely in half.

With a sound like thunder, four thousand square feet of heavy oak planking and iron bracing collapsed into the Calder gorge. Kestrel's vanguard had no time to leap back. Horses screamed, men in sixty-pound plate tumbled through the empty air, and thirty elite mercenary veterans plunged fifty feet into the boiling brown foam below. The current dragged them under instantly, battering their steel against the riverbed boulders.

Through the drifting cloud of white powder smoke, the surviving mercenary ranks across the river stared in stunned horror at the yawning, thirty-foot gap where their bridge had been.

On the north bank, the herald scrambled onto his horse, frantically blowing his horn for a general retreat. The sight of their vanguard wiped out in a heartbeat broke the will of the mercenary host; within an hour, their baggage wagons were turning around on the river road, hauling their siege engines back down the valley toward the coast.

Logan stood at the edge of the jagged, smoking stone abutment, cold river wind whipping through his torn coat. He watched the last of Kestrel’s cavalry disappear into the distant alder trees, then unbuckled his heavy tool belt and handed it to Tobias.

"Fetch the carpenter’s crew," Logan said, looking across the empty gorge. "We have forty feet of new bridge timbers to cut before nightfall."
