# Ending 5: The Iron Redoubt

Logan reached over the coping, caught the vellum charter by its silk ribbon, and hoisted it onto the wall. He did not read a single line. He gripped both ends of the calfskin in his calloused fists and tore it straight down the middle with the sound of snapping bone.

He tossed the halves over the parapet. They fluttered through the freezing rain, tumbling into the frothing brown torrent sixty feet below.

"Tell Baron Kestrel that Mor-Riven was built on granite, not parchment!" Logan roared over the wind. "If he wants the water-gate, he can come buy it with iron!"

The herald’s face turned gray. He wrenched his roan’s bit, wheeled around in the mud, and galloped back toward the siege lines, his green banner trailing in the muck.

"Keller!" Logan shouted, spinning toward the stairs. "Drop the outer portcullis! Form the pike-wall behind the second archway! Masons to the murder-holes with the boiling lime!"

The iron portcullis dropped with an earth-shaking clang, its five-inch steel tines biting into the stone sill. A minute later, the mercenary horns sounded along the riverbank—a harsh, braying chorus that signaled an all-out assault.

Two hundred armored footmen surged out of the alder woods, crowding onto the narrow timber approach bridge behind heavy mantlets. Their iron-shod boots beat a frantic drumroll against the wet planks as their crossbowmen sent a flurry of black-fletched bolts clattering against Mor-Riven’s battlements.

"Hold the line!" Keller barked in the gatehouse passage. His voice was hoarse, drowned out by the metallic din.

Forty garrison pikemen braced their twelve-foot ash shafts against the flagstones, their iron points bristling through the iron grill of the portcullis like a bed of thorns. Behind them, Tobias and the masons hauled smoking kettles of slaked lime and boiling tallow up the hoist, dumping the burning slurry through the machicolations directly onto the heads of the assault column.

The bottleneck was brutal. On the twelve-foot-wide stone approach, Kestrel’s numbers counted for nothing. His men-at-arms pressed forward into the archway only to be impaled upon the braced pikes, their breastplates screeching against the steel points. Crossbow bolts fired at point-blank range through the embrasures punch through cuirasses and shields alike.

For three hours, the fighting raged across the blood-slick bridgehead. Logan stood upon the catwalk directly above the inner gate, directing the crossbow fire, levering loose granite blocks over the parapet, and personally knocking out the grapple-hooks with his iron pry-bar whenever the mercenary vanguard attempted to scale the masonry.

By sunset, the rain had turned into a freezing sleet. The timbers of the bridge were strewn with shattered shields, broken pike shafts, and forty dead mercenaries lying in the gray pools of slush.

Across the gorge, Baron Kestrel’s horns blew three low, somber notes. The assault faltered. The remaining mercenaries dragged their wounded back into the dripping woods, abandoning their mantlets in the mud.

The bridge grew quiet, save for the eternal, hollow roar of the Calder River below.

Logan set his pry-bar down against the battlements, his breath steaming white in the twilight. His leather coat was soaked through with sweat and freezing sleet, his knuckles bruised purple, but the iron portcullis remained unbroken, and the flag of Mor-Riven still flew above the highest tower.

He wiped the grease from his face with his cuff, looked down at Keller leaning against his pike in the archway, and nodded once before walking toward the guardroom to check the evening watch rotation.
