# Chapter 2B: The Herald's Parchment

The wind atop the water-gate parapet cut like a wet rasp. Mist blew off the Calder gorge in freezing sheets, carrying the smell of pine smoke from the siege lines and the sharp, sour tang of horse sweat from the road below.

Logan stood between two embrasures, his hands resting on the cold granite coping. Beside him, Sergeant Keller kept four crossbowmen kneeling behind the oak mantlets, their heavy steel bowstrings cranked to the notches, quarrels resting in the grease grooves.

Thirty yards out, where the timber approach bridge met the masonry abutment, three horsemen waited in the drizzle.

The herald sat on a roan gelding, his green velvet surcoat plastered to his mail by the rain. A square-jawed clerk with a quill case strapped to his belt held a tin cylinder under his arm. Behind them, two mounted men-at-arms in dark iron cuirasses kept their visors raised, their eyes scanning the murder-holes above the gateway.

"Master Sapper Logan Finch!" the herald called out. His voice was shrill, pitched to carry over the roar of the water beneath the timbers. "I speak for Baron Kestrel, High Warden of the Calder Marches and Syndic of the Iron League!"

"Speak from your saddle, Orson," Logan answered, leaning over the stone. "The wind is cold, and my men have heavy fingers on the triggers."

The clerk dismounted, stepped onto the wet oak planks of the drawbridge, and withdrew a roll of vellum bound in yellow silk ribbon. He held it up against the gray sky.

"Commander Aldous is dead," the herald shouted. "Your eastern supply road is cut by two companies of mercenary lances. You hold four days of dry meal in your stores and sixty feet of river between your walls and forty siege engines. Baron Kestrel offers a covenant under the seal of the League."

"What covenant?" Keller growled under his breath, spitting tobacco onto the flags. "The Baron hangs garrisons that cost him siege pay."

"Listen to the ink, Sergeant," Logan said quietly. "Kestrel is a merchant before he is a soldier. Every day he keeps five hundred mercenaries in these alder swamps costs him forty silver marks."

The clerk unrolled the parchment, reading with brisk, nasal cadence: "The garrison of Mor-Riven shall yield the water-gate, the sluice-controls, and the timber flumes into the custody of the Iron League. In restitution, the League guarantees the lives and chattels of every artisan, apprentice, and pikeman within these walls. The garrison shall be enrolled into the League's river security corps under full mercenary wages, with three months' back-pay advanced in solid thalers upon the morrow. Master Sapper Finch shall retain the office of Chief Surveyor of the Calder Works with a seat upon the toll arbitrations."

The wind whipped the bottom of the parchment. Logan studied the lines of iron gall ink, his mind turning like a water-wheel.

Kestrel wanted the gorge intact. The river was the only trade vein capable of hauling pig iron and mountain lumber from the upper valleys down to the coastal foundries. If Logan destroyed the weir, Kestrel would go bankrupt within two seasons. The terms were real, legally bound by the League's commercial courts in the south, but they required handing over the keys of the realm's strongest frontier stronghold.

Logan glanced down at the timber bridge. Beyond the three riders, fifty paces back along the alder road, Kestrel’s vanguard captains sat their chargers in full plate, waiting for the drawbridge to lower.

Three paths stood open before him on the parapet.

He could accept the terms. By signing the parchment, he would secure the lives of seventy pikemen, spare the fifteen civilian masons, and preserve Mor-Riven’s water infrastructure under an independent guild charter, ending the siege with silver rather than blood.

He could feign compliance. He could accept the initial articles, invite Kestrel's vanguard officers onto the drawbridge span to take ceremonial possession, and have Tobias drop sixty pounds of blasting powder through the machicolations directly onto the bridge timbers, taking the Baron's elite captains in a devastating counter-ambush.

Or he could reject the offer entirely. He could snap the herald's quill, drop the heavy iron portcullises, and rally the garrison on the narrow bridgehead redoubt, forcing Kestrel's men to pay for every foot of stone in a bloody, close-quarters slugfest against the granite walls.

Logan looked down at the waiting herald.
