# Ending 4: The Cat Who Stayed

The open-air hot spring pool was silent save for the gentle bubbling of the thermal spring and the soft whisper of the night wind rustling through the mountain pines. Plumes of warm steam rose gracefully into the starry sky, catching the silver moonlight before dissolving into the cool night air. The faint, sweet perfume of the Moonpetal Sap that had sparked our mutual desire still lingered in the mist, though our passion had long surpassed any artificial catalyst.

Karyl lay curled up in my lap on the shallow stone bench of the pool, her lithe, naked body completely relaxed against mine. The warm mineral water lapped gently around her slender waist and thighs. Her dark cat ears were folded flat against her dark hair in pure contentment, and a loud, deep, resonant purr vibrated continuously from her chest—a purr so rhythmic and powerful that its vibrations rippled through the water surrounding us.

Her slender back bore the light, rosy flush of our shared release. My own shoulders and collarbone were marked with a dozen thin red claw scratches where she had held onto me during her explosive climax, gripping me with desperate intensity as her magical defenses shattered.

"Yuuki..." Karyl murmured softly, her purple eyes peering up at me through wet strands of dark hair. Her small hands rested against my bare chest, her slender fingers idly tracing the water droplets glistening across my collarbone.

"Yes, Karyl?" I replied gently, resting my hand on the crown of her head and lightly stroking the velvet-soft fur behind her right ear.

Karyl let out a tiny, involuntary meow at my touch. Her entire body melted against mine, and the purr in her chest deepened in pitch. A sudden surge of crimson heat rushed to her cheeks, and she quickly buried her face into the crook of my neck to hide her mortification. Under the water, however, her sleek black cat tail remained coiled tightly around my waist, refusing to let me go.

"If... if you ever breathe a single word to Pecorine or Kokkoro about the purring..." Karyl grumbled into my collarbone, her voice muffled and lacking even an ounce of true bite, "I'll blast you with Grim Burst until you're nothing but cinders. I'll turn you into a blue river toad and feed you to an ogre."

I chuckled softly, wrapping my arms around her slender waist and pulling her closer against my chest. "Your secret is completely safe with me, Karyl. But for what it's worth, I think it's the most adorable sound in the world."

"I am NOT adorable!" she hissed back, though her little feline fangs only grazed playfully against my skin in gentle token retaliation. Her tail gave my hip another possessive squeeze beneath the ripples. "I'm a wicked, terrifying black mage! I studied dangerous astral spells! I can summon shadow spheres that obliterate dungeon guardians! People in the capital cross the street when they see my staff!"

"You are also the girl who stayed up until two in the morning patching Kokkoro's traveling satchel, and the one who secretly leaves the best cuts of roasted meat on Pecorine's plate when she isn't looking," I whispered into her ear. "You pretend to be prickly and aloof, but your heart is warmer than anyone else's. And tonight, you gave me your trust."

Karyl went completely still in my arms. The playful defiance drained from her posture, replaced by a quiet, trembling stillness. Her head rested heavily against my shoulder, her breath coming in slow, warm puffs against my neck. She buried her face deeper into my collarbone, her small hands clutching the wet skin of my shoulders.

"You notice way too much, you jerk..." she whispered, her voice wavering with a delicate mixture of embarrassment and profound relief. "Nobody ever bothered to look past the spells and the scowls before. To everyone else, I was just a stray cat with a bad attitude and dangerous magic. Even when I joined your group, I kept waiting for the catch. I waited for you to demand something impossible, or throw me out the second I made a mistake."

"We would never throw you out, Karyl," I said, my voice steady and firm. "You're not a tool, and you're not an errand runner. You're our comrade. You're our family."

"Yuuki..." she whispered again, her voice barely carrying over the water. "All my life... I believed I was meant to be an outcast. Back in the slums, and later when I was taken in by... by people who only wanted to use my magic... nobody ever cared about what I wanted. I was always a tool. A disposable pawn to be shoved into the dark when I wasn't needed. I learned early on that if you don't keep your claws out, people will step on you until there's nothing left."

I reached down under the water, finding her left hand, and twined my fingers through hers. Her grip was tight, almost desperate, clinging to my palm as if terrified I might vanish into the steam.

"When I first met you and the others," she continued, her throat tight with emotion, "I told myself it was just an assignment. That you were all hopeless idiots who couldn't survive three days without getting eaten by wild beasts. I told myself I wouldn't care. I wouldn't get attached."

She turned her face toward mine, her large purple eyes shimmering with unshed tears that caught the moonlight.

"But then Pecorine would drag me into bakeries and force sweet buns into my mouth with that stupid, sunny grin. And Kokkoro would mend my cloaks and brew hot tea without ever asking for praise. And you... every time we got into a fight, you jumped in front of monster blades to protect me. You smiled at me as if I actually mattered. Tonight... when you held me, when you looked at me without a single trace of judgment or fear... I realized I don't want to run anymore. I don't want to be alone. I want to stay with you. With the Gourmet Guild."

A single tear escaped her lash, tracing down her cheek before dropping into the warm spring water.

I raised my free hand, cupping her cheek and brushing away the dampness with my thumb. Leaning in, I pressed my lips gently against hers. Karyl gasped into the kiss, her lips parting softly to meet mine. The kiss was slow, tender, and deeply reassuring—a silent vow that needed no formal words.

"You are never going to be alone again, Karyl," I promised against her lips. "No matter what shadows from your past come calling, we will face them together. You have a home with us. You have a home with me."

Karyl let out a shaky, contented sigh, pressing her forehead against my chin. Her cat ears twitched forward, and the vibrating purr in her chest resumed its steady, comforting rhythm.

"You're an idiot, Yuuki," she murmured softly, her fingers tracing the red scratch marks on my shoulder. "A total, reckless idiot. But... you're my idiot."

We remained together in the warm shallows of the pool for another half hour, watching the moon begin its slow descent toward the western ridge. Eventually, our fingertips began to prune from the extended soak, and the cool night air reminded us that even the best hot springs could drain one's strength if overstayed.

"Let's get out before we turn into prunes," I said with a smile.

"Fine," Karyl agreed, though she took her time untangling her legs and tail from mine. "Help me up. My legs feel like cooked noodles."

I stood up first, the cool night breeze striking my wet skin with an invigorating chill. Reaching down, I took Karyl by both hands and helped her step up onto the stone rim of the bath. Her lithe, porcelain body gleamed under the starlight, every curve delicate and beautifully proportioned. Her dark cat tail flicked nervously as she realized how exposed she was in the open air, a fresh blush spreading across her cheeks.

I immediately grabbed two large, fluffy cotton yukata robes that had been left on the wooden bench under the cedar roof. I wrapped one around Karyl's shoulders, pulling the thick fabric snugly around her trembling frame, before slipping into the other.

Karyl clutched the robe tightly around her chest, tying the sash with quick, self-conscious tugs. She looked up at me, her cheeks still rosy, her cat ears perked with curiosity.

"Better?" I asked, smoothing the damp hair away from her face.

"Much better," she muttered, though she immediately leaned her shoulder against mine as we walked over to the wooden veranda overlooking the pine valley.

We sat down side by side on the dry cedar deck, letting our bare feet dangle over the edge into the cool night air while the warm robes kept our bodies toasty. On a low wooden table nearby sat a ceramic pitcher of chilled spring water and two wooden cups. I poured a cup for each of us.

Karyl took the cup with both hands, drinking deeply. The crisp, chilled water quenched the thirst brought on by the long soak and our passionate exertion.

"Ahhh... that hits the spot," she sighed, setting down the cup. She pulled her knees up to her chest inside the roomy yukata, resting her chin on her knees. Her black tail slipped out from beneath the robe's hem, snaking across the wooden planks to wrap loosely around my ankle.

"Tired?" I asked, leaning my head against hers.

"Exhausted," she admitted without her usual pride. "Magic takes a lot of mental energy, but whatever that was tonight... it drained every drop of mana from my core. In a good way, though. My mind feels completely quiet for the first time in years."

She closed her eyes, letting the cool mountain wind ruffle her dark bangs. We sat in companionable silence, watching the constellations drift across the velvet expanse of the night sky. The great silver moon dipped behind the western peaks, casting long, dramatic shadows across the forest below.

Gradually, the deep black of the sky began to soften into charcoal, then into deep slate blue. A faint band of pale amber and coral light appeared along the eastern horizon, heralding the arrival of the morning sun.

As the dawn light strengthened, the sounds of the mountain inn began to stir. From the garden pavilion on the other side of the courtyard, we saw Kokkoro walking along the mossy stone path, carrying a small woven basket of fresh herbs and medicinal leaves she had harvested. She wore her green and white priestess robe, looking refreshed and serene.

Catching sight of us sitting on the veranda, Kokkoro paused, her long elf ears twitching politely. A warm, understanding smile graced her lips. She bowed respectfully toward us from across the courtyard, raising one hand in a cheerful morning greeting before continuing toward the kitchen to brew the morning tea.

Karyl's cat ears shot straight up, and her face turned the color of a ripe beet.

"D-Did Kokkoro just see us?!" she squeaked in terror, pulling her tail back so fast it nearly knocked over her water cup. "She knows! I know she knows! Look at that smug little elf smile! She definitely heard us!"

"Kokkoro is happy for us, Karyl," I laughed, catching her hand before she could bolt into the guest rooms. "There's nothing to be ashamed of."

Before Karyl could mount a counter-argument, a loud, energetic bang echoed from the main hallway. The wooden shoji doors of the dining hall slid open with tremendous force, revealing Pecorine standing in the entryway with her fists on her hips, her long golden hair glowing brilliantly in the morning sun.

"GOOD MORNING, EVERYONE!" Pecorine's booming voice rang out across the entire courtyard, sending a flock of mountain sparrows fluttering into the sky. "The sun is up, the air is fresh, and I can smell sweet soy sauce, grilled salmon, and steamed rice all the way from the kitchen! Let's eat until we burst!"

Karyl groaned, slapping a hand over her face while her cat ears flattened in exasperation.

"Why is she always so loud at dawn?" she complained, though the fond smile playing on her lips completely betrayed her annoyance. "She's like an overgrown golden retriever that runs on grilled meat."

"Come on," I smiled, tugging gently on her hand to pull her to her feet. "If we don't get in there right now, Pecorine will eat our portions before the innkeeper can even set the bowls down."

Karyl sighed theatrically, straightening her yukata and brushing off the hem. She looked up at me, her purple eyes bright and steady, completely free of the defensive walls she had worn for so long.

"Fine. But you're sitting next to me," she said, her voice dropping into a soft, private register. Her fingers curled tightly around mine, squeezing with unmistakable affection. "And if anyone asks why you have scratches on your shoulder... you fell into a thorny blackberry bush while gathering firewood. Understood?"

"Understood," I laughed, leaning down to plant a quick, tender kiss on the tip of her pointed nose.

Karyl blushed, her cat ears giving a happy little flick. Together, hand in hand, we stepped off the veranda into the crisp morning sunlight, ready to join our guild for breakfast and face whatever adventures awaited us in the wide, wondrous world of Astraea.
