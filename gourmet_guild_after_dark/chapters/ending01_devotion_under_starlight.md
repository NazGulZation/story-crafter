# Ending 1: Devotion Under Starlight

The silver moonlight filtered through the weeping willow leaves, painting delicate patterns of radiance across our intertwined bodies on the bed of crushed moonflower petals. The cool night air of the high mountain ridge drifted down through the bamboo grove, but beneath the thick wool cloak I had pulled over our shoulders, our shared heat was as comforting and unyielding as a warm hearth.

"Yuuki... Aruji-sama..." Kokkoro sobbed softly, her small hands clutching at my shoulders as the final, shuddering tremors of our shared release pulsed through her slender frame. Her short fingernails dug gently into my back, and her small chest heaved in rapid, shallow gasps against mine. "Kokkoro's heart... it is overflowing. Every part of me... belongs to you."

Her inner walls clamped down in gentle, fluttering contractions around my softening shaft, milking the last drops of my warmth into her womb. A soft, breathless whimper slipped from her parted lips as she rested her forehead against the crook of my neck, her skin damp with perspiration that carried the sweet, lingering perfume of crushed jasmine and wild honey.

I tightened my arms around her narrow waist, pulling her small, delicate body completely flush against mine. "You gave me everything tonight, Kokkoro. Thank you."

Tears of pure, overwhelming happiness welled in the corners of her pink eyes, reflecting the starlight like tiny silver dew drops before spilling down her flushed cheeks. "It is Kokkoro who must thank you, Aruji-sama... You have honored your humble guide with a love deeper than the ocean."

I smiled gently, pressing a soft kiss to her forehead, then to the tip of her delicate nose, and finally against her warm, swollen lips. "When we are alone like this, Kokkoro... you can just call me Yuuki."

Kokkoro's pointed elf ears twitched with shy wonder, their tips burning with a vivid rose tint. She hid her face in my shoulder for a quiet moment, her breath tickling my collarbone, before looking up at me through silver-white bangs.

"Yuuki..." she whispered, testing the name on her tongue with reverent care, as if handling a fragile glass ornament. A radiant, blushing smile bloomed across her face. "Yuuki... It feels so wondrous to say your name without any titles between us. In my heart, you will always be my Lord, my Aruji-sama, chosen by the Great Goddess Ames. But when we hold each other like this... you are simply my beloved."

After a few quiet minutes catching our breath in the dewy grass, Kokkoro sat up slowly, holding the edges of the wool cloak around her chest. The cool mountain air touched her bare shoulders, making her shiver softly. She looked down at our bodies, her cheeks dimpling with a modest, bashful flush as she saw the sheen of mingled sweat and release coating her pale thighs.

"We must tend to ourselves, Yuuki," she murmured softly, her nurturing instincts returning with gentle grace. "Allow Kokkoro to fetch warm water from the stone basin. A guide must never allow her beloved to catch a chill after such intense exertion."

"Let's go together," I said, rising onto my knees and wrapping my arm around her waist.

Supporting her small frame, I helped her stand. Her legs trembled slightly on the smooth river pebbles, the aftermath of our passionate union leaving her knees delightfully soft. Together, wrapped in the wide wool cloak, we walked slowly to the edge of the stone basin near the bamboo grove. The clear spring water piped into the basin was pleasantly warm, fed by an auxiliary branch of the thermal channel beneath the garden.

Kokkoro picked up a clean linen cloth from the wooden tray on the stone wall, dipping it into the warm water before wringing it out with practiced, delicate hands. She knelt beside me, her pink eyes shimmering with boundless devotion as she gently wiped the dried perspiration and honeyed herbal residue from my chest, my collarbones, and my shoulders. Her touch was feather-light and immensely soothing, carrying the quiet, reverent tenderness of a sacred priestess tending her temple hearth.

"Does your left forearm still ache from the labyrinth battle, Yuuki?" she asked softly, carefully dabbing the damp cloth around the linen bandage.

"Not at all," I chuckled softly, reaching out to smooth a stray silver lock behind her pointed ear. "Your love and the warm spring air cured every ache I had."

Kokkoro giggled quietly, her cheeks flushing a delicate rose. "You say such sweet things, Yuuki... but Kokkoro will inspect your bandages again before we return to Landosol. Your health is the foundation of our guild."

I took the cloth from her hands, rinsed it in the warm water, and gently tended to her in return. I wiped the beads of moisture from her delicate throat, her smooth collarbones, and the tender curve of her ribs. When the warm cloth traced the hollow beneath her arms and smoothed over her small, firm breasts, Kokkoro let out a soft, breathy gasp, her elf ears twitching with sensitive delight as she leaned her forehead against my chest.

"To be cared for by you like this..." she whispered, her eyes fluttering shut, "it fills my heart with a quiet peace I have never known."

Once we had cleansed our skin, we carried the wool cloak and a pair of dry cotton yukatas up the shallow steps onto the polished timber veranda. The night breeze had settled into a gentle, fragrant murmur through the pine canopy. Kokkoro walked over to the corner of the veranda where a small charcoal brazier glowed with faint red embers. With efficient, graceful movements, she revived the heat with a small wooden fan, setting a cast-iron kettle filled with roasted barley tea over the grate.

Within minutes, the rich, nutty aroma of simmering barley tea joined the cool mountain air. Kokkoro poured the hot, amber liquid into two small ceramic cups, offering one to me with both hands and a polite, formal nod that made me smile at how naturally her manners returned.

"Please enjoy some warm tea, Yuuki," she said, sitting close beside me on the veranda floorboards.

We sat side by side, our shoulders touching beneath the shared cloak, sipping the hot, soothing tea while watching the moon traverse the velvet sky. The warmth of the ceramic cup seeped into my fingers, matching the profound, lingering heat in my chest.

"When I first left the elven village in the sacred forest," Kokkoro murmured, gazing out over the dark silhouette of the mountain valley, "the elders recited the ancient mandates of our people. They told me that guiding the chosen Lord was a solemn, solitary duty. I was prepared to walk three paces behind you, to cook your meals, wash your travel cloaks, and offer counsel when asked, all while keeping my own heart locked away in quiet prayer."

She set her tea cup down and turned to look at me, her fingers resting lightly across my hand.

"I never imagined how kind you would be," she continued softly, her voice filled with deep emotion. "On the day we met, you had lost your memories, yet you smiled at me without hesitation. You shared your very first apple with me. When beasts ambushed us on the road to Landosol, you threw yourself in front of me even though you barely knew how to hold a sword. You treated me not as a servant or an instrument of prophecy, but as someone precious. Day by day, quest by quest, my heart began to belong to you. Tonight... when you held me beneath these stars... Kokkoro felt as though her soul had finally arrived home."

"You are home, Kokkoro," I answered, wrapping my fingers around hers and bringing her small knuckles to my lips. "Ever since I opened my eyes in this world with no memory of who I was, you were my compass. You showed me the beauty of this land, taught me how to live, and believed in me when I didn't believe in myself. You're the heart of our guild."

"Yuuki..." Kokkoro's eyes shimmered with fresh tears of pure happiness. She leaned against my shoulder, her head resting comfortably over my collarbone as we watched the deep indigo of the night sky gradually soften at the eastern horizon.

Hours passed in sweet, unhurried tranquility until pale ribbons of lavender, rose, and gold stretched across the mountain peaks, illuminating the thermal mist rising from the valley below. The cool morning breeze stirred the bamboo grove, scattering dewdrops over the garden grass like a shower of liquid gems.

Kokkoro stirred gently against me, her long elf ears twitching as the morning sunlight touched her face. Her pink eyes fluttered open, blinking against the dawn, before focusing on me with boundless affection. A soft, sleepy smile curved her lips, and she nuzzled her nose affectionately against my neck.

"Good morning, Yuuki..." she whispered, her voice sweet and husky with sleep.

"Good morning, my sweet Kokkoro," I replied, smoothing a stray silver lock behind her ear. "Did you sleep well?"

"Wonderfully," she murmured, stretching her arms with a soft elven sigh. Her slender body flexed gracefully beneath her yukata, her pale skin glowing in the morning light. "I felt so safe in your arms that I did not want the night to end. But look... the sun is rising over the ridge."

In the distance, the roof of the mountain lodge was bathed in golden sunlight. Smoke curled steadily from the kitchen chimney, carrying the unmistakable, mouth-watering aroma of sizzling mountain trout, rolled sweet tamagoyaki, hot miso soup with wild mushrooms, and freshly steamed rice.

"Pecorine-sama must already be awake," Kokkoro chuckled softly, her pink eyes sparkling with amusement. "Her stomach is the most reliable clock in all of Landosol. And Karyl-chan will undoubtedly grumble about being awakened by the smell of roasting fish."

"We should go inside and join them," I smiled, taking her hand.

We stood up, straightened our yukatas, and walked through the sliding veranda screens into the warm interior of the lodge. The corridor smelled richly of polished cedar and morning cooking. As we entered the main dining hall, the sight that greeted us brought an immediate grin to my face.

Pecorine was already seated at the oak table, wearing a fresh red tunic, holding a pair of wooden chopsticks in one hand and waving a steaming rice paddle with the other. A colossal spread covered the table—heaping platters of salted river trout grilled over charcoal, bowls of pickled mountain greens, glistening yellow egg rolls, and a massive wooden tub of polished white rice.

"Ah! Yuuki! Kokkoro-chan! Good morning!" Pecorine roared cheerfully, her bright blue eyes lighting up like twin stars. "Look at this breakfast! The innkeeper brought out river trout caught at dawn! It is incredibly delicious, yabai desu ne! Come sit down quickly before I devour everything in sight! My stomach was rumbling so loud Karyl thought an earth golem was attacking the foundation!"

Sitting across from her, Karyl held a porcelain teacup with both hands, her dark cat ears twitching irritably at Pecorine's boisterous shouting. Her dark hair was neatly brushed, but her pale face held a deep, lingering blush as her purple eyes flicked over to Kokkoro and me. Her black cat tail rested against the tatami mat, flicking in small, thoughtful twitches.

"Would you keep your voice down, you hopeless glutton?" Karyl grumbled into her tea, though her tone held zero malice. Her eyes lingered on our clasped hands, and a faint, knowing smirk touched the corner of her lips before she quickly turned away. "Honestly... you two took forever out in the garden. I was about to send a search party to see if you'd been swallowed by a giant carnivorous plant. But... I guess you look well-rested."

"Karyl-chan, don't be jealous!" Pecorine giggled shamelessly, nudging Karyl's elbow. "There's plenty of rice for everyone!"

"I am NOT jealous, you colossal airhead!" Karyl squeaked, her face turning as red as a ripe tomato.

Kokkoro's cheeks flushed a delicate shade of rose, but she held her head high with quiet, radiant poise. She walked gracefully to the table, taking her seat beside me, and immediately poured fresh green tea into my cup with her usual meticulous elven care.

"Thank you for your concern, Karyl-chan," Kokkoro answered softly, her pink eyes shining with unmistakable happiness. "Aruji-sama and I were admiring the starlight. The night was peaceful, and our hearts are completely refreshed."

"Ehehe~ That's wonderful!" Pecorine beamed, heaping a mountain of steaming rice into my bowl with hearty enthusiasm. "Eat up, Yuuki! We have a whole new day ahead of us, and the Gourmet Guild needs all its strength to find new ingredients across Landosol!"

"Thank you, Pecorine," I smiled, picking up my chopsticks.

Surrounded by the warmth of the fire, the rich flavors of a morning feast, and the laughter of the people I loved most in the world, a profound sense of gratitude settled over my heart. Kokkoro sat beside me, her small hand brushing against mine under the table, her pink eyes meeting mine with a quiet, eternal promise.

Together, four members of the Gourmet Guild sat beneath the morning sun, bound by shared trials, shared meals, and an unbreakable love that would guide us wherever the road led.
