# Chapter 1: Steam & Moonpetal

The heavy cedar doors of the mountain lodge creaked as a cool evening draft swept across the polished floorboards, carrying the sharp, mineral-rich scent of boiling sulfur springs, damp moss, and mountain pine needles. I sat on the edge of the low futon, slowly unwrapping the linen bandages bound around my left forearm. The skin beneath was tender and flushed pink, marked by the concussive force of an armored golem's fist we had barely managed to shatter three hours prior in the lower depths of the labyrinth. The cool mountain air felt soothing against my bruised skin, but deep inside my chest, an entirely different sensation was taking root.

Around us, the lodge was silent save for the rhythmic, droning chirp of late-summer mountain cicadas and the muffled clatter of earthenware coming from the kitchen alcove. We had rented this secluded retreat high in the ridge woods above Landosol for a rare day off, seeking to rest our bruised limbs after a grueling week of continuous dungeon exploration. Every cedar plank beneath my bare soles radiated the gentle, persistent heat of subterranean hot spring channels running directly beneath the timber foundation. The thermal warmth seeped up through the soles of my feet, loosening the tightness in my calves and thighs, yet doing nothing to ease the restless energy accumulating in my blood.

I flexed my fingers, feeling a strange, creeping warmth spreading upward from my palm through my wrist. An unmistakable, thrumming heat surged through my tendons, far sharper than the dull ache of overused muscles or healing bone. The warmth pooled heavily at the base of my throat, making my chest tighten with every breath I took. My skin felt curiously tight, sensitive to the slightest change in temperature, vibrating with a low, electric frequency that made my pulse double its normal rhythm.

On the low tea table sat a heavy brass basin filled with steeping dried herbs we had gathered near the subterranean water vaults. Among the white petals and crushed roots floated several bruised violet stalks of Moonpetal Sap—a rare alpine bloom known to mountain herbalists for its potent restorative properties when dissolved in boiling spring water. But as the dense steam rose from the basin, filling the small room with a thick, honeyed aroma like crushed night-blooming jasmine, wild honey, and warm skin musk, the air felt dense enough to taste on the back of my tongue. It was a intoxicating fragrance, heavy and luxurious, settling over the wooden room like a velvet veil.

My pulse hammered against my ribs with unusual force. My skin felt hyper-sensitive, reacting to the slight drag of my cotton yukata sleeve as if the fabric were made of coarse raw wool. The Moonpetal Sap was doing far more than soothing minor cuts and bruises; its potent natural aphrodisiac essence was leeching into the atmosphere, steadily stirring the blood of everyone breathing inside these wooden walls. Every inhalation brought more of the sweet, potent herbal oil into my lungs, warming me from the inside out and turning my thoughts toward thoughts of soft skin and uninhibited closeness.

I stood up, adjusting the indigo sash at my waist, and stepped out into the quiet corridor. The oiled wooden planks felt smooth and warm beneath my feet, comforting after days spent trekking through damp stone tunnels. From the end of the hall, near the central dining alcove, came the distinct sound of laughter, punctuated by the sharp clatter of lacquered bowls and a loud, dramatic sigh of pure contentment.

Pecorine sat cross-legged at the head of the long oak table, her long orange hair catching the amber glow of the hanging paper lanterns. She was already halfway through a massive wooden platter stacked high with roasted mountain fowl, glazed river trout, and steaming mounds of wild rice. Her royal tiara sat slightly askew on her brow, and her white and red adventuring dress had been loosened at the collar, revealing the deep, supple curve of her full chest as her skin glistened with a light sheen of perspiration. The golden trim of her tunic gleamed in the firelight, but my eyes were drawn to the flush rising from her collarbones up to her round, sun-kissed cheeks.

"Ah! Yuuki! You're awake!" Pecorine called out, waving a half-eaten poultry leg through the air with unbridled enthusiasm. Her bright blue eyes sparkled under the lantern light, though her cheeks bore a deep, feverish crimson flush that went far beyond her usual post-battle appetite. "Come sit down! The innkeeper brought out the local mountain roast! It is incredibly delicious, yabai desu ne! My stomach was making noises loud enough to wake the forest beasts! If we don't eat now, I might collapse right onto the tatami!"

Beside her on the tatami mat, Karyl sat leaning back against a polished wooden pillar, holding a small ceramic cup of warmed plum wine. Her dark cat ears twitched erratically at Pecorine's boisterous voice, flattening against her dark hair before standing tall again. Her slender black cat tail swished back and forth across the woven floorboards in sharp, agitated flicks that revealed her internal agitation. She looked intensely uncomfortable, her pale face burning with a fiery blush that reached all the way to the tips of her furry ears.

"Must you chew so loudly, you unrefined princess?" Karyl grumbled, though her voice lacked its usual sharp, biting edge. She took a quick sip from her cup, her cheeks burning an intense rose color as she adjusted the dark fabric of her mage robes. Her purple eyes darted toward me as I entered, then immediately snapped away toward the garden window. She pulled her knees tight against her chest, her tail curling tight around her ankles in an effort to regain her composure. "And put your clothes on properly! Your chest is practically spilling out of your bodice! Have you no shame at all in front of Yuuki?!"

"Ehh? But it's so hot in here, Karyl!" Pecorine giggled, fanning herself with a wooden rice paddle. The movement caused her generous bust to sway enticingly beneath the loosened laces of her gown. "It feels like someone lit a furnace directly under the floor! Besides, we're all guildmates here, right? We've bled together in dark caves, shared cold rations under rainstorms, so why get flustered over a little bare skin?"

"That's not the point, you hopeless airhead!" Karyl snapped, her cat ears flattening back against her skull. She pressed the back of her hand against her forehead, taking a shaky, ragged breath. Her skin was noticeably hot to the touch, and her chest heaved beneath her tight dark leather corset as she tried to calm her racing pulse. "There's... there's something weird about the steam in this lodge. My head feels light, my skin is tingling, and my body feels... strange and heavy. It's like my magic is melting inside me."

I leaned against the doorframe, watching them both. Karyl was right. The sweet, heavy scent of the steeping Moonpetal Sap hung thick in the warm air of the dining hall, intensifying with every minute that passed. Pecorine's boundless physical energy was transforming into a restless, radiant heat, while Karyl's defensive prickliness was visibly crumbling into flustered, trembling vulnerability. The air was charged with a palpable tension, a sweet tension that filled the quiet spaces between their words.

As I looked around the room, I noticed one member of our party was missing from the table.

Outside the sliding shoji screen that opened onto the rear veranda, a soft mountain breeze rustled the tall bamboo stalks in the moonlit courtyard garden. Through the translucent paper panels, I caught the delicate silhouette of Kokkoro standing near the stone water basin. Her pale white hair shone like spun silver under the full moon, and her long elf ears picked up the subtle whisper of the night wind. She held her ceremonial green staff loosely in one hand, her petite frame wrapped in her sleeveless priestess garment. She seemed to be breathing deeply, her small shoulders rising and falling rhythmically as she tried to master the sudden heat surging through her veins.

The entire lodge seemed to hold its breath around us. The sap's influence was growing stronger by the moment, weaving a heavy, electric heat between all four of us, simmering beneath the fragrant cedar steam.

As I stepped closer to the veranda, Pecorine looked up from her platter, her blue eyes dark with a sudden, breathless hunger. Her breath came in shallow rasps, her chest rising and falling beneath her loosened gown. Meanwhile, Karyl let out a tiny, stifled whimpering moan as she took another gulp of wine, her tail lashing the air while her fingers clutched the edge of her skirt. Outside, Kokkoro turned slightly, her pink eyes meeting mine through the open screen with an expression of quiet, desperate longing that pierced right through me.

The warm timber of the lodge, the rhythmic sound of bubbling hot springs, and the intoxicating perfume of the Moonpetal Sap filled every alcove of the building. In the dining hall, the amber oil lanterns flickered against the damp cypress beams, casting long, swaying shadows across the table where Pecorine and Karyl sat flushed and panting. Out on the garden veranda, Kokkoro waited under the silver light of the moon, the cool mountain mist curling around her bare ankles.

Every breath brought the fragrant herbal vapor deeper into my bloodstream. My pulse beat heavily in my ears, and the hot mineral waters churned softly through the bamboo flutes outside the shoji doors.
