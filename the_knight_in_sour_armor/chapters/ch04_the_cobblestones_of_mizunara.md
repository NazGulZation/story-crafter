# Chapter 4: The Cobblestones of Mizunara

The autumn rain had dwindled to a fine, needlesharp mist that drifted between the steep slate roofs of Mizunara Keep, slicking the granite cobblestones of the Mid-Tier Guild Row with a skin of black ice. The air smelled of wet horse hair, smoking bituminous coal from the armorer forges, and the greasy, savory vapor of skewered river eels sizzling over charcoal braziers in the merchant arcade.

Kazuki walked with his hood drawn low over his brow, his hands buried deep in the pockets of a dark wool traveler's cloak the Doctor had provided. Every step felt startlingly solid. For three years, his gait had been a lurching, painful rhythm of dragging his right heel and leaning hard to the left to spare his torn ligaments. Now, his weight transferred smoothly from heel to toe, the cobblestones meeting his boots with a firm, painless rebound that made his chest feel strangely light.

Beside him, the Doctor strode through the crowd with long, impatient paces, his hands shoved into the pockets of his dark velvet trousers, his red silk lining flashing with every swing of his coat. He wore a heavy black wool scarf wrapped carelessly around his throat and a pair of dark, rectangular spectacles perched on the bridge of his hawkish nose.

"Fascinating ecosystem," the Doctor murmured in his dry, rolling northern cadence, his head darting left and right as he observed the bustling street. "Feudal mercantilism disguised as martial heroism. Look at the architectural hierarchy. Granite ramparts for the military elite, limestone colonnades for the financiers, and pine timber rotting in the ditch for the laborers. It’s thoroughly Dickensian, with fewer chimney sweeps and considerably more leather straps."

"Dickensian?" Kazuki asked, glancing sideways at him, the foreign syllables sounding like complete gibberish to his frontier ears.

"A very clever man who wrote about miserable people in miserable towns," the Doctor said dismissively. "You’d have liked him. He had a great deal to say about self-righteous hypocrites who pretend to run orphanages."

A crowd had gathered before the grand portico of the Adventurer Guildhall. The building was an imposing three-story edifice of dressed limestone, its arched doorway flanked by two massive bronze braziers spitting scented pine resin into the damp morning air. Above the entrance hung an enormous painted silk banner, newly unfurled and dripping with rainwater.

Kazuki stopped, his boots freezing against the wet flagstones.

The banner depicted a tall, golden-haired swordsman in polished silver plate mail, his rapier raised high toward an embroidered sunburst. His teeth were painted a dazzling porcelain white; his cape billowed in artistic ripples. Below the likeness, stamped in bold vermilion ink, ran the proclamation:

> **THE GOLDEN DAWN ASCENDANT**
> *By Decree of the High Sovereign Inquest, the party of Takumi of House Kaneshiro is elevated to Gold Rank. Master of the Star-Silver Blade, accompanied by the Pure Grace of Lady Saki, Maiden of the White Dawn, the Crimson Comet Reiko, and the Shadow-Fang Kaede. Subjugators of the Red Ridge Faults.*

A guild crier in a mustard-yellow livery stood upon a stone mounting block, ringing a brass bell and shouting to the gathered novices and apprentice smiths.

"Witness the triumph of the frontier!" the crier bellowed, his voice echoing across the square. "Gold Rank conferred within five short seasons! Takumi of the Gilded Flash has proven that pedigree and divine talent overcome all dark subterranean perils! New contracts open for the Royal Iron Mine expedition! Fifty silver royals bounty for qualified apprentices to join the supply vanguard!"

Kazuki stared at the painted face of Takumi. Then his gaze drifted to the right corner of the banner, where Saki's likeness had been rendered in delicate, flowing brushstrokes. She wore a high-collared mantle of pristine white silk embroidered with silver lilies, her hands clasped around an ornate platinum holy symbol. 

There was no mention of a five-man party. There was no mention of an anvil. There was only the neat, four-member formation of the triumphant elite.

"Look at that," a raspy voice muttered from the edge of the crowd.

Kazuki turned. An old man stood beside a handcart loaded with dented iron greaves and notched billhooks. His left arm ended in a stump wrapped in grease-stained linen, and his back was curved like an aged pine tree. It was Master Gorou, the lower-district armorer who had patched Kazuki's ash-wood tower shield twice every season for four years.

Gorou spat into the gutter, wiping his mouth with the back of a calloused hand.

"They threw fifty silvers to the guild clerks for that banner," the old armorer grumbled to nobody in particular, nodding toward the silk. "And eighty silver royals to the temple for the maiden's silk mantle. But when the boy who carried them on his back split his kneecap in the deep vaults, they didn't even pay for a pine box. Just left him behind the latrines to rot. That’s the guild for you. They sell your blood by the pint, and when you’re dry, they charge your ghost for the bucket."

Kazuki felt a tightening in his throat. He took a half-step toward the cart, the habit of greeting the old smith pulling at his boots.

A firm, iron-hard grip seized his elbow.

The Doctor was standing beside him, his dark spectacles reflecting the flickering pine fire of the guild braziers. He didn't look at Kazuki; his face was turned toward the limestone steps of the guildhall, where armed bailiffs in lacquered leather kept the commoners back with ash-wood pikes.

"Don't," the Doctor said quietly. His rolling northern voice was low, steady, and devoid of mockery. "Not yet. If you step forward now, you’re just a ghost making a scene in the rain. Ghosts get arrested for vagrancy. We aren't here to make a scene. We’re here to take measurements."

The Doctor slipped his right hand beneath the cuff of his velvet coat. A tiny, high-pitched hum—so quiet it was swallowed by the ringing of the crier's bell—vibrated through the air. The tip of his sonic screwdriver glowed with a faint, pulsing emerald line, pointing directly at the massive iron registry board mounted beside the guildhall doors.

"What are you doing?" Kazuki whispered.

"Reading the ledger," the Doctor replied, his dark spectacles sliding down his nose as his pale eyes darted across the invisible data stream reflected in the lenses. "Fascinating. The entire ranking system is an algorithmic confidence trick. The guild charges a thirty percent surcharge on all defensive equipment sold to novices, but caps the bounty payouts on defensive contracts at twelve percent. That means every frontline fighter in this city is running a structural deficit from the moment they sign the register."

The Doctor clicked the device off and slid it back into his pocket, his mouth twisting into a tight, severe grimace. As they stepped away from the portico balustrade, his long, bony fingers casually swept across the public registry clerk's writing lectern, scooping up three blank, heavy petition folios stamped with the three-crowned seal of the Sovereign High Inquest and slipping them effortlessly into his velvet coat pocket alongside a printed tract of the Frontier Charter.

"They force you into debt for your armor," the Doctor continued, turning Kazuki around and steering him away from the square with a firm hand on his shoulder. "Then they offer high-risk contracts to pay off the debt, which damages the armor, which requires more repairs, which creates more debt. It’s an extractive cycle. A hamster wheel powered by gangrene and broken collarbones."

"And Takumi?" Kazuki asked as they navigated the crowded market lane.

"Takumi didn't pay for his armor," the Doctor said coldly. "His father is a high magistrate in the capital. The guild writes off his maintenance costs as 'promotional overhead' because having a nobleman's son on the registry attracts three hundred fresh farm boys a month, all eager to die in a hole so the guild masters can buy more imported plum wine."

They turned into a narrow alley lined with drying fish and salted kelp. The noise of the crier's bell faded behind the high stone walls. A chill gust of wind swept down the passage, carrying the smell of woodsmoke and damp cedar.

On the corner of the alley, an elderly woman in a patched hemp coat was tending a small iron drum filled with glowing charcoal. A basket of roasted chestnuts sat beside the coals, their brown shells split open to reveal the steaming, sweet yellow meat inside.

The Doctor stopped in front of the cart.

"How much for the chestnuts?" he asked, his strange, rolling vowels drawing a startled, squinting look from the vendor.

"Two coppers for a paper cone, sir," the woman rasped, holding out a weathered palm.

The Doctor fished in his coat pocket and pulled out a heavy, circular copper token stamped with the guild's official seal—another souvenir he had lifted from the clerk's railing two minutes earlier without breaking stride. He dropped it into her palm.

"Keep the change," the Doctor said. "Give me the ones that aren't burnt."

The woman's eyes widened at the guild stamp. She hurriedly scooped a double portion of roasted chestnuts into a cone of coarse brown paper and handed it over with a bow.

The Doctor took the paper cone, plucked out a steaming chestnut, cracked the charred shell between his thumb and forefinger, and popped the hot meat into his mouth. He chewed thoughtfully, nodding once.

"Not bad," the Doctor muttered. "A bit heavy on the woodsmoke, but acceptable."

He tossed the warm paper cone into Kazuki's chest. Kazuki caught it instinctively, the heat of the roasted chestnuts soaking through the wool of his mittens.

"Eat," the Doctor ordered, walking forward down the cobbled alley toward the secluded courtyard where the blue box stood invisible beneath a perception field. "You've been starving yourself on sour gruel for five years. Your body needs carbohydrates, protein, and glucose. Especially if you intend to hold a shield again."

Kazuki looked down at the paper cone. The steam rose into the freezing drizzle, carrying the rich, nutty fragrance of roasted chestnuts. He peeled one, feeling the heat seep into his fingers, and bit into the soft, sweet meat.

"Doctor," Kazuki said, chewing slowly.

"What?"

"If the whole system is built on lies... why do people still join?"

The Doctor didn't stop. His velvet coat tails swept against the grey stone walls as he walked with his long, measured stride.

"Because they want to believe the universe has a door marked 'Hero'," the Doctor's voice floated back through the alley. "And they don't realize until they're through it that the door only opens one way, and the floor is made of iron teeth."
