# Chapter 14: The Beggars in the Rain

The cold hit them first—not as a chill in the air, but as an invasive, liquid agony that seeped through threadbare burlap and soaked into the marrow of their bones.

Takumi opened his eyes to a world of grey water and suffocating filth. His mouth was filled with gritty black silt that tasted of rotten cabbage, stale beer, and the rancid mutton tallow greasing the axles of freight wagons parked twenty paces away. Freezing autumn sleet drummed against the back of his skull, plastering his greasy, matted hair across his eyes.

He tried to push himself upright with his left hand, but a spike of blinding agony shot through his fractured shoulder, forcing a ragged shriek from his throat that choked on sewage water.

He was in the ditch.

The lower drainage ditch of Mizunara Keep.

Wedged between the exact two rotted hemlock posts where Kazuki had lain dying two weeks before, half-submerged in the overflow of the butcher stalls and the municipal privy trench.

"No," Takumi whimpered, coughing up black sludge, clawing at the mud with his right hand. "No, no, no... this is a nightmare... an illusion... I am Lord Takumi of House Kaneshiro... I am Gold Rank..."

Beside him in the slurry, Lady Reiko was thrashing like a drowned animal. The wet burlap wrapping her shoulders was plastered with mud, and her blistered cheek burned with fiery agony as the dirty water seeped into the raw flesh left by her backfired pyromancy. She was sobbing into the silt, a muffled, hysterical wail that died in the roar of the downpour.

Five paces down the trench, Kaede dragged herself across a rotted timber beam, clutching her splinted right forearm. The cowhide ties had broken, leaving her fractured radius and ulna grinding together beneath her bruised purple skin with every movement. Her teeth chattered so violently they clicked like dice in a leather cup.

And beside the hemlock post sat Saki.

She was staring down at her lap. Her white silk mantle—the proud, shining garment she had worn to the High Inquest—was gone, trampled into the dirt by the bailiffs' boots. In its place was a coarse, grease-stained grey hemp smock, torn at the elbows, soaked with ditch water, and reeking of horse dung. 

Around her neck, where the sacred platinum sunburst had hung for three years, there was only the cold touch of the freezing rain.

"The temple..." Saki whispered, her voice hollow, cracking into a dry croak. She lifted her dirt-caked fingers to her bare collarbone, feeling only the rough weave of the excommunicated penitent's tunic. "My ordination... the Deacon... the white lilies... they threw it in the gutter..."

Above their heads, twenty feet up the stone retaining wall, the stained-glass lanterns of the Gilded Tankard tavern glowed like warm, molten amber through the sleet.

The heavy thud of an oak keg of spiced cherry wine echoed through the closed shutters, followed by a bright burst of laughter and the cheerful clinking of pewter mugs.

Takumi’s eyes flared with manic, feverish hope.

"The tavern!" Takumi scrambled onto his knees in the mud, dragging his broken shoulder, spitting sewage from his lips. "The Gilded Tankard! Master Kroll! Kroll knows me! I paid him sixty silver royals for the private booth! My uncle gave him the municipal beer concession! He will give us blankets! He will give us wine!"

He grabbed the hem of Saki’s hemp smock with his good hand, hauling her violently through the sludge.

"Get up, you idiot!" Takumi snarled, his voice cracking with hysterical panic. "Get up! We go to the door! We tell him the Inquest made an error! We tell him House Kaneshiro will double his concession!"

Saki stumbled through the muck, her bare feet slipping on the slick stones of the drainage trench. Reiko and Kaede crawled behind them like broken insects, their fingernails clawing at the muddy embankment until they reached the stone stairs leading up to the tavern's delivery door.

Takumi reached the heavy oak door first.

He didn't knock. He threw his entire body against the timber, hammering on the iron-studded oak with his right fist, screaming until the veins stood out like cords against his neck.

"Open the door!" Takumi shrieked. "Open the door, you peasant dogs! It’s me! Takumi! Let me in!"

Inside the tavern, the singing paused.

Heavy, deliberate footsteps approached the door. A sliding iron shutter at eye level groaned open with a rusty scrape.

A broad, bearded face appeared behind the iron grate—Master Kroll, the veteran tavern keeper whose bald head glistened with sweat from the kitchen fires. In his beefy hand, he held a heavy iron-banded ash-wood cudgel.

Takumi lunged toward the grate, pressing his soot-blackened face against the iron bars.

"Kroll!" Takumi cried out, tears of wild relief washing clean streaks through the filth on his cheeks. "Kroll, thank the gods! Open the door! The Inquest was a conspiracy! An old warlock and a rogue auditor framed me! Bring hot wine! Bring a physician for my shoulder! I will pay you fifty gold sovereigns the moment my uncle—"

"Shut your lying mouth," Master Kroll growled.

His gruff, gravelly voice was cold as slate, devoid of the fawning deference he had shown two weeks before. His small, piggish eyes narrowed in profound disgust as he looked at the shivering wretch in rags.

"Kroll?" Takumi stammered, blinking through the rain. "What... what are you saying? You know me! I am your best patron!"

"I know who you are, Takumi," Kroll spat, his lip curling into a vicious sneer. "The entire city knows who you are. The Sovereign Auditor chiseled your name off the Gold monument two hours ago! The whole plaza watched the blacksmith smash your pretty rapier on the anvil and melt it into coins to pay the poor lads you cheated! Your uncle is locked in the citadel dungeons, your family's estates are seized for sovereign debt liens, and you owe this house thirty silver royals on your private tab!"

"No!" Takumi wept, clawing at the bars. "My family will pay! I swear to you—"

"Your family doesn't have a copper pot to piss in, boy," Kroll barked. "You're an Unbonded Vagrant. A stripped thief. A coward who threw his own vanguard in my drainage ditch and tried to murder him in a quarry to cover your embezzling. If the City Watch catches me harboring a foreclosed debtor on my premises, they'll pull my tavern license before sundown."

Saki pushed herself past Takumi, throwing her hands against the iron grate, her voice shrill, frantic, and cracked with desperation.

"Master Kroll, please!" she begged, tears streaming down her face. "I am Saki! You know me! I gave the blessing over your daughter's cradle! I gave you holy water from the shrine! Just a bowl of broth... just a corner by the hearth... I am freezing to death!"

Kroll looked down at her, his eyes hard and merciless.

"The Deacon stripped your white silk and threw it in the manure pile, girl," Kroll said flatly. "You're an excommunicated penance-beggar. You used the holy prayers of the Dawn to help that peacock steal hazard wages from starving apprentices. My daughter would spit on you if she saw you. Get off my steps."

"Wait!" Takumi screamed, throwing his shoulder against the door. "Please! Just one flagon! Just a dry blanket!"

"I'll give you what you gave your vanguard," Kroll said.

The tavern keeper reached down beside the doorframe, hoisted a heavy wooden wash-tub filled with greasy, gray dishwater, cold cabbage scraps, and rancid mutton grease, and hurled the entire contents through the iron grate.

*SPLASH.*

The foul, lukewarm slop hit Takumi and Saki full in the face, knocking them backward down the slippery stone steps.

The heavy iron shutter slammed shut with a concussive bang, followed by the heavy rattle of the oak crossbar dropping into place. Inside, a roar of mocking laughter rose from the carters, followed by the bright, jaunty strumming of the tavern lute.

Takumi lay on his back in the mud at the base of the stairs, spluttering dishwater and cabbage leaves, his eyes staring blankly up at the freezing rain.

Beside him, Lady Reiko let out a shrill, hysterical screech, clawing at her face, spitting mutton grease from her mouth. "You did this, Takumi!" she shrieked, her voice cracking with pure, concentrated venom. "You and your pathetic vanity! You said the vanguard was disposable! You said the Inquest would never look at the books! You dragged me into the dirt, you filthy, cowardly bastard!"

"Shut up!" Takumi screamed back, sobbing in the mud. "You wanted the gold royals just as much as I did! You wanted the silk robes from the capital! You were the one who demanded sixty fire-scrolls for your stupid grimoire!"

"You shoved me toward the beast!" Saki whispered, her voice trembling with cold, venomous hatred. She sat in the mud, staring at Takumi with eyes full of murder. "In the Iron Ravine... you pushed me into the dirt so you could crawl away... you coward... you miserable, rotten coward..."

"I should have left you in Tanizaki!" Takumi spat, coughing up ditch water. "You were always a dirty peasant! You brought the curse on us! You and your stupid handwriting!"

They lay in the mud, screaming and cursing each other, their voices rising in a shrill, pathetic chorus of mutual loathing that was swallowed by the howling wind.

Then, from the head of the alleyway, came the sound of footsteps.

It was not the panicked slosh of fleeing beggars, nor the heavy clatter of armored bailiffs.

It was a slow, steady, rhythmic stride. The crisp crunch of whole leather soles stepping over wet gravel.

The shouting stopped.

Saki turned her head in the mud, wiping the grease from her eyes with the tattered sleeve of her burlap smock.

A figure was walking down the alley.

It was Kazuki.

He wore his dark traveler’s tunic, clean and dry beneath a heavy wool cloak that turned aside the freezing sleet. Strapped across his shoulders was a well-oiled leather haversack; across his left forearm rested the matte-grey kite shield of meteoritic iron, its dark, unadorned rim catching the faint amber light of the tavern lanterns.

His boots were whole, stitched with stout waxed thread, stepping firmly upon the stone curb. His center of gravity was low, fluid, and unbroken. His right knee moved with effortless, silent grace, bearing his weight without a trace of the old, grinding hitch.

He carried no anger on his face. There was no triumphant smirk on his lips. There was only a calm, quiet stillness—the serene clarity of a man who had walked out of the grave and left its dust behind him.

Takumi froze, his eyes widening in superstitious terror. He pulled his good arm over his chest, trying to press himself into the slime of the ditch to hide from the man he had tried to slaughter.

Beside him, Reiko and Kaede shrank against the hemlock posts, their heads bowed, shivering in silent, humiliated dread.

And Saki...

Saki stared at him. She looked at his clean, unscarred hands. She looked at his steady, peaceful gaze. She looked at the boy who had carried her across the frozen rivers of their childhood, whole, unbroken, and completely beyond her reach.

A strangled cry broke from her throat.

She threw herself forward onto her hands and knees, crawling through the black sewage, dragging her wet burlap tunic through the silt until she reached the edge of the stone curb where his boots rested.

"Kazuki..." Saki wept, tears pouring down her cheeks, washing through the grease and dirt on her chin. She reached out with trembling, filthy fingers, her nails black with ditch grime, hovering an inch from the clean leather of his boots. "Kazuki, look at me... please, look at what they did to me..."

Kazuki stopped. He did not pull away in disgust. He did not raise his shield. He simply stood looking down at her, his dark eyes deep and clear as a winter pond.

"They took everything, Kazuki," Saki sobbed, her voice breaking into a ragged, desperate plea that tore at the cold air. "The temple... the robes... the people look at me like I'm a monster! I have no food! I have no hearth! I am sleeping in the mud, Kazuki! In the cold mud!"

She looked up at his face, her hands trembling as she tried to grasp the hem of his dark traveler's cloak.

"You were our vanguard," Saki choked out, her words tumbling over each other in frantic, terrified supplication. "You always protected me. You took the claws for me. You swore you would never let me starve in Tanizaki. Please, Kazuki... you have money now... you have the shield... take me with you! I’ll carry your pack! I’ll wash your clothes! I’ll sleep in the dirt behind your tent! Just don't leave me here... please, Kazuki... don't leave me with them!"

Beside the ditch, Takumi raised his tear-stained face, his lip trembling, mumbling a broken, incoherent plea: "Kazuki... tell the guild... tell them we were comrades..."

Kazuki looked at Takumi. He looked at Reiko and Kaede.

And then he looked down at Saki, kneeling in the black slurry at his feet.

He remembered the bitter cold of this exact ditch. He remembered the necrotic wyvern venom burning through his carotid artery. He remembered listening to their laughter leaking through the tavern floorboards while he drowned in his own blood.

He remembered the silver-haired traveler who had stepped into the sewage, ruined the leather of his fine shoes, and told him that a human being was an articulated marvel that didn't belong in the mud.

Kazuki took half a step backward, smoothly sliding his cloak beyond Saki’s reaching fingers.

"No, Saki," Kazuki said softly.

His voice carried no malice. It carried no vengeance. It was quiet, gentle, and absolute as the falling snow.

"You wanted to be noble," Kazuki said, looking down at the girl in the burlap smock. "You wanted to live without the smell of the mud. But nobility isn't white silk, Saki. It isn't a silver chain from a temple. Nobility is what you do when someone is lying at your feet."

He shifted the meteor-iron shield on his arm, the cold metal dark and steady against his side.

"I stood in front of you for five years," Kazuki continued. "I gave you my bread. I gave you my blood. I broke my bones so you could sleep warm. And when my body was spent, you kicked me into this ditch and bought cherry wine with the copper from my wounds."

"I was afraid!" Saki wailed, her hands clutching at the wet cobblestones. "I was terrified of being poor!"

"Fear is a choice, Saki," Kazuki replied. "Kindness is a choice. You made yours. Now the kingdom has given you your balance."

He looked past her, toward the northern gate of the city where the great road wound away into the misty alpine valleys.

"Kazuki..." Saki wept, her forehead touching the cold, wet gravel. "Please... what about the debt? What about Tanizaki?"

Kazuki looked at her one last time.

"The debt is paid, Saki," Kazuki said softly. "Every copper. Every drop of blood. Settled in full."

He turned his boots toward the road.

He did not look back. He did not quicken his pace. With calm, unhurried strides, Kazuki walked down the delivery alley, stepping cleanly over the muddy puddles, and passed out beneath the iron archway of the lower gate.

Behind him in the ditch, Saki collapsed into the black silt, burying her face in her bare arms, weeping aloud into the freezing downpour.

Beside her, Takumi cursed through his tears, Reiko whimpered in the dark, and Kaede stared at the empty alley with dead, hopeless eyes.

Four broken, stripped parasites, trapped together in the ditch where they had cast their savior, listening to the laughter of the tavern above, with nothing left in the world but their own bitter, enduring hatred.

The autumn rain washed over them, cold, gray, and completely indifferent.
