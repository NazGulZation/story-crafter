# Chapter 12: The Arithmetic of Treason

The wind howled through the granite cuts of Karasuma, driving a bitter spray of freezing mist across the stone terraces.

Twelve Guild Bailiffs stood in an iron crescent, their ash-wood halberds leveled, their heavy steel arbalests cocked and aimed directly at the four figures trembling in the gravel. The iron-bound folio of the Sovereign Board of Auditors rested upon Chief Auditor Ren’s forearm, its thick calfskin pages open to the master registers of the Eastern Marches.

The Doctor stood upon the wet slate in his dark velvet coat, his silver hair ruffled by the mountain gale. He turned to Kazuki, pulled a narrow, leather-bound ledger from his waistcoat pocket, and placed it directly into the young bulwark’s right hand.

"Read them the balance, Kazuki," the Doctor murmured in his sharp, rolling cadence, his pale eyes steady and encouraging. "It was your blood they spent. Speak the arithmetic."

Kazuki looked down at the ledger, then stepped forward across the loose stone.

His boots crunched with heavy, deliberate rhythm. The dark meteor-iron kite shield hung at his side, solid and cold. He stopped three paces from Kaede, who was huddled in the red mud, clutching her fractured forearm against her ribs.

"Arithmetic, Kaede," Kazuki said.

His voice was calm, clear, and devoid of hatred. It carried the quiet, undeniable weight of an iron anchor dropped into deep water.

"That is what you told me in the tavern," Kazuki continued, opening the ledger and looking down at the ink-written tallies. "You told the guild clerks that four shares yielded a twenty-five percent higher dividend than five. Let us look at your accounting."

Kaede sat frozen in the mud, her splintered arm trembling violently, her knuckles white. She stared at Kazuki’s boots—whole, oiled leather without a single rip—and her throat tightened into a dry knot.

"Sixty-two subterranean sorties over five continuous years," Kazuki read aloud, his words ringing against the granite cliffs. "Total gross bounty disbursed by the Crown to our party: four thousand, two hundred gold royals. Total expenditure on offensive fire-scrolls, star-silver blade polish, and silk cloaks for the duelist: two thousand, eight hundred royals. Total compensation paid to the vanguard who absorbed eighty-seven concussive trauma impacts, twelve acid burns, and four venomous punctures: three hundred royals."

Kazuki turned the page, looking directly into Kaede’s terrified, darting eyes.

"Minus two hundred and forty royals in mandatory guild maintenance fees deducted directly from my share. A net profit of sixty gold royals for five years of pulverized cartilage and broken bones."

Kaede flinched as though struck across the cheek, her forehead sinking toward the wet slate.

"Do you remember the Black Fen, Kaede?" Kazuki asked, his voice steady as iron. "When the river leeches tore through the boat timber? I held the stern for three hours with water up to my chin so you could carry the chest of star-ore across the marsh. When we reached the dry bank, my boots were full of blood. You sat on the dry turf, dipped your goose quill, and deducted twelve silver pennies from my share because the pack-straps had gotten damp. You told me it was 'standard municipal depreciation.' You weren't managing a party, Kaede. You were running a slaughterhouse on credit."

"I... I was following the guild guidelines..." Kaede choked out, pulling her knees tight to her chest.

"You were stealing from the man who kept you breathing," Kazuki said. "You called it liquidation. You saw an asset with rising medical overhead, and you calculated that dumping me in a sewage ditch behind the tavern was good business. But you forgot the load-bearing geometry, Kaede. When you went into the Iron Ravine without a shield, two burrowers broke Takumi's sword, roasted Reiko's face, and shattered your arm in less than four minutes. Your profit was gone. Your capital was destroyed. And you ended up in a back-alley cellar buying illegal crossbows from deserters to murder me."

Kaede buried her face in her splinted arm, weeping silently in the clay.

Kazuki turned his boots, walking five paces across the terrace until he stood before Lady Reiko.

The noblewoman was curled against the rock face, her fine silk robes soaked in muddy runoff, her charred grimoire lying abandoned in the puddle at her feet. She held her blistered hands to her chest, her single exposed eye rolling in panic.

"Lady Reiko," Kazuki said.

"Stay back..." Reiko whimpered, pressing her spine against the granite. "You have no right... a peasant from the lower valley cannot speak to the bloodline of the Northern Spire..."

"Bloodline did not hold the line in the Iron Ravine, Lady Reiko," Kazuki replied, his voice firm and unwavering. "Do you remember the Wyvern's Bluff? When the matriarch swooped down with burning pitch in her gullet? I knelt before you in the heather, taking the white flame on my oak shield until the leather cooked to charcoal and the flesh of my forearms peeled away in strips. You stood behind me, adjusting the pearls in your hair, complaining that the smoke was irritating your eyes. You told the guild scribes that your grand incandescent stanza had banished the beast, while I lay in the infirmary packing my burns with lard and salt. You convinced yourself that your family's crest was a shield. But when the beasts came in the Iron Ravine, your crest didn't stop a single claw, did it?"

Reiko let out a choking wail, burying her face in her soot-stained sleeves, weeping in utter, broken humiliation.

Kazuki closed the ledger and turned toward Takumi.

The gilded duelist was kneeling in the mud, his fine breastplate dented and blackened, his dislocated right shoulder hanging limp against his side. He glared up through greasy strands of golden hair with eyes full of poisonous, impotent spite.

"Get him up," Kazuki said to the bailiffs.

Two guardsmen seized Takumi by his armpits, hauling him upright. Takumi screamed in agony as his shoulder wrenched, his boots dragging through the gravel until he stood swaying before Kazuki and Chief Auditor Ren.

"Look at your boots, Takumi," Kazuki said, pointing down at the fine gilded calfskin boots now caked in grey muck. "Three hundred silver royals for imported Cordovan leather, paid out of the sovereign vanguard fund. You told the novice guild recruits that you were the blade of the dawn. You bragged in every tavern that you fought alone at the Red Ridge. But at the Red Ridge, Takumi, you were hiding behind a wagon axle with your eyes squeezed shut while I took twelve spear-thrusts to my ribs. When the battle was won, you came out, drew your rapier, and posed for the guild sketch-artist while the bailiffs carried me out on a blood-soaked canvas litter."

"Kazuki!" Takumi spat, coughing up blood and grit. "You traitorous peasant dog! You think a stolen ledger can touch House Kaneshiro? My uncle sits on the Governor's Council! He will have you hanged from the citadel ramparts! Chief Auditor Ren, arrest this vagrant! He is extorting my family's treasury!"

Chief Auditor Ren stepped forward, his measuring staff striking the slate with a sharp crack.

"Your uncle, Lord Kaneshiro, was arrested at sunrise this morning," Auditor Ren stated with chilling, bureaucratic finality. "Following an unannounced midnight inspection of the Western Vaults. Three thousand sovereigns in fraudulent death-indemnity vouchers were discovered in his private strongbox—all bearing your signature, Takumi."

Takumi’s jaw dropped slack. The color completely drained from his face, leaving his skin the color of wet tallow.

"No..." Takumi whispered. "No... that's impossible..."

"Nothing is impossible to an auditor who understands basic double-entry bookkeeping," the Doctor said, stepping forward beside Kazuki with his hands resting casually in his waistcoat pockets.

The Doctor turned to Chief Auditor Ren, reaching into his velvet coat to produce two small glass vials and a folded square of parchment.

"Exhibit B," the Doctor announced, his rolling northern voice cutting through the damp chill like a razor. "The chemical evidence. Recovered from the ditch behind the Gilded Tankard: wyvern bile residue matching the necrotic toxins in Kazuki’s neck. And recovered from the shattered obsidian blade on this terrace: concentrated basilisk venom, purchased under a forged sovereign pest-control license from the back room of the Crimson Flask."

Auditor Ren took the vials, uncorking one and holding it to his nose. His severe brow furrowed in dark disgust. "Article Forty-Two of the Charter. Unlawful possession of sovereign military venoms. A capital offense."

The Doctor turned his pale, hawkish gaze upon Takumi. When the Time Lord spoke, his voice carried the terrifying, ancient weight of a creature that had judged kings and emperors across a thousand worlds.

"You stole his money, Takumi," the Doctor whispered, each word hitting the trembling duelist like an iron hammer. "You stole his victories. You strutted through the market square wearing a tin shell, telling the apprentices that highborn courage conquers all darkness. But when the beasts charged in the ravine... where was your highborn courage?"

Takumi stared into the Doctor's eyes, his chest hitching in breathless terror.

"You threw your sword in the dirt," the Doctor snarled softly. "You crawled on your belly behind a rotted ore cart like a worm. And when the girl who gave up her home for you screamed for help, you shoved her straight toward the jaws of the beast to buy yourself thirty seconds of crawling room."

A strangled sob tore from Takumi’s throat. He hung in the bailiffs' grip, his legs giving out beneath him.

"You are a parasite, Takumi," the Doctor said with icy finality. "A hollow, fragile, pathetic creature stuffed with stolen glory and borrowed courage. You are not a warrior. You are not a leader. You are an empty husk living on the blood of a man who had more dignity in his broken kneecap than your entire family has possessed in ten generations."

The Doctor stepped back, gesturing to Kazuki.

Kazuki walked over to where Saki knelt beside the rotted winch, her hands locked in heavy debtor-shackles, her body shaking with silent, convulsive tremors.

"Saki," Kazuki said softly.

Saki did not look up. Her forehead was pressed against the cold granite between her knees.

"You told me you hated me because I was a chain tying you to the mud," Kazuki murmured. "You thought if you wore white silk, you could pretend you were never hungry. But a shield isn't an ornament, Saki. And honor isn't silk. I took every blow because I believed in you. And you looked at my broken body and saw only an embarrassment."

"Kazuki..." Saki wept, her voice muffled against the wet stone. "Forgive me... I was terrified... I was so terrified of being poor..."

Kazuki looked down at her for a long, quiet moment.

"The boy who took your wounds died in the ditch behind the tavern," Kazuki said. "The man standing here owes you nothing, and asks for nothing."

Chief Auditor Ren stepped to the center of the terrace, unfurling a wide vellum scroll sealed with the imperial gold ribbon.

"Under the powers granted to the Sovereign Board of Auditors by Royal Decree: Takumi of House Kaneshiro, Lady Reiko of the Northern Spire, Scout Kaede of the Western Reach, and Saki of Tanizaki are hereby found guilty of Sovereign Treason, Premeditated Fratricide, and Commercial Bounty Embezzlement."

Auditor Ren looked at Kazuki, dipping his head in official respect.

"Vanguard Kazuki. As the petitioner of this Sovereign Lien, your claims are substantiated in full. The assets of the Golden Dawn are forfeit to the Crown for public restitution. The prisoners will be transported in iron chains to the High Inquest Plaza of Mizunara Keep for public degradation and foreclosure at the noon watch."

"No!" Takumi shrieked, thrashing against his shackles as the bailiffs dragged him backward. "Not the plaza! My name! What about my name!"

Kazuki looked at the weeping duelist, his hands resting steady on his kite shield.

"Your name," Kazuki said, "was paid for with my blood. Now the kingdom takes it back."

The bailiffs marched the four prisoners down the rocky defile in heavy iron chains, their wailing echoing off the granite cliffs until the mist swallowed them whole.

Chief Auditor Ren walked over to Kazuki, his severe, weathered face softening with genuine professional respect. He laid a gloved hand upon the dark iron rim of the kite shield.

"Vanguard Kazuki," Auditor Ren said, his voice carrying the calm gravity of the law. "Under Article Nineteen of the Frontier Charter, the petitioner must stand beside the tribunal upon the high steps at noon. The Crown requires your physical presence to ratify the dissolution of the Golden Dawn and to formally direct the disbursement of their liquidated assets to the defrauded apprentices. Will you attend?"

Kazuki met the Chief Auditor's gaze with steady, unwavering resolve. "I filed the claim, Chief Auditor. I will see it settled to the last penny."

"Very good," Ren nodded, pulling his fur-lined cloak tight against the rising wind. "The bells will ring at the twelfth hour. Do not be late."

With a sharp wave of his staff, the Chief Auditor turned and followed the guardsmen down the canyon path, his boots ringing on the wet slate.

The quarry fell silent.

Kazuki stood on the terrace with the Doctor, the cold north wind blowing across their shoulders as the leaden autumn clouds began to break in the west, revealing long, pale ribbons of clean sky.

Kazuki handed the leather-bound ledger back to the Doctor, his hands steady and whole.

"For five years, Doctor," Kazuki said softly, looking out over the misty amphitheater, "I believed my duty was simply to endure. I believed that if I absorbed every claw, took every fracture, and never complained, that was what made a vanguard noble."

The Doctor tucked the notebook into his waistcoat, his silver hair catching the first ray of golden sunlight cresting the ridge, his attack eyebrows drawn into an expression of fierce, solemn clarity.

"Endurance is just biology, Kazuki," the Doctor replied in his dry, rolling northern voice. "Trees endure winter. Granite boulders endure rivers. Even a mule can endure being beaten with a stick. But agency? Justice? Demanding an honest ledger from a world that profits from your blood? That is an act of sovereign will. A shield is not an anvil waiting to be pulverized by cowards. A shield is a boundary. You drew the line, you filed the claim, and you held the cut. That wasn't a victim being rescued. That was a free man balancing his own books."

A faint, wry smile touched Kazuki's lips—dry, slightly sour, and utterly at peace.

"The bells ring at noon," Kazuki said, shifting the dark shield on his arm.

"Then let us go to the city," the Doctor said, pulling his velvet coat tight. "I have always found midday foreclosures remarkably invigorating."
