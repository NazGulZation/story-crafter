# Chapter 7: The Gaze Across the Square

The mid-day market of Mizunara Keep was a storm of noise, color, and commerce. Beneath canvas awnings dyed indigo and safflower, merchants haggled over barrels of salt herring, bolts of imported Kyoto silk, and bundles of fragrant camphor wood brought upriver on the morning barges. The air was rich with the scent of burning incense from the nearby temple precinct, mingling with the sharp aroma of roasted sesame oil and damp fleece.

Saki walked along the central flagstone arcade with her chin tilted high, her white silk mantle billowing gently behind her in the crisp autumn breeze. Her robes were trimmed with delicate silver thread that caught the pale sunlight, and around her throat hung the platinum sunburst pendant of a fully ordained Gold-Rank acolyte. 

Flanking her were two young shrine novices in coarse grey linen, carrying her cedar baskets loaded with imported rosewater, jars of purified mountain honey, and fine goose-feather quills.

"Is it true, Lady Saki?" one of the novices asked, her voice trembling with breathless admiration. "The guild herald said you channeled the divine light for four continuous hours during the Red Ridge campaign, single-handedly burning away the cavern miasma while Lord Takumi drove back thirty ogre chieftains!"

Saki touched the platinum pendant with manicured, lotion-softened fingers. A small, practiced smile parted her painted lips.

"The light flows through those who maintain absolute purity of purpose, little sister," Saki replied, her tone melodic and smooth as polished jade. "In the dark faults, one must banish all hesitation. Any weakness, any lingering attachment to the coarse mud of the lowlands, and the grace falters. That is why our party achieved Gold standing so swiftly. We tolerate no dead weight."

The young novices nodded eagerly, bowing their heads as though receiving sacred scripture.

Saki turned toward the stone apothecary arcade, intending to purchase a phial of refined almond essence for her evening bath. She paused near the octagonal fountain of white limestone that marked the center of the square.

Across the water, thirty paces away, a man was standing before an ironmonger's open stall.

He was examining a heavy leather cinch strap, testing the tension of the brass buckle between strong, steady fingers. He wore a dark, hooded traveler's cloak over a plain charcoal brigandine, and at his left hip hung a matte-grey kite shield forged of dark, unadorned metal.

Saki stopped. The breath seized in her throat, cold and sharp as broken glass.

Her fingers clamped around the platinum sunburst so tightly the metal edges dug into her palm.

The man turned his head to speak to the blacksmith. The hood fell back slightly, catching the midday sun.

It was Kazuki.

There was no broken limp. He stood with his weight balanced squarely over both feet, his posture upright, broad-shouldered, and easy. There was no rattling cough in his throat, no blackened tracks of necrotic wyvern venom snaking up his neck, no hollow, purple shadows of exhaustion beneath his cheekbones. His jaw was cleanly shaved, his dark eyes clear, calm, and steady.

As though sensing the weight of her gaze, Kazuki turned his eyes across the splashing water of the fountain.

Their gazes locked.

Saki felt the world drop out from beneath her embroidered slippers. A cold, suffocating wave of nausea surged up from her stomach into her throat. In her nightmares over the past two weeks, she had imagined this moment a hundred times: Kazuki crawling back from the ditch, weeping in the mud, screaming her name, begging for a copper coin or a healing draught, demanding to know why she had abandoned him. She had rehearsed the cold words she would use to have the guild bailiffs drag him away to the debtor's pit.

None of that was in his eyes.

There was no fury. There was no hatred. There was no desperate, pleading sorrow of the boy who had stolen bread for her in the freezing winters of Tanizaki.

There was nothing.

Kazuki looked at her with the cool, indifferent appraisal of a traveler looking at a milestone by the road. His gaze slid past her white silk mantle, past her platinum sunburst, and past the breathless novices, regarding her as though she were a stranger whose name had never crossed his lips.

He picked up the cinch strap, laid two copper coins on the smith's anvil, and walked away into the crowd of the northern gate.

Saki’s hand jerked violently.

The cedar basket slipped from her fingers. Ceramic jars of perfumed rosewater and mountain honey struck the granite flagstones with a brittle crash, shattering into a dozen sharp shards and spilling amber syrup across the boots of the horrified novices.

"Lady Saki!" the novice cried out, dropping to her knees. "Your pardon, my lady! I will clean it—"

Saki didn't hear her. She gathered her white silk skirts in both hands, turned her back on the fountain, and ran through the market square toward the limestone gates of the Upper Bastion.

***

The private suite of the Golden Dawn in the Upper Bastion smelled of roasted pheasant, spiced plum wine, and sweet woodsmoke from a marble hearth. Gilded tapestries depicting behemoth hunts lined the walls, and the floor was covered in plush, dyed lambskin rugs.

Takumi was lounging in a high-backed walnut armchair, his boots propped carelessly on a velvet footstool. He was polishing the hilt of his star-silver rapier with a silk handkerchief, his handsome face relaxed into a complacent, easy smirk.

Beside the hearth, Lady Reiko was sipping amber wine from a fluted crystal glass, reading a leather-bound grimoire of high-tier pyromancy. In the corner by the arched balcony, Kaede sat cross-legged on a low chest, cleaning the venom channels of a set of bone throwing needles.

The heavy oak door flew open, slamming against the plaster wall with a violent thud.

Saki stumbled into the chamber, her chest heaving, her white mantle streaked with street dust and honey. Her face was the color of curdled milk.

"He's alive," she choked out, slamming the door shut and leaning her back against the timber, her knees trembling so badly she could barely stand.

Takumi didn't even look up from his rapier. He breathed on the silver pommel and rubbed it with the silk. "Who is alive, Saki? Calm yourself. You sound like a scullery maid who saw a mouse in the larder. Have some wine."

"Kazuki!" she screamed, the name tearing through the perfumed quiet of the room. "Kazuki is in the lower market! I saw him with my own eyes!"

Takumi’s hand paused on the silk. 

He slowly lowered the rapier, his pale eyes narrowing as a mocking sneer spread across his lips. "You’ve had too much temple incense, girl. Kazuki is three feet deep in ditch sludge behind the Gilded Tankard. His lungs were flooded with black bile, his knee was powdered bone, and his ribs were broken in three places. A stray dog couldn't have survived that downpour, let alone a broken vanguard."

"I am not imagining it!" Saki took three frantic steps into the room, gripping the back of Reiko’s chair with white-knuckled fury. "He wasn't limping! He was walking like a high dragoon! His face... his skin was completely clear! The venom was gone! He carries an uncrested black iron kite shield, and he was buying a leather cinch strap right in front of old Master Gorou's forge stall by the fountain! He looked straight through me!"

Reiko set her crystal wine glass down on the side table with a sharp, delicate click. Her aristocratic brow furrowed into an ugly, haughty line. "A dark shield? He had no coin left. We took his guild voucher and his armor deposit. If someone healed him, who paid the temple? A full restoration of necrotic tissue requires high-circle restoration draughts that cost fifty gold royals a vial."

"It doesn't matter who paid for it," Kaede’s voice cut through the room like a cold draft under a door.

The scout had stopped cleaning her throwing needles. She sat motionless on the chest, her dark eyes wide, calculating with frightening speed.

"What do you mean it doesn't matter?" Takumi snapped, swinging his boots off the footstool and standing up. "If the beggar survived, we simply have the town watch throw him outside the palisade for vagrancy."

"You fool," Kaede whispered, leaning forward. "Think for three seconds. What did we submit to the High Sovereign Inquest to claim our Gold Rank?"

Silence fell over the chamber, heavy and suffocating.

Takumi’s jaw tightened. "We submitted the Red Ridge Subjugation Report."

"And what did Section Four of that report state?" Kaede asked, her voice dropping to an icy hiss. "It stated that Vanguard Kazuki of Tanizaki fell heroically in the deep faults, crushed beneath an ogre rock-slide while holding the rear line so House Kaneshiro could secure the victory. We collected eighty gold royals in sovereign death indemnities from the crown. We claimed his equipment salvage share. We used his 'martyrdom' to bypass the two-year trial period required for provincial commoners to achieve Gold standing."

The color drained from Takumi’s cheeks, leaving his skin sickly and grey beneath his golden hair.

"If Kazuki walks up the steps of the guildhall," Kaede continued, pointing a bone needle at Takumi's chest, "and shows a whole body and an uncrushed skull, the entire report is exposed as perjury. Perjury before a Royal Inquest carries immediate stripping of guild rank, forfeiture of all personal estates, five years in the salt mines, and public branding on the right cheek."

"No," Saki gasped, pressing her hands to her mouth. "No! My temple ordination! The High Priestess... they will cast me out! They will take my white robes! I will be a beggar again!"

"Shut up!" Takumi roared, kicking the velvet footstool across the room. It struck the stone fireplace and bounced away with a thud. "Shut your mouth, Saki!"

He began pacing across the lambskin rug, running his fingers through his golden hair, his chest heaving with frantic, ugly breaths. The easy grace, the theatrical chivalry, the noble poise—all of it peeled away like wet paper, revealing the snarling, cornered rat beneath.

"We cannot let him speak," Reiko said coldly, her fingers curling around the leather spine of her grimoire. "If he speaks, we are ruined. My family in the Northern Spire would disown me before nightfall."

"He won't speak," Takumi said. His voice had dropped from panic into a dark, venomous gravel. He gripped the hilt of his star-silver rapier, his knuckles turning white. "He went straight to old Gorou's stall. That one-armed cripple was the only smith in the Lower Tier who ever gave the penniless dog credit on scrap iron. If the ditch-rat is sniffing around the city, Gorou's forge is where he shows his face. Kaede, watch the Lower Tier. We find where he sleeps, and then he’s going to have a proper accident. A real one this time. Far from the city walls, where no passing meddler can dig him out of the dirt."

He stopped pacing, his pale eyes fixing on Saki.

"You," Takumi said, pointing his blade at her chest. "You are going to write a letter."
