# Chapter 9: The Forged Dispatch

The cellar beneath the herbalist shop in the mid-district was cold, damp, and smelled of dried valerian root, rubbing alcohol, and festering burns.

A single tallow candle sputtered in a ceramic saucer on the rough-hewn pine table, casting long, erratic shadows across the four figures huddled in the gloom. Lady Reiko sat in the corner, her face wrapped in thick linen bandages soaked in witch-hazel, only her right eye and swollen lips visible through the gauze. Every breath made her whimper as the weeping blisters on her cheek pulled against the cloth. Beside her, Kaede cradled her splinted right forearm against her ribs, her dark eyes hollow, glazed with the fever of bone trauma.

Takumi stood over Saki, his left arm bound tightly to his chest with silk cords, his golden hair greasy and unwashed. The fine duelist’s poise had vanished, replaced by the twitching, feral agitation of a gambler whose last counterfeit coin had been refused at the table.

"Write it cleanly," Takumi hissed, his breath hot against the back of Saki’s neck. "No highborn phrasing. No temple blessings. Write it like an illiterate dirt farmer."

Saki sat on a three-legged stool, a sheet of coarse, rough-edged mulberry paper spread out on the timber before her. In her right hand, she held a chipped bamboo reed pen, dipping the tip into a dish of lampblack ink mixed with vinegar. 

Her fingers trembled so hard the ink dripped from the nib, leaving black splatters across the wood.

"I know how to write it," Saki whispered. Her voice was cracked, raw from the sulfur smoke of the Iron Ravine. "I know how he thinks. I know every word that pulls at his ribs."

She closed her eyes for three heartbeats, inhaling the sharp, bitter scent of the vinegar. She remembered the freezing winters in Tanizaki—the howling blizzards that blew through the chinks in the clay walls, the sound of her younger brother weeping from the gnawing pain of an empty belly, and Kazuki climbing through the snowdrift with two frozen turnips he had dug out of the landlord's frozen compost with his bare, bleeding fingernails.

*He never could walk away,* she thought, a cold, sickening knot tightening behind her breastbone. *He was born an anvil. Even when you beat him to scraps, he stands in the doorway.*

She set the pen to the rough paper. Her strokes were deliberately clumsy, curving the characters in the thick, unpracticed hand of Master Genzo, the elderly miller of Tanizaki who had taught them both their first phonetic syllables on the flat stones of the millrace.

> *Kazuki,*
> 
> *The autumn levy broke the valley. The bailiffs burned the lower mill. Twelve families fled over the pass with the harvest children, but the road is blocked by scavenger hounds. We are sheltering in the deep cut of the Karasuma Stone Quarries, five leagues north of the keep. The cart axle is broken. We have no vanguard. If you are alive, bring your iron. Hold the cut until dawn. For Tanizaki.*

Saki lifted the pen. A heavy drop of ink pooled on the word *Tanizaki*, soaking into the coarse vegetable fibers of the mulberry sheet.

"Good," Takumi grunted, snatching the wet paper from the table. He blew on the ink, his pale eyes burning with a desperate, manic fever. "Kaede, where are the siege bolts?"

Kaede pointed with her left hand toward a heavy hemp sack resting against the wall. "Three heavy mechanical arbalests, purchased from the deserters' broker in the lower ditch. Spring-loaded with tempered dwarven steel. They fire square-headed iron quarrels designed to crack city gates. At twenty paces, they punch through two inches of oak and shatter plate armor like dry glass. Even that heavy black iron slab he scavenged cannot disperse three simultaneous impacts at point-blank range. It has no guild stamps, no temple enchants—just a crude chunk of uncrested metal he probably pulled off a dead caravan guard in the muck."

"And the fire?" Reiko rasped through her bandages, her single exposed eye bloodshot and venomous.

"Six flasks of concentrated subterranean sulfur oil," Takumi said, tapping the iron cylinders resting in his satchel. "The moment the bolts bring him to his knees, we douse the gorge. No bones. No shield. Just charred basalt and grey ash."

Takumi reached down, sliding a slender, triangular blade from a sheath inside his left boot. The poignard was carved from black obsidian, its needle-sharp point gleaming with a greasy, milky-green film that gave off a faint scent of rotten copper.

"And if the fire leaves a breath in him," Takumi whispered, a sickening, triumphant grin twisting his pale lips, "this finishes the account. Concentrated basilisk venom. I used my family signet and filed an emergency pest-control permit under House Kaneshiro's name at the Lower Registry desk, then bought the vial from the smugglers in the back room of the Crimson Flask. One scratch dissolves the heart muscle in six heartbeats. The watch won't find a single puncture in the ashes."

He slid the poignard back into his boot and thrust the folded mulberry letter into Saki's trembling hands.

"Take off the white mantle," Takumi ordered. "Put on a beggar’s cloak. Find a street urchin in the lower market, give him two coppers, and tell him to deliver this to old Master Gorou's forge stall by the fountain. That one-armed cripple was the only smith in the city who ever gave Kazuki credit on scrap iron. Gorou will see that the ditch-rat gets it."

Saki took the letter. Her fingers shook against the rough vegetable paper, her chest tightening with an icy, suffocating dread.

"Takumi..." Saki whispered, her voice cracked and small. "His knee was powdered bone. The necrotic venom had blackened his veins. In the canonical teachings of the White Dawn, only a fifth-circle arch-prelate's divine benediction can purge rot that deep. It costs three hundred gold crowns and an imperial dispensation. What if... what if some high temple authority or royal inquisitor took him in?"

"Don't be a superstitious idiot," Takumi snarled, clutching his broken shoulder in its sling. "High prelates don't wash ditch-mud off provincial commoners. He had twelve copper pieces to his name."

"The tavern whispers in the Lower Tier tell a different story," Kaede murmured from the shadows, her dark eyes cold and analytical. "Two wool-merchants by the fountain saw him yesterday. He wasn't alone. An eccentric foreigner in an odd dark velvet coat was with him, buying dried chicory and persimmons at Master Kenzo's stall. Gray hair, sharp northern vowels that snap like pruning shears, tall and gaunt as a heron."

Takumi spat into the dirt, his face contorting with arrogant contempt. "A hedge-doctor. Some vagrant herbalist peddling dead-root distillates and pig-bile tinctures to gullible laborers. The ditch-rat bought a cheap numbing draught to deaden the agony in his joints so he could walk upright for a few days. The rot is still in his bones. When three siege quarrels hit that scavenged slab of iron, his fake knee will splinter under the shock, and the dead-root potion won't save him."

Takumi leaned closer, his feverish eyes glaring into Saki's face. "And if his wandering hedge-doctor follows him to Karasuma? Then the vagrant dies in the ditch beside him. Deserters' quarrels and sulfur oil ask no names. The dead do not file guild grievances."

***

Inside the TARDIS, the scent of steeping peppermint tea mingled with the sharp, clean aroma of beeswax polish and the deep, humming vibration of the central chronal rotor.

Kazuki sat at a small, circular mahogany table near the edge of the library gallery, the folded sheet of mulberry paper resting beside his teacup. He had read the five short lines eight times. The paper was smudged with soot and dust, smelling faintly of vinegar and the damp cellar where it had been penned.

The Doctor was leaning over the central console forty paces away, his dark velvet coat tails tossed back over his hips, his spectacles perched on his nose as he adjusted a delicate brass vernier scale with a pair of long-nosed pliers.

"Interesting specimen," the Doctor said, his sharp, rolling vowels echoing off the soaring bookshelves without looking up from his machinery. "Mulberry pulp harvested from a low-altitude river basin. Inexpensive lampblack ink. But the binder..."

The Doctor set down his pliers, pulled his sonic screwdriver from his waistcoat, and pointed the emitter tip at the paper from across the room.

The device purred with a shrill, buzzing emerald tone. A faint green beam washed over the mulberry sheet, illuminating the faded brushstrokes in bright, pulsing relief.

"Almond oil," the Doctor stated, switching off the device with a flick of his wrist. "Refined bitter almond oil, triple-distilled, with trace particulates of lavender and crushed mountain pearls. The kind of cosmetic paste sold exclusively in the high-tier apothecary shops of the Upper Bastion for forty silver coins an ounce. Your childhood miller must have remarkably supple hands for a man who hauls barley sacks."

Kazuki didn't smile. He looked down at the clumsy characters, his thumb tracing the heavy, blotted curve of the word *Tanizaki*.

"She curved the character for *water* to the left," Kazuki said quietly. "Saki broke her wrist when she was seven years old, falling from the mill flume. It healed crooked. Whenever she writes the three-dot radical, the third stroke hooks upward. She tried to disguise it as an old man's tremor, but the hook is still there."

The Doctor walked down the iron steps of the console platform, his hands clasped behind the small of his back, his attack eyebrows drawn into a sharp, severe line. He stopped beside the mahogany table, looking down at Kazuki through his dark lenses.

"Then you know what it is," the Doctor said.

"Yes," Kazuki replied. "A slaughterhouse."

"And an expensive one," the Doctor said, pulling off his spectacles and sliding them into his coat pocket. "Three heavy mechanical arbalests and six flasks of high-combustion volcanic distillate."

Kazuki looked up, his brow furrowing. "How do you know the weapons?"

"Because your old friend Master Gorou has ears like a hunting hound," the Doctor replied, tapping the edge of the mahogany table with a long, bony finger. "When I stopped by his stall an hour ago to check the temper on your harness buckles, the old smith pulled me into the coal shed. Two of Takumi's retainers were down in the deserters' ditch at daybreak, buying dwarven siege arbalests with House Kaneshiro silver. And as for the sulfur oil..."

"The Karasuma quarries," Kazuki said, his voice hard and quiet. "It’s a natural bottleneck. High granite terraces, thirty paces across, with only one narrow gorge leading in. In my third season with the guild, we cleared an ogre den in that exact cut. If you post arbalests on the second terrace and dump sulfur oil down the gorge, the draft turns the entire cut into an open blast furnace. No vanguard can hold that terrain head-on."

"Precisely," the Doctor murmured, his attack eyebrows drawn into a sharp, approving line. "Military arithmetic. They are terrified, Kazuki. And a frightened narcissist with access to heavy ordnance is the most predictably murderous creature in the cosmos."

The Doctor walked around the mahogany table, leaning against the curved rail.

"So," the Doctor said, his pale blue-grey eyes fixing on Kazuki with razor-sharp intensity. "We stay in the box. We brew another pot of tea. We let them sit in the freezing wind of an abandoned quarry until their toes turn black and they realize their little execution has no victim. Eventually, the guild inspectors will audit their forged reports, and the law of this miserable rock will do the dirty work for us."

Kazuki was silent for several long moments.

The only sound in the vast chamber was the rhythmic, hypnotic respiration of the time rotor: *hum-hum-thrum.*

Kazuki reached out and picked up the folded letter. He folded it cleanly along its creases, sliding it into the breast pocket of his dark woolen tunic, right over his heart.

He stood up.

His posture was balanced, easy, and unyielding. The broken vanguard from the privy ditch was gone, replaced by a young man who had stood on the centerline of the world and learned the difference between an anvil and a wall.

"If I don't go, Doctor," Kazuki said, his voice quiet, steady, and stripped of all fear, "they will keep looking over their shoulders. They will poison another novice. They will leave another porter to bleed out in the mud. And Saki will spend the rest of her life telling herself that she did what was necessary to survive."

Kazuki met the Time Lord's gaze without blinking.

"I need to stand in that quarry. I need to hold the shield. Not to beg for their mercy, and not to kill them. I need to look Saki in the eyes and show her that her debt is dead, that her fear is hollow, and that she has no power over me."

The Doctor stared at him for three long seconds.

Then, slowly, the severe lines of the Doctor’s face relaxed, a fierce, razor-sharp grin tugging at the corners of his thin mouth.

"You're an idiot, Kazuki," the Doctor said softly, his rolling northern voice resonant and brisk. "An absolute, stubborn idiot. But at least you're an idiot with an instinct for leverage. However, walking into a crossfire with nothing but a shield and a noble speech is not a strategy. It's adolescent martyrdom. If you walk in alone, you're a dead saint. But if you walk into that quarry as the official petitioner of a Crown Sovereign Lien... you turn their ambush into an open courtroom."

The Doctor reached into his velvet coat, pulled out a thick, folded sheet of official vellum stamped with the three-crowned seal of the High Inquest, and slapped it down onto the wooden table between them.

Kazuki recognized the parchment immediately—it was one of the blank official petition folios the Doctor had lifted from the public registry desk at the guildhall portico during their morning reconnaissance.

"What is this?" Kazuki asked, looking down at the dense rows of iron-gall script the Doctor had inscribed.

"Article Nineteen of the Frontier Guild Charter," the Doctor replied, tapping the parchment with a long forefinger. "A Sovereign Vanguard Lien. Any registered frontline defender who has suffered systemic wage theft, hazard voucher withholding, or contracted abandonment possesses the legal authority to petition the High Inquest for an emergency forensic audit of their party’s assets."

The Doctor leaned forward, his pale blue-grey eyes piercing Kazuki with absolute tactical clarity.

"You take this into Mizunara Keep right now. You go to the High Inquest Archives on the third floor of the guildhall. You demand to see Chief Auditor Ren. You show him this petition, you show him Saki's forged dispatch, and you show him the stolen guild wax. You tell Ren that the Golden Dawn is preparing to execute a Crown witness in Karasuma Quarry at noon. You give him the time. You give him the terrain. And you agree to walk into the quarry to draw out their confession and lethal attack, while Ren and twelve armed bailiffs follow twenty minutes behind your tracks."

Kazuki looked down at the petition, then up into the Doctor’s fierce, unwavering gaze. 

The phantom weight that had lingered in his chest for five years—the lingering fear of helplessness, the dread of confrontation—evaporated completely, replaced by the cold, exhilarating certainty of an artisan taking up his tools.

"I file the lien," Kazuki said, his fingers closing around the heavy vellum. "I hold the gorge. And when they draw poisoned steel to silence me, the kingdom catches them red-handed."

"Exactly," the Doctor murmured, stepping back with his hands resting calmly in his waistcoat pockets. "They think they're hunting a frightened stray dog. Let them discover what happens when the dog brings the tax auditor."

Kazuki bowed his head—a crisp, warrior’s salute to the mentor who had given him the geometry to stand—swung the dark meteor-iron kite shield onto his back, and strode through the blue wooden door.

***

Forty minutes later, within the quiet, parchment-scented chambers of the High Inquest Archive, Chief Auditor Ren looked up from his iron-bound ledgers.

Before his heavy bog-oak desk stood Kazuki, his boots firm upon the polished limestone floor, his traveler's cloak dusted with autumn rain. On the desk between them lay the forged dispatch, the broken emergency wax seal, and the signed Sovereign Vanguard Lien.

Chief Auditor Ren read the document line by line, his lined face hardening into the severe, terrifying stillness of an imperial magistrate. He pulled a massive, iron-bound register of that morning’s emergency filings from the shelf behind his desk, running a calloused forefinger down the fresh ink columns.

"An emergency pest-control permit," Ren murmured, his eyes narrowing into cold, dangerous slits as he tapped an entry. "Filed at the second bell by an orderly bearing the signet of House Kaneshiro. Authorizing the purchase of high-grade toxic serpent distillates from licensed vermin merchants in the Lower Tier. It seems Lord Takumi was already preparing his butchery before the ink was dry on this dispatch."

Ren looked up, his deep voice carrying the resonance of an iron bell. "You are certain of the location, Vanguard Kazuki?"

"Karasuma Stone Quarries. The second terrace," Kazuki replied, meeting the Auditor’s gaze without a flicker of hesitation. "They expect me alone at midday. They have poisoned steel, and they intend to make certain I never testify before your board."

Chief Auditor Ren dipped his heavy quill into black iron-gall ink and pressed his personal seal of office onto the vellum with a concussive thud.

"The Sovereign Board accepts the petition," Auditor Ren decreed, rising from his chair and reaching for his fur-lined cloak. "Captain of the Watch! Marshal twelve bailiffs in full battle harness. Load repeating arbalests and iron chains."

Ren looked at Kazuki, a grim, assessing respect in his eyes.

"Depart on foot, Vanguard Kazuki. Keep to the main cart road so my scouts can track your boot-prints. Hold the terrace until the twelfth bell tolls."

"I will hold it," Kazuki said.

Turning on his heel, Kazuki walked out of the archive and descended into the rainy streets of Mizunara Keep, striding northward toward the Karasuma cliffs with the cold iron of justice strapped to his back.
