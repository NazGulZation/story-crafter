# Chapter 10: The Quarry of Karasuma

The Karasuma Stone Quarries stretched across the northern gorge like an open, stepped amphitheater of grey granite, carved into the bedrock by two centuries of forced labor. Giant vertical terraces of sheer stone descended three hundred paces to a flooded quarry floor, where pools of stagnant black water mirrored the leaden autumn sky. Rotted timber derrick frames stood perched upon the cliff edges like the skeletons of forgotten siege engines, their iron haulage chains creaking in the biting north wind that howled down through the rocky clefts.

The air was bitterly cold, smelling of crushed granite dust, wet moss, and the stale sulfur of old excavation blasts.

Kazuki walked into the lowest cut of the quarry through a narrow gorge barely three paces wide. His boots crunched softly across the bed of loose slate chips. His dark traveler’s cloak was unpinned, draped loosely over his left shoulder, leaving his right arm free and his hand resting lightly against the weighted grip of the meteor-iron kite shield strapped to his left forearm.

He had timed his strides with surgical discipline—forty-five minutes up the main freight road from Mizunara Keep, leaving clear, deep boot-prints in the red clay for Chief Auditor Ren's trackers to follow. The signed Sovereign Vanguard Lien was already registered in the archives. Now came the execution. The Doctor had drawn the blueprint; it was Kazuki's duty to hold the ground and draw the traitors into committing their felony upon the official record.

He stopped in the center of the flooded basin.

There were no refugee wagons. There were no hungry harvest children huddled in the cut. There was no elderly miller with flour-dusted hands.

There was only the howling of the wind through the stone notches, and the faint, rhythmic drip of water falling from a fractured granite terrace thirty feet above his head. Down the gorge, the noon bells of Mizunara Keep were faintly beginning to toll across the valley.

"You really came," a voice called down from the rocks.

Kazuki didn't flinch. He slowly raised his eyes to the second terrace, twelve paces above the basin floor.

Takumi stood upon a ledge of split granite, his gilded breastplate dented and blackened by the fire of the Iron Ravine, his left arm bound tightly to his ribs with blood-stained linen. In his right hand, he held a broad-bladed executioner's falchion of heavy military steel, its edge ground down to a razor finish. His golden hair was wild, plastered to his forehead with cold sweat, his blue eyes bloodshot and dilated with feverish, manic intensity.

Flanking him on the upper steps were the others.

Lady Reiko stood on a high outcrop, her face almost entirely concealed beneath thick, yellowing bandages that smelled of vinegar and carbolic wash. In her blistered hands, she held three heavy scarlet parchment scrolls stamped with the black wax seal of forbidden pyromantic arts. 

Ten paces to her left, Kaede crouched behind a heavy oak tripod anchored to the granite with iron pitons. Mounted upon the timber was a heavy military arbalest, its thick recurve bow of tempered steel drawn taut by an iron windlass, aiming a four-sided, hardened iron bolt directly at the center of Kazuki's chest.

And five paces behind Takumi, standing on the edge of the shadow, was Saki.

She wore no white silk mantle. She was dressed in a travel-stained grey wool robe, her hair knotted back with a strip of hemp cord. Her hands were tucked into her sleeves, her face pale, drawn, and sharp as an obsidian chip.

Kazuki looked up at them. He felt the cold granite dust biting into his throat, the wind tugging at the hem of his dark cloak.

"The letter was written in your hand, Saki," Kazuki said, his voice quiet, carrying through the amphitheater with unnatural clarity. "You used the three-dot radical with the upward hook. Master Genzo died three winters ago in Tanizaki, during the great frost. You knew that. You were the one who held the prayer over his frozen body."

Saki didn't blink. Her jaw was set into an unyielding, rigid line.

"Genzo died of hunger, Kazuki," Saki said, her voice flat, devoid of warmth or remorse. "Just like my brother. Just like every miserable wretch who stayed behind in the valley to chew dried roots and pray to stone idols. I told you five years ago that I would never die in the mud. I swore that I would never look at a turnip again as long as I lived."

"And you thought murdering me would keep the mud off your boots?" Kazuki asked.

"You are the mud, Kazuki!" Takumi shouted down from the ledge, his voice cracking into a shrill, hysterical roar that echoed off the granite walls. "You are the peasant scum who crawled out of a ditch to soil our reputation! Gold Rank! We earned that title! We bled for it! And you think you can walk through the market with a clean face and make fools of House Kaneshiro?"

Takumi pointed his heavy falchion at Kaede. "Fire the bolts! Break the shield!"

Kaede’s left hand struck the iron trigger lever.

*THWACK-SHK!*

The heavy mechanical arbalest released its tempered spring with the crack of a thunderbolt. The four-sided iron quarrel, an inch thick and weighing two pounds, tore through the air at three hundred feet per second, aimed straight at Kazuki’s sternum.

Kazuki moved with fluid, liquid precision.

He didn't drop into the clumsy, bone-crushing three-point brace of the guild yard. He shifted his weight onto the ball of his right foot, pivoted his hips thirty degrees to the side, and brought the matte-grey kite shield up in a tight, angled sweep.

*CLANG!*

The iron quarrel struck the curved bevel of the meteoritic plate. 

Instead of blowing through the metal or shattering the vanguard's arm, the massive kinetic shock slid along the curved surface. The bolt ricocheted off the dense alloy with a blinding flare of blue sparks, shrieking into the granite cliff behind Kazuki and blowing a crater of powdered rock the size of a trencher.

Kazuki’s feet never left their mark. His left arm absorbed the deflected vibration through the balanced ox-hide straps, his shoulders loose, his breathing steady.

Kaede’s breath caught in her throat. "Impossible! The bolt was rated for gate breaching!"

"Scrolls! Burn him!" Takumi screamed, backing toward the rock wall in wild panic. "Reiko! Open the seal!"

Reiko ripped the black wax from the first scarlet scroll with her teeth. 

"Consume the flesh!" she shrieked through her bandages, hurling the parchment into the gorge.

The scroll detonated mid-air. A roiling torrent of liquid subterranean fire erupted in a blinding sheet of crimson flame, cascading down into the basin like an avalanche of molten pitch, heating the air to a blistering furnace that turned the puddles of stagnant water into shrieking steam.

Kazuki stepped forward into the blast.

He brought the kite shield tight against his chest, dipping the top rim forward at forty-five degrees. The liquid flame struck the dark meteor-iron. The specialized alloy swallowed the thermal shock, radiating the heat across its molecular lattice without glowing red. The boiling pitch washed over the curved face and split into twin rivers of fire that hissed into the wet slate on either side of his boots, leaving him standing untouched in the center of the blaze.

Kazuki emerged from the smoke. His cloak was singed at the hem, his face calm, his dark eyes fixed on the terrace above.

"My turn," Kazuki said.

He took three explosive strides across the slate rubble. Reaching the base of the terrace, he planted his right boot on a fractured ledge, leaped upward with the restored strength of his healed ligaments, caught the stone rim with his right hand, and vaulted onto the second tier.

Takumi shrieked, stumbling backward as Kazuki landed four paces from him with a dull thud of heavy boots.

"Stay back!" Takumi yelled, slashing wildly with the heavy falchion in a panicked, two-handed sweep. "I am a nobleman of the capital! You cannot touch me! You are a commoner!"

Kazuki didn't draw a weapon. He didn't have one.

As the heavy blade descended toward his shoulder, Kazuki swept the outer edge of his kite shield in a short, rising parry. The dark iron struck the flat of Takumi's falchion, knocking the heavy steel wide with effortless geometric leverage. Before Takumi could recover his balance, Kazuki drove the flat boss of the shield directly into Takumi's chest.

*THUMP.*

The concussive blow blew the breath from Takumi’s lungs in a wet gasp. The gilded duelist flew backward, crashing into the stone wall, his falchion clattering across the ledge, his head snapping against the granite with a sickening thud. He slumped into the dirt, clutching his ribs, sobbing and wheezing like a kicked cur.

Reiko threw her remaining scrolls into the mud, backing away with her hands raised, her single eye rolling in absolute terror as she pressed herself flat against the rock face. 

Kaede dropped the arbalest windlass, her splinted arm hanging useless, freezing in place as she calculated her survival odds and found them to be zero.

Silence fell over the terrace, broken only by the whimpering of Takumi in the dust and the whistling of the north wind.

Kazuki stood on the ledge. He did not raise his shield to strike. He did not reach for a stone. He turned slowly, his boots grinding into the slate, and looked at Saki.

Saki had not run. She stood five paces away, her back against the rotted timber derrick, her grey wool robe whipping in the gale. Her hands were clenched at her sides, her fingernails digging into her palms until droplets of dark blood welled beneath the skin.

"Why, Saki?" Kazuki asked. His voice was not angry. It was heavy with the sorrow of a man standing over a grave. "Five years. I took every tooth. I took every claw. I broke my ribs so you could sleep dry. I broke my knee so you could cast from safety. Did I ever take a copper of your share? Did I ever ask you for anything?"

A wild, ugly laugh tore out of Saki’s throat.

The laugh grew, rising into a shrill, hysterical screech that set the ravens wheeling from the high quarry cliffs. Tears streamed down her cheeks, washing through the grime on her chin, but her eyes burned with a venomous, unyielding hatred that terrified him more than the charging burrowers of the Iron Ravine.

"That was your crime, Kazuki!" Saki screamed, her voice cracking with pure, concentrated bitterness. "You never asked for anything! You never complained! You just stood there, bleeding and smiling like an idiot saint, handing me the clean bread while you chewed the burnt crusts!"

She stepped toward him, trembling with rage, pointing a shaking finger directly at his heart.

"Do you know what it felt like to look at you every morning?" Saki hissed, her chest heaving. "Every time I saw your scarred hands, I remembered that I was a beggar who had to be saved! Every time your knee clicked when you walked into the guildhall, the highborn arbiters looked at us and smelled the cabbage and the pig manure of Tanizaki! You were the chain, Kazuki! You were the chain tying me to the mud!"

She wiped her tears with the back of her sleeve, her lips curling into a vicious snarl.

"Takumi is a coward, yes! He is a vain, greedy little boy who runs when the monsters bite! But Takumi has an uncle on the High Council! Takumi has an estate with marble floors and white silk sheets! When I stand beside Takumi, people bow to me! They call me 'Lady Saki'! They forget where I came from! But when I stand beside you, I am just the barefoot orphan girl who stole flour from the bailiff's cart!"

Saki’s voice dropped to a cold, jagged whisper that cut deeper than any dagger.

"I don't hate you because you were weak, Kazuki. I hate you because you were my salvation. And I cannot become a queen while the man who bought my life with his own flesh is still breathing to remind me of the price."

Kazuki looked at her.

He looked at the girl he had pulled through the snowdrifts when they were seven years old. He looked at the acolyte whose first prayer book he had purchased with the pawned hair-pin of his dead mother.

And as he looked at her, the last lingering ember of warmth in his chest—the fragile, foolish hope that she had simply been misled or frightened—finally burned out, leaving only a calm, cool expanse of grey stone.

"You are free, Saki," Kazuki said softly. "The debt is cancelled. It was cancelled the moment you left me in the ditch."

"It isn't cancelled until you are dead!" Takumi shrieked from the dirt behind him.

The duelist had dragged himself upright against the rock wall. In his right hand, pulled from his boot sheath, was a slender, triangular poignard of black obsidian, its needle-sharp tip glistening with the milky green sheen of concentrated basilisk venom.

With a desperate, animal lunge, Takumi lunged forward, aiming the poisoned poignard straight at Kazuki’s unguarded neck.

At the exact same instant, Kaede kicked the secondary tripwire on the arbalest mount with her good boot.

A hidden spring snapped beneath the timber platform. An iron portcullis—a heavy grid of sharpened oak beams shod in iron spikes—dropped from the derrick frame directly above Kazuki, hurtling downward with crushing velocity to pin him to the terrace.

Kazuki reacted. He brought his kite shield upward to meet the falling timber, bracing to deflect the iron spikes, but the poisoned blade was already three inches from his throat.

There was no space to turn. There was no angle to step.

Takumi’s face was twisted in a manic, triumphant grin of imminent slaughter.

"Die in the quarry, dog!" Takumi screamed.
