# Chapter 13: The Sovereign Foreclosure

The bells of the midday watch tolled from the highest limestone belfry of Mizunara Keep, their heavy bronze resonance rolling across the tiered rooftops and echoing against the northern cliffs.

In the Great Plaza of the High Inquest, beneath the soaring portico of the Grand Guildhall, three thousand citizens had gathered in the cold autumn wind.

They filled every tier of the amphitheater—common carters in sheepskin coats, flour-dusted millers, blacksmiths with bare forearms, apprentice vanguards leaning on scarred pine bucklers, and highborn merchants wrapped in heavy wool mantles. A perimeter of forty armed Guild Bailiffs in black iron cuirasses held the crowd back behind stout barricades of seasoned oak, their poleaxes held upright in a bristling forest of steel.

At the top of the broad limestone staircase stood the Sovereign Tribunal.

Chief Auditor Ren stood behind a heavy lectern of black bog-oak, flanked by the City Magistrate’s Chief Bailiff and the High Inquest Scribes. To his left stood the venerable Elder Deacon of the Temple of the White Dawn, his white linen vestments embroidered with silver thread, holding a ceremonial sickle of dull temple iron.

To the right of the tribunal, standing quietly in the shadow of a colossal fluted pillar, were Kazuki and the Doctor.

Kazuki was dressed in his simple dark traveler’s brigandine, the unadorned kite shield of meteoritic iron resting lightly against his left leg. He stood tall, his right knee straight and painless, his breath rising in steady, translucent plumes into the freezing midday air. Beside him stood the Doctor, his velvet collar pulled tight against the draft, his attack eyebrows arched in cool, detached appraisal as he watched the crowd below.

"Look at the sheer demographic efficiency," the Doctor murmured in his rolling northern cadence, his eyes gleaming with intellectual satisfaction. "You gather three thousand witnesses, read the exact arithmetic of their fraud aloud, and dismantle their vanity in broad daylight. Far cleaner than a dungeon. A dungeon creates martyrs. A public ledger creates clarity."

At the base of the guildhall steps, an iron cage-wagon pulled by two heavy dray horses rattled to a halt over the wet cobblestones.

The barred rear gate swung open with a shriek of rusted hinges.

A chorus of furious shouts, jeers, and hissed curses erupted from the crowd as four prisoners were hauled down into the square by armed bailiffs.

Takumi of House Kaneshiro was pushed onto the first limestone step. His golden hair was caked with grey stone dust and dried blood; his right shoulder hung limp and dislocated within the stained linen sling tied across his chest. He stumbled on his boots, weeping openly, his gaze fixed on the ground to escape the burning eyes of the apprentices he had once mocked.

Behind him came Lady Reiko, clutching the shredded remains of her noble silk robes around her bruised shoulders, her unbandaged face smudged with soot and tears. Kaede followed, her broken forearm bound in splints of coarse pine, her mouth drawn in a thin, bloodless grimace of bitter humiliation.

And at the rear walked Saki.

She wore her white acolyte surplice, but the fine linen was torn at the hem and spattered with red clay from the Karasuma terrace. Around her neck still hung the platinum sunburst medallion of the White Dawn. Her hands were locked in heavy iron debtor-shackles, the links dragging against the stone steps with a heavy, rhythmic clatter as the bailiffs forced her to kneel beside Takumi before the high lectern.

Chief Auditor Ren raised his bronze measuring rule.

The crowd fell silent, the angry murmur dying away until only the cold wind whistling through the guildhall banners could be heard.

"Let all citizens, guildmasters, and apprentices of the Eastern Marches hear the decree of the Sovereign Board of Auditors," Ren proclaimed, his voice booming across the cobblestones with the weight of an iron hammer. "Upon the fifteenth day of the autumn season, a formal inquest was conducted into the accounts, bounties, and conduct of the Gold Rank party registered as the Golden Dawn."

Auditor Ren turned the heavy calfskin folio on his lectern, pointing his rule at the black iron-gall lines written upon the page.

"The findings of the Inquest are absolute," Ren continued. "Over five consecutive seasons, the party known as the Golden Dawn engaged in systematic sovereign embezzlement, submitting forty-two falsified casualty vouchers for funds diverted directly to personal luxury. Furthermore, the party committed felony treason against the Frontier Charter by deliberately abandoning their registered vanguard in a municipal drainage ditch to conceal their financial crimes."

A collective gasp rippled through the assembled apprentices at the foot of the stairs. Wounded novices who had spent months limping through the lower district looked at Takumi with eyes full of blistering hatred.

"And finally," Auditor Ren bellowed, his gaze searing down upon the trembling duelist, "the four prisoners stand convicted of premeditated fratricide, having forged an emergency imperial dispatch and utilized prohibited basilisk venom to ambush Vanguard Kazuki within the Karasuma Stone Quarries."

"Lies!" Takumi screamed, lifting his tear-streaked face to the assembly. "My uncle... my uncle sits on the council! I demand an appeal to House Kaneshiro! You cannot treat me like a common thief!"

"Your uncle sits in a stone cell beneath the citadel, Takumi," the City Magistrate’s Chief Bailiff barked from the platform. "Your house has disowned you to preserve their remaining estates from imperial confiscation. You have no appeal. You have only the balance."

Chief Auditor Ren stepped from behind the lectern, raising his bronze measuring rule toward the fluted pillar.

"Under Article Nineteen of the Frontier Charter," Ren proclaimed, his voice rolling across the three thousand assembled citizens, "the claimant of the Sovereign Vanguard Lien must ratify the decree before the Crown. Vanguard Kazuki of Tanizaki, step forward."

Kazuki stepped out from the shadow of the colossal stone pillar.

His boots were firm and measured upon the polished limestone. Strapped to his left forearm was the matte-grey kite shield of meteoritic iron, its dark, unadorned rim catching the bright autumn sun. He moved with the quiet, fluid balance of a whole man—unburdened by vanity, untouched by shame, standing tall in the presence of the law.

A hushed murmur of respect rippled through the front ranks of the crowd as the young vanguard ascended the dais to stand beside the Sovereign Auditor.

Ren presented the black goose-quill and the open vellum scroll of the Decree of Sovereign Dissolution.

"Vanguard Kazuki," Auditor Ren said, his tone carrying the deep gravity of the realm. "As the surviving frontline defender who bore the sorties and filed the lien, do you ratify the findings of the Inquest, and do you demand the total foreclosure of the party registered as the Golden Dawn?"

Kazuki looked down at the four figures kneeling upon the lower steps. He looked at Takumi shivering in his mud-stained doublet, at Reiko clutching her face, at Kaede with her splinted arm, and at Saki staring into the dirt.

There was no hesitation in his fingers. Kazuki took the quill, dipped the nib in black iron-gall ink, and signed his name at the base of the imperial parchment:

*Kazuki, Vanguard of Tanizaki.*

"I ratify the findings," Kazuki spoke, his voice clear and resonant as a struck bell. "By my hand as the vanguard who held the line, the Golden Dawn is dissolved."

Chief Auditor Ren pressed his heavy bronze signet into the hot red wax beside Kazuki’s signature, sealing the document with a concussive thud.

"The dissolution is sealed!" Ren bellowed. "By law of the Frontier Charter, the assets of the dissolved party are forfeit. Claimant Kazuki, how shall the liquidated property be disbursed?"

Kazuki looked down the steps toward the barricades, where the young apprentice vanguards stood with their bandaged ribs, splintered pine bucklers, and limping knees.

"Liquidate the duelist's weapons and the party's stolen gold," Kazuki directed, his words ringing across the vast plaza. "Melt the star-silver into treasury coin. Disburse the wages in full to every novice vanguard who bled in the lower tiers and was cheated of his hazard pay."

"It shall be done!" Chief Auditor Ren decreed. "Master Blacksmith! Bring the anvil!"

The Master Blacksmith of the Guildhall strode forward from the portico, carrying a pair of heavy iron tongs and a flat-headed splitting hammer.

He knelt beside Takumi. With cold, practiced brutality, the smith seized the wire-wrapped hilt of the star-silver rapier lying on an inspection tray.

"Forged from imperial silver," the smith growled, turning the blade toward the crowd. "Stolen by a coward."

The smith laid the slender, elegant blade across a portable iron anvil set upon the fourth step. He raised the heavy splitting hammer.

*CLANG.*

A shower of bright silver shards sprayed across the limestone. The fine star-silver rapier snapped into three pieces, the enchanted azure light within the steel dying away with a faint, sputtering hiss.

Kazuki stepped down the wide stone steps, accompanied by two guild clerks carrying an iron-bound chest of heavy silver coin minted from the seized accounts of House Kaneshiro. 

Kazuki knelt beside the barricade where the novice vanguards stood. He personally placed the first heavy pouch of silver into the trembling hands of young Apprentice Jiro.

"For your ribs, Jiro," Kazuki said softly, meeting the boy's tearful gaze. "Your courage was real. You do not owe your life to thieves."

"Thank you, Vanguard Kazuki!" Jiro wept, clutching the silver to his chest. "Thank you!"

Kazuki handed the second pouch to Apprentice Kenji. "For your shoulder. Rest your arm, mend your boots, and never let a duelist tell you that a shield is an anvil."

The crowd of three thousand erupted into a thunderous roar of cheering that shook the limestone portico, while Takumi shrieked in helpless agony upon the fourth step, clawing at the stone as the smith pulled the dented silver breastplate from his torso, stripping away the gilded tabard and leaving him shivering in a thin, tattered undershirt that smelled of sour fear.

The smith moved to Lady Reiko. With a single jerk of his tongs, he ripped the charred, leather-bound grimoire from her belt and tossed it into an iron brazier filled with glowing red charcoal. The pages blackened and curled, the hoarded fire-spells turning to grey ash that drifted away on the north wind. Reiko collapsed onto her stomach, weeping into the dirt.

Kaede’s concealed throwing bodkins, lock-picks, and poisoned darts were dumped into an iron bucket filled with corrosive vinegar, the toxic grease dissolving with a vile, hissing foam.

Then, the Elder Deacon of the White Dawn stepped forward.

His pale eyes were heavy with righteous fury as he looked down at Saki.

"Saki of Tanizaki," the Deacon spoke, his aged voice trembling with quiet indignation. "You took the holy vows of the Dawn. You swore upon the sacred hearth to mend the broken, to comfort the destitute, and to pour the balm of grace upon those who bleed for the innocent. Instead, you used your sacred office to barter for silk. You withheld the mending prayers from the man who gave you bread, so that you might curry favor with a vain aristocrat."

"Deacon, please..." Saki sobbed, tears splashing onto the cold limestone between her knees. "I was poor... I was starving... I didn't want to go back to the dirt..."

"The dirt is where your soul belongs, woman," the Deacon said coldly.

He raised the iron ritual sickle.

With a swift, downward slice, the curved blade caught the platinum chain around Saki’s neck.

*SNAP.*

The platinum sunburst medallion fell from her throat. It bounced twice down the limestone steps, tumbled through the iron grate of the terrace gutter, and splashed into the black, stagnant sewage below.

The Deacon reached down and seized the collar of her white silk acolyte robe. With two tearing strokes, he ripped the white fabric from her body, casting the shredded silk onto the wet cobblestones where the boots of the bailiffs trampled it into the mire.

Two acolytes stepped forward and threw a rough, unbleached burlap tunic over her shivering shoulders—the scratchy, prickly wool of an excommunicated vagrant.

Saki clutched the coarse burlap to her chest, her fingers clawing at the rough hemp fibers, her forehead pressed to the stone as she wailed in utter, desolate ruin.

"Second," Chief Auditor Ren’s voice boomed across the roaring square, "the penalty of Civil Disincorporation."

Two guild stonemasons, carrying heavy iron chisels and four-pound wooden mallets, ascended the tall wooden scaffold beside the Great Portico.

High above the steps stood the massive obelisk of polished black granite—the monument where the names of every Gold Rank adventurer in the history of the Eastern Marches were carved in two-inch letters filled with gleaming gold leaf.

The crowd pointed upward as the master mason placed the edge of his chisel against the first name on the newest gold plaque:

*TAKUMI OF HOUSE KANESHIRO.*

*CLACK.*

The mallet struck the chisel.

A sharp, acoustic crack echoed through the amphitheater. The gilded marble letters shattered, spraying black granite chips and gold dust down onto the portico floor.

*CLACK. CLACK. CLACK.*

The stonemason moved along the line with ruthless, rhythmic precision.

*SAKI OF THE WHITE DAWN.*

*REIKO OF THE NORTHERN SPIRE.*

*KAEDE OF THE WESTERN REACH.*

Strike after strike, the chisels carved deep, jagged, ugly trenches into the stone, obliterating every vowel, every consonant, and every trace of gold. Within five minutes, the polished marble plaque was reduced to a mutilated, scarred void—an empty, uncarved gouge in the granite face of the monument.

The Doctor watched the falling dust with quiet, unmoving focus.

"That," the Doctor whispered, "is what remains of their eternity. A hole in a rock where the rain can collect."

Chief Auditor Ren rolled up his master folio, snapping the iron clasps shut with a final, decisive click.

"Takumi, Reiko, Kaede, and Saki," Auditor Ren proclaimed, his voice echoing to the furthest corners of the crowded plaza. "You are hereby stripped of all rank, all standing, and all civil identity under the law. You are designated Unbonded Vagrants. You are forbidden to bear steel, forbidden to practice magic, and barred from ever entering an adventurer guildhall across the seven provinces."

Auditor Ren pointed his staff toward the narrow exit alley leading down to the Lower Slums.

"Bailiffs! Expel the vagrants from the High Inquest!"

The twelve guardsmen surged forward with their halberd butts, prodding the four shivering, ragged figures down the long flight of limestone steps.

The crowd parted before them with hisses of contempt. Common carters spat on their burlap tunics; wounded novices threw bruised turnip tops and handfuls of wet manure at their heads. Takumi stumbled blindly into the mud, his broken shoulder throbbing, weeping as he covered his face with his uninjured arm.

Reiko and Kaede clung to each other in the dirt, dragging themselves forward through the rain and the filth, their fine noble pride drowned in the jeers of three thousand working men.

And Saki dragged her feet through the wet gravel, her eyes hollow, staring at the mud caked between her bare toes, realizing with terrifying, irreversible certainty that the grand dream she had sold her soul for had vanished like steam on a cold morning.

At the top of the steps, beside the great fluted pillar, Kazuki watched them disappear into the lower alleys.

His hands were calm. The dark kite shield hung at his side, solid, whole, and completely untouched by the shame that had drowned his former companions.

The Doctor turned toward him, wiping a speck of granite dust from his velvet lapel, his attack eyebrows softening as he met Kazuki’s eyes.

"Proper accounting," the Doctor said, his rolling northern voice quiet and resonant in the clearing air. "No ghosts. No ballads. Just four broken nouns heading for the ditch where they belong."

"You could have used your machine," Kazuki noted quietly, looking across the vast stone plaza as the apprentices began to disperse with their silver. "You could have done something impossible that made the sky shake."

"Impossible things are terribly overrated, Kazuki," the Doctor scoffed gently, stuffing his hands into his velvet pockets. "If a wizard shoots lightning from the clouds, people bow down in terror, but the moment the lightning stops, they go right back to cheating each other at dice. But a public audit? An honest ledger read aloud before the butcher, the carter, and the smith? That is permanent. You didn't just break their swords today. You broke the myth that allowed them to devour you."

Kazuki nodded once, his chest rising with a long, clean breath of the mountain wind. The air entering his lungs tasted of autumn frost, pine resin, and honest earth.

"Yes, Doctor," Kazuki said, shifting the dark kite shield on his arm. "The arithmetic is settled."
