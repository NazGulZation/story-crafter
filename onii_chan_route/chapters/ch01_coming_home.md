# Chapter 1: Midnight Commute and Blaring Speakers

The rain over the Chuo Line smelled of wet asphalt, rusted brake shoes, and the collective sour breath of two hundred salarymen crammed shoulder to shoulder inside a fluorescent metal tube. My umbrella had collapsed three blocks outside Koenji Station, snapping a fiberglass rib with a wet crack that left the left sleeve of my dark trench coat soaked through to the lining. By the time I jammed my key into the tarnished brass lock of apartment 304, the skin between my shoulder blades carried the persistent, grinding ache of fourteen straight hours spent debugging legacy enterprise billing services that some contractor wrote during the second Bush administration. 

I kicked the heel of my right shoe against the iron doorframe. The latch gave way with an oily sigh.

The air inside the genkan hit my nostrils instantly, a dense domestic fog that belonged to an entirely different dimension than the cold drizzle outside. It carried the salty fat of instant tonkotsu broth, the cloying sugary vapor of canned royal milk tea, the faint chemical ozone rising from an overtaxed graphics card, and beneath all of it, the unmistakable citrus sting of cheap yuzu shampoo. 

And then, before my damp socks even touched the raised hardwood threshold, the speakers hammered my eardrums.

*"Ah... Senpai, please! If you touch me there... my soul will shatter into pieces! Kyaaa—!"*

A burst of brassy, synthesizer-heavy midi trumpets roared through the paper-thin drywall, accompanied by the wet, squelching foley effects of an eroge sound designer earning every yen of his freelance invoice. The bass vibrated directly through the floorboards under my heels, rattling the empty plastic umbrella stand near the breaker box.

I dropped my leather work briefcase onto the shoe bench. It landed with a dull, leathery thud. I squeezed the bridge of my nose, feeling the greasy film of office air that coated my forehead, and dragged my knuckles down my temples. 

"I'm home," I muttered. My voice sounded like gravel ground between two millstones.

No response came from the living room, save for another digitized gasp and the rapid clatter of gamepad triggers being mashed in frantic succession.

I kicked off my wet dress shoes, stepped into the frayed guest slippers that had lost their foam padding six months ago, and slid back the slatted wooden partition separating the entryway from the living room.

My modest 2LDK apartment had long ceased to resemble a respectable home for a twenty-eight-year-old software engineer. It had become a fortified encampment. To the right stood the second bedroom—the door sealed shut by three towering stacks of unopened Amazon shipping boxes, two plastic laundry baskets overflowing with plush fleece blankets, and a cardboard cutout of a twin-tailed magical girl whose left ear had been chewed by some long-forgotten stray cat. That room was an unlivable hazard zone, an archaeological stratum of limited-edition acrylic stands, empty calorie-mate wrappers, and dust-covered visual novel collector boxes. 

Because her assigned bedroom had collapsed under the weight of her hoarded hobbies, its sole inhabitant had staged a permanent amphibious landing on the rest of my living space.

Nozomi lay sprawled diagonally across my freshly washed cotton futon, which I had made the fatal mistake of airing out on the low tatami platform before leaving for the morning rush.

She had colonized the bed with imperial efficiency. A corded white controller dangled from her pale, slender fingers, its wire snaking across the rumpled grey duvet toward the desktop rig idling on the low mahogany coffee table. Both twenty-seven-inch monitors blazed with the vivid pastels of an interactive adult visual novel: currently displaying an elaborate CG illustration of a shrine maiden pinned against a torii gate by a shadowy apparition. The external studio monitor speakers—which I had bought with my winter bonus to edit sound design projects—were cranked to sixty percent volume, filling the room with high-pitched digital whimpers.

Nozomi herself looked like a disaster sculpted by an artist who specialized exclusively in domestic ruin.

She was twenty-one, barely two years past the threshold of legal adulthood, though her daily habits suggested the life cycle of a pampered feline. Her frame was lean and slender, with pale, long legs that stretched across two-thirds of the mattress, totally bare except for the faint indentation of sheet wrinkles pressed into her calves. She wore nothing except a faded, washed-out charcoal graphic t-shirt bearing the faded print of an eighties kaiju monster. The collar had stretched so wide from careless laundering that it slumped entirely off her left shoulder, exposing a pale, delicate collarbone, a faint scattering of freckles, and the ivory curve of her shoulder blade. The hem barely brushed the upper third of her slender thighs, riding dangerously high every time she shifted her weight against the cushions.

Her dark brown hair was a nest of unruly, sleep-tangled waves, pinned back clumsily on the right with a plastic chip clip. Heavy pink gaming headphones with cat-ear moldings hung loosely around her slender neck, discarded in favor of letting the desktop speakers fill the apartment with unadulterated auditory carnage.

"Nozomi," I said, projecting my voice above the synthetic moans of the shrine maiden.

She did not flinch. Her thumbs danced across the analog sticks with furious, twitching precision. "Hold on, hold on, hold on! Don't talk, Nii-san! If I miss this quick-time prompt, I get locked into the Bad End where the cursed spirit feeds me to the koi pond! Mash, mash, mash—!"

"It's midnight on a Tuesday," I said, walking over to the coffee table. My feet felt heavy as wet cement. "The elderly couple downstairs sleep at nine. You are shaking their ceiling fixtures with high-definition hentai audio."

"They're deaf, onii-chan! Mr. Sato told me last week that he took his hearing aids out whenever the garbage truck comes by! It's fine! Just two more dialogue boxes!"

With a sharp snap of her right thumb, she cleared the prompt. A victorious chime echoed through the speakers, followed by a theatrical sigh of relief from the voice actress. The screen dissolved into a wall of descriptive pink text describing the spiritual purification of the heroine's garments.

Only then did Nozomi crane her neck backward over the edge of the mattress, staring at me upside down. Her large, amber-tinted brown eyes widened, rimmed with the faint violet shadows of a seventy-two-hour visual novel binge. A smear of red chili oil from a cup of spicy seafood noodles glistened at the left corner of her bottom lip.

"Welcome home, hard-working corporate drone," she chirped. She let her arms flop outward across the duvet like an overturned beetle. The hem of her oversized shirt rode up another two inches, revealing the pale, taut dip of her hip bone and the faint white edge of cotton panties that had seen better decades. "Did you bring home the melon pan from the convenience store near the south exit? The bakery one with the crunchy sugar crust?"

"I brought home an umbrella with a broken spine and a migraine that pulses in B-flat," I replied, staring down at her with dead eyes. "Why are you in my bed?"

"My room is too cold," she said instantly, puffing out her cheeks in a practiced, weaponized pout. "The air vent in the corner makes this weird whistling noise that sounds like a ghost crying about its student loans. Plus, your mattress smells like lavender laundry soap. Mine smells like stale pocky sticks."

"Your room smells like stale pocky sticks because there are four half-eaten boxes under your desk that have achieved sentience."

"They're preserving the ecosystem, onii-chan. You shouldn't tamper with biodiversity." She rolled over onto her stomach with a lazy, supple twist of her spine, propping her chin on her hands and swinging her bare ankles back and forth in the air behind her. Her slender heels kicked against the wooden bed frame with rhythmic, hollow thumps. "Besides, you're always complaining that I don't socialize. Look at me! I'm in the common room! I'm interacting with family! I'm a model citizen of this apartment!"

"You're a parasite in an oversized Godzilla shirt."

"A cute parasite," she corrected without a shred of hesitation, blinking up at me with wide, glittering puppy-dog eyes that had saved her from eviction at least fourteen times over the past calendar year. "A slender, conventionally attractive, legally adult maiden who happens to be related to you by a very tenuous piece of civil paperwork that our parents signed in a municipal ward office six years ago and then immediately forgot about when they moved to separate sides of the country."

She tilted her head, tapping the controller against her chin. "Honestly, if this were a light novel, you'd be thanking the author for this living situation. I'm providing high-tier domestic atmosphere. People pay sixty dollars on Steam just to look at girls who look forty percent as good as this."

The sheer audacity of her logic hit me like a splash of cold ditch water. I stood over the low platform, looking at the rumpled sheets, the glowing monitor screens, and the slender girl who had turned my home into her personal arcade. 

The heat of the room was beginning to seep through my wet socks and trousers. My muscles trembled with the urge to simply collapse—to let gravity claim my skull, drop onto the cotton sheets, and forget that Jira tickets, deployment pipelines, and quarterly reviews existed in the waking world. But right beside that exhaustion stood a smoldering spark of sheer exasperation. If I let this slide tonight, tomorrow she would be eating curry over my pillowcases while streaming to four hundred anonymous degenerates on the internet.

I took a deep breath, drawing the sweet scent of peach tea and salty pork broth into my lungs. The rain beat a steady, relentless tattoo against the balcony glass, drumming against the thin metal frame of the screen door. Across the mattress, Nozomi adjusted her posture, the cotton of her oversized tee shifting over the curve of her hip as the twin monitors bathed her bare legs in cool, flickering cyan. She didn't look up from the display, her thumbs resting lightly on the triggers of the white controller, completely certain that the room belonged to her.
