# Chapter 3B1: Break the Vine

The thick amber sap, mixed with sweat and your own spilled seed, coats the oak bark and your forearms in a heavy, slick layer of aromatic resin. As Lashvine steps back from your trembling groin, panting and grinning with feral, predatory triumph, you refuse to surrender to the leaden exhaustion dragging at your limbs.

You suck air through your teeth, feeling the hot, rhythmic pulse of blood in your wrists where the black thorns bit deep into your flesh.

The wood-fey have made a fatal error in their arrogance. In their frenzy to claim your seed and break your martial spirit, they drenched the vine-bindings in their own lubricating nectar. The fibrous knots that held your left arm fast to the oak have become slippery, saturated with warm, oily sap that loosens the grip of the tendrils against the bark.

You are a trained border-rider. Five years in the garrison yard taught you to recognize when an opponent overextends their guard. You wait for the precise second when Lashvine turns her head to mock Knothollow, letting her attention wander.

You gather every shred of strength remaining in your shoulders, chest, and back. You plant both bare feet firmly against the mossy root-arch beneath you, dig your heels into the loam, and twist your left arm with savage, bone-cracking torque.

The slick, sap-drenched vine rolls over your wrist. The fibrous cords stretch, screech with the sound of snapping rope, and tear free with a wet, splintering crack.

Your left arm flies loose.

Before the dryads can register the movement, you drive your freed left fist straight into the center of Lashvine's chest. The blow lands with the dull, solid thud of knuckle against dense walnut timber. The impact sends a jolt of sharp pain through your hand, bruising your knuckles against her hard bark, but the raw kinetic force of the strike staggers the dark dryad backward into the ferns, knocking the breath from her lungs in a sharp, wooden rasp.

Beside her, the heavy split-oak dryad, Sapwound, roars and lunges forward with outstretched, sap-coated hands to pin you against the trunk. You anticipate the rush. You drop your center of gravity, ducking cleanly beneath her massive, oak-armored arms. As she crashes heavily into the trunk where you were pinned a second before, you reach across your chest with your freed hand, grip the vine-tendril pinning your right arm, and sink your fingers into the fibers. 

You brace your forearm, grit your teeth, and rip the tendril bodily from the oak bark with a ferocious, guttural roar that shakes the silence of the clearing.

Your right arm tears free, taking a strip of skin with it.

You stumble forward onto your hands and knees in the cold mud, completely naked, your chest heaving, your skin covered in a sticky mixture of sweat, sap, and bright crimson blood. Your breath comes in ragged, wheezing gasps, but you have your limbs back.

A collective, furious hiss erupts from the canopy above like a nest of disturbed vipers.

"The buck breaks the fence!" Knothollow screams, her lichen-green eyes flashing with sudden, venomous wrath. "He strikes the circle! Tear his heels! Bind him to the soil!"

You lurch to your feet. You do not look for your broadsword or your discarded cuirass. Steel and boiled leather mean nothing now; speed and distance are your only hope of survival. You spin on your heels, duck low under a swinging bough of blackthorn, and plunge headlong into the dense briar thicket behind the ancient oak.

The sovereign forest comes alive to bar your path.

Low-hanging branches of black alder lash across your bare chest and shoulders like oiled whips, raising angry purple welts and tearing long, bleeding gashes across your skin. Beneath your bare feet, thorny blackberry canes and sharp flint stones slice into your soles, painting the damp moss with bright crimson drops at every stride. 

Yet the surge of adrenaline coursing through your veins burns away the pain, turning your terror into raw, forward momentum.

You throw yourself down the steep bank of a limestone gully, sliding through wet ferns, slick dead leaves, and icy shale before landing knee-deep in the black, freezing water of a woodland creek. The sharp, concussive jolt rattles your teeth and jars your knee-joints, but you keep moving. The shock of the cold water against your overheated skin is like a bucket of lye, shocking your lungs and clearing the intoxicating haze of the dryad spores from your skull. You splash through the rocky stream bed, your boots forgotten, using the swift running water to mask your tracks and scent from the searching roots beneath the bank.

Behind you, the woods echo with the terrifying, inhuman hunting calls of the Ashmere Circle. The wood-fey move through the high canopy with horrifying speed, leaping from bough to bough like shadow-panthers. Their wood-talons scrape across the bark with the dry, terrifying sound of scythes being honed for harvest.

"Run, little soldier!" Lashvine's mocking screech echoes through the branches above, close behind your right shoulder. "Every step you bleed into the loam belongs to our roots! Every drop of sweat feeds our hunger! You cannot outrun the sovereign wood on wounded heels!"

A thick root suddenly buckles upward through the leaf-litter beneath your right foot, trying to snare your ankle in a wooden loop. You spot the movement a fraction of a second before impact and leap over it with desperate agility, tucking your shoulder and rolling across a wet bed of rotting leaves before scrambling back to your feet without losing a stride.

You remember the lessons of the border-scouts: keep the prevailing wind in your face; navigate by the patches of moss growing on the northern bark; never stop moving in sovereign timber. You push yourself to sprint, legs burning, calves cramping from the strain of running barefoot over jagged rocks and tangled roots.

You crash through a wall of stinging nettles and dense hawthorn. Thorns rake across your ribs, your thighs, and your buttocks, leaving burning trails of fire along your flesh. The cold autumn wind begins to pierce the dense, humid canopy, chilling the sweat and sap upon your skin into an icy glaze.

Your lungs burn like raw coals. Your vision blurs at the edges as exhaustion claws at your legs, yet you drive your feet forward, ignoring the stones cutting into your arches.

Up ahead, through the dense screen of dark pine needles, you catch the faint, silvery gleam of open moonlight and the sharp, clean smell of cold peat and open moorland heather.

With a final, desperate burst of speed, you throw your shoulder through a heavy curtain of hanging moss, tear through a final wall of brambles, and burst out of the deep old-growth canopy into a wide, moonlit clearing on the edge of the border verge.

You collapse onto your hands and knees in the damp heather, gasping for air as your heart hammers against your ribs like a battering ram. The open sky stretches above you, vast, grey, and cold, dotted with distant, uncaring stars.

Behind you, the edge of the ancient forest looms like an impenetrable, towering wall of black timber. Standing among the dark trunks at the tree-line, the massive silhouette of the Elder dryad, Yewthara, watches you. She does not pursue. She simply stands in the shadow of the boughs, her glowing amber eyes piercing the gloom like two distant lanterns.

Beside her, Lashvine crouches in the branches, hissing in frustrated fury, while Knothollow and Peelbirch watch in cold silence.

Yewthara raises one massive hand, silencing her daughters. Her deep, resonant voice carries across the clearing on the chill night wind, vibrating with ancient certainty:

"You run on wounded heels, Aldric Wetherne. You flee back to your cold stone walls. But the sap of the Ashmere is already inside your blood. You carry our mark upon your flesh, and the wood never forgets its debt."

You drag yourself up from the heather, shivering violently with cold and exhaustion, looking out across the moonlit hollow toward the distant border ridge. 

You look down at your trembling hands. Your palms are smeared with black bog-mire, bright arterial blood, and sticky golden resin that clings stubbornly to your skin. The air out here on the open verge is bitter, smelling of frost, sheep-dung, and damp stone, yet whenever you draw breath, the ghost-scent of crushed sweetfern and warm walnut milk clings to the roof of your mouth. Your cock throbs against your belly, rigid and aching, pulsing in strange, unnatural harmony with the dark woods behind you.

You survived the bower. You broke the wood-fey's hold and proved the strength of your mortal will. Yet as you turn your back on the ancient trees and take your first limping stride toward the southern hills, the cold earth beneath your bleeding feet feels hollow and dead compared to the wild, living soil of the grove.
