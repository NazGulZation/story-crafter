# Chapter 13: The Drowned Crypts and the Eternal Iron Harvest

The bedside clock in Room 304 read a quarter past eleven.

The floor around King Halo’s study desk was littered with crumpled notebook paper, dried highlighter pens, and empty sparkling water cans. On the whiteboard easel, the earlier tactical drawings had been erased, replaced by an imposing, double-underlined column of numbers titled: `PROJECT METALLIC HEGEMONY: IRON REQUIREMENTS`.

King pulled her hair back into a low ponytail, her eyes sharp behind her blue-light glasses.

"Drink your tea," King commanded, her voice crackling with late-night intensity. "The preliminary phase is concluded. The bridge is secure. Our wargear is repaired. Now, we enter the Drowned Crypt."

At the base of their ancient treehouse, the party gathered upon the raised dirt causeway. 

The rain poured in relentless sheets, but their boots remained dry on the elevated stone embankment, far above the dark pools where leeches circled in vain.

Seventy yards down the stone path stood the entrance to the dungeon.

It was a colossal tomb constructed of dark, moss-slick granite blocks, sunk half-deep into the bog. Flanking the heavy iron-barred portcullis were two ancient bronze lanterns burning with eerie, undying green flame.

King Halo stepped forward. From her inventory, she selected the heavy brass key taken from the ashes of The Elder.

She pressed the interaction key.

*SKRRRRRRR-CLANG.*

With a screech of rusted iron that echoed through the swamp, the portcullis unlocked, sliding upward into the stone lintel.

A dungeon transition prompt appeared:

`Drowned Crypt`

The four avatars stepped down the dripping stone steps into the subterranean tomb.

The interior was a flooded, claustrophobic labyrinth of submerged granite corridors. Stagnant greenish water reached their avatars' knees. The air was thick with the scent of ancient damp silt and rusted metal.

Blocking the very first arched doorway stood a massive, five-foot-tall mound of petrified muck, calcified human bones, and twisted black metal:

`Muddy Scrap Pile`

"Obstruction identified!" Bakushin shouted, unholstering her Antler Pickaxe. "Class Representative initiating excavation!"

Bakushin swung her pickaxe into the mound.

*CHINK. CHINK. CRACK.*

Dense clots of black swamp peat and shattered rib bones fell away beneath the hardened antler tines. 

On the third strike, a heavy, jagged chunk of dark, oxidized metal rolled out of the muck onto the flooded stone floor with a dull metallic clink:

`Scrap Iron`

"IRON!" Gold Ship bellowed, scooping it up. "WE STRUCK OIL, BOYS! WE'RE RICH! GOLSHI CORPORATION IS GOING PUBLIC!"

"Do not celebrate prematurely, there are sentries behind the obstruction!" King warned, planting her Bronze Buckler directly in the narrow opening Bakushin had carved.

Through the hole in the muddy pile, two glowing red embers flared to life. A Draugr warrior thrust a rusted iron blade through the gap.

*CLAAANG!*

King’s buckler caught the strike on the rim, parrying the blade and staggering the Draugr backward into the flooded tomb chamber. 

Through the narrow opening, Urara and Bakushin thrust their spears in synchronized rhythm: *Thrust! Pierce!* The Draugr dissolved into a pile of entrails and leather scraps.

For forty-five minutes, the team worked like a seasoned siege crew. 

They cleared corridor after corridor, smashing through nine separate Muddy Scrap Piles, dodging poison gas clouds from crypt Blobs, and collecting forty-two heavy chunks of Scrap Iron, sixteen ancient withered bones, and two chests of rubies and silver coins.

"That's forty-two chunks!" Gold Ship crowed, her inventory bulging. "Let’s take the magic elevator home and turn this into shiny armor!"

Gold Ship and Haru Urara splashed out of the crypt, dashed across King's raised causeway, and bounded up the spiral staircase into the warmth of their treehouse.

Gold Ship walked straight toward the spinning, sapphire vortex of the `SWAMP_01` Portal.

She stepped into the light.

*BUZZZZZZZZZZZZT.*

A harsh, discordant rejection chime rang out through their headsets. A bright, glaring crimson error message flashed across the center of Gold Ship's screen:

`An item in your inventory prevents you from teleporting: Scrap Iron (x42)`

Gold Ship stopped dead. 

She took a step back, turned around, ran forward, and slammed her avatar into the portal again.

*BUZZZZZZZZZZZZT.*

`An item in your inventory prevents you from teleporting: Scrap Iron (x42)`

"WHAT?!" Gold Ship shrieked in real life, kicking King’s tea table so hard the pencils danced. "WHAT DO YOU MEAN IT PREVENTS ME?! I CARRIED IT UP TWENTY FLIGHTS OF STAIRS! LET ME IN! OPEN THE DOOR, YOU STUPID BLUE HOOP!"

"Wait," King Halo said, her fingers already clicking through the survival wiki on her secondary monitor. Her eyes tracked down a critical mechanics paragraph, and the color slowly drained from her face.

"King-chan?" Urara asked timidly, clutching her mouse. "Why won't the blue door let us go home?"

King slowly removed her glasses, pressing the bridge of her nose.

"Metal," King whispered, her voice trembling with rising disbelief. "Raw ore. Refined ingots. Scrap iron. Copper. Tin. The Allfather's magic... strictly rejects heavy metallurgy. Metals cannot pass through portals."

Silence descended upon Room 304.

Bakushin sat frozen. Gold Ship’s eye twitched violently.

"So..." Gold Ship began, her voice dropping into a deadly whisper. "You're saying... to get this iron back to our smelters in the Meadows..."

"We must haul it on foot," King said. "Down the tree. Across the causeway. Down to the ocean cove. Load it into the Karve. And sail it across the ocean."

"Gyahaha..." Gold Ship let out a hollow, manic laugh that sounded like a dying bird. "I love this game. I love it so much. It's so wholesome."

"And that," King added, picking up her dry-erase marker and turning to the whiteboard with terrifying executive resolve, "is merely the logistical prologue. Look at the ledger."

King began writing, the squeak of the marker loud in the silent room.

`[IRON CAPITAL BUDGET]`  
`- Iron Pickaxes (20 Iron x 4) = 80 Iron`  
`- Iron Bucklers / Banded Shields = 40 Iron`  
`- Full Iron Scale Mail Sets (60 Iron x 4) = 240 Iron`  
`- Iron Atgeir & Ancient Weapons = 60 Iron`  
`- Forge Toolrack & Smith's Anvil Upgrades = 35 Iron`  
`- Stonecutter = 2 Iron`  
`----------------------------------------------------`  
`TOTAL SCRAP IRON REQUIRED: 457 UNITS`

Bakushin stared at the final number. Her jaw dropped. Her soul visibly departed her body.

"Four hundred... and fifty-seven units?" Bakushin whispered, her voice cracking. "That is not a sprint. That is not even a marathon. That is an eternal cross-country death march through forty thousand furlongs of mud!"

"Indeed," King Halo declared, capping the marker with a sharp, decisive click. "Welcome to Valheim. Now pick up your pickaxes."

What followed was a masterclass in industrialized obsession.

The complaints dissolved into pure, manic teamwork. The four girls transformed their operations into a relentless, synchronized machine.

Bakushin and Gold Ship became the front-line demolition crew, alternating pickaxes as the tools wore down. 

King Halo established an emergency field workbench directly outside the crypt entrance, allowing the miners to repair their Antler Pickaxes instantly without losing a single second of labor.

Urara became the quartermaster, sorting ancient bones and leather scraps into designated crates, ensuring a continuous supply of fresh Poison Resistance Mead.

Then came the hauling.

Scrap Iron weighed ten kilograms per chunk. Carrying thirty chunks placed an avatar right on the razor edge of the 300-kilogram encumbrance threshold. 

They formed a living bucket brigade: walking in single file down the crypt steps, marching across King's raised stone causeway, climbing the coastal knoll, and packing every single pound of ore into the four cargo slots of the Karve moored in the cove.

When the hold was full to bursting with ninety-six chunks of raw iron, Gold Ship took the helm, King navigated the wind, and they sailed across the dark ocean swells back to the Meadows.

At King's Outpost, the Charcoal Kilns roared day and night. 

Black smoke billowed toward the stars. King fed coal and scrap into the twin stone smelters, watching with fierce, vindicated pride as the white-hot crucibles poured out solid, gleaming bars of pure, heavy black **Iron**.

*CLINK. CLINK. CLINK.*

Row upon row of black iron ingots filled the reinforced storage chests:

`Iron (x96)`

At the forge, the very first fruits of their labor materialized:

`Iron Pickaxe (Durability: 250 - Piercing: 33)`  
`Stonecutter (Building: Stone Walls, Stone Pavement)`  
`Iron Buckler (Block Armor: 40 - Parry Bonus: 2.5x)`

King Halo equipped the newly forged Iron Buckler. It was dark, heavy, rimmed in riveted steel, and carried the weight of an unbreakable fortress.

She walked out onto the longhouse porch.

The dormitory clock in Room 304 pointed to 1:30 AM.

Around the table, the exhaustion was absolute—and glorious.

Gold Ship lay slumped across two floor cushions, a peaceful, goofy grin on her face, muttering something about cargo manifests in her sleep. 

Bakushin sat leaned back in her chair, a warm, satisfied smile on her lips, her arms resting on her keyboard after mining through four separate crypts. 

Urara was already curled under her pink fleece blanket, clutching an empty mead bottle like a doll, breathing softly.

King Halo looked down at her screen. 

Her avatar stood clad in dark iron, bathed in the warm golden light of their longhouse hearth. Behind her, the Meadows were peaceful; far across the sea, the murky bog had become their mine. Their forge. Their domain.

King Halo reached out, picked up her fountain pen, and crossed off the final line on her whiteboard ledger.

She took a slow, refreshing sip of cool barley tea, set the glass down with a gentle chime, and clicked *Save and Exit*.

She turned off the desk lamp, pulled the heavy velvet curtains shut against the quiet campus night, and slipped beneath her duvet, resting her eyes with the deep, unshakeable satisfaction of a truly first-rate victory.
