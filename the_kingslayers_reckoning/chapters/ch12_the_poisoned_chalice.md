# The Poisoned Chalice

The Great Hall of the Margrave’s Keep smelled of crackling pine pitch, roasted haunch of wild boar, and spiced apple cider simmering in copper kettles.

Flambeau torches guttered in iron wall-sconces every eight paces, their orange glare washing across long tables of scrubbed ash-wood where the captains of the City Watch and the masters of the merchant guilds sat elbow to elbow. Outside the high lancet windows, the autumn gale drove sheets of rain against the leaded panes, rattling the iron casements with the relentless fury of the mountain heights. Yet inside the stone vaults, the atmosphere hummed with the noisy relief of men who believed their troubles were settled.

I sat at the right hand of Margrave Carrow, wearing a tailored doublet of dark grey wool over a fresh linen shirt, my plain steel arming sword resting across my knees in its oiled scabbard. 

Ten fingers flexed against the leather wrapping of the grip. Each knuckle moved cleanly, the tendon gliding over bone with sweet, unhindered perfection. In the Red Keep, I had spent years watching my golden stump catch on silk sleeves and velvet cloaks, a gilded monument to my own mutilation. Here, in the wiry shell of nineteen-year-old Theron Marrow, my sword hand was whole, callused, and swift. 

"Eat, Captain Marrow," Margrave Carrow said, his deep baritone cutting through the laughter of the guild-masters. The lord of Barrow-Gate looked more rested than he had in weeks. His greying beard was combed, and over his dark velvet tunic he wore the silver collar of his house, heavy with five-pointed stars. "The kitchens turned three pigs on the spit tonight. You earned your meat on the tournament sand."

"Boar is sweet enough, my lord," I replied, slicing a thick slab of crackling with my dagger. The grease coated my tongue, rich with garlic and dried rosemary, washed down with a swallow of dry, bitter cider. "Though I have seen men choke on sweeter feasts than this."

Across the table, Lady Aurelia leaned forward, resting her chin upon slender, ringless fingers. She wore a gown of hunter’s green wool trimmed with silver marten fur, her sharp hazel eyes catching the torchlight with unmistakable amusement. "Our champion suspects an ambush behind every roasted parsnip, Father. A true hedge-mercenary."

"A knight who stops looking for steel usually finds it between his shoulder blades, my lady," I said, meeting her gaze with an easy grin. "I spent twenty years guarding kings who thought their crowns made them immortal. Every one of them died in a puddle of blood."

The Margrave chuckled, raising an engraved silver chalice. "A grim philosophy, Captain, but a sound one for the man who holds the keys to my bastions." He turned to face the upper dais, rapping the pommel of his dagger against the oak boards until the hall fell silent.

The laughter died away. The carters, factors, and watch sergeants set down their trenchers, their eyes turning toward the high seat. 

At the far end of the dais, seated among a cluster of white-robed clerics, sat High Prelate Malakor of the Church of Sol-Aethel. 

The prelate had not touched his pork. He sat wrapped in three layers of heavy white samite embroidered with golden sunbursts, his fleshy jowls trembling with suppressed fury. On his liver-spotted fingers, amber and topaz rings clicked against the arms of his chair. Beside him stood Grand Inquisitor Justinian—a tall, ascetic fanatic with sunken cheeks, skin the color of dry parchment, and cold, pale eyes that stared at me with unblinking hatred. 

"Citizens of Barrow-Gate," Margrave Carrow proclaimed, his voice echoing off the limestone rafters. "For three seasons, the treasury of this realm has bled silver into the temple coffers under the pretext of divine defense. We paid for holy banners, blessed water, and golden armor for heroes who swore to protect our roads."

A low rumble of agreement passed through the merchant tables. Factor Vallow, sitting beside Deputy Brand with a leather ledger case on his knees, nodded in grim satisfaction.

"Today, on the sands of the Midsummer List," the Margrave continued, "we saw what that gold bought us. We saw sixty pounds of gilded brass buckle under the strike of an honest iron blade. The Church promised us a savior; they gave us a peacock who pissed his breeches before five thousand freemen."

A harsh burst of laughter erupted from the lower tables, where the teamsters and pikemen banged their wooden cups on the timber. 

High Prelate Malakor stiffened as though struck across the mouth, his jowls flushing a dark, congested purple. Justinian’s hand dropped to the hilt of the ceremonial dagger sheathed beneath his scarlet inquisitorial stole.

"Therefore," the Margrave declared, his voice hard as quarry granite, "the charter of the Marcher Territory is hereby amended. By municipal decree, the Church of Sol-Aethel’s exemption from the iron tax is revoked. The temple armories are placed under municipal oversight. And the command of the town bastions, the gate garrisons, and the Marcher Auxiliary is vested in Captain Theron Marrow."

The watch captains roared their approval, raising their flagons high. 

"To the Captain of the Bastions!" Sergeant Kester bellowed from the center aisle. "To the Lion of the List!"

Margrave Carrow smiled, lifting his chalice toward me. "Drink with me, Captain. Let the city see that steel and sovereign law govern Barrow-Gate."

The Margrave raised the silver cup to his lips and drained half its contents.

I lifted my own flagon of cider, but before the rim reached my mouth, the Margrave gasped.

It was a wet, strangulated sound, like water bubbling through crushed gravel.

The silver chalice slipped from the Margrave’s fingers, clattering across the oak tabletop. Dark purple wine spilled over the linen cloth, hissing faintly as it touched the salt-cellar. 

Margrave Carrow’s hands flew to his throat. His knuckles turned greyish-white; his eyes rolled back until only the bloodshot whites showed in the torchlight. A thick, yellowish foam broke from between his clenched teeth, carrying the sharp, chemical stench of burning sulfur and bitter almonds. His heavy body jerked violently, his heels drumming against the stone flags as his chair toppled backward.

"Father!" Lady Aurelia shrieked, lunging across the table to grasp his shoulders.

The hall dissolved into absolute pandemonium. Benches crashed to the floor as two hundred men surged to their feet, shouting in panic and drawing daggers.

"Poison!" Factor Vallow shouted, overturning his chair as he backed away. "The wine is poisoned!"

I dropped my flagon, vaulting over the high table in a single motion. My right hand caught the Margrave’s tunic before his head struck the floor, lowering his heavy frame onto the stone. His skin was burning hot, drenched in oily sweat, his pulse hammering against his neck like a trapped bird.

*Sun-Spite.*

I knew the symptoms before my mind finished the thought. In the royal courts of King's Landing, the Grand Maester kept locked cabinets of rare southern venoms. Sun-Spite was brewed from boiled yellow scorpion-tails and concentrated solar phosphorus. It boiled the blood from within, paralyzing the lungs in twenty heartbeats while leaving the victim locked in conscious agony.

"Draw steel!" a piercing voice thundered from the hall entrance.

The heavy oak doors of the banquet hall slammed open against the stone walls.

Fifty paladins of the Order of the Dawnguard marched into the hall in double ranks, their silver-plated kite shields locking together to form an impenetrable wall of steel across the entrance. Behind them strode sixty temple crossbowmen, their weapons cocked and leveled at the crowd, their steel quarrels glinting in the smoky glare.

At their head walked Grand Inquisitor Justinian, his scarlet stole billowing behind him like fresh blood. In his right hand he held an iron scroll-case sealed with the golden beeswax sunburst of the High Council.

"Treason!" Justinian shouted, his voice ringing through the cavernous vaults with chilling authority. "Sacrilegious murder in the hall of the realm!"

High Prelate Malakor rose from his seat on the dais, his trembling fingers raised to the rafters in theatrical despair. "The gods weep! The Margrave has fallen to the black sorcery of the pit! The hedge-mercenary Theron Marrow has struck down our sovereign lord with forbidden ash-curses!"

"Liar!" Lady Aurelia cried, standing over her father’s twitching body, her hand drawing a slim hunting dagger from her belt. "He drank from the cup your cup-bearer brought from the vestry! You poisoned him!"

"Silence the heretic's whore!" Justinian barked. He raised the scroll-case high. "By authority of the High Council of Sol-Aethel and the sacred canon of the Radiant Dawn, Barrow-Gate is placed under Holy Interdict! The civil charter is suspended! All magistrates, guild-masters, and watchmen are ordered to lay down their weapons under penalty of fire and excommunication!"

The double doors parted once more.

A figure stepped into the hall behind the wall of shields.

It was Evander Montfort.

The sight of him made the skin on my neck tighten with disgust. The boy had attempted to mend his shattered vanity with metal and paint. A cage of thin silver wires held his crooked nose straight, bolted directly into leather straps wrapped around his skull. His swollen jaw was bound with crimson silk, through which three carved bone false teeth poked like jagged tomb markers. His golden curls were shaved close to the bone, revealing jagged scabs where my boots had torn his scalp. 

Over his chest he wore a new cuirass of darkened steel inlaid with beaten electrum. His violet eyes burned with a wild, manic delirium—the frantic gaze of a fanatic who had traded his sanity for vengeance.

"Theron," Evander hissed through his wire-bound teeth, his voice a ragged whistle of spit and broken bone. "Did you think the tourney sand was the end of it? The Goddess has purged my weakness. I am named Lord Protector of the Faith."

He drew his six-foot electrum greatsword with a screech of polished metal. A sickly, yellow halo flared along the blade, pulsing with an unnatural, feverish luminescence that smelled of burnt brimstone.

"Seize the assassin!" Evander shrieked, pointing the glowing tip at my chest. "Cut his hands off! Burn the witch upon the sands!"

Twelve Dawnguard paladins surged forward, their heavy broadswords raised, closing the distance toward the high table.

"Kester! Brand!" I roared, drawing my three-foot arming blade in a clean arc that hissed through the smoky air. "Pantry doors! Move!"

Sergeant Kester did not hesitate. A veteran of twenty border skirmishes, he recognized an organized slaughter the moment the shields formed. He kicked a heavy serving trestle directly into the shins of the two lead paladins, sending them crashing to the flagstones in an iron tangle of limbs and plates.

Deputy Brand lunged forward with a heavy iron meat-spit seized from the carving hearth, driving the glowing point straight through the throat of a third paladin who stepped past the table. 

"Take the Margrave!" I commanded Lady Aurelia, catching the wrist of the fourth paladin as he lunged with a broadsword. 

I twisted his arm outward with the mechanical torque of my hips, feeling the joint dislocate with a dry crunch. Before the man could cry out, I drove the steel pommel of my sword into his cheek-guard, caving the plate inward and dropping him like an empty sack.

"We cannot carry him!" Aurelia shouted, her voice shaking as she knelt beside her father. "He cannot walk!"

"He lives for two hours if the venom stays in his gut," I snapped, parrying a descending blade with my plain crossguard, the impact ringing like a church bell. "If Justinian takes him, he dies in twenty seconds and you hang from the battlements by midnight. Grab his shoulders!"

Sergeant Kester and Deputy Brand hoisted the Margrave’s heavy body between them, their boots slipping on the spilled wine as they dragged him toward the narrow arched doorway that led to the buttery and the cellar stairs.

"Archers!" Justinian screamed from the rear of the hall. "Loose! Loose upon them!"

Crossbow strings slapped like whip-cracks. 

Three heavy quarrels slammed into the thick oak boards of the high table, splintering the timber inches from my shoulder. Another bolt took a fleeing city magistrate in the spine, driving him face-down into his trencher.

Evander charged up the dais, his glowing greatsword trailing yellow sparks across the tapestries. "Die, porter! Die in the light!"

He swung a massive, two-handed diagonal cleave meant to cut me in two. 

Without his divine balance corrections, the strike was wildly telegraphed. His left foot lagged behind his hip, his shoulders overcommitted to the forward swing. 

I did not invoke the Sovereign Duel. Sixty seconds of divine nullification was a weapon to be spent when an army stood between life and death, not on a panicked scuffle across a banquet floor. 

I stepped inside his arc, letting the glowing blade bury itself three inches deep into the oak lintel of the doorway. 

Before Evander could yank the steel free, I drove the heel of my left boot into his damaged knee.

The joint popped. Evander howled in agony, his grip slipping from the leather hilt as he collapsed onto the steps, clutching his leg with both hands.

"You never learned to clear your blade, boy," I muttered, turning on my heel and slipping through the narrow pantry door.

"Theron!" Evander screamed behind me, his voice echoing down the stone corridor in a frenzy of impotent rage. "You cannot hide in the dark! I will burn this city to ash! Every stone! Every child!"

I slammed the iron-studded oak door shut, throwing the heavy cross-bar into its stone sockets with a dull, final thud.

The hallway beyond was narrow, cold, and plunged into pitch darkness save for the sputtering tallow lamp held in Deputy Brand’s left hand. The air smelled of damp cellar earth, stored potatoes, and ancient limestone mortar. From the hall behind us came the muffled thunder of halberd butts smashing against the oak planks, accompanied by the muffled shouts of Justinian’s inquisitors.

"Where do we go, Captain?" Kester panted, his chest heaving as he supported the Margrave’s slack weight. "The main gates will be barred by now. The Dawnguard holds the courtyard."

"Down," I said, wiping a smear of the Margrave’s blood from my sleeve. "The old drainage flumes lead beneath the foundry quarter. The Church owns the altars, but the smelters own the dark."

I checked the edge of my blade against my thumb, sheathed the steel, and took the tallow lamp from Brand’s hand, leading our small company into the black belly of Barrow-Gate.
