# The Desperate Peacock

The Great Council Chamber of Barrow-Gate was built of dark basalt slabs carved directly into the mountain bedrock.

Iron braziers burned with fragrant juniper logs, their white smoke rising to blackened cedar beams hung with iron lanterns. A long council table of polished bog-oak dominated the room, covered in sheepskin campaign maps, tallow candles, brass dividers, and grain manifests. Outside the narrow lancet windows, the first winter sleet was turning to dry, stinging snow, dusting the rooftops of the High Ward in white.

At the head of the table sat Margrave Carrow, his greying beard neatly trimmed, wearing a heavy mantle of wolf-fur over his dark steel cuirass.

Beside him sat Lady Aurelia, dressed in dark hunter’s green wool trimmed with ermine, her sharp green eyes watching the gathered captains. Across from them sat the High Prelate of Sol-Aethel, wrapped in three layers of white silk, his jowls trembling with suppressed rage.

I sat at the foot of the table, wearing my newly commissioned captain’s brigandine of boiled leather and blackened steel links, with five thousand silver crowns safely deposited in the Syndicate vaults. 

Beside me sat Factor Vallow, his spectacles perched on his thin nose, running a quill over the army muster rolls.

For two hours, we had audited the realm’s winter reserves. 

The picture was grim. The mountain frosts had arrived three weeks early, locking the northern sheep pastures under four feet of drift. The eastern passes were already blocked; the iron smelters along the Black Ditch had barely ten days of charcoal remaining; and the highland clans were fleeing south before the frost-fiends of the Grey Spires.

"If the Citadel of Ashen-Crag falls before the winter blizzards seal the high defile," Margrave Carrow said, tapping a brass divider against a mountain pass on the map, "the entire northern march will be cut off until spring. Five mining towns, twelve stone quarries, and three thousand imperial subjects will be butchered in their beds."

The double oak doors swung open with a theatrical crash that rattled the candle flames.

Evander Montfort entered the council chamber.

The transformation since the Midsummer Tourney was stark. 

His magnificent golden plate had been replaced by a functional breastplate of blued steel, but his face told the true story of the sands. His broken nose had set crooked, thick and twisted like an old tavern brawler’s. His left jaw was swollen, three false teeth of carved bone visible when he spoke. 

His vanity was wounded, curdling into a desperate, feral arrogance.

Behind him shuffled Seraphina Kestrel, clutching her amber wand with twitching, chalk-white fingers, and two junior paladins with nervous eyes. 

Renata Fell was absent; her shattered wrist and broken ribs had left her bedridden in the Lower Ward infirmary. And Lyra Cole was nowhere to be seen—relegated to the baggage stables after Evander’s landlord seized their suite for back rent.

"You summoned me, Margrave," Evander said, his voice grating and thick through his broken nasal passage. Refusing a bow, he planted his hands on the oak table, glaring down the length of the room straight at me.

"Sit, Lord Montfort," Margrave Carrow said, his tone cold and flat as a slate slab. "The council has already begun."

"I will stand," Evander snapped. "The Church of the Radiant Dawn does not sit before ditch-mercenaries."

"You will stand or sit as you please," the Margrave replied without looking up from his map. "The scout riders returned from the northern border at dawn. The frost-hordes have breached the Skittering Gorge. The Citadel of Ashen-Crag is under siege. We need iron. We need discipline. And we need an army that can march through snow without dying of exposure."

The High Prelate cleared his throat, his soft, jeweled hands fluttering over his silk lapels.

"The Church has already declared this an S-rank crusade, Lord Carrow," the Prelate proclaimed in an oily, sanctimonious cadence. "The divine will of Sol-Aethel demands that her chosen champion lead the sovereign host. Lord Montfort will march at the head of five hundred temple retainers. He will plant the sunburst banner upon the citadel ramparts and cleanse the defile of heathen vermin."

"Lord Montfort could not hold his bladder on the tourney sand, Prelate," Lady Aurelia spoke, her voice dropping into the silent chamber like a dagger on ice.

Evander flinched as though whipped, his crooked nose turning bright red. "Lady Aurelia! The tourney was fouled by witchcraft! A coward's trick that snuffed my sacred light!"

"There was no witchcraft on the sand, boy," Margrave Carrow cut him off, slamming an iron-clad fist onto the sheepskin map. The brass dividers jumped three inches into the air. "I watched you fight. You swung that six-foot spit like an old woman with a broom. When the boy put steel to your throat, you begged for your life. That is bad fencing, cowardice, and worse breeding."

The chamber fell into a heavy, suffocating quiet. 

The temple paladins behind Evander looked at the floorboards, unable to meet the old lord's gaze.

"The crusade goes forward," the Margrave continued, his grey eyes sweeping the table. "Because the realm needs iron and grain, not because the temple demands glory. Two thousand conscripts, four hundred supply drays, and three companies of heavy pikes will march for Ashen-Crag tomorrow at second bell."

He turned his gaze toward me.

"Captain Marrow," Margrave Carrow said.

I leaned forward in my chair. "My lord."

"Factor Vallow informs me you brought twenty grain barges down the river without losing a single bushel of rye," the Margrave said. "And my veterans tell me you understand supply lines, pickets, and marching orders better than any captain who ever wore a badge in this town."

He pulled an iron gorget stamped with the Margraviate’s falcon crest from a wooden tray and slid it across the oak table.

"You are appointed Captain of the Marcher Vanguard and Commissary-General of the Sovereign Host," Margrave Carrow declared. "You command the scout screens, the supply trains, and the four hundred auxiliary pikes. If a wagon axle breaks, you mend it. If an officer steals salt, you hang him. The army feeds from your hand."

The High Prelate gasped in horror, rising from his chair with trembling silk sleeves. "Margrave! This is an insult to the Holy See! To give sovereign command of the commissary to a common unranked wanderer—"

"A common wanderer who knows how to hold an edge and count oat sacks," the Margrave barked. "Sit down, Prelate, before I audit your tithe ledgers for the last seven years."

The Prelate collapsed into his chair, his jowls quivering, shutting his mouth tight.

Evander slammed both fists onto the table, his violet eyes bulging with fury.

"I will not take orders from him!" Evander snarled, spit flying from his swollen lips. "I am an S-rank champion! The Goddess herself whispers in my blood! If you give this ditch-dog the baggage train, keep your pikes! Keep your wagons! My temple vanguard will march ahead! We will take the mountain road on fast horses! We will breach Ashen-Crag and claim the relic citadel before his slow, creaking carts even reach the foothills!"

I looked at him across the table.

His armor was light; his men had no winter cloaks; his horses were corn-fed southern chargers unaccustomed to mountain ice. He had no pack-mules, no salt rations, no trenching tools, and no mountain scouts. He was marching into a sub-zero blizzard at the onset of a northern winter with nothing save golden plumes and a dying drug-addicted sorceress.

It was the exact folly that cost Prince Rhaegar his army at the Trident. 

A hero who believed his own songs was the most dangerous creature on a battlefield—not to the enemy, but to the poor bastards who marched behind him.

"Take the high road, then, boy," I told him in a mild, conversational drawl. "March your white horses into the snow. The frost-trolls of the Grey Spires will enjoy the tender meat."

Evander laughed—a harsh, barking noise that failed to conceal the desperation in his chest.

"Enjoy hauling your flour sacks, Theron," Evander spat, spinning on his heel. "When you reach Ashen-Crag, my sunburst banner will already be flying from the highest tower! And you will be sweeping the horse dung from my courtyard!"

He stormed out of the chamber, his white cape dragging on the stone flags, the door slamming shut behind him.

Margrave Carrow watched the doorway for three heartbeats, then let out a slow, tired sigh that smelled of old wine and pipe-weed.

"The boy is an idiot," the Margrave muttered.

"The boy is a corpse who hasn't stopped talking yet," I corrected him quietly.

Lady Aurelia rose from her chair, walking slowly to my side of the table, her green eyes locked onto mine. She held a heavy vellum writ stamped with the green falcon seal of House Carrow.

"This is the sovereign warrant for the commissary," Lady Aurelia said, handing me the vellum with a firm, elegant grip. "It grants you summary power of the high justice on the march. If any guild factor or temple officer attempts to commandeer your bread or disrupt your lines, hang them from the nearest timber."

"I am familiar with hanging thieves, my lady," I said smoothly, taking the vellum.

"I know you are," she murmured, a faint, dangerous smile playing upon her red lips. "Come back from the mountain with the army intact, Captain Marrow. When you return, the High Ward will have need of an iron hand."

I dipped my chin in a polite, aristocratic bow. "The vanguard marches at dawn."
