# The Fall of the Sun-Gilded

Ten thousand people ringed the Great Forum of the Cathedral of Sol-Aethel under a sky the color of hammered pewter.

The city of Barrow-Gate had emptied itself into the cold winter air. Guild-masters in fur-trimmed cloaks, iron-smiths with soot-stained aprons, carters, teamsters, tavern maids, and highborn marcher nobility stood packed shoulder-to-shoulder behind the double cordon of city watchmen. Four hundred marcher pikes formed an iron ring around the central dueling ground—an eighty-pace circle of polished white marble flags that had been swept clear of snow and bordered by eight massive iron braziers roaring with burning birch logs and pitch.

The air smelled of burning resin, cold stone, sweat, and the sharp, metallic tang of expectant violence.

I stood in the southern corner of the circle, resting my weight on my right heel.

My kit was the same practical gear I had worn through the blizzards of the Grey Spires: a blackened iron kettle-helm with a horsehair chin-strap, a riveted steel brigandine lined with boiled ox-hide, greaves of plain grey iron, and a round buckler faced with hardened ash and rimmed with double-folded steel. In my right hand I held my arming sword. The three feet of steel had been ground clean on the armory wheel; its double edges caught the dull grey light like a ribbon of water.

My right fingers flexed around the wire-bound leather grip. 

The hand was whole. It was warm. It possessed every ounce of the terrifying, effortless lethality that had once made Kingsguard knights drop their eyes in the yard of the Red Keep. Beneath my breastplate, resting against the skin above my heart, the soul-brand of the Anvil of Ash thrummed with a slow, heavy pulse like an iron piston waiting for steam.

Across the circle, the golden gates of the cathedral portico swung wide.

A fanfare of six long electrum trumpets sounded from the cathedral balcony, their brassy notes splitting the frost-laden air.

Evander Montfort stepped out onto the marble flags.

The crowd let out a collective gasp that rippled through the forum like a sudden gust of wind.

The boy was no longer dressed as a mortal man. He wore full ceremonial plate crafted of beaten electrum and white silver, each fluted ridge inlaid with radiant gold filigree. The breastplate depicted the golden sunburst of Sol-Aethel, its rays extending down his greaves and along his vambraces. His golden curls tumbled over his gorget in gleaming waves, washed and scented with jasmine oil.

His eyes, however, revealed the truth.

They were not human eyes. They glowed with an eerie, liquid gold fire that pulsed in time with the veins on his temples. The skin around his eye sockets was bruised dark purple, swollen and distended. In his left hand he held an empty crystal phial that dropped from his fingers and shattered against the marble flags with a musical chime.

The boy had drunk the *Solar Tear*—the forbidden draught of the Dawn, a lethal concentrate of divine quintessence that burned the consumer’s lifeforce into an uncontrollable blaze of magical fury.

Steam rose from the plates of his electrum armor in thick, hissing plumes. The falling snowflakes that drifted toward him evaporated six feet away with sharp pops of steam. 

In his right hand, Evander raised his six-foot electrum greatsword.

The blade ignited with a roar.

A torrent of blinding golden fire engulfed the steel, leaping five feet into the air. The intense heat radiated across the eighty-pace circle, hitting the front row of spectators like the blast from an iron smelter, forcing the front ranks to shield their faces with their cloaks. The white marble flags beneath his iron-shod boots began to hiss and crack, spiderwebs of black fissures spreading across the stone from the radiant heat.

"Behold the judgment of the Dawn!" High Prelate Malakor cried from the cathedral balcony, raising his electrum staff. "Let the light cleanse the heretic from the earth!"

Evander stepped forward into the circle. His gait was strange—jerky, unnaturally fast, his muscles spasming beneath the silver plate as the divine drug raced through his blood. His broken nose gave his face the twisted, feral look of an enraged hound.

"Theron!" Evander roared, his voice layered with a strange, harmonic resonance that made the teeth in my skull ache. "You crawled out of the ditch! You dared look upon my glory with insolent eyes! I will burn your bones to white ash! I will scorch your very soul into nothingness!"

I did not answer. I settled my kettle-helm, checked the strap on my left buckler, and raised my three feet of plain iron into a balanced mid-guard.

*Ding-dong.*

The cathedral bell struck noon.

Evander moved.

He covered forty paces in two heartbeats, a blur of golden light and burning heat that left scorch marks across the marble flags. He brought the massive six-foot greatsword down in a sweeping overhead arc designed to cleave me and my armor in half with pure magical destruction.

*The Cleave of the Sun.*

The air screamed as the burning blade descended, trailing a crescent of liquid solar fire that threatened to vaporize the stone.

I did not attempt to catch the blow on my buckler. Meeting seventy pounds of enchanted steel and magical flame with three pounds of ash-wood is the act of a dead man.

I pivoted on my right heel, stepping sharply to the inside of his leading foot.

The burning greatsword crashed into the marble flags where I had stood a fraction of a second before.

*BOOM.*

The impact shattered three marble flags into smoking rubble, throwing up a shower of white dust and glowing embers. The wave of displaced air tore at my cloak.

Before Evander could recover the momentum of his heavy swing, I stepped into his guard.

My arming sword flicked out like an adder's tongue.

The point struck the gap beneath his left shoulder cop, penetrating through the velvet arming doublet into the flesh of his armpit. 

The steel bit three inches deep.

Evander screamed—a sharp, shrill yelp of mortal pain that pierced through his divine bluster. Hot blood squirted over my crossguard, sizzling as it hit his heated armor.

He spun wildly, backhanding with his armored left gauntlet to crush my face.

I caught the backhand on the canted rim of my buckler, deflecting the blow upward into the empty air, and brought my right knee violently into his groin. 

The electrum tassets absorbed some of the impact, but the blunt force drove him back two paces, his boots skidding across the slippery marble.

"Die!" Evander shrieked, spitting blood and froth.

He swung the greatsword in a frantic, horizontal scythe of blinding fire. 

The heat singed the horsehair crest of my helm; the blinding brilliance burned afterimages into my retinas. I gave ground with disciplined, measured strides, parrying the outer edges of his slashes with the iron rim of my buckler, letting the boy exhaust his drug-fueled fury. Every strike he threw was wild, overcommitted, and telegraphed by the twitch of his golden shoulders. He fought like an enraged tavern brawler who had been given the power of an erupting volcano.

"Is this all your Goddess gave you, boy?" I asked, ducking beneath a sweeping slash that severed a bronze candlestick on the perimeter. "A loud voice, a hot sword, and the footwork of a drunken ox?"

"I am a god!" Evander howled.

He leaped backward, planting both boots on the cracked marble, and raised the glowing greatsword high above his head with both hands.

The solar fire around the blade swelled to twice its size. A sphere of blinding white light gathered at the tip, roaring like an open furnace draft. The marble flags around his boots began to melt into glowing slag. The spectators screamed in panic, falling back against the pikemen as the heat grew unbearable.

*He is channeling the Solar Pillar,* my mind registered with cold clarity. *He intends to vaporize the entire square.*

I closed the distance.

I took three long, deliberate strides forward across the smoking stone.

My left hand reached inward, touching the cold iron brand burned into my skin beneath my breastplate.

I released the lock.

*The Sovereign Duel.*

A dull, grey ring of absolute void expanded from my chest with the sound of a closing iron vault door.

*CLANG.*

The effect was instantaneous and terrifying in its finality.

The sixty-foot circle turned the color of cold hearth-ash. 

The blinding white light vanished. 

The roaring golden fire around Evander’s blade died with a pathetic, choking hiss, like a tallow candle dropped into a bucket of slops. 

The supernatural heat evaporated from the air, replaced instantly by the bitter, freezing mountain wind of Barrow-Gate. The liquid gold glow in Evander’s eyes snuffed out, leaving two pale, bloodshot pupils staring in paralyzed horror at the dead weapon in his hands.

The divine elixir’s enhancement ceased. 

Instantly, the full, crushing weight of reality crashed down upon the boy.

Without his Goddess’s blessing, without the supernatural strength of the Dawn, the six-foot electrum greatsword was an eighty-pound iron bar.

The tip of the massive blade plummeted downward, dragging Evander forward off-balance. His back muscles seized in violent, screaming agony under the sudden, brutal leverage.

"My... my light..." Evander gasped, his face draining of all color, his lips turning blue. "Where... where is my light?"

"Sixty seconds, Lord Montfort," I whispered, stepping into his reach. "Just you and me. And sixty seconds of plain iron."

I moved with the clinical, merciless economy of Ser Jaime Lannister.

I executed the three-strike rhythm that Ser Arthur Dayne had taught me thirty years ago on the tourney grounds of King's Landing.

*Strike one.*

I stepped to his blind side, brought the steel pommel of my arming sword around in a short, brutal arc, and smashed it squarely into Evander’s mouth.

*CRUNCH.*

The iron ball crushed his lips against his teeth, splintering his front incisors into bloody fragments and snapping his already-broken nose sideways across his cheek. Blood and white bone sprayed across his electrum gorget.

Evander staggered backward, dropping the greatsword onto the marble with a dull, clattering crash that resonated across the silent square.

*Strike two.*

As he stumbled, his right arm flailed out to regain his balance.

My blade whipped through the cold air in a flat, horizontal cut.

The edge took him clean at the crook of his right elbow, biting through the leather straps and severing the bicep tendon and brachial artery with the clean, effortless precision of a surgeon’s scalpel.

Blood gushed from his right arm in a thick crimson arc.

The limb went dead, hanging limp and useless at his side, the fingers curling into rigid claws as the nerves parted.

Evander shrieked—a high, piercing wail of absolute despair that echoed off the cathedral towers. He clutched at his severed arm with his left hand, hot blood pouring through his trembling fingers, soaking his ivory tunic in dark, spreading gore.

*Strike three.*

I did not stop. 

I drove the heavy steel rim of my buckler forward with all the weight of my shoulders, striking his right knee directly from the front.

*SNAP.*

The patella shattered like dry pine kindling. The joint folded backward with a sickening crack.

Evander collapsed.

The great champion of the Dawn hit the marble flags on his hands and knees, groveling in his own blood and vomit, shivering violently as the freezing winter wind bit into his wounds.

The forum was dead silent. Ten thousand people watched without breathing, paralyzed by the sudden, absolute destruction of their golden idol. 

High Prelate Malakor stood on the balcony, his jaw slack, his electrum staff trembling in his withered hands.

Evander looked up through the curtain of his bloody, ruined curls.

His face was a grotesque ruin of swollen purple flesh, shattered teeth, and leaking mucus. The arrogance, the vanity, the golden majesty—all of it had been stripped away, leaving only a terrified, broken boy drowning in his own blood.

"Please..." Evander whimpered, his voice a pathetic, bubbling rattle. "Please, Theron... I am of noble blood... House Montfort will pay... ten thousand silver marks... twenty thousand... spare my life... please, by the gods, spare me..."

He tried to crawl backward on his one good knee, dragging his severed arm through the slush, leaving a red trail across the white stone.

I walked up to him.

The grey ash-light of the Sovereign Duel was fading, the sixty seconds running out like sand through an hourglass. 

I looked down at the boy with the cold, pitiless contempt of a lion looking at a mangy jackal that had barked too loud in the tall grass.

"You stole a boy's party," I told him, my voice carrying clearly to the front rows of the crowd. "You stole his women. You laughed in your warm tent while he froze in the mud. You hacked off your own soldier's fingers to save your horse. And you hid behind a Goddess because you never had the stomach to be a real man."

I reversed my grip on the hilt of my arming sword.

"A sword is not an ornament for a handsome fool," I said. "And iron does not care about your golden curls."

I drove the three feet of plain steel straight down.

The point found the seam in his electrum breastplate, punched through the silver-plated steel, pierced his sternum, and drove through his heart, pinning him to the marble flags of the cathedral forum.

Evander Montfort arched his back. His eyes bulged; a single, choking gasp escaped his shattered lips; his good left hand clawed weakly at the blade.

Then, his fingers went slack.

His head fell sideways into the red slush.

The champion of the Dawn was dead.

The Sovereign Duel collapsed. The cold grey void vanished, leaving only the real winter day and the quiet whisper of falling snow.

Silence hung over the Great Forum of Barrow-Gate.

I reached down with my right hand, grasped the leather hilt of my sword, and pulled the steel free with a wet, scraping sound.

I wiped the blood from both sides of the blade using the hem of Evander’s fine ivory cloak. 

I slid the clean iron into its scabbard.

I turned toward the cathedral balcony.

High Prelate Malakor met my gaze for three seconds, then turned and fled into the dark interior of the cathedral, his robes trailing behind him like the feathers of a frightened vulture.

From the northern side of the forum, Margrave Carrow stepped through the pike line, flanked by sixty armored marcher men-at-arms.

The old lord looked down at the corpse of the hero, then looked at me, his weathered face breaking into a slow, grim nod of profound respect.

"The judgment of iron is delivered," Carrow proclaimed in a voice that reached every corner of the square. "The field belongs to Captain Marrow."

A cheer began among the marcher pikemen. It spread to the teamsters, to the carters, to the guild-masters, until ten thousand voices roared into the grey winter sky, shaking the snow from the cathedral eaves.

I did not wave to the crowd. I did not smile.

I took off my kettle-helm, tucked it under my left arm, and walked toward the gate of the Margrave's citadel to see to the garrison's evening muster.
