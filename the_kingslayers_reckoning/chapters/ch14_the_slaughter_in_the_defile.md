# The Slaughter in the Defile

Temple firebolts burst against the wet basalt cliffs in blinding sprays of crimson sparks, painting the mountain mist with the harsh color of fresh blood. 

The high, whistling screams of highland frost-fiends echoed through the swirling blizzard flakes, accompanied by the sickening crunch of iron plate being ripped from living bone. The air tasted of sulfur, frozen blood, and horse bile. Sub-zero winds roared through the narrow mouth of Black-Mist Gorge, whipping sleet into our faces like handfuls of river gravel. Every breath burned the throat with the dry sting of mountain ice.

Out of the freezing fog galloped the vanguard.

A stampede of terrified animals broke from the gorge, the shattered remnants of an army that had forgotten the meaning of discipline.

Forty temple paladins rode with their heads down, their white capes torn to bloody rags, their silvered armor dented and smeared with black frost-grease. Half had discarded their broadswords to cling frantically to the manes of their blowing horses. Behind them ran sixty dismounted conscripts, slipping and screaming on the black ice, pursued by towering, white-furred shapes that bounded across the scree with terrifying speed. Pack mules with broken hams dragged their loads through the snow, braying in agony as iron-hard claws tore their flanks open from behind.

At the head of the rout rode Evander Montfort.

His grand white charger was covered in foam and crimson lather, its eyes rolling white as its iron shoes struck sparks from the frozen rocks. Evander had lost his crested tourney helm; his golden curls were matted with frozen mud and dark gore; his crooked, broken nose was leaking yellow mucus and blood down his chin. The fine velvet collar of his tunic was torn open, exposing a chest heaving with frantic, animal panic.

His six-foot electrum greatsword was sheathed, untouched at his hip. The polished gold filigree along the scabbard was spattered with common mud and ditch-water.

In his right hand he held a heavy iron skinning blade.

As a wounded junior paladin ran beside his stirrup, reaching out a bloody hand to grasp his leather saddle-fender, crying for help, Evander ignored the plea.

He slashed downward with the skinning knife.

The iron blade severed the paladin’s fingers. The wounded soldier screamed, pitching headfirst into the shale, trampled instantly under the hooves of the horses behind him. The boy's body rolled into the wheel-rut, crushed beneath the iron-shod wheels of an abandoned cart.

"Out of my way!" Evander shrieked, his voice cracking in shrill, hysterical madness. "The pass is lost! Out of my way, you cattle!"

He rode straight for the opening in our wagon wall, eyes wild, foaming at the mouth, blind to everything save the open road to the south.

"Pikes!" I bellowed, stepping onto the tongue of the central ore cart. "Hold the line! Level weapons!"

Two hundred ash-wood pikes dropped with a synchronized, iron clatter, presenting an impenetrable hedge of twelve-foot steel points across the opening. The butt-spikes dug deep into the frozen shale, braced by the boots of eighty marcher veterans whose faces were weathered like old oak bark.

Evander hauled back on his reins with a frantic yank that tore the charger’s mouth, the stallion skidding on its haunches, showering my boots in loose shale and frozen gravel.

"Theron!" Evander screamed, spit flying from his swollen lips. "Drop the pikes! Open the wall! I am the chosen champion of the Dawn! Let me through!"

"Where are your five hundred men, Lord Montfort?" I asked, looking down from the wagon bed, my arming sword resting point-down on the oak boards. "Where is your rear guard? Where is the Holy Vanguard of the Sun?"

"They are dead!" Evander wailed, his eyes darting toward the swirling grey fog behind him. "The fiends... they came from the cliffs... thousands of them! My light could not burn them in the freezing mist! The frost chilled my blood! Drop the pikes, you peasant bastard, or I will have the Margrave hang you from the battlements!"

"A captain stays with his command," I said, my voice cold as the ice beneath his horse's hooves. "You left your rear guard to be slaughtered. You butchered your own man's fingers to save your saddle. You will find no entry here."

"You dare—" Evander began, reaching for the hilt of his greatsword with trembling, shaking fingers. "I command this crusade! I am blessed by the Goddess herself! You are a ditch-born carter, nothing more! Move these spears or I will cut you down where you stand!"

"Sergeant Kester," I commanded without shifting my gaze. "If his horse takes another step forward, put a crossbow bolt through the beast's skull."

Sergeant Kester stepped to the wagon rail, bringing his heavy stirrup-crossbow to his shoulder, the square iron quarrel aimed dead between the white charger's eyes. The steel cord creaked under forty pounds of tension. Beside him, six more marcher marksmen cocked their levers, iron points tracking the hero's gilded chest.

Evander looked at the iron bolt. He looked at the wall of two hundred pikes. He looked at my cold, unblinking grey eyes.

The hero broke.

A strangled, animal whimper escaped his throat. 

Wheeling his white charger violently to the right, he spurred the bleeding beast into the narrow sheep path that skirted the outer gorge, fleeing south down the mountain alone, disappearing into the swirling white blizzard like a kicked cur.

The temple survivors who reached the wagons fell upon their knees before the pikes, sobbing, throwing their weapons into the snow, begging for mercy.

"Open the ranks," I ordered. "Pull the wounded inside. Disarm them. Shove them behind the third wagon line."

The pikes parted with disciplined precision.

Eighty shivering, blood-soaked survivors stumbled into the safety of the laager, collapsing onto the frozen shale behind the wagons. Teamsters threw wool horse-blankets over their shaking shoulders, stripping their ruined cuirasses and stacking their dented swords in the salvage cart.

Then came the horde.

Out of the Black-Mist Gorge poured two hundred highland frost-fiends.

They were monstrous, ape-like brutes, nine feet tall, their bodies covered in thick, matted coats of white fur caked with blood and ice. Their arms were long, corded with grey muscle thick as timber logs, ending in five curved iron talons that dragged in the snow. From their heavy, sloping jaws protruded two yellow tusks, each curved like a scythe blade. They smelled of rotten tallow, frozen entrails, and wet musk.

They charged in a roaring, howling wave, brandishing rough clubs of shattered basalt and frozen pine logs.

"Crossbowmen!" I shouted, raising my right hand. "Wait for the chalk line! Fifty paces!"

The frost-fiends closed the distance with terrifying speed, their heavy feet shaking the frozen earth. Their guttering roars bounced off the sheer granite canyon walls, multiplying into a deafening thunder that drowned the wind.

Forty paces. Thirty paces.

"Loose!"

*THWACK-THWACK-THWACK.*

Eighty heavy crossbows snapped simultaneously.

The volley was devastating.

At thirty paces, the square iron quarrels tore through thick white fur and muscle like dry linen. The front rank of six fiends collapsed instantly, their chests pierced by three bolts each, their massive bodies tumbling end-over-end across the shale, tripping the beasts behind them.

"Second rank, loose!" Kester bellowed.

A second wave of eighty iron bolts slammed into the charging mass.

Dozens more screamed in agony, clutching at shattered knees and pierced throats, black blood spraying across the white snow. The smell of hot gore steamed in the sub-zero air.

The remaining horde hit the wagon wall.

*CRASH.*

The impact struck like a battering ram against an oak gate.

Three nine-foot brutes hurled their bodies against the oak sideboards of the lead cart. The heavy hemp lashings groaned; the iron ingots shifted with a dull, grinding clang; but the wheels—dug six inches into the frozen shale and braced with iron stakes—held fast.

"Thrust!" I commanded.

From behind the wooden sideboards, eighty marcher pikemen drove their steel points forward in a disciplined, rhythmic slaughter.

*Thrust. Recover. Thrust.*

Twelve-foot ash shafts punched into white-furred bellies, through leather-hard hide, through throats and eyes. The screaming of the impaled beasts was deafening, a chorus of bestial fury and dying rage.

"Captain!" Kester shouted from the left wagon, pointing an iron-clad glove toward the western rim. "The scree! Look to the shelf!"

High above the gorge neck, a pack of twenty frost-fiends was clambering across the jagged granite shelf that hung above our right flank. 

They moved on all fours like bloated white spiders, digging their iron talons into the fissures of the cliff. They carried massive boulders ripped from the crag, aiming to scramble above our wagon revetment and drop down directly into the baggage lines, crushing the teamsters and team oxen from the rear. If they took the high rock, our barricade would be enveloped in minutes.

"Kester! Keep the pikes locked on the neck!" I shouted, leaping down from the ore cart into the slush. "Give them three volleys on reload! Do not let them turn the wheels!"

"Aye, Captain!" Kester roared back, his voice cutting through the din of breaking bones. "Third rank, span bows! Front rank, set butt-spikes!"

I turned to the reserve company standing in the snow behind the supply drays.

"Twenty halberds, on me!" I barked. "Shields up! Take the ledge!"

Twenty marcher veterans in boiled leather and iron mail gripped their seven-foot halberds, stepping out from the wagon shadow with steady, heavy steps. We scrambled up the icy sheep path that cut into the western cliff face. Loose shale rattled down behind our boots; the wind tore at our woolen mantles; the steep granite face was coated in slick black ice.

At the crest of the shelf, four frost-fiends crested the rock, brandishing jagged boulders the size of beer casks.

The lead brute snarled, raising a three-hundred-pound slab of granite over its horned brow, intending to hurl it down into the mass of archers below.

I closed the distance at a dead run across the frost-slick stone.

My boots found purchase on a shelf of frozen quartz. I ducked beneath the brute's upward reach, stepping directly inside the arc of its hairy arms, and drove the point of my arming sword straight through the soft flesh behind its collarbone.

The steel sank six inches into the chest cavity. 

The creature roared, dropping the boulder onto its own foot with a sickening crunch of shattered bones. It swung a massive, clawed left paw toward my neck.

I twisted the blade, ripped the edge sideways across its throat, and kicked its knee with my iron-toed boot. 

The brute’s leg gave way. With a wet gurgle, it tumbled backward off the precipice, screaming as it plummeted eighty feet onto the sharp basalt boulders below.

"Halberds, hook and cleave!" I shouted to the men behind me. "Clear the rim!"

The marcher veterans formed a tight, three-man wedge along the narrow ledge. Their halberd hooks bit deep into white shanks and hairy arms. When a brute lunged with its scythe-tusks, two steel hooks dragged its snout down into the shale, while a third man brought the heavy axe-blade down across the neck vertebrae with the dull, butcher’s thud of split bone.

For three minutes of frantic, bloody exertion on the narrow granite shelf, we hacked and thrust. 

A beast lunged at my chest, its talons grazing the iron plates of my brigandine, leaving four deep silver scratches in the blackened metal. I parried the sweeping claw with my buckler, pivoted on my right heel, and drove the three-foot blade upward beneath its jaw. The point entered the mouth, drove through the palate, and pierced the brain. 

I hauled the steel free as the carcass collapsed like a felled pine.

The remaining fiends on the shelf broke. Terrified by the cold, mechanical butchery of the halberd line, the five survivors leaped wildly from the rocks, sliding and tumbling down the scree slope, fleeing back into the gorge mist.

"Shelf is secure!" the halberd corporal shouted, panting hard, wiping black bile from his visor.

"Hold the rim!" I called down. "Cover the ditch!"

Below us, at the central wagon barricade, the roar of battle reached a fever pitch.

A massive alpha fiend—ten feet tall, its face scarred by old fire, missing its left tusk, its thick white fur caked with frozen gore—had leaped clear over the tongue of the central freight wagon. It landed inside the laager with an earth-shattering thud that shook the shale.

It swung a six-foot club of solid basalt with both hands.

The blow caught two marcher pikemen, shattering their wooden shafts, splintering their ribs, and throwing their broken bodies ten paces across the snow.

The line faltered. A dangerous gap opened in the central defense. Three more fiends prepared to vault the sideboard into the opening.

I scrambled down the sheep path, my boots skidding down the shale slope, landing in the snow inside the laager just as the alpha brute turned to face the baggage train.

The creature roared, a foul spray of hot saliva hitting my kettle-helm, its single scythe-tusk gleaming in the torchlight. It caught sight of me, rolled its yellow eyes, and brought the six-foot basalt club overhead in a crushing, two-handed downward smash intended to pulp my skull.

I held my ground.

I stepped forward into the arc of the strike, closing the distance before the heavy stone club could reach maximum velocity. 

I raised my iron-rimmed buckler at a sharp, forty-five-degree slant, meeting the wooden haft just below the stone head.

*CRACK.*

The impact jarred my teeth in my skull, sending a burning shockwave down the bone of my left forearm. The wood of my buckler splintered, but the steep angle guided the stone head into the frozen dirt beside my boots.

The brute overcommitted, its chest exposed.

My right arm fired like a piston.

The three feet of plain iron drove upward in a textbook knight's thrust. 

The blade punched through the matted fur beneath the creature's chin, slicing through the windpipe, penetrating deep into the base of the skull. 

The beast’s eyes rolled white. It stiffened into a rigid arch, its hot blood gushing over the crossguard of my sword, soaking my leather gauntlet in thick, steaming gore.

With a sharp wrench, I twisted the steel and kicked the corpse backward over the wagon tongue.

The ten-foot carcass crashed into the charging fiends behind it, blocking the breach with its massive bulk.

"Pikes forward!" I roared, wiping the black gore from my eyes with my sleeve. "Close the gap! Drive them back!"

The marcher pikemen surged forward with a furious roar, their iron points bristling through the gap, turning the breach into a wall of impaling steel.

Crossbows fired continuously from the wagon beds, raining iron quarrels into the packed ranks outside the wall.

For ten minutes, the butchery was relentless. 

The frost-fiends hurled themselves against the wagon revetment, only to be impaled on pikes, broken by crossbows, and cleaved by short-swords. The narrow defile became an abattoir of white fur, shattered rock, and steaming black blood.

Then, the mountain wind screamed.

A sudden, freezing blizzard blast tore down from the Grey Spires, howling through the gorge with sub-zero fury. 

The surviving fifty fiends hesitated. Their alpha was dead; their charges had shattered against the iron wall; eighty of their kin lay butchered in the snow; the high shelf was held by disciplined halberdiers.

With low, guttural howls of frustration, the survivors turned and fled back into the swirling white fog of the gorge, abandoning the field.

Silence returned to the mouth of Black-Mist Gorge.

The only sound was the howling of the wind, the crackle of pitch torches, and the labored gasping of two thousand men leaning on their weapons.

I stood upon the front wagon tongue, my boots coated in black blood, my breathing steady.

Not a single wagon had been lost. The sovereign host stood unbroken.

Master Kroll walked up beside me, holding a lantern, his walrus mustache trembling as he looked out over the field of eighty slaughtered fiends.

"Gods in heaven, Captain," Kroll whispered, his voice shaking with awe. "Two hundred of them... and you broke them with a wagon wall and eighty pikes."

"A wall of iron beats a herd of wild beasts every day of the week," I replied, sliding my blood-smeared blade into its scabbard. "Post double pickets on the flanks. Tend the wounded. And bring the prisoners to the command pavilion."

I turned my back on the frozen defile and walked toward the firelight of the camp.
