# The Eclipse of the Sun-Gilded

The Grand Forum before the Cathedral of Sol-Aethel was an ocean of white marble flags, iron pikes, and ten thousand silent faces.

Midday sun broke through the retreating storm clouds in cold, pale shafts of light that illuminated the cathedral’s towering limestone facade. High above the colonnade, the colossal golden sunburst mounted over the bronze doors gleamed with false splendor, casting long, sharp shadows across the square. But the pomp and holy majesty of the priesthood had vanished. 

Surrounding the perimeter of the plaza stood six hundred marcher spearmen, four companies of city watchmen, and three hundred soot-stained ironworkers from the Smelter-Guild, their heavy pavises locked together to form an iron ring around the holy precinct.

In the center of the forum, surrounded by forty mounted knights of the Margraviate, sat Margrave Carrow on his black charger.

The lord of Barrow-Gate looked pale from the lingering effects of the poison, but his back was straight as an ash spear, his wolf-fur mantle draped broad across his iron breastplate. Beside his stirrup stood Lady Aurelia, clad in light chainmail beneath a green surcoat, her hand resting on the pommel of her side-sword.

Between the Margrave’s horse and the cathedral stairs lay the evidence of treason: six copper-bound barrels of Liquid Sun, their blackened bungs marked with temple seals, surrounded by stacks of confiscated inquisitorial ledgers and the severed ropes of the undercroft fuses.

"Prelate Malakor!" Margrave Carrow’s voice boomed across the marble flags, amplified by the soaring acoustic curve of the cathedral portico. "By authority of the sovereign charter, the Council of Barrow-Gate summons you to answer for attempted regicide, sacrilege, and high treason against the realm!"

The massive bronze doors of the cathedral groaned as they swung open.

A contingent of thirty temple priests in flowing white robes filed out onto the high marble terrace, led by High Prelate Malakor and Grand Inquisitor Justinian. 

The prelate had discarded his heavy cloth-of-gold cope; he stood wrapped in a simple tunic of ivory wool, his fleshy jowls sagging, his hands clutching a gilded staff of office with desperate, white-knuckled intensity. Beside him, Justinian stood rigid, his pale ascetic face twisted into a mask of righteous fury, his scarlet stole billowing in the cold north wind.

"Sacrilege!" Justinian’s voice shrieked over the plaza, thin and shrill as a rusted hinge. "You bring iron blades to the threshold of the Living Dawn! The Margrave is a sorcerer of the dark pits! You stand with an apostate who bears the mark of the black ash! Repent, citizens of Barrow-Gate, before the wrath of Sol-Aethel consumes your flesh!"

A low, furious growl rose from the gathered crowd. 

A butcher from the Lower Ward hurled a heavy paving stone that shattered against the bronze terrace rail, showering the priests in limestone grit. "Liar! You tried to burn our children in their beds!"

"Where is your champion, Prelate?" Lady Aurelia called out, her voice clear and cutting as glass. "Where is the golden hero who swore to protect this city? Is he hiding beneath your skirts like a frightened lapdog?"

From the dark interior of the cathedral nave came a sound that froze the shouts on the crowd's lips.

It was a deep, thrumming hum, like the vibration of an iron bell struck with a sledgehammer, rising in pitch until the stone flags beneath our boots began to tremble. 

The air in the plaza grew suddenly, suffocatingly hot. 

The scent of incense and damp limestone was incinerated in an instant, replaced by the blistering stench of scorched marble, ozone, and boiling brass.

Out of the cathedral doors strode Evander Montfort.

A collective gasp ripped through the ten thousand spectators.

The boy had consumed the ultimate blasphemy. In his left hand he held an empty crystal phial that had contained concentrated Solar Tears—the forbidden essence of the goddess, distilled from the hearts of dying stars, meant only for divine avatars. 

His entire body was engulfed in a blinding, white-hot furnace of divine flame. 

Sheets of radiant solar fire danced along his blued-steel plate, turning the metal white-hot without melting the joints. His golden hair had turned to pure, crackling light that whipped around his head like a halo of solar flares. His violet eyes had dissolved into twin orbs of molten gold that burned with insane, apocalyptic ecstasy. 

Where his boots touched the marble flags, the stone scorched black, hairline fractures webbed across the terrace, and clouds of white limestone steam hissed into the freezing air.

In his right hand he swung the six-foot electrum greatsword. 

The blade blazed as a solid beam of blinding yellow fire, humming with enough concussive force to shatter a castle gate with a single blow.

"I am the Sun!" Evander’s voice roared from his throat—not a human voice, but an ear-splitting, dual-toned resonance of thunder and holy fury that rattled the glass in the cathedral dome. "I am the chosen son of the Dawn! You worms... you dung-heap peasants... did you think your little contracts could bind a god?!"

He took three strides down the marble steps, the sheer heat radiating from his frame forcing the front rank of marcher spearmen to throw their arms over their faces as their leather doublets began to smoke.

Evander’s molten eyes locked onto me.

"Theron!" he bellowed, raising the blazing greatsword toward the grey sky. "You broke my bones! You disgraced my name before the court! Now I will burn your soul until not even your shadow remains upon this stone! Come forward and die in the light!"

Margrave Carrow drew his sword, his face pale in the blistering glare. "Archers! Put sixty quarrels through his chest!"

"Hold your fire, my lord," I said quietly.

I stepped through the gap in the pike-wall, walking alone out onto the wide, open marble flags of the forum.

I wore no gilded plate. I wore my plain captain's brigandine of boiled leather and steel scales, scorched and stained with soot from the keep's undercroft. My left forearm was wrapped tightly in clean linen bandages over the burns from the sulfur fuse. 

In my right hand, I held my three-foot arming blade of honest, dark Westerosi steel. 

Ten whole, living fingers gripped the leather-wrapped hilt. 

"Captain Marrow!" Lady Aurelia called out, her voice tight with panic. "You cannot face divine fire with mortal steel! Fall back behind the pavises!"

"Steel is always mortal, my lady," I replied without looking back. "It is the only honest thing the gods ever gave us."

I stopped twenty paces from the bottom of the cathedral stairs.

Evander laughed. It was a terrifying, booming roar that shook the columns of the portico. "With an iron tooth-pick?! You face the fury of heaven with an unranked scrap of pig-iron?!"

He leaped down the remaining six steps, landing on the plaza flags with a concussive shockwave of heat that scorched the marble five paces in every direction. 

He raised the six-foot flaming greatsword above his right shoulder, muscles bulging with divine power, preparing a single downward cleave that would split me and the pavement beneath my feet in two.

I closed my eyes for half a heartbeat.

Deep within the center of my chest, beneath my ribs and breastbone, I reached into the cold, ancient stillness where the Anvil of Ash waited in the silence of the black pines.

*Sixty seconds,* I whispered to the dark. *Let the false light die.*

The Forgotten God answered.

*BOOM.*

It was not an explosion of sound, but an instantaneous, crushing vacuum of absolute silence.

A shockwave of freezing, ash-grey air erupted outward from my boots in a perfect thirty-pace circle. 

The temperature in the plaza plummeted sixty degrees in the space of a single pulse. 

The blinding, white-hot fire enveloping Evander Montfort died instantly.

It did not sputter. It did not fade. It was extinguished like a tallow candle dropped into a vat of liquid ice.

The white-hot luminescence on his armor collapsed into dull, scorched blued-steel. His hair snapped back into limp, greasy golden strands matted with sweat. His molten gold eyes turned back into terrified, bloodshot violet. 

And the six-foot electrum greatsword, stripped of its weightless divine blessing, instantly became eighty pounds of solid, unwieldy iron and brass.

The sudden, brutal return of gravity took Evander completely unprepared.

The eighty-pound blade wrenched downward with savage torque, tearing his right shoulder joint from its socket with a loud, sickening *CRACK.*

"Aaaagh!" Evander shrieked, his knees buckling under the sheer, unassisted weight of his own armor. 

The greatsword slammed into the marble flags with a deafening clang, its point chipping the stone, dragging the boy forward onto his face in the soot.

*Fifty-eight.*

The silence in the forum was absolute. 

Ten thousand people watched in petrified disbelief as the "living god" lay groveling on his stomach, weeping in agony, clutching a dislocated shoulder.

"My light..." Evander sobbed, his voice cracking into a thin, ragged squeak of pure animal terror. "Where is my light?! What did you do to my light?!"

"Your light was a lie, boy," I said, stepping forward with smooth, balanced strides.

*Fifty-two.*

Evander scrambled frantically backward on his elbows and knees, clawing at the slick marble, leaving smears of sweat and blood on the stone. "Justinian! Prelate! Help me! The witchery... he broke the goddess! He broke the altar!"

On the high terrace, High Prelate Malakor dropped his gilded staff. It clattered down the marble steps, ringing hollowly against the stones. The prelate backed away until his spine hit the cathedral doors, his jaw slack, his white robes shaking like laundry in the wind. 

Grand Inquisitor Justinian fell to his knees, clutching his holy sunburst medallion to his chest, whispering frantic, incoherent prayers to an empty sky.

*Forty-five.*

Evander reached down with his left hand, fumbling frantically for the side-dagger at his belt. 

He drew the twelve-inch steel blade, screaming with hysterical, frantic fury as he lunged upward from the ground, aiming a wild, uncoordinated thrust at my belly.

Arthur Dayne had taught me the counter to a low thrust when I was fifteen years old in the yard at Starfall. *A blade held with hate is always slow, Jaime. Let the fool extend his arm; then take the hinge.*

I did not even shift my hips.

My wooden buckler swept downward, deflecting the dagger tip two inches to my left.

My right arm moved in a clean, effortless passing arc.

The honed steel of my arming blade sliced through the leather strapping at the bend of his right elbow, cutting the tendon cleanly to the bone.

A bright ribbon of crimson blood sprayed across the white marble.

Evander shrieked, his right arm dropping limp and useless to his side, the fingers uncurling into bloody meat.

*Thirty-six.*

"Theron! Please!" Evander wailed, curling into a ball on the stone, his broken nose leaking blood and yellowish mucus across his silk neck-guard. "I surrender! I yield! The rules of chivalry... I am a highborn son of the Montforts! You must grant me ransom!"

"Chivalry?" I laughed, a cold, dry sound that carried to the farthest edges of the colonnade. "You put poison in an old man's wine. You tried to burn fifty thousand commoners with Liquid Sun. You severed your own retainer's fingers to save your saddle at the locks. You are no knight, Evander. You never were."

*Twenty-eight.*

I stepped on the instep of his right boot, pinning his foot to the marble.

My left boot drove forward, the iron toe catching him dead under the point of his chin.

*CRACK.*

The impact snapped his head back, shattering his jawbone and sending his three carved bone false teeth flying into the crowd. 

Evander collapsed flat onto his back, gasping for air through a mouth filled with blood and shattered teeth, his violet eyes wide with the realization that no god was coming to save him.

*Eighteen.*

I stood over him, my chest rising and falling in slow, measured rhythm.

My arming sword was raised, the point hovering two inches above the beaten electrum sunburst on his breastplate.

"Look at me, boy," I commanded.

Evander looked up. 

Through the red haze of his own blood and tears, he looked into the cold, pitiless grey eyes of Ser Jaime Lannister—the man who had walked through the blood of kings and dragons and lived to laugh at their graves.

"I... I am sorry..." Evander whimpered, a thin, strangulated wheeze bubbling through his lips. "I... I was vain..."

"You were a fool," I said.

*Ten.*

I drove the steel downward with the full weight of my body and shoulders.

The honed tip of my plain iron blade pierced through the gilded filigree of the electrum sunburst, shearing through the softened steel of the breastplate, biting deep into the dark heart beneath.

Evander’s back arched in a violent, final spasm.

His breath escaped his lips in a long, rattling hiss.

His head fell back against the marble, his violet eyes glazing over into dull, unseeing glass, staring up into the cold, empty blue of the autumn sky.

*Zero.*

The ash domain collapsed.

A faint, freezing gust of wind whispered across the forum, carrying the scent of pine needles and cold iron away into the midday air.

The sixty seconds were done.

The forum of ten thousand people was as silent as a graveyard.

Not a man breathed. Not a horse shifted. 

Every eye was fixed on the golden champion of the Radiant Dawn lying dead in a pool of his own dark blood beneath the boots of an unranked sellsword.

Slowly, deliberately, I planted my boot on Evander's gilded chest and drew my arming blade free with a wet, sucking scrape.

I reached into my belt pouch, drew out a square of clean linen, and wiped the red grease from the steel from hilt to tip.

Then, with a crisp, resonant *snick*, I slid the sword back into its plain leather scabbard.

I looked up at the cathedral terrace.

High Prelate Malakor stared down at the corpse of his golden idol. His face was the color of curdled milk; his hands twitched at his sides; his breath came in short, panicked squeaks.

A sudden, wet stain spread across the ivory wool of the prelate's breeches, dripping down onto the marble steps.

The high priest of the Radiant Dawn had soiled himself before the entire city.

Margrave Carrow rode his black charger forward, drawing rein ten paces from the bottom of the terrace steps.

"Deputy Brand," the Margrave said, his voice ringing through the quiet plaza with unquestioned sovereign power.

"My lord!" Brand barked, stepping forward with his billhook.

"Arrest the High Prelate and the Grand Inquisitor," Margrave Carrow commanded. "Chain them in common iron and convey them to the deep dungeons beneath the keep. The civil magistrates will convene court on the morrow to try them for high treason, sacrilegious arson, and attempted regicide."

"With pleasure, my lord," Brand grinned, leading twelve burly watchmen up the terrace steps.

The crowd broke.

A roar erupted from ten thousand throats—a sound so massive, so deafening, that it echoed off the Grey Spires and shook the rooks from the cathedral eaves. 

Pikemen banged their shafts against their pavises; ironworkers hurled their leather caps into the air; common women wept and cheered, screaming the name of the man who had saved their homes from the fire.

"Theron! Theron! The Lion of the Gate!"

Lady Aurelia rode up beside me, her green eyes shining with fierce, triumphant light. 

She looked down at the dead boy in the ruined armor, then up at my face. 

She did not offer a courtly speech. She reached into her saddle-bag, drew out a flask of cold spring water, and handed it down to me.

"Drink, Captain," she said softly, a rare, genuine smile touching her lips. "You look like a man who has done a hard day's work."

I took the flask, pulled the wooden stopper with my teeth, and drank deeply. 

The water was sweet, icy, and clean against my parched throat.

I wiped my chin with the back of my gloved hand, turned my back on the dead peacock, and walked beside the Margrave's horse toward the city gates.
