# The Shattering of the Sun

The championship horn blew three long, deafening blasts that shook the timbers of the grandstands.

The afternoon sun hung directly overhead, baking the white arena sand into a shimmering glare that mirrored the heat of an iron foundry. Five thousand men and women stood upon their feet, screaming, stomping their boots, waving ribbons of gold silk and red wool. The air smelled of roasting ox-marrow, scorched sand, ozone, horse sweat, and the electric tension that precedes an execution.

In the royal box, Margrave Carrow sat forward, his iron-plated forearms resting on the velvet rail, his grey brow furrowed with grim, unblinking focus. 

Beside him, the High Prelate stood with arms outstretched toward the sky, chanting archaic liturgical hymns to the Radiant Dawn, calling down the favor of Sol-Aethel upon the golden champion of the realm.

The northern iron gates swung open with a screech of rusty hinges.

Evander Montfort entered the sand.

He paraded across the sand with theatrical arrogance, riding the cheers of the crowd like a wave, his head thrown back, his teeth flashing in a brilliant, triumphant smile. He wore his full parade plate—gilded steel chased with intricate sunburst filigree, every plate reflecting the noon light in blinding white spears. From his shoulders billowed a mantle of ermine-trimmed crimson velvet, trailing five paces in the sand behind him.

In his right hand he held his greatsword, the six-foot double-edged blade forged of pale electrum-steel. 

The weapon was already blazing with divine fire. 

A shimmering halo of bright solar aura danced along the edge, crackling with heat that caused the sand beneath his boots to smoke. His violet eyes glowed with an unnatural, incandescent golden luminescence—the supreme blessing of the Goddess Sol-Aethel, granting him the physical strength of a highland giant and the speed of a falling bolt.

The grandstands roared with fanatical devotion. 

"Evander! The Sun-Gilded! Slay the ditch-rat! Purge the lists!"

The southern sally-port opened.

I walked onto the sand alone.

I wore my boiled leather brigandine, the steel plates dull grey beneath the oiled hide. On my head sat my plain iron kettle-helm, its leather chin-strap cinched tight beneath my jaw. My left arm bore my round wooden buckler; my right hand held my three-foot arming sword, the dark blade resting comfortably against my thigh.

The contrast was absolute. 

A golden god clad in five hundred silver marks of gilded plate, versus an unranked sellsword in thirty-copper leather and battered iron.

Evander stopped ten paces from me, planting his six-foot sword tip-down into the sand. 

The heat from his blade sent ripples through the midday air, the smell of burnt sulfur and hot metal washing over my face.

"Look at this crowd, Theron," Evander boomed, his voice amplified by the divine magic at his throat, carrying to the highest rafters of the arena. "They came to see a hero. They came to see the glory of the Dawn. The Margrave himself sits in judgment. And what do you bring to this sacred ground? A notched pig-sticker and the stink of sheep dung."

He drew the greatsword from the sand with an effortless flick of his right wrist, resting the heavy flat across his gilded pauldron.

"You should have stayed in the lime-kiln," Evander whispered, his violet eyes narrowing with cold, murderous certainty. "You should have died in the ditch where you belonged. I will incinerate your flesh until there is nothing left to bury. The province will remember what happens to those who challenge the Sun."

I answered with silence.

I set my feet into the sand, twelve inches apart, sinking my hips into the low guard. 

Deep beneath my breastbone, the soul-brand of the Anvil of Ash pulsed with cold, dark iron fury. The ancient grey god was watching from the granite heights. The law of iron and bone was ready to be spoken.

Margrave Carrow raised his iron truncheon from the royal pavilion.

"Champions to your marks!" the herald’s voice screamed through the horn. "No quarter asked! No quarter given! Begin!"

*CLAAANG.*

Evander exploded into motion.

His divine solar aura erupted into a blinding pillar of golden fire that towered twenty feet into the azure sky. The sheer kinetic force of his takeoff sent a shockwave through the arena floor, spraying sand thirty paces in all directions. 

He crossed the ten paces in half a heartbeat, his six-foot greatsword brought overhead in a two-handed cleave meant to vaporize my blade and split my torso from crown to pelvis.

The heat was blistering, singing the hair on my forearms.

*Now,* my spirit whispered.

I invoked the covenant.

*The Sovereign Duel.*

The domain fell upon the arena like a mountain dropping into the sea.

There was no flash of light. There was no thunderclap. There was only a sudden, terrifying, absolute silence.

The blinding golden pillar of solar fire vanished instantly, snuffed out like a tallow wick pinched between wet fingers. 

The incandescent halo on Evander’s electrum greatsword died, leaving only dull, cold, dead metal. The glowing violet fire in his eyes went dark, returning to the flat, panicked pupils of a frightened boy.

The world stripped down to pure, unassisted human meat.

The effect on Evander was catastrophic.

In the middle of his leap, the divine strength that had lifted his hundred-pound armor and forty-pound greatsword evaporated into thin air. 

Suddenly, the boy was carrying the full, crushing weight of fifty pounds of steel plate on unconditioned muscles. His six-foot greatsword, which had felt light as a birch twig under his divine blessing, instantly became an unmanageable iron spit that pulled his shoulders forward with brutal, unbalanced momentum.

His eyes went wide with blind, suffocating terror as he realized his magic was gone.

"What—" Evander gasped, his breath hitching in his throat.

The heavy greatsword crashed downward, not with divine precision, but with the clumsy, dragging inertia of dead metal falling from a height.

I stepped three inches to the right.

The massive electrum blade struck the sand beside my left boot, burying itself six inches into the dirt with a dull thud.

The downward momentum yanked Evander forward onto his toes, his chin jutting out, his chest completely unprotected.

Sixty seconds.

The clock in my mind began to tick. *Fifty-nine.*

I stepped into his guard, planting my left foot beside his knee.

My left arm drove forward in a short, explosive piston stroke. The iron-rimmed face of my buckler slammed squarely into the bridge of Evander’s straight, aristocratic nose.

*CRACK.*

The sound of cartilage and nasal bone exploding echoed across the silent arena. 

Blood erupted from his nostrils in a thick crimson sheet, splashing across his gilded breastplate. Evander let out a high-pitched, girlish shriek of pure, unadulterated agony—the cry of a spoiled boy who had never felt true physical pain in his entire pampered life.

*Fifty-five.*

His hands released the greatsword as he stumbled backward, clawing frantically at his shattered face.

"My face!" Evander wailed, blood bubbling between his gilded fingers. "My nose! What did you do to my light?!"

"Your light is dead, boy," I whispered.

I closed the distance with smooth, economic passing steps. 

My plain three-foot arming sword blurred through the air. 

*Forty-eight.*

The honed steel edge swept low, striking the back of his right knee where the gilded greave parted from the thigh plate. 

The steel sliced through the leather strapping, biting deep into the thick tendon beneath. 

A bright ribbon of arterial blood sprayed across the white sand. 

Evander’s right leg gave way instantly, his knee collapsing inward with a sickening pop. He crashed down onto his left knee, his magnificent crimson velvet cape pooling in his own blood.

*Forty.*

He reached for the side dagger at his belt with a frantic, trembling left hand, sobbing in terror. "Prelate! Help me! The gods... the witchery... he broke the blessing!"

I stepped on his left wrist, driving his hand and the half-drawn dagger deep into the sand beneath my iron-capped boot.

*CRUSH.*

The small bones in his wrist ground together under my weight. Evander shrieked again, his face twisting into an ugly, slobbering mask of tears, snot, and dark gore.

*Thirty-two.*

I raised my arming sword and brought the flat of the heavy blade across his left jaw with the full rotational torque of my hips.

*SMACK.*

Three of his perfect, milk-white teeth flew into the sawdust, accompanied by a spray of bloody foam. The force of the blow snapped his head around, fracturing his jawbone, sending his golden hair whipping across his eyes.

The arena of five thousand spectators was frozen in petrified, suffocating horror.

In the royal box, the High Prelate was shaking the velvet rail with both hands, screaming incoherently: "Halt the bout! Sacrilege! Unholy blasphemy! The hero is cursed!"

Margrave Carrow stood with both hands clamped onto his sword hilt, his face pale as chalk, his eyes fixed on the slaughter below. 

His truncheon remained still in his grip. 

The old marcher lord was a veteran of thirty years of war. He saw no witchcraft on the sand. He saw no black magic, no summoned demons, no curses. He saw a seasoned swordsman systematically dismantling an arrogant boy who had forgotten how to fight without cheat-powers.

*Twenty.*

Evander was on his hands and knees in the red-stained sand, weeping like a whipped dog, crawling toward the royal pavilion, his gilded armor caked in mud, blood, and filth.

"Mercy..." Evander blubbered through his shattered mouth, blood dripping from his chin onto his fingers. "Theron... please... I gave you three coins... take the silver... take the gold... I will make you a lord..."

I walked behind him, pacing his slow, agonizing crawl with casual, leisurely steps.

"You gave me three zinc bits in the mud, boy," I told him, my voice carrying cleanly across the silent sand to the lowest grandstands. "You laughed on the tavern porch while your women mocked my bread. You told me a lion had no place among sheep."

*Ten.*

I reached down with my left hand, seized the back of his gilded collar, and hauled him upright onto his knees.

*Five.*

I placed the cold, razor-honed edge of my plain iron sword against his exposed throat, pressing the steel until a bright red bead of blood welled along the blade.

*Two.*

*One.*

The sixty seconds expired.

The Sovereign Duel collapsed.

A sudden, faint flicker of golden light sputtered along Evander's shoulders as the divine blessing returned—weak, pale, flickering like a dying candle in a gale. His greatsword across the ring gave a feeble hum.

The return of his magic was utterly useless.

A god can grant a man the strength of an ox, but when a sharp blade is already pressed against his jugular, moving a single muscle means death.

Evander felt the steel against his throat. 

His body went rigid as stone. The breath froze in his chest; his weeping stopped, replaced by the hollow, animal rattle of absolute, bowel-loosening terror.

A dark, warm stain spread across the crotch of his gilded plate, dripping down his leather thigh-guards into the white sand.

The hero of the Radiant Dawn had wet his breeches before five thousand spectators.

The silence in the grandstands was heavier than a tombstone.

Every noble lord, every court lady, every carter and pikeman stared down at the golden pet of the Church of the Dawn kneeling in his own urine and blood, begging for his life at the feet of an unranked sellsword in thirty-copper leather.

I looked up at the royal box.

My eyes met the grey, steady gaze of Margrave Carrow.

I withheld the killing blow. 

A corpse was quickly forgotten, buried under marble and praised in lying songs. A broken, humiliated coward who had pissed himself in front of the court lived forever as a warning to fools.

I slowly lowered my sword.

I stepped back, wiped the edge of my blade with a clean wool rag from my pouch, and slid the steel into its plain leather scabbard with a crisp, final snick.

The herald stood on his dais, his mouth opening and closing like a landed carp, unable to speak.

Margrave Carrow finally lowered his iron truncheon, his voice carrying down from the pavilion with grim, unquestioned authority:

"The match is concluded. Winner of the Midsummer Grand List... and Champion of the Pale March... Captain Theron Marrow."

A single beat of silence hung over the arena.

Then, the lower grandstands exploded into an earth-shattering roar. 

Ten thousand boots stomped the cedar boards; silver coins were hurled onto the sand; carters screamed my name till their veins bulged.

Across the royal gallery, Lady Aurelia stood tall, applauding with slow, deliberate grace, her green eyes locked onto mine with unmistakable fascination.

Beside her, the High Prelate sank into his velvet chair, his face grey as death, his jeweled staff resting forgotten in his lap.

Ignoring the cheering stands, I turned my back on the weeping boy in the red sand, picked up my round wooden buckler, and walked toward the dark cool of the southern gates.

Inside the competitor's tunnel, the roar of the arena faded into a dull, muffled vibration through the stone vaults.

Deputy Brand stood waiting by the iron portcullis, a wooden chest bound in polished brass resting on a table beside him. Beside the chest stood the Margrave’s High Seneschal, a silver balance-scale pin fastened to his velvet lapel.

"Five thousand silver crowns," the Seneschal said, his voice quiet, respectful, and entirely devoid of the courtly arrogance he had displayed three days ago. He extended a heavy brass key. "Minted in the sovereign treasury, Captain Marrow. Factor Vallow has already arranged for three armed drays to transfer the bullion to the Syndicate vaults in your name."

"A fair accounting," I said, pocketing the brass key.

A page in crimson livery stepped from the shadows of the archway, bowing low. He held a small scroll of parchment sealed with emerald wax, bearing the personal device of House Carrow—a green falcon with an iron ring in its beak.

"From the Lady Aurelia, Captain," the boy whispered nervously.

I broke the emerald seal with my thumbnail.

The parchment bore four lines written in a sharp, elegant hand:

> *The church’s pet is broken, but winter approaches from the northern heights. The Margrave convenes the War Council at fourth bell tonight. A seat at the high table has been set for the Lion of the River.*

I smiled thinly, rolling the parchment and slipping it into my tunic.

Beside me, Deputy Brand handed me a wooden cup of dark, bitter ale. 

"You didn't just break his nose, lad," Brand said, shaking his scarred head in disbelief. "You broke the temple's spine. The priests won't forgive this."

"Let them pray," I replied, taking a slow draught of the cold ale, feeling the sting of the malt against my tongue. "Steel does not listen to prayers."

I set the cup on the table, buckled my arming sword tight against my hip, and walked out into the cool evening air of Barrow-Gate.
