# Catalog of Forbidden Names, Locations, Creatures & Narrative Tropes

This reference document serves as the authoritative catalog of forbidden story elements across all narrative workspaces, expanding upon Section 1 of `GEMINI.md`.

---

## 1. Forbidden Locations & Settings

The following locations are strictly banned from all worldbuilding, outlines, and story prose:
- `Sunken Pass`
- `Oakhaven`
- `Whispering Wood`
- `Sunken Crypt` / `Sunken Hollow`

*Directive*: Develop original, geologically and culturally grounded toponyms (e.g. *Black Ditch*, *Skittering Ridge*, *Barrow-Gate*, *Oster-Reach*, *High Colonnade*).

## 3. Forbidden Creatures & Beast Tropes

The following generic fantasy beasts are strictly banned:
- `Dragon` / `Drakes`
- `Monster Crawler`
- `Monster Dog`

*Directive*: Design setting-specific fauna with believable ecological niches, anatomy, and behavioral patterns (e.g. *crag-stalkers*, *scythe-hounds*, *bog-drakes* replaced by unique reptilian amphibians).

## 4. Forbidden Narrative & Plot Tropes

### 4.1. Opening Alley Thug / Mugger Encounter
- **Prohibition**: Having the newly awakened or destitute protagonist ambushed or harassed by street thugs/cutpurses in the opening chapter to establish danger or loot starter coins.
- **Alternative**: Ground early chapters in commercial pressure, legal contracts, institutional audits, or realistic martial recalibration.

### 4.2. Accountant Assistant / Junior Ledger Clerk Subordination
- **Prohibition**: Having the protagonist take a subordinate, junior salaried position as a clerk or assistant bookkeeper under a petty bureaucrat.
- **Alternative**: Protagonists operate as independent principals, brokers, arbiters, syndicators, or creditors from inception.

### 4.3. Mary Sue / Gary Stu Frictionless Execution
- **Prohibition**: Omniscient, infallible masterminds whose schemes always succeed effortlessly without resistance, setbacks, or meaningful opposition; sycophantic allies existing only to marvel at the MC.
- **Alternative**: Protagonists face real logistical and tactical friction, make miscalculations, endure vulnerability, and earn hard-won victories.

### 4.4. Cardboard / Bland Evil Tropes
- **Prohibition**: Flat, 2D villains who are evil for the sake of evil without internal rationale, distinct voice, or psychological grounding; rushed off-screen downfalls.
- **Alternative**: Antagonists are "fun-to-hate" with recognizable human vices (hubris, greed, vanity, insecurity) whose retributions are cathartic and poetic.

### 4.5. Northern Invader / Frost Horde / Barbarian Winter Defile Invasion
- **Prohibition**: Using the recycled narrative trope of a savage northern barbarian, orc, wildling, or frost-fiend horde invading down through frozen mountain defiles or blizzard passes as the secondary arc crisis to force a military expedition, bail out bankrupt rulers, or stage the antagonist's battlefield rout.
- **Why It Is Prohibited**: Already heavily utilized in *The Lion's Debt* (where the frost-orc horde under Mor-Goth invades through Skelter-Ridge, forcing Tywin's winter campaign). Recycling this exact structure across different stories results in repetitive, copy-paste narratives.
- **Alternative**: Design original, setting-authentic secondary crises tailored specifically to each story's unique themes:
  - *Internal Political & Religious Coups*: An overreaching church inquisition, corrupt judicial purges, or patrician rebellions.
  - *Resource Embargoes & Trade Wars*: Downstream river barons seizing sluices/locks, merchant guild strikes, or mining cartel boycotts.
  - *Subterranean / Industrial Crises*: Ancient mine collapses, toxic gas leaks, subterranean labyrinth exploration, or guild worker insurrections.
  - *Frontier Border Disputes*: Clashes between rival baronies over mineral rights, toll-roads, or fertile river valleys.

### 4.6. Recycled Narrative Tropes & Arc Templates Across Existing Stories (Cross-Project Originality Mandate)
- **Prohibition**: Reusing, cloning, or thinly reskinning plot tropes, conflict dynamics, crisis templates, military/tactical set-pieces, or antagonist downfalls from any existing written story in the workspace.
- **Why It Is Prohibited**: Reusing identical story skeletons or major plot devices (e.g., using a northern winter horde invasion in *The Kingslayer's Reckoning* after it was already the central conflict in *The Lion's Debt*, or repeating the exact same legal foreclosure trick across different protagonists) makes the workspace's stories feel like formulaic copies. Every story must possess a distinct narrative fingerprint.
- **Mandatory Prior Story Audit Protocol**:
  1. *Pre-Planning Catalog Review*: Before outlining or drafting any new story or arc, examine all existing story projects in the workspace (e.g., *The Lion's Debt*, *The Kingslayer's Reckoning*).
  2. *Plot & Crisis Deduplication*: Ensure the primary and secondary conflicts (how the protagonist builds power, what external crisis threatens the region, how the antagonist is overthrown) do not duplicate existing stories.
  3. *Unique Thematic Vehicles*: Each project must employ distinct conflict vehicles—such as judicial inquests, religious schisms, subterranean excavation crises, naval/river trade blockades, agrarian peasant uprisings, succession intrigues, or artisan guild monopolies—rather than repeating what was already written.
