# Earthly Words, Eponyms & Demonyms Reference Guide

A comprehensive, authoritative reference guide for identifying, eliminating, and translating Earth-derived terms, historical and mythological eponyms, real-world geographies, national demonyms, and Earth religious concepts within secondary-world fantasy narratives.

---

## 1. The Principle of Diegetic Linguistic Immersion

In secondary-world fantasy (such as *The Kingslayer's Reckoning*, *The Mockingbird's Ledger*, or *The Rogue Prince of Blackweir*), the setting possesses its own distinct history, mythologies, geography, languages, and religions. 

Using terms rooted in Earth's history, ancient Greece, Rome, the Bible, European nation-states, or Earth geography punctures the reader's suspension of disbelief. An adventurer in Barrow-Gate cannot reference Achilles, because Troy never fell and the river Styx never flowed. A merchant in Weirs-End cannot invoke Christian charity, because Christ was never crucified in that world. A highland officer cannot drink French wine or wear Spanish merino, because neither France nor Spain exists.

Every concept must be grounded in setting-authentic equivalents rooted in the world's own physical reality, metallurgy, anatomy, and regional lore.

## 2. Prohibited Categories & Catalog of Forbidden Terms

| `Pyrrhic victory` | King Pyrrhus of Epirus (279 BC) | Presumes ancient Hellenistic warfare. |
| `Trojan horse` | The Fall of Troy (Virgil, Homer) | Presumes the Trojan War. |

| `Draconian measures` / `laws` | Draco of Athens (c. 621 BC) | Presumes the Athenian archon. |
| `Spartan discipline` / `living` | Ancient Sparta (Laconia) | Presumes Greek city-state culture. |
| `Machiavellian scheme` | Niccolò Machiavelli (1513) | Presumes Renaissance Florentine politics. |
| `Damoclean sword` / `Damocles` | Damocles of Syracuse (Cicero) | Presumes the court of Dionysius II. |

## 4. Exceptions & Genre Calibration

### 4.1. The Transmigration / Close-POV Caveat
When a protagonist transmigrates from Earth or an established external franchise into a secondary fantasy realm:
1. **Internal Monologue of Earth Transmigrants**:
   - If the protagonist is explicitly from historical Earth (e.g., *Standartenführer* Viktor Vogel in *The Iron Order of Karst*), Earth terms reflecting his personal lived memory (such as *Ost-Paris*, *Burgundian*, *Waffenamt*, *concrete*) are permitted **strictly within his internal stream-of-consciousness or private operational logs**.
   - However, the secondary fantasy world itself, its native inhabitants, its geographical records, its spoken dialogue, and third-person narrator descriptions must NEVER use Earth terms.
2. **Transmigrants from Established Fictional Worlds** (e.g., Jaime Lannister or Daemon Targaryen from Westeros):
   - Internal thoughts, memories, and comparisons must draw **exclusively from their canonical world's lore** (*King's Landing*, *Casterly Rock*, *the Red Keep*, *Arthur Dayne*, *Dark Sister*, *Gods Eye*, *Seven Hells*).
   - They must **never** use Earth-specific idioms or eponyms (*Achilles*, *Christian*, *French*, *Spartan*).

### 4.2. Universal Physical Units vs. Cultural Descriptors
- **Permitted Universal Units**: English distance, weight, and volume measures (*paces*, *feet*, *inches*, *yards*, *miles*, *leagues*, *pounds*, *stones*, *ounces*, *bushels*, *casks*, *barrels*) are standard English language conventions for medieval fantasy and are fully permitted.
- **Metric System in Pre-Industrial Fantasy**: Avoid metric terms (*kilometers*, *centimeters*, *grams*) in pre-industrial or medieval fantasy unless specifically justified by an industrialized sci-fi or modern militarist protagonist (e.g., *The Iron Order of Karst*).

## 5. Automated Detection & Auditing Runbook

To detect accidental leakage of Earth terms in narrative files, execute this standardized Python audit command in the project directory:

```python
import os, glob, re

earthly_patterns = {
    'achilles': r'\bachilles\b|\bachillean\b',
    'christian/christ': r'\bchristian\b|\bchrist\b|\bchristianity\b|\bjesus\b',
    'french/france': r'\bfrench\b|\bfrance\b',
    'spanish/spain': r'\bspanish\b|\bspain\b',
    'flemish': r'\bflemish\b',
    'swedish': r'\bswedish\b|\bsweden\b',
    'baltic': r'\bbaltic\b',
    'persian': r'\bpersian\b|\bpersia\b',
    'andalusian': r'\bandalusian\b|\bandalusia\b',
    'parisian': r'\bparisian\b|\bparisienne\b',
    'troy/trojan': r'\btrojan\b|\btroy\b',
    'pyrrhic': r'\bpyrrhic\b',
    'gorgon': r'\bgorgon\b',
    'sisyphean': r'\bsisyphean\b|\bsisyphus\b',
    'herculean': r'\bherculean\b|\bhercules\b',
    'draconian': r'\bdraconian\b',
    'spartan': r'\bspartan\b|\bsparta\b',
    'machiavellian': r'\bmachiavellian\b|\bmachiavelli\b',
    'damoclean': r'\bdamoclean\b|\bdamocles\b',
    'gordian': r'\bgordian\b',
    'pandora': r'\bpandora\b',
    'narcissist': r'\bnarcissis\w*\b',
    'zephyr': r'\bzephyr\b',
    'romanesque': r'\bromanesque\b',
    'gothic': r'\bgothic\b',
    'byzantine': r'\bbyzantine\b',
    'damascus/damascened': r'\bdamascus\b|\bdamascened\b',
    'bohemian': r'\bbohemian\b',
    'baroque': r'\bbaroque\b',
    'latin': r'\blatin\b',
    'biblical/bible': r'\bbiblical\b|\bbible\b|\bgospel\b',
    'saint (canonized earth)': r'\bsaint ursula\b|\bsaint jude\b|\bsaint cecilia\b|\bsaint martin\b'
}

for f in sorted(glob.glob('chapters/*.md')):
    with open(f, 'r', encoding='utf-8', errors='ignore') as fp:
        lines = fp.readlines()
    for idx, line in enumerate(lines, 1):
        for name, pat in earthly_patterns.items():
            for m in re.finditer(pat, line, re.IGNORECASE):
                print(f'{f}:{idx} [Earthly Term: {name} -> {m.group(0)}]: {line.strip()[:100]}')
```
