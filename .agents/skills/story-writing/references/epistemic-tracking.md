# Epistemic Tracking & Information Asymmetry Reference Guide

This document defines the authoritative framework for eliminating **Epistemic Plot Holes**—narrative flaws where characters possess knowledge, titles, names, forensic data, or technical assessments without an on-page, plausible, in-world delivery vehicle.

---

## 1. What Is an Epistemic Plot Hole?

In creative writing and narrative craft, an **Epistemic Plot Hole** occurs whenever a character acts upon, speaks, thinks, or reveals information that they could not have plausibly acquired through:
1. **Direct Sensory Observation**: Seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, or touching in real time.
2. **Formal On-Page Introduction**: An explicit exchange where a person, place, or entity is identified diegetically.
3. **Plausible Intelligence Gathering**: A documented, in-world communication channel (messengers, letters, overheard tavern gossip, stolen ledgers, scout reconnaissance).
4. **Logical Deduction from Established Premises**: Drawing a conclusion from facts the character demonstrably knows, without psychic leaps or authorial hand-waving.

Whenever a narrator or character uses a secret name, specialized material label, or private off-screen event without this chain of causality, **the narrative illusion breaks and an unearned plot hole is created**.

---

## 2. The Four Pillars of Epistemic Integrity

### Pillar 1: Name & Identity Isolation
- **Rule**: In limited POV (third-person limited or first-person), neither the POV narrator nor any character may use a character's true name, title, alien origin, or secret alias before that name is spoken in dialogue, read on a document, or established via credible report.
- **Application**:
  - Before an introduction, describe characters purely through **physical, sensory, and acoustic traits** (e.g., *"the silver-haired stranger"*, *"the man in the dark velvet coat"*, *"the tall, severe traveler"*).
  - An introduction must occur naturally on-page. Only after the dialogue beat (*"Who are you?" / "I am the Doctor..."*) may the POV narrator and character adopt that specific name.

### Pillar 2: Material & Asset Assay Isolation
- **Rule**: Outsiders, rivals, adversaries, and laypeople cannot magically know the metallurgical composition, cosmic origin, or enchantment pedigree of unfamiliar artifacts or weapons.
- **Application**:
  - If a protagonist carries a kite shield made of *cold-pressed meteoric iron alloyed with carbonized titanium*, adversaries who see it must perceive only what is visible: *"an unadorned, heavy black iron slab"*, *"a crude chunk of scavenged metal without guild runes or noble crests"*.
  - Adversaries cannot say *"Even his meteor shield cannot stop us"* unless they stole the armorer's receipt, captured an assay tablet, or subjected the metal to alchemical testing on-page.

### Pillar 3: Off-Screen Causal & Medical Isolation
- **Rule**: Characters cannot possess omniscient awareness of secret injuries, clandestine meetings, or miraculous medical recoveries that occurred off-screen.
- **Application**:
  - When an adversary encounters someone they left for dead, their reaction must be governed by **their own background, professional limitations, and class biases**:
    - *Healer / Cleric*: Recognizes the medical impossibility under known doctrine (e.g., necrotic venom requires a 5th-circle high cathedral arch-prelate miracle costing hundreds of crowns), triggering authentic superstitious dread.
    - *Scout / Rogue*: Gathers physical clues from the street (e.g., tavern rumors of an eccentric foreigner in a velvet coat seen buying dried chicory and persimmons at the market).
    - *Arrogant Leader*: Rationalizes the anomaly away through class hubris (e.g., dismissing the healer as a back-alley charlatan hedge-doctor selling temporary dead-root pain-draughts that will buckle under pressure).
  - Never allow an adversary to state flatly: *"What if the man who healed him is with him?"* without showing how they learned an older man healed him.

### Pillar 4: Grounded Intelligence Transmission (Anti-Sci-Fi Hand-Waving)
- **Rule**: In fantasy or pre-industrial settings, information cannot travel through anachronistic, unseeded, or pseudo-scientific short-cuts (e.g., *"the guild's black-market telegraph system"*, *"scanned the local communication network"*, *"telepathic surveillance"*).
- **Application**:
  - Intelligence must travel through **setting-authentic vehicles**:
    - A trusted local artisan (e.g., the old armorer who patches equipment and hears what mercenaries are buying in the ditch).
    - Public or clandestine registers (e.g., municipal tax ledgers, emergency pest-control permit filings, portico bounty lists).
    - Sensory deduction and military experience (e.g., a 5-year veteran vanguard analyzing the terrain of a quarry bottleneck and deducing an arbalest kill-box and alchemical fire).

## 3. Epistemic Plot Hole Audit Matrix

Use this 5-step checklist whenever drafting, editing, or auditing scenes:

| Check | Audit Question | Failure Mode (Plot Hole) | Mandated Remediation Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Name Check** | *Did the POV narrator or character use a name before it was spoken or read?* | Calling a stranger "the Doctor" in narration during Chapter 1 or Chapter 2 before he says "I am the Doctor". | Replace premature names with sensory descriptors (*"the silver-haired man"*, *"the traveler"*); add an explicit on-page introduction scene. |
| **2. Asset Check** | *Does an adversary know the secret material or origin of an item?* | An enemy shouting *"His meteor shield is too strong!"* when the enemy has never assayed the metal. | Revise adversary dialogue to describe the item's surface appearance (*"that heavy black iron slab"*, *"an unadorned hunk of iron"*). |
| **3. Medical Check** | *Does an enemy know who cured a fatal wound or how?* | Enemies magically knowing a stranger cured the protagonist in a private vessel. | Show the enemy relying on their own profession/biases: superstitious cleric dread, scout tavern rumors, arrogant leader rationalizations. |
| **4. Origin Check** | *Where did that official document, tool, or weapon come from?* | A character producing an official stamped High Inquest petition out of nowhere. | Plant an upstream scene showing the character obtaining or lifting the blank petition from a registry desk. |
| **5. Transmission Check** | *How was tactical intelligence communicated?* | The protagonist or mentor citing an ungrounded "black-market telegraph system". | Replace with grounded channels: an alert from a local armorer, official registry logs cross-referenced by an auditor, or tactical deduction from terrain. |

## 4. Remediation Protocols for Common Epistemic Flaws

1. **The Overnight Interrogation Bridge**:
   - If a mentor or scholar suddenly possesses detailed historical combat statistics (e.g., 62 sorties, 314 engagements, names of minor party members) that the protagonist did not recite in their first scene, insert an overnight transition showing the mentor rigorously cross-examining the protagonist over tea and translating their memories into chalk arithmetic.
2. **The Dual-Seeding Protocol for Forensic Evidence**:
   - If an investigator or magistrate reveals the exact origin of contraband (e.g., *"basilisk venom purchased under a forged pest-control permit from the Crimson Flask"*), ensure:
     1. The adversary is shown procuring or bragging about the contraband and permit in an upstream scene.
     2. The auditor or magistrate is shown cross-referencing that morning's permit filings or black-market seizure logs.
3. **The Local Artisan Informant Vehicle**:
   - When the protagonist needs to anticipate an adversary's black-market ambush preparations, route the warning through an established, grounded working-class NPC (such as an armorer who supplies the city's guards or scavengers).
