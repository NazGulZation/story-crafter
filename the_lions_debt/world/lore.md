# World Lore & Political Economy: The Grand Duchy of Dun-Carrow

---

## 1. Geography & Settlements

- **Dun-Carrow**: The sovereign seat of the Grand Duchy, situated upon the tidal estuary where the Skelter River joins the Grey Gulf. Built upon black basalt foundations dating to the Old Imperial Age, the city rises in concentric terraces from the muddy Lower Wharves through the Mercantile Exchange up to the high citadel of the Archon's Palace.
- **The Red Kilns**: A volcanic highland valley three leagues northeast of Dun-Carrow, rich in sulfur, cinnabar, and copper ores. The site of perilous subterranean labyrinths where the *Dawn-Seekers* earned their initial fame.
- **Skelter-Ridge**: A rugged spine of frozen limestone mountains flanking the northern border. Beyond Skelter-Ridge lie the barren tundras inhabited by the frost-orcs and mountain reavers.
- **The Southern Reach**: Broad agricultural floodplains producing the bulk of the Grand Duchy's winter wheat, rye, and barley. Connected to Dun-Carrow by a network of timber tow-paths and river barges.

## 2. Currency, Weights & Commercial Law

- **The Currency System**:
  - **Gold Sovereign**: High-denomination bullion coin (95% fine gold), stamped with the crowned sun of Dun-Carrow. Used for wholesale cargo, noble ransoms, and state loans.
  - **Silver Crown**: Standard trade coin (80% silver), valued at 20 silver crowns to 1 gold sovereign. The primary coin of mercenary contracts, livestock sales, and guild fees.
  - **Copper Farthing**: Fractional coin for common laborers, bread, and ale. 12 copper farthings to 1 silver crown.
- **The Admiralty Registry & Salvage Code**: Under the *Charter of Maritime and Expeditionary Liberties*, an adventuring party must hold an active corporate charter registered with the Municipal Assize. All bounties, salvaged relics, and monster carcasses are subject to a 15% ducal excise unless counter-signed by the company's designated legal factor. If a charter's designated quartermaster resigns or dies without an endorsed successor, all company assets are placed into sovereign escrow for sixty days at a 40% court demurrage penalty.
- **Debt Covenants & Foreclosure Law**: Debt markers endorsed with the Great Wax Seal of the Merchant Assize allow the holder to impound pledged chattels, ships, real estate, and municipal tax-farming rights without prior ducal consent.

## 3. The Metaphysics of Divine Favor & Arcane Limitations

- **The Goddess of the Dawn**: An ancient solar deity worshipped by the populace and the nobility. Her favors are aesthetic and martial: she bestows blinding solar blades, physical beauty, and radiant shields upon champions who strike heroic postures. However, her blessings are entirely dependent on public spectacle and pride; they provide zero protection against cold hunger, logistics failures, economic starvation, or cold iron dampening fields.
- **Cold Iron & Anti-Arcane Dampening**: Pure meteoric bog-iron, forged without coal at low heat, emits an innate resonance that disrupts divine mana and sorcerous incantations. When laid into floor lattices or arbalest quarrels, cold iron snuffs out magical auras within paces.
- **Sorcerous Mana-Crystals**: High sorcery requires physical catalysts. Refined blue crystals mined from the northern frost-crags are strictly controlled as state contraband; without them, sorcerers suffer severe mana-exhaustion, bleeding from the gums and ears if they attempt high-tier evocation.

---

## 4. Military Organization & Feudal Factions

- **The Archon's Guard**: Gouty, decorative knights clad in velvet and chased steel, useful only for palace parades and ceremonial escorts.
- **The City Wardens**: Six hundred professional watchmen and crossbowmen commanded by hard-bitten sergeants. Severely underfunded, regularly waiting three to six months for their pay.
- **The Northern Levies**: Peasant conscripts armed with ash pikes and quilted jacks, dispatched to the border forts to freeze and die whenever frost-orcs raid across the river.
- **The Mercantile Syndicates**: Powerful shipping and banking houses (The Inland Mercantile Trust, The Norrmark Factors, and House Belmonte) who hold the state's debts and dictate policy through financial leverage.
