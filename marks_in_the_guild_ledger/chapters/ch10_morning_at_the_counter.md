# Chapter 10: Morning at the Counter

The guild hall at opening hour smells like floor wax, cold hearth, and the particular soap Luna uses on the counters. You are early — earlier than the quest board has been restocked — and she is behind the main counter in her full uniform: blonde bun, burgundy band, the stubborn tuft, the low-cut white top, the red bow at her throat, the three orange toggles, the gold star pinned to the right sleeve. She looks, at first glance, exactly as she looked four days ago when she stamped your registration note. Then she glances up, and her gold eyes go warm, and the corner of her mouth hooks up in that particular way that says *I know what you look like without any of this on.*

"Morning, Arith." She says the name like she's testing a seal. "You're two minutes early. That's a two-minute fine in my ledger."

"I'll pay it in tarts."

"You'll pay it in *interest*," she says, and stamps a quest slip, and slides it across the counter to you. "Grain shipment at the east mill gate. Ostrow's asking for you personally — he said, and I quote, 'that broker tricked four goblins into their own chute and he wants the same for the rats in the silo.'" She lowers her voice. "I checked the rate. It's a good one."

"And after?"

"After, you come back here and tell me about it, and I'll pretend to file it in the record book the way I pretend I don't know what you look like under that jacket." She says it deadpan, professional, and only the faint tremor at the corner of her mouth gives her away. The counter is open. The hall is filling. The world is watching — and neither of you is playing a part that needs hiding anymore.

---

The morning goes the way mornings go. You clear the silo — the rats aren't goblins, but the funnel trick holds up; Ostrow pays in small Eris coins and a jar of plum jam "for the lady at the counter, on account of her liking sweet things" — and on the way back to the guild you pass the prosecutor's office at the corner of the forum, where a woman with long black hair and gold-rimmed glasses is standing in the doorway with a stack of docket folders, watching the street with the patient wariness of someone who has seen too many confessions turn into nonsense.

"Adventurer," she calls, sharp as a papercut. "That's a fresh collar. You'd better have a quest slip or a very good story." Then her gaze flicks past you to the guild hall. "Ah. *The* adventurer." Her tone changes entirely — bright, delighted, predatory in an entirely different way. "The one who's been keeping our receptionist's calendar busy. Excellent. In that case, I have a form you should sign." She introduces herself in two words — "Sena. Prosecutor." — and produces a membership card from her stack as if she's been carrying it for this exact moment. "The Association to Protect Women's Chances of Marriage. Luna's a member. Terrifyingly committed, actually — says she has no use for it anymore, which I take to mean the opposite."

"I'll consider it signed."

"See that you do." She tucks the card back with a decisive snap, already moving on, and over her shoulder she calls, "The association has very high standards, so I'll be watching the guild records. Don't make a liar out of me."

You pocket the acceptance in your mind the way you pocket everything that matters, and walk the last few streets to the guild hall wondering whether the joke is on her or on you. It is neither. The first thing you hear through the open doors is the magical speaker crackling to life overhead:

"-and to the adventurer registered as Arith, second floor, main counter: your payment voucher for the Funnel Mill silo work has been approved by the collator's desk, which notes that your account is in *perfect standing*, a condition it calls, quote, 'the best paperwork I've handled in a decade.'"

A few heads turn. A familiar, muffled voice — the same one you've heard ordering quest slips, swearing at Dust, and whispering *where were we* in the lamplight — continues, dry as old parchment:

"Additionally, the collator requests that Arith report for a *settlement meeting* at closing, to review the standing account and, if acceptable, to renew the deposit for another term."

A goblin-killer at the next table snorts into his ale. "Did the collator just ask him on a date *over the speaker*?"

"I keep impeccable records," Luna calls back through the speaker, serenely, "and my records show a standing appointment."

You are already smiling like an idiot, and you can't stop.

---

The settlement meeting comes at closing. The hall empties; the quest board goes home for the night; the lamps dim to their evening wicks. Luna is the only receptionist left — because she *arranged* it, you realize, watching her lock the deposit cabinet with a small private key and pocket it: a second collator has been hired, a serious woman with a pinned bun who left at the last bell to pick up her own life. Luna lowers her voice.

"The three wrappers from the other morning went into the guild bin at dawn, tied in their cloth, exactly as I said." She says it plainly, without embarrassment, like the honest entry it is. "Three in, three out. No one asked. No one will. My records are, as always, immaculate."

"Then the count's clean," you say.

"The count was never the point." She comes around the counter — a breach of guild protocol she performs like a private joke, the receptionist stepping out of her booth to stand in the customer's lane — and takes your hand in that quick, precise grip of hers. "The *counter* was the point. The whole time." Her gold eyes find yours in the dim. "You stepped up to it the first morning and asked for credit, and I let you have it, and that's the part I'll never file away."

Around you both the hall settles into shadow: the quest board, the card-crystal machine gone dark, the smell of floor wax and the ghost of toad-skewers. The stack of quest slips waits where she left it, and beside it, open to a fresh page, the counter's big record book — and Luna takes up her pen in the lamplight, and turns to a clean leaf.

"One last entry," she says, and writes, in her precise looping hand, in the margin of the open record book — not the ledger, not the quest slips, but the book itself, in that space where the guild's clerk records quiet, important events:

> *Arith — in good standing. Renewed by mutual agreement, the collator's terms. Term: indefinite.*

She caps the pen. The gold star on her sleeve catches the lamplight, and she looks up at you, and the smile she wears is the one from the morning kitchen — warm at the edges, hungry in the middle, keeping the last page of the record open on purpose.

The reader closes the book.

The counter is closed. The records are kept.

And the ledger that no one in the guild was ever meant to see balances — three in, three out, and one name written in the margin, in a hand that means to keep it.
