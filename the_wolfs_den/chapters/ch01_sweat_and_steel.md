# Sweat and Steel

The heavy oak training blade struck my parry with a crack that vibrated straight through the bones of my forearms.

I set my rear heel into the woven straw of the dojo floor, absorbing the impact, and turned the edge aside. The blade slid past my shoulder with a sharp hiss of seasoned timber. Immediately, the rush of displaced air brought the scent of wild cedar shavings and hot, salt-slicked skin.

"Still too stiff on your pivot, Yuuki!" Makoto barked.

She didn't reset her stance. Instead, she drove inside my guard on the rebound, her bare feet slapping the tatami in a tight two-step shuffle. The white cotton tank top she wore clung translucent against her ribs, drenched nearly grey with perspiration from three uninterrupted hours of forms and sparring. Across her forehead, strands of deep violet hair stuck in jagged spikes to her flushed brow. Her wolf ears—perked high, alert to the smallest shift in my weight—twitched as she leaned into my chest, locking our wooden crossguards together in a tight, breathless press.

I held my ground, leaning my shoulder into hers. The heat coming off her body struck me like opening a furnace door. Through the thin linen of our dogi, I could feel the rapid hammer of her pulse against my collarbone. Her breath puffed in short, hot bursts against the side of my jaw, smelling faintly of roasted tea and bitter lemon peel.

"Look at that stubborn jaw," she muttered, her amber eyes narrowing until the pupils sharpened into golden slits. Her canine fangs caught the low evening light filtering through the high paper transoms. "You think you can outlast a wolf on stamina? In your dreams!"

She twisted her wrists, throwing her full body weight into a sharp disengage to sweep for my lead ankle. I read the flex of her calves, hopped the sweeping foot, and countered with a firm step inside her reach, laying the flat of my blade lightly against her ribs.

Makoto froze. Her ribs expanded against the oak wood as she drew in a deep, ragged lungful of air. Her amber eyes darted down to the timber resting on her side, then snapped back up to meet mine. For three long seconds, neither of us moved. The only sound in the vast training hall was the harsh, rhythmic rasp of her lungs and the steady drip of sweat falling from the tip of her chin onto the polished floorboards between our toes.

Then a loud, cheerful clap shattered the silence from the edge of the veranda.

"Aha! That's a clean point to our Princess Knight!"

Kaori sat perched on the low cedar railing overlooking the courtyard bamboo, swinging her bare, grass-stained legs back and forth. She wore her signature black brawler crop top fastened with a knotted rope bow, paired with dark workout shorts and a vibrant red-patterned Ryukyu jacket tied casually around her waist. Her dark brunette hair, cut in a choppy mullet around her temples and cascading down her back in a thick, knotted tail, bounced with every swing, while the twin ahoge cowlicks atop her head twitched merrily. She held half a cold mountain melon in one hand and a carved wooden spoon in the other, completely unbothered by the three-mile sprint drill she had completed twenty minutes earlier.

"Nankurunaisa, Makoto!" Kaori called out with a sunny grin, flashing her canine teeth. "You went charging in head-first like a wild boar again. You gotta flow with the strike, like the ocean tide rolling over the reef! Right, Yuuki?"

Makoto clicked her tongue, lowering her practice sword with an irritated snap. She caught the bottom hem of her soaked tank top and dragged it up across her face to wipe the stinging salt from her eyes, heedlessly exposing the tight, corded muscle of her stomach and the pale curve of her lower ribs glistening with perspiration.

"Shut it, Kaori! I wasn't rushing, I was testing his close-quarters defense. Besides, who asked you to sit there gorging on melon while the rest of us are still putting in the sweat?"

"Because eating when you're hungry is the best way to get strong!" Kaori laughed, scooping another bright crimson bite of melon into her mouth. "Here, catch!"

She flicked a fresh wooden skewer loaded with melon cubes across the mats. I caught it out of the air by the bare shaft and held it out toward Makoto.

The wolf-girl glared at the sweet fruit, then at my outstretched hand. Her chest heaved beneath the damp cotton. Her ears pinned back against her hair in brief, defensive embarrassment before she leaned forward, clamped her sharp teeth around the top melon cube, and ripped it off the skewer. The sweet red juice glistened on her lower lip. She chewed furiously, her jaw working as she stared me down.

"Fine. You got me on the pivot," she grumbled, swallowing hard, her voice dropping into a rough, scratchy rasp. "Next time, I'm taking your feet out before you even blink."

"I would strongly advise against any further kinetic excursions this evening, Makoto-san."

A quiet, crisp voice carried across the dojo from the archive alcove beside the weapons rack.

Kasumi stepped out from behind a row of tall cedar scrolls, holding a brass-rimmed magnifying glass in one hand and an ink-stained ledger in the other. Her dark, straight purple hair fell neatly across her shoulders, completely untouched by the humid summer air, and her neat linen detective's coat was draped neatly across a nearby stool. Beneath her thin cotton under-robe, the pale, delicate lines of her collarbones showed clearly where the collar had been loosened to let the evening draft cool her neck.

She pushed her oval spectacles up the bridge of her small nose with the tip of her forefinger, her purple-pink eyes assessing us with surgical precision.

"According to my precise chronological accounting," Kasumi said, tapping the open page with the flat of a charcoal stylus, "the two of you have engaged in four hours and twelve minutes of continuous combat conditioning. Your resting heart rates are elevated by forty-seven percent, core body temperatures exceed normal operating thresholds, and lactic acid accumulation in the quadriceps will inevitably induce muscle tremors within the hour. Continuing beyond this parameter is not rigorous vigilance—it is elementary obstinacy."

Her pointed Doberman ears twitched as she took three measured steps toward us. Her slender tail flicked behind her skirt, betraying an undercurrent of agitation that her composed tone tried to conceal.

"Furthermore, Assistant," Kasumi added, turning her gaze directly onto me, her voice softening into a subtle, pointed drawl, "you took two glancing blows to your left shoulder in the second round that you have failed to properly ice. A detective cannot allow her primary assistant to degrade his physical utility through sheer carelessness."

"Look who's lecturing on health," Makoto snorted, leaning her wooden sword against the rack. "You've been hunched over those dusty tax ledgers since noon, Kasumi. Your neck looks stiffer than a dried squid."

"My investigative labor serves the public order of Landosol," Kasumi countered smoothly, though a faint dusting of pink crept across the tips of her pointed ears. "The Shadows do not pause their illicit machinations simply because the weather is warm."

"Now, now, everyone. Such fiery spirits even after the sun has set behind the Western Gate."

The heavy sliding doors leading to the main corridor slid open with a soft, whisper-light hum of polished cherry wood runners.

Maho stepped onto the tatami, carrying a broad black-lacquered tray bearing a heavy iron kettle and five ceramic cups. She had already changed out of her ceremonial day robes into a loose, unlined summer yukata of pale celadon silk, tied casually at the waist with an emerald sash. Her voluminous walnut-brown hair was gathered over one shoulder in a soft braid, leaving the slender, porcelain nape of her neck bare to the evening air. Behind her, her massive, plush fox tail swayed in a gentle, mesmerizing arc, brushing against the lacquered floorboards with every delicate step.

"I have brewed a kettle of chilled roasted barley tea," Maho announced in her melodic, lilting Kyoto cadence, tilting her head with a tranquil, elegant smile. Her bright emerald eyes settled upon me, sparkling with playful warmth. "Our brave Prince has labored so valiantly in defense of the realm today. Surely even a royal champion requires sweet water to cool his burning brow, does he not?"

"You're laying it on thick again, Maho," Makoto grunted, though her eyes immediately tracked the frost clinging to the sides of the ceramic cups. "Is there ice in that?"

"Naturally, Makoto-han," Maho replied, kneeling with fluid grace onto a round straw cushion and placing the tray between us. The motion caused the front of her loose yukata to part by a fraction of an inch, revealing the pale, smooth hollow between her collarbones and the delicate swell of her chest before she adjusted the silk with a gentle sweep of her sleeve. "A princess must always see that her gallant protectors are well tended. Come, sit."

Kaori was already off the railing in a single agile leap, landing soundlessly on the mats beside the tray. She dropped cross-legged onto the tatami, her bare knees still dusty from the yard, and eagerly wrapped both palms around a condensation-beaded cup.

"Uwah, that's ice cold! Bless you, Maho-san!" Kaori took a deep, greedy gulp, sighing with uninhibited satisfaction as a drop of tea escaped the corner of her lips and rolled down the warm column of her throat, soaking into the collar of her cropped tank. "Cold tea after sweating out your soul is the best feeling in the whole world. Hey, Yuuki, drink up before Makoto steals the whole pot!"

I took a seat on the woven rush mat opposite Maho. The tatami felt cool against the soles of my feet, but my shoulders and chest radiated heat like an iron stove. I reached for the cup Maho poured for me. As our fingers brushed over the coarse glaze of the ceramic, her soft, pale hand lingered against my knuckles for a heartbeat longer than necessary. Her emerald eyes held mine, crinkling at the corners in quiet amusement.

"Your hands are trembling ever so faintly, Ouji-sama," she murmured softly, her Kyoto accent rolling like honey. "Such fierce exertion. The bath furnace in the west wing has already reached a pleasant rolling heat. Soaking in the cedar tub will draw the fire out of your weary limbs."

Makoto sat down heavily beside me, cross-legged, dropping her elbows onto her knees. Her posture was wide, uninhibited, and thoroughly exhausted. With every breath she took, the scent of her sweat—sharp, salty, and tinged with the distinct musky warmth of a wolf beastfolk—curled around me, mingling with the earthy aroma of the barley tea. Her bare arm pressed against mine from shoulder to elbow, her skin slick and hot against my sleeve.

"The bath sounds damn good right now," Makoto muttered, draining her cup in one pull and setting it down with a heavy clatter. "My back feels like someone took a sledgehammer to it. But I've still got to lock up the yard and sharpen the perimeter stakes before anyone turns in."

"I have already inspected the perimeter wards," Kasumi said, settling gracefully onto her knees beside Kaori, holding her teacup with both hands. She blew gently across the surface, her gaze flicking between Makoto's bare shoulder pressed against my arm and the quiet stillness of my face. Her glasses caught the orange glow of the lantern hanging from the central beam. "The locks are secure. However, the internal records for our patrol expenditures require cross-referencing before morning. I had intended to review the ledgers with my Assistant tonight."

"Eh? More paperwork, Kasumi?" Kaori groaned, slumping backward onto her elbows with a carefree pout that stretched the fabric over her chest. "Come on, the bathwater is gonna get cold! I was gonna ask Yuuki to help me stretch out my hamstrings in the warm water. My calves are like rocks today!"

"Stretching in a communal bath is an egregious breach of etiquette," Kasumi reprimanded, though her cheeks colored slightly beneath the lantern's glow. "And entirely inappropriate for a mixed company."

"We're all pack here, aren't we?" Kaori countered, grinning cheekily. "Nothing to hide!"

Maho chuckled behind the sleeve of her yukata, her plush fox tail curling around the edge of the tray until the soft, golden-brown fur brushed lightly against my knee.

"Each of you presents such urgent matters," Maho said, her voice dropping to a smooth, teasing hum. "Yet the evening is still young, and the guildhall is quiet. Perhaps our Prince should decide for himself where his attention is most gravely needed."

The silence settled over the circle again, thick and heavy with the ambient heat of the summer night.

To my left, Makoto leaned back against her hands, her bare shoulders rolling as she stretched her neck. The deep violet fur of her ears twitched, angled directly toward my face, listening for my breath. Across the low table, Kaori stretched like a lazy hound, her ruby eyes bright with open, uncomplicated warmth, while Kasumi methodically adjusted her spectacles, her fingers tightening around her teacup as her gaze searched my eyes for an answer. And directly before me, Maho smiled serenely, her tea cup cradled in her lap, the tip of her luxurious tail resting warmly against my leg.

The lantern on the beam sputtered softly in the evening draft, casting long, wavering shadows across the cedar floor.
