# Ending 7: Evidence & Instinct

The pale gold rays of dawn slanted through the wooden slats of the archive transoms, cutting long geometric bands across the cedar floorboards.

Outside, the morning sparrows chirped among the courtyard bamboo, their brisk calls mingling with the distant chime of the bell from Landosol’s central square. The sharp, clean chill of the morning air crept through the foundation vents, slowly washing away the dense, musky heat that had occupied the lower wing throughout the night.

In the center of the archive alcove, Kasumi sat cross-legged on a thick straw cushion, her linen gown buttoned up to her throat. A heavy wool blanket was draped securely over her shoulders like a scholar's mantle, but the fabric did little to conceal the unsteady tremor in her hands.

Her brass-rimmed spectacles sat straight on the bridge of her small nose, though every three minutes she paused to push them up with a trembling forefinger. Before her lay a fresh scroll of cream-colored parchment, pinned down at the corners with brass weights. Beside it, a shallow stone inkwell held freshly ground sumi ink, emitting its distinct, earthy fragrance into the cool air.

Kasumi dipped her horsehair brush into the black well, took a steadying breath, and touched the tip to the paper.

"According to standard municipal protocol," she muttered aloud, her voice sounding thin, raspy, and thoroughly hoarse, "the third-watch incident report must contain an itemized summary of nocturnal expenditures, perimeter integrity, and... and physical readiness of the investigative unit."

Her brush hesitated over the page. A heavy droplet of black ink gathered on the hairs, swelled, and fell with a soft *plip*, creating an unsightly dark blot directly beneath the date header.

Kasumi’s ears drooped until they rested flat against her purple hair. Her face flared into a brilliant, incandescent crimson, the color rushing down her slender neck and disappearing beneath the high collar of her robe.

"Tch. Still obsessing over your stupid paperwork?"

A lazy, rough voice called out from the adjoining tatami platform.

Makoto lay sprawled on her stomach across a wide futon, her long athletic limbs tossed outward in complete disregard for propriety. She wore only an unbuttoned training gi that exposed the defined valley of her spine and the pale curve of her ribs, mottled with faint purplish bruises and dry streaks of salt. Her violet wolf ears swiveled lazily toward the sound of the ink droplet, twitching with contented exhaustion.

She propped her chin on her crossed forearms, her golden eyes half-lidded as she watched the petite detective struggle with the brush.

"Your hand is shaking so hard you're gonna poke a hole through that paper, Kasumi," Makoto smirked, her canine fangs glinting in the morning light. "What's the matter? Did four hours of getting your hips rattled scramble your handwriting?"

Kasumi’s brush snapped in two between her rigid fingers.

She turned slowly, her purple eyes wide and blazing with mortified fury behind her spectacles.

"Makoto-san! That commentary is an intolerable breach of guild decorum!" she hissed, though her voice lacked its usual crisp authority, sounding instead like a squeaky, overextended reed. "What transpired in the kitchen was... was a severe regulatory anomaly induced by environmental heat and temporary neuro-chemical impairment! It does not warrant your vulgar vulgarities!"

"Anomaly my foot," Makoto chuckled, rolling onto her side with a slow, feline stretch that made her vertebrae pop in three distinct cracks. She winced slightly, rubbing the back of her neck with a calloused hand, her wolf tail swishing over the edge of the futon. "You were begging like a puppy five minutes in. 'Assistant, touch the bottom, my brain is melting!' Ha! I thought you were gonna pass out and drool all over my boots."

"I did not articulate such unscientific gibberish!" Kasumi gasped, throwing both hands over her burning face, her Doberman tail thumping in rapid, frantic beats beneath her blanket. "I was attempting to formulate a tactical appraisal of his mechanical force! Any vocalizations emitted during the event were purely autonomic respiratory reflexes!"

"Yeah, sure. Autonomic reflexes that soaked the entire third tatami mat," Makoto retorted, her grin widening until her sharp teeth showed in full triumph. "Don't worry, little detective. The wolf kept your secrets. I even wiped down the prep table before dawn so Kaori wouldn't ask why the wood smelled like a breeding pen."

Kasumi lowered her hands by an inch, peeking through her trembling fingers at the smug wolf-girl. Her lower lip quivered with sheer, unadulterated embarrassment, yet the faint curve of her mouth betrayed a quiet, helpless warmth that she was powerless to conceal.

"You... you cleaned the counter?" Kasumi murmured softly.

"Course I did," Makoto grunted, turning her face away toward the garden window, a sudden rush of pink dusting the high points of her cheekbones. "I'm the vanguard, aren't I? I take care of the camp after a raid. Besides... if Maho saw those stains, she’d have had us roleplaying as court concubines for the next six months."

I stepped through the sliding shoji into the archive, carrying a heavy cedar tray laden with three steaming bowls of white rice, grilled salted salmon, and a clay teapot of roasted barley tea.

The aroma of savory fish, toasted sesame, and fresh steam immediately filled the room, cutting through the lingering tang of ink and cedar.

Makoto’s ears shot straight up. Her stomach let out a ferocious, thunderous rumble that echoed off the book scrolls, causing her to clamp both hands over her midriff with an embarrassed scowl.

"Food," she groaned, scrambling off the futon onto her knees and planting her elbows on the low lacquer table between us. "Thank the gods. I feel like my stomach's eating my backbone."

Kasumi hastily adjusted her blanket, smoothing her rumpled skirt over her bare knees as I set the tray down on the floorboards. When I sat beside her, our shoulders brushed lightly through the linen of our clothes.

Kasumi glanced sideways at me, her violet eyes soft and hesitant behind her spectacles.

"Good morning, Assistant," she said quietly, her voice dropping into a gentle, intimate cadence. "Did you... did you manage to secure adequate rest after the conclusion of our... physical excursion?"

"Better than you, by the look of it," Makoto interrupted, already shoveling a massive mouthful of steaming rice and crispy salmon skin into her mouth with wooden chopsticks. She chewed vigorously, letting out a deep, satisfied groan. "His arms are steady as stone. Look at him! He's pouring tea without spilling a single drop, while you're over there looking like you got hit by a charging minotaur."

Kasumi ignored the barb, reaching out with both hands to accept the hot ceramic cup I poured for her. As her fingers wrapped around the coarse clay, she allowed the pads of her thumbs to rest gently against my knuckles for a long, quiet moment. The tremor in her hands slowly subsided, replaced by a deep, steady calm that radiated from her small palms.

"Your pulse has stabilized to sixty-eight beats per minute," she observed softly, her gaze dropping to the visible bite mark on my left shoulder—the clear, twin puncture wounds left by Makoto’s fangs, now ringed by a faint, purplish bruise. Her fingertips lightly brushed the edge of the mark, her expression a mix of quiet possessiveness and gentle reprimand. "Though the epidermal trauma on your deltoid will require an application of arnica salve before the noon patrol. Certain individuals lack all semblance of moderation."

"Hey! That's a mark of the pack!" Makoto barked around a mouthful of pickled radish. "In wild wolf tribes, marking your mate keeps stray scavengers from getting any funny ideas. You ought to be thanking me for protecting your precious Assistant."

"I am perfectly capable of safeguarding my subordinates through administrative vigilance and sound tactical deduction," Kasumi countered, lifting the cup to her lips and taking a delicate sip of the hot tea. The warmth brought a healthy, rosy glow back to her pale cheeks. "Physical mutilation is entirely superfluous."

"Is that so?" Makoto challenged, leaning across the table with a sly, knowing smirk. "Then why did you leave those three scratches down his ribs with your fingernails, Miss Deductive Genius?"

Kasumi choked on her tea, coughing into her sleeve as her cheeks flared once again into bright, unmistakable crimson.

"T-That was... an accidental kinetic traction error!" she sputtered, setting her teacup down with an unsteady clatter. "My equilibrium was compromised by the sheer velocity of the—"

"Traction error," Makoto snorted, laughing heartily as she reached across the table and ruffled Kasumi’s hair, messing up the neat purple bangs and sending her spectacles askew once more. "You're unbelievable, Kasumi. Absolutely hopeless."

Kasumi batted at Makoto’s hand with half-hearted annoyance, but she didn't pull away. Instead, she leaned slightly into the warmth of the room, her pointed Doberman ears relaxing, swiveling toward the courtyard breeze with quiet contentment.

The three of us sat together on the woven straw, sharing the simple breakfast in the golden morning light.

Between us, the half-written incident report lay forgotten on the floorboards, its single dark blot of ink drying slowly in the cool draft from the garden. The sword racks in the yard gleamed in the rising sun, awaiting the afternoon drills, but for now, the quiet hall belonged entirely to the pack—bound by shared sweat, spoken challenges, and the undeniable truth written upon our tired bodies.

Makoto drained the last of her miso soup, set the empty ceramic bowl down on the tray, and let out a long, relaxed sigh that sounded almost like a purr. She reached down, took a fresh slice of sweet red melon from the side dish, and held it out toward Kasumi.

"Here," the wolf grunted, looking away with gruff affection. "Eat something, bookworm. You're going to need the sugar if we're doing perimeter rounds today."

Kasumi looked at the fruit, then at Makoto’s flushed, honest face. A small, genuine smile touched the corner of the detective’s lips. She reached out, took the melon, and took a small, crisp bite, the sweet juice glistening on her lower lip.

"Acceptable," Kasumi whispered, adjusting her spectacles one final time before meeting my eyes with quiet, unwavering devotion. "The logistics for today's patrol are hereby approved."

I took a final sip of the warm barley tea and set the cup down upon the tray, watching the morning light creep across the courtyard stones.
