# Happily Ever After

The morning sun filtered through the gossamer canopies of the second-floor suite in soft, golden ribbons of light.

I woke to the rhythmic, soothing stroke of a wooden comb gliding through dense fur.

Maho sat beside me on the edge of the low sleeping platform, draped in a fresh morning yukata of pale rose silk. Her walnut-brown hair cascaded down her back in loose, glossy waves, and between her delicate hands, she held the luxuriant golden-brown plume of her massive fox tail, patiently working a boxwood comb through the soft down.

Beside her on the velvet bolster, Arthur the rabbit sat proudly upright once more, his little felt crown carefully re-centered between his plush ears. Beside him, Lancelot the penguin kept watch from the windowsill, and Gawain the puppy rested happily against her embroidered slippers.

Maho paused her combing, glancing over her shoulder as she felt me stir. Her emerald eyes sparkled with serene, tranquil warmth, her triangular fox ears swiveling forward to catch my waking breath.

"Good morning, my noble Prince," she purred in her melodic Kyoto cadence, her voice rich with sleepy contentment. "Arthur has delivered his morning report. The dawn is bright, the skies are clear of storm clouds, and the kingdom awakens in perfect peace."

I sat up, the silk quilt pooling around my waist. My shoulders and chest carried a pleasant, dull ache from the night's exertions, and across my collarbones and ribs, faint pink impressions of her small fingernails remained like delicate courtly emblems.

I reached out, resting my hand against the warm, plush fur of her tail.

Maho drew in a soft, trembling breath, her ears flattening in sudden, involuntary pleasure before she tilted her head with a gentle smile.

"Even in the morning light, your hands are so greedy, Ouji-sama," she murmured, leaning into my touch. Her tail twitched within my fingers, curling affectionately around my wrist. "It took half an hour of warm lavender water and scented cedar soap to wash your royal seed from my tail fur. The scent of you is woven into every strand. If I walk into the city like this, every beastfolk hound in Landosol will know who claimed the guild master of Caon."

"Let them know," I said quietly.

Maho froze, her comb hovering in mid-air. Her emerald eyes widened by a fraction of an inch, swimming with sudden, profound emotion. Then a radiant, breathtaking blush bloomed across her cheeks, turning her pale porcelain skin the color of morning cherry blossoms.

"My, my," she whispered, her lips parting in a soft, breathless gasp as she pressed her fingertips to her cheek. "Such unyielding boldness from our quiet champion. A princess's heart could truly faint under such tender authority."

She set her comb onto the cedar vanity, turned toward me on the edge of the bed, and took both of my hands into hers.

"When I established this guildhall," she confessed softly, her gaze dropping to our intertwined fingers, "many in the capital dismissed me as an eccentric heiress indulging in childish fantasies. My father’s trading consortium wanted me to arrange a polite political marriage to a wealthy textile factor in the Eastern Ward. They saw a pampered merchant's daughter who played at being a princess because the real world was too harsh. But I wanted Caon to be a sanctuary—a place where beastfolk who had been cast aside could stand tall, protect the innocent, and find a family that truly loved them. Makoto had her hot-blooded justice, Kaori had her island warmth, and Kasumi had her brilliant deductive mind. I wanted to give them a home where they never had to apologize for being who they were."

She looked up, her emerald eyes shining with tears of genuine, unguarded gratitude.

"Yet in caring for everyone else... in playing the benevolent queen who always smiles and never weeps... I was lonely, Yuuki. I yearned for someone who wouldn't just revere the guild master from afar. I wanted a champion who would take me into his arms, strip away the courtly pretense, and love the woman behind the crown. Someone whose touch makes my tail quiver and my heart race without fear."

I leaned forward, cupping the nape of her neck, and kissed her forehead right between her twitching fox ears, letting my thumbs stroke the delicate, velvet tufts of fur at their base.

Maho closed her eyes, letting out a long, shuddering sigh of pure, tranquil peace. Her arms looped around my neck, holding me close as the morning breeze stirred the gossamer curtains, her luxuriant tail wrapping warmly around my waist.

"And now," she murmured against my ear, her breath sweet with honey, "my fairy tale is complete."

She rose with fluid grace and walked to the sliding shoji overlooking the private veranda.

The morning breeze swept into the room, carrying the clean aroma of dew-soaked pines and the distant hum of the bustling market square. Maho lifted a bronze kettle from the charcoal brazier by the balcony, pouring boiling water into a celadon pot filled with toasted green tea leaves and dried lotus petals.

Before she could pour the first cup, the sound of heavy, rapid footsteps clattered up the cherry-wood staircase.

"Maho! Did you take the extra ledger keys from the—"

Makoto barged through the open doorway, carrying a wooden tray loaded with four steaming bowls of barley porridge and grilled salted trout. She wore her clean training dogi, her violet hair pulled into its usual high ponytail.

She stopped dead in the center of the room.

Her golden amber eyes swept over the rumpled velvet bedding, the discarded emerald sash lying crumpled beneath the writing desk, and the unmistakable, sweet aroma of raw fox musk and spent passion lingering beneath the scent of burning lotus tea.

Makoto’s gaze locked onto Maho’s glowing, thoroughly satisfied face, then drifted to me sitting bare-chested on the silk cushions, bearing the faint scratches of the guild master’s nails.

The wooden tray in Makoto’s hands gave a violent, rattling jolt.

"You... you two..." Makoto stammered, her voice cracking as her wolf ears shot straight up toward the rafters. Her face flushed a furious, incandescent crimson that rivaled a sunset. "In the guild master's quarters?! On the royal pillows?!"

"Good morning, Makoto-han," Maho greeted serenely, not batting an eyelash as she poured the pale green tea into two porcelain cups. "You are up exceptionally early. Did you sleep soundly beneath our humble roof?"

"Did I sleep soundly?!" Makoto yelled, waving her wooden ladle in the air like a broadsword. "How was anyone supposed to sleep when you were screaming about falling castle gates at three in the morning?! I thought the guildhall was being besieged by an army of Shadows! I had my hand on my sword for an hour!"

"A knight must always remain vigilant, Makoto-san," Kasumi’s calm, analytical voice drifted through the doorway.

The petite detective stepped into the room, holding her casebook and wearing her freshly pressed pleated uniform. Her purple hair was neatly brushed, her oval spectacles polished, though as she stepped over the threshold, her sharp eyes immediately noticed Arthur the rabbit’s slightly rumpled fur and the distinctive scent permeating the silk curtains.

Kasumi pushed her glasses up her nose with a single, measured finger. Her slender Doberman tail flicked with quiet, clinical satisfaction.

"According to my acoustic analysis from the ground floor archives," Kasumi stated with devastating precision, "the auditory emissions from this chamber between two-fifteen and three-forty-five in the morning exceeded eighty-two decibels. The linguistic content consisted of ninety-four percent courtly romantic allegories and six percent unadulterated primal shrieking. The probability that an external hostile incursion occurred is zero point zero percent. The probability that the Assistant successfully conquered the Maho-Maho Kingdom is one hundred percent."

"Kasumi! Don't encourage her!" Makoto groaned, slamming the breakfast tray down onto the low cedar table with a heavy clatter, burying her burning face in her hands. "Why is everyone in this guild completely shameless?!"

"Because being happy is the whole point of living, Makoto!"

A cheerful shout came from the veranda as Kaori vaulted lightly over the second-floor railing from the trellis outside, landing on the floorboards with a sunny, athletic thud. She carried a wooden bowl filled with freshly picked mountain peaches, still cool and beaded with dew. Her brunette mullet bounced over her shoulders, her short dog tail wagging with boundless enthusiasm.

"Ooh! Peaches and grilled trout! Breakfast is served!" Kaori cheered, dropping cross-legged onto the velvet carpet beside the table. Her nose twitched in the air, catching the sweet, heavy scent of Maho’s bedroom, and her scarlet eyes crinkled with pure, mischievous joy. "Nankurunaisa! Looks like our Prince finally made Maho's fairy tale come true! Did you get your happily ever after, Maho-san?"

Maho smiled—a radiant, untroubled expression of pure royal contentment.

"Indeed, Kaori-han," Maho replied, floating over to the table and setting down her celadon teapot. "The chronicle has concluded its most glorious chapter. The Prince fought valiantly, the princess surrendered with honor, and the kingdom has never known greater joy."

"I am going to jump out that window," Makoto muttered through her fingers.

"Do not attempt an egress from the second floor without proper deceleration, Makoto-san," Kasumi advised dryly, taking a seat beside the desk and picking up a pair of chopsticks. "You will sprain your ankles, and our patrol efficiency is already compromised."

"Eat your porridge, Captain!" Kaori laughed, thrusting a wooden bowl into Makoto’s hands. "You're always grumpy when your stomach is empty! Besides, look at how pretty Maho’s tail looks today! It’s all brushed and shiny, like a freshly polished gold coin!"

"Kaori, I swear to the gods, if you mention tails one more time today, I will make you do two hundred laps around the outer courtyard in full iron plate," Makoto growled, though she finally lowered her hands.

Her cheeks were still glowing a bright, flustered pink, but as she looked at Maho’s serene, radiant smile and my quiet, steady posture, the irritation melted from her golden eyes, replaced by an affectionate, exasperated warmth. She sat down cross-legged on the velvet carpet, took up her carved cedar chopsticks, and began to devour her porridge with hearty, brawling appetite.

"So," Makoto grunted between mouthfuls of savory fish, her golden eyes flicking toward me with a sharp, sidelong glance, "does this mean the Prince is officially staying in the royal suite from now on? Or do the rest of us still get a say in how the guildhall is run?"

"A true kingdom," Maho answered with graceful authority, her Kyoto accent singing like morning bells as she poured tea into four porcelain cups, "is governed by love, fellowship, and shared devotion. The Prince is the sword that guards our gates, but every knight and scholar of Caon holds an honored place at the royal table. There is plenty of affection in this court for all who dwell beneath our roof."

"Hear, hear!" Kaori cheered, holding up a sweet peach slice like a celebratory goblet. "Long live the Maho-Maho Kingdom! And long live our Prince!"

Kasumi took a quiet, delicate sip of her lotus tea, her oval spectacles reflecting the golden sunlight. A tiny, pleased smile played at the corner of her lips.

"While the sociopolitical terminology remains completely unconstitutional," Kasumi remarked with dry, deliberate poise, "the internal harmony of this organization has demonstrably improved by seventy-eight percent. I shall append an addendum to the guild charter recognizing the Prince's executive authority in domestic affairs."

"Does that mean we have to salute him before we spar, Miss Detective?" Makoto chuckled, wiping a speck of sauce from her chin. "Because if he thinks I'm bowing before I kick his shins, he's got another thing coming."

"A physical salute is optional, Makoto-san," Kasumi replied smoothly, adjusting her glasses with an immaculate flick of her wrist. "However, acknowledging the Prince's superior tactical stamina is now supported by overwhelming empirical evidence."

Kaori burst out laughing, clapping Makoto on the shoulder so hard the wolf-girl nearly spilled her tea.

The four beastfolk girls laughed together, their voices mingling with the warm summer breeze that drifted through the open veranda screens.

Around the low cedar table, the feast continued in joyful, unhurried warmth. Makoto bickered cheerfully with Kaori over the last piece of salted trout, while Kasumi made neat notes in her ledger between bites of sweet mountain peach. Maho sat close beside me, her pale silk yukata brushing against my bare arm, radiating peaceful, regal contentment.

Under the table, away from the teasing eyes of our companions, Maho’s massive fox tail slid across my lap once more.

The luxuriant golden-brown fur was warm, heavy, and delightfully soft, resting across my thighs like an emerald-and-gold royal mantle. As I rested my hand on the dense, silky down, her triangular fox ears twitched with exquisite, secret pleasure.

She leaned her head against my shoulder, her breath warm against my neck, smelling of sweet honey and lotus petals.

"The story may have found its ending, Ouji-sama," she whispered softly, her emerald eyes looking out over the sunlit rooftops of Landosol. "Yet in our kingdom... every dawn begins a new adventure. And as long as you wield your strength beside us... our fairy tale will never fade."

I smiled, took her soft, cool hand in mine, and lifted my teacup to greet the bright, peaceful summer morning.
