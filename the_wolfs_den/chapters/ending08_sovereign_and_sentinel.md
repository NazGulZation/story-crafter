# Ending 8: Sovereign & Sentinel

The soft murmur of morning wind swept through the courtyard bamboo, rustling the slender green stalks with the rhythmic whisper of dry parchment.

Across the low cedar veranda overlooking the koi pond, the first golden rays of dawn spilled over the tile eaves, warming the polished floorboards and scattering the cool blue shadows of night. The crisp, clean scent of damp garden earth and pine needles drifted through the open sliding screens, carrying away the lingering heat of the lower quarters.

Maho knelt gracefully upon a wide velvet cushion at the head of the low cedar dining table, dressed in a fresh, unlined yukata of soft lavender silk tied with a plum-colored sash. Her voluminous walnut-brown hair was neatly combed and gathered over her left shoulder in an intricate ceremonial braid, though the subtle, rosy glow coloring her porcelain cheeks belied her immaculate presentation.

Behind her, her massive, plush golden-brown fox tail rested in a wide, possessive arc across my lap, the luxuriant down radiating a pleasant, comforting warmth against my thighs. Every few moments, the soft tip would twitch in a slow, undulating curl, stroking the tops of my knees with rhythmic affection.

With steady, elegant hands, Maho lifted a pair of carved cedar chopsticks and turned two seasoned river trout over the glowing coals of a small clay brazier set into the table.

"The morning coals have reached a gentle, even heat," she announced in her melodic Kyoto cadence, tilting her head with a tranquil, radiant smile. Her emerald eyes settled upon me, sparkling with serene, courtly warmth. "In the royal annals of the Maho-Maho Kingdom, a sovereign must always ensure that her heroic Prince is nourished with the finest bounty of the mountain streams after a night of arduous valor."

Across the table, a loud, irritated snort shattered the refined atmosphere.

Makoto sat cross-legged on the rush mats, slumping heavily against the cedar pillar behind her. She wore a loose grey sleeveless shirt and a pair of dark linen trousers, but she had left the collar wide open, unashamedly displaying the dark, purplish hickey prominent beneath her collarbone and the faint red scratches lining the curve of her muscular neck. Her violet wolf ears twitched lazily in the morning draft, one ear drooping slightly in contented exhaustion.

"Royal bounty, my ass," Makoto grunted, though her golden eyes were locked greedily onto the sizzling fish skin crispening over the embers. "You slept like a dead log until an hour ago, Maho. I'm the one who had to haul two fresh water buckets from the courtyard well to scrub the kitchen tiles before Kaori woke up."

Maho chuckled behind the wide sleeve of her yukata—a rich, bell-like sound that carried the undisturbed serenity of a queen presiding over her court.

"And such devotion is precisely why you hold the sacred rank of vanguard sentinel, Makoto-han," Maho murmured smoothly, her emerald eyes dancing with teasing delight. "A loyal knight guards the inner keep, cleans the royal halls, and... when duty calls, offers herself body and soul to bolster the realm’s defenses alongside her sovereign."

Makoto’s cheeks flared instantly into a violent, incandescent crimson.

She choked on her breath, reaching out with both hands to grab a cup of chilled spring water and gulping it down with desperate haste. Her wolf tail whipped behind her, thumping loudly against the baseboard.

"W-Who offered themselves body and soul?!" Makoto spluttered, slamming the wooden cup onto the table with an unsteady clatter. "You're the one who lost your mind five minutes after he touched your stupid tail! 'Hauuu, Ouji-sama, melt the princess's brain!' You were screaming so loud I thought the royal palace sentries three miles away were gonna raid the guildhall!"

Maho’s fox ears flattened back by a mere millimeter, the faintest dusting of pink tinting the shell of her ears, but her courtly smile remained serene and untouchable.

"A royal lady expresses her appreciation with profound musicality," Maho replied with majestic poise, lifting a lacquered bowl of steaming white rice and setting it before me with two hands. "Unlike certain wild forest sentinels who resort to howling at the ceiling beams like an undisciplined street cur."

"I was not howling like a cur!" Makoto roared, leaning across the table with her fangs bared, though her blush had deepened until it engulfed her entire throat and the tips of her ears. "I was... I was asserting my authority as the alpha! And for the record, he filled me up with twice as much seed as he gave you!"

Maho paused, her chopsticks hovering over the sizzling trout. She tilted her chin, regarding Makoto with a slow, knowing gaze that dripped with quiet, unshakeable victory.

"Is that your official claim, Makoto-han?" Maho purred softly. "Yet, if my royal memory serves me correctly, you were begging on your knees beneath the prep table, asking our Prince to breed the wolf and break your wild pride until your jaw went completely slack."

Makoto froze. Her jaw dropped open, her golden eyes wide and round with sheer, mortified shock. For three long seconds, she looked like she wanted to melt straight through the cedar floorboards and bury herself in the earth beneath the foundation.

"I... you... that was..." she stammered, completely stripped of her tough brawler vocabulary.

She looked frantically toward me, her lower lip quivering with an endearing mix of fierce embarrassment and raw, vulnerable devotion.

"Yuuki! Tell her that's not what happened!" she pleaded in a hoarse, desperate rasp. "Tell this smug fox she's making things up!"

I looked at Makoto's burning face, then at Maho's tranquil, teasing smile, and reached across the table to lay my hand over Makoto’s calloused knuckles.

The effect was instantaneous.

Makoto’s frantic sputtering ceased. Her fingers, rough and scarred from years of broadsword drills, curled tentatively around mine, gripping my hand with tight, possessive warmth. Her violet wolf ears relaxed, swiveling forward to catch the sound of my breathing, while her tail slowed its frantic thumping, settling into a gentle, rhythmic wag against the floor.

"See?" she muttered softly, turning her face away toward the courtyard garden with a gruff, bashful scowl. "He knows who kept him on his feet."

Maho watched our joined hands with quiet, affectionate warmth. She lifted the second bowl of rice, placed a generous portion of grilled trout over the steaming grains, and slid the bowl directly in front of Makoto.

"Eat, my valiant guardian," Maho said, her voice dropping into a tender, genuine cadence that held no mockery, only the deep, protective love of a guildmaster for her family. "You carried the heaviest burden on the mats yesterday, and your strength protects us all. A princess must always ensure her champions are well fed."

Makoto blinked at the bowl, her golden eyes softening. She picked up her chopsticks, broke them apart with a clean snap, and bowed her head with a gruff, murmured thanks.

"Itadakimasu," she muttered, before attacking the food with her usual ravenous gusto, shoveling huge bites of savory fish and rice into her mouth with a contented hum that resonated deep in her throat.

Maho turned back to me, her cool, porcelain fingers resting over my free hand. Her plush golden tail tightened its coil around my thighs, the luxurious fur soft and warm against my skin.

"The storybook of our guild turns to a wondrous new chapter today, Ouji-sama," she whispered softly, her emerald eyes locking onto mine with deep, unwavering loyalty. "With our wild sentinel guarding the gate and our noble Prince seated upon the royal throne, the peace of Landosol remains unbroken."

We ate together in the quiet golden light of the morning, sharing the simple, grounded feast as the guildhall slowly stirred to life around us.

From the distant western wing, the faint sound of Kaori singing a cheerful island tune drifted along the breezeway, accompanied by the muffled click of Kasumi’s spectacles case as the detective began her morning rounds. Down in the pond, the orange koi fish broke the surface with soft, gentle ripples that caught the sunlight like scattered coins.

Makoto finished her bowl, set it down with a satisfied clatter, and wiped her mouth with the back of her wrist. She stretched her arms overhead, her muscles flexing smoothly beneath her skin, before leaning her shoulder lightly against mine with a quiet, peaceful sigh.

I finished my tea, set the clay cup upon the tray, and looked out across the sunlit courtyard, ready for the day ahead.
