# The Wolf's Mark

The first pale grey sliver of dawn pierced the cedar transoms above the dojo, turning the drifting dust motes into flecks of suspended silver.

I woke to the heavy, warm weight of Makoto’s arm draped across my chest.

Her face was pressed into the hollow of my throat, her breath coming in slow, steady sighs that brushed warm against my collarbone. In sleep, the fierce, sharp lines of her features had softened into quiet tranquility. Her long violet eyelashes rested against flushed cheeks, and her triangular wolf ears were turned sideways, twitching occasionally as they tracked the distant morning cries of crows rising above Landosol’s western quarter.

Her purple hair was scattered across my shoulders like wild silk, tangled and damp with dried perspiration. Beneath my hands, her bare back felt smooth and hot, the defined ridges of her shoulder blades rising and falling with deep, untroubled rhythm.

"Morning," she croaked, her voice rough and gravelly. She buried her face back into my neck for three stubborn seconds, groaning into my skin, before reluctantly levering herself up on one elbow.

I reached up, caught her wrist, and turned her hand so my lips pressed against the calloused meat of her palm.

Makoto shivered, her golden eyes softening into an expression so tender it looked alien on the face of Caon’s fiercest brawler. Her thumb stroked the curve of my jaw, feeling the overnight stubble.

"Idiot," she whispered, her lips parting in a small, crooked smile that showed the sharp tip of her canine fang. "You're not supposed to kiss the hand that clawed you."

She wiped herself down with quick, efficient swipes, scrubbed her face with the cold cloth, and stood up with renewed energy. She scooped her torn shorts and shredded tank top off the floor, balling them under her arm, and pulled a fresh pair of loose linen trousers and an oversized dark blue tunic from her personal locker beside the weapons rack. She tied the sash around her waist, gathered her wild violet hair into a high, practical ponytail with a leather cord, and turned toward the galley kitchen.

"You stay there and rest your legs," she commanded over her shoulder, rolling her sleeves up to her elbows. "I'm making breakfast. And don't give me that look—I know how to feed a warrior after a hard night."

I pulled on a clean pair of cotton training trousers and followed her into the kitchen.

The morning light was flooding through the barred window above the sink, illuminating the heavy slate counters and copper kettles. Makoto was already a whirlwind of domestic efficiency. She stoked the embers in the stone hearth with iron tongs, threw on dry kindling until bright orange flames leaped under the heavy iron cauldron, and set a pot of water to boil.

With quick, rhythmic thuds of a broad cleaver, she sliced a thick block of aged white bean curd into neat cubes on the butcher block, chopped wild spring scallions into fragrant emerald rounds, and laid four whole salted salmon fillets onto the cast-iron griddle. The aroma of sizzling fish skin, toasted sesame oil, and rich, simmering fermented miso immediately filled the kitchen, completely washing away the sharp tang of sex and sweat with the hearty, comforting scent of home.

"Hand me that wooden bowl of dried kelp flakes from the top shelf, will you?" she asked without looking up, skillfully flipping the sizzling salmon with a pair of metal chopsticks.

She leaned into the contact for a brief second, resting the side of her head against my shoulder with an easy, unforced familiarity that spoke of something far deeper than mere guild camaraderie.

Before she could speak, the sound of loud, energetic yawning echoed down the wooden breezeway from the sleeping quarters.

"Wah-ha-a-ah! That was the best sleep ever!"

Kaori strolled into the dining hall, wearing loose cotton shorts and an unbuttoned island shirt over a faded tank. Her long brown mullet bounced across her bare shoulders, and the twin cowlick strands on top of her head stood at full attention. She stretched her arms toward the ceiling, arching her back until her knuckles popped, sniffing the air with an ecstatic grin.

"Ooh! Is that grilled salmon I smell? Makoto, you angel! My stomach was growling so loud it woke the sparrows!"

Kaori bounded into the kitchen archway, but the moment her ruby eyes landed on us, she pulled up short.

Her sharp dog nose twitched twice in the doorway, catching the lingering ambient musk beneath the smell of frying fish. Her gaze flicked from Makoto’s flushed, glowing skin down to the slightly tender, wide-legged stance the wolf-girl was maintaining at the hearth, then darted to my bare shoulders, zeroing in directly on the fresh claw scratches and the unmistakable purple bite mark on my collarbone.

A wide, mischievous, toothy grin spread slowly across Kaori’s sun-kissed face.

"Heh... heheh. Nankurunaisa, huh?" Kaori chuckled, crossing her arms behind her head with an exaggerated nod of approval. "Looks like someone had a *really* intense late-night sparring session. Did the big bad wolf finally catch her prey, Yuuki?"

"Shut your mouth and set the bowls on the table, Kaori!" Makoto snapped, her face turning as red as a ripe tomato. She brandished her metal cooking chopsticks like twin daggers, her wolf ears twitching furiously. "If you say one more word, you're getting cold rice and water for breakfast!"

"Hey, I'm not complaining!" Kaori laughed cheerfully, grabbing a stack of ceramic bowls from the drying rack. "I'm just saying, your punches usually leave bruises, not teeth marks! Though honestly, I'm kinda jealous. Next time, you gotta save some of that energy for the rest of the pack, Makoto!"

"Out! Get out of my kitchen before I throw this ladle at your head!"

"Good morning to the household."

She was already fully dressed in her crisp, pleated detective's uniform, her purple hair brushed to a glossy sheen, her brass-rimmed spectacles resting squarely on her small nose. She carried a leather-bound notebook under her arm, but as she stepped to the edge of the dining table, her sharp purple-pink eyes swept the room with clinical precision.

"Subject A displays an elevated cutaneous temperature, acute vasodilation localized to the facial capillaries, and an atypical reluctance to maintain eye contact. Conclusion: the nocturnal peace of this guildhall was breached in an extraordinarily thorough manner."

"I am merely recording the factual parameters of our domestic environment, Makoto-san," Kasumi replied with an innocent blink, though the subtle, knowing smirk playing at the corners of her lips was unmistakable. She looked toward me, her eyes softening by a fraction of a degree. "However, assuming my Assistant has retained sufficient physical vitality to hold a stylus, we still have patrol expense reports to audit this afternoon."

"Oh, my. What a delightfully lively morning in our humble kingdom."

The wooden screen at the bottom of the stairs slid aside, and Maho stepped into the hall. She was dressed in her pristine morning robes of pale lilac and cream, holding a small wooden teapot in both hands. Her massive, golden-brown fox tail swayed with tranquil, hypnotic grace behind her, brushing the tatami in a rhythmic sweep.

Her emerald eyes took in the kitchen chaos—Kaori laughing with a bowl in each hand, Kasumi smirking behind her notebook, Makoto fuming red at the stove, and me standing beside the hearth bearing the undeniable marks of a wild night.

Maho’s smile widened, radiant and serene.

"It seems our gallant Prince has defended the realm with extraordinary valor throughout the night," Maho said, her Kyoto accent singing like a courtly harp. She set her teapot on the table and tilted her head toward Makoto, her fox ears swiveling playfully. "And our fierce wolf looks so radiant and well-nourished this morning. A feast prepared with such tender devotion must surely be celebrated."

"I am going to strangle every single one of you," Makoto muttered under her breath, though she was biting her lower lip to keep from smiling.

She ladled the steaming, fragrant soup into four large ceramic bowls, garnishing each with a generous mountain of fresh scallions and sweet tofu, and scooped mounds of steaming white rice into the vessels Kaori had laid out.

"Food's ready," Makoto grunted, carrying two heavy wooden platters bearing the sizzling salmon fillets to the low table. "Sit down and eat before it gets cold. And if anyone makes another comment about my ears, you're doing five hundred sword swings in the midday sun."

The guild gathered around the low cedar table, kneeling on the woven straw cushions. The morning sun poured through the open veranda screens, warming the polished floorboards and casting long golden rectangles across the dojo.

Kaori clapped her hands together enthusiastically. "Itadakimasu!" She immediately tore into a piece of crispy salmon skin, humming with pure, unadulterated joy. "Mmm! Makoto's cooking really is the best in Landosol!"

Kasumi took a quiet, delicate sip of her miso broth, nodding in polite agreement before casting another subtle, approving glance in my direction. Beside her, Maho poured tea for the table, her plush tail resting comfortably against the edge of the mat, radiating peaceful contentment.

Makoto sat down heavily on the cushion next to mine, crossing her legs.

Under the wooden edge of the table, hidden from the view of the others, her rough, calloused hand reached out and found mine. Her fingers slid between my knuckles, gripping tightly, anchoring herself to my palm with quiet, unshakeable certainty.

"Eat up, Yuuki," she murmured softly, her thumb brushing the back of my hand. "We've got training again this afternoon. And I'm not going easy on you."
