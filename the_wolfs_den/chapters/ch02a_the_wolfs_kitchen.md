# The Wolf's Kitchen

The rhythmic scrape of Kaori’s straw sandals faded down the covered breezeway, accompanied by the wet slosh of a wooden cedar bucket.

"Don't take all the hot water, Kaori!" Kasumi’s sharp voice echoed from the corridor, followed by the soft clack of her oval spectacles against the ceramic bath tray she carried under her arm. "And remember to scrub down at the drainage basin before entering the tub. Immersing oneself with garden mud on one's knees is sanitary anarchy!"

"Yeah, yeah, Miss Detective! First one in gets the prime spot by the cold spring pipe!"

Their footsteps drifted past the kitchen partition toward the steam-shrouded west wing, until the heavy cedar sliding door of the bathhouse rolled shut with a deep, hollow *thud*. A moment later, the gentle rustle of silk swept across the tatami as Maho gathered her teacups onto the lacquered tray. She paused by the doorway, her emerald eyes meeting mine in the twilight. Her plush fox tail swept the doorframe in a slow, knowing curl.

"Do not strain your shoulders overmuch tonight, Ouji-sama," she murmured, her voice smooth and playful as a summer stream. "The quiet hours belong to those who know how to claim them."

She turned and glided toward the central staircase, the faint aroma of dried chamomile and sandalwood drifting in her wake until her footsteps dissolved into the quiet upper floor.

The vast training hall fell abruptly silent.

Outside, the cicadas in the bamboo grove had finally ceased their chorus, replaced by the cool, intermittent chirp of bell-crickets hidden beneath the foundation stones. A light breeze stirred the paper transoms, carrying the scent of damp garden moss, but it did little to dispel the dense heat trapped within the cedar walls.

Makoto let out a long, heavy breath that fluttered the stray violet bangs plastered to her forehead. She bent down, scooped up three fallen practice bokken from the mats, and marched toward the weapon rack along the northern wall, racking them beside her heavy two-handed iron greatsword and folded blue vanguard capelet. Her movements were deliberate, but the tight set of her shoulders and the rapid twitch of her triangular wolf ears revealed the nervous energy still thrumming beneath her skin.

"Well," she muttered, sliding the wooden blades into their notched slots with a sharp *clack-clack-clack*. "That gets the noisy bunch out of the way. Now I can actually get this place secured without Kaori tripping over the door latches."

I stood up from the rush mats and stepped toward her, taking the heavy iron kettlebells from the corner to rack them against the stone baseboard. As I walked past, the heat radiating from her body seemed to ripple the air between us. Her cotton tank top was soaked completely through at the center of her back, the translucent fabric sticking to the defined groove of her spine.

"Hey, don't strain your back with those thirty-pounders," she said quickly, reaching out to catch the iron handle just above my fingers.

Her skin was hot and slick with perspiration. When our fingers met on the rough cast iron, she didn't pull away immediately. Her hand was calloused at the palm from years of gripping broadswords, her fingers long and strong. Her golden eyes flicked up from our joined hands to my face, her pupils widening as she held my gaze.

I took the weight of the second kettlebell with my free hand and set both down in their wooden cradles without breaking our locked eyes.

"You're always doing that," she grumbled, her voice dropping into a low, husky register that vibrated in her chest. She pulled her hand back and wiped her damp palm against the coarse linen of her training shorts. "Acting all quiet and steady while the rest of us make fools of ourselves. It's annoying."

She turned abruptly toward the rear of the hall, marching into the adjoining galley kitchen.

"I've got to check the pantry stores anyway," she called out over her shoulder, her voice carrying a forced bravado. "The old man at the tavern gave me a side of salted boar ribs and half a basket of wild scallions. If we don't hang them in the cold cellar tonight, the summer heat will turn them sour by morning."

I followed her through the open wooden archway into the kitchen.

The room was cool and dim, illuminated only by the faint amber lantern light spilling from the dojo and the silver moonlight slanting through a narrow barred window above the stone sink. The air here was heavy with the rich, grounded scent of seasoned cast iron, dried kelp, smoked wild boar, and pungent cloves of mountain garlic hanging from the overhead rafters. Along the southern wall, rows of earthen jars containing fermenting miso, pickled plums, and coarse sea salt stood on cedar planks, exuding a sharp, vinegary tang that cut through the humid summer heat.

Makoto reached up toward a wicker basket suspended from a copper hook near the ceiling. As she stretched, the hem of her cropped tank top lifted high above her hips, exposing the pale, tight curve of her waist, the dip of her navel, and the firm, athletic swell of her lower abdomen glistening with a thin sheen of sweat. Her shoulder blades shifted like supple steel plates beneath her sun-browned skin, every muscle defined by years of merciless conditioning.

Her fingers caught the rim of the basket, but the hemp rope snagged on a rusted iron pulley set into the ceiling beam.

"Tch. Damn thing always binds up when the humidity spikes," she hissed, straining upward on her toes. The muscles in her calves flexed into tight, corded knots, her bare soles gripping the cool slate tiles.

I stepped up directly behind her, reached overhead, and placed my hand over hers on the rough hemp rope.

Makoto went utterly rigid.

My chest pressed flush against the damp curve of her back. Through the thin cotton of her soaked tank top, her body heat burned straight into my linen dogi, searing and immediate. I could feel every expansion of her ribs, the frantic hammer of her pulse against her spine, and the involuntary shiver that rippled through her entire frame. Her tail, usually held with confident swagger, tucked sharply against her thigh before trembling, its coarse tip brushing against the side of my knee.

Her wolf ears flattened back into her violet hair until the soft fur brushed against the hollow of my throat. The scent of her rolled over me in the close kitchen alcove—the sharp, wild musk of a predator after a hunt, mixed with the clean, sea-salt tang of her sweat and the faint, sweet warmth of the barley tea she had gulped down minutes earlier. It was a potent, intoxicating aroma that filled the cramped room, stripping away the lingering quiet of the evening.

"Yuuki..." she whispered, her voice barely a rough scrape in the darkness.

She didn't pull away. She didn't drop her arms. Instead, her head tilted slightly to the side, exposing the pale, glistening slope of her neck and the dark, damp tendrils of hair clinging to her collarbone. Her breath was coming fast, each exhalation rattling in her throat with a low, primal cadence that sounded almost like a stifled growl.

I eased the tension on the hemp rope, freeing the snagged pulley with a single firm pull, and lowered the heavy wicker basket down to the wooden countertop. But even as the rope slackened and the basket settled onto the slate with a dull thud, neither of us stepped back.

My hands rested on the counter on either side of her hips, boxing her in against the heavy timber prep block. Her breath came in short, jagged gasps, her chest rising and falling so rapidly that her back pressed hard against my chest with every intake of air.

"You're... too close," she murmured. Her hands gripped the edge of the countertop until her knuckles whitened. Her canine fangs caught her lower lip, pressing into the flesh until it flushed a deep, swollen crimson. "You know you are. Ever since we started sparring today... every time you stepped into my guard, you were looking at me like that."

I leaned in closer. My chin brushed the warm crest of her shoulder, feeling the damp heat radiating from her skin.

"Like you're waiting for me to slip up," she panted, her voice trembling with an edge that was half-growl, half-plea. Her triangular ears twitched against my cheek, exquisitely sensitive to the heat of my breath. "Like you want to see what happens when the wolf loses her temper."

She twisted her hips suddenly, spinning within the circle of my arms until her back was pressed against the wooden counter and her golden eyes locked fiercely onto mine. Her amber gaze burned in the moonlight, wild and challenging, yet her chest was heaving so violently that her breasts strained against the thin, wet fabric of her top, her hardened nipples clearly visible through the damp cotton.

"Well? You've got me cornered, Princess Knight," she growled softly, her teeth bared in a fierce, trembling smirk, though her fingers reached out and hooked into the lapels of my training gi, pulling me an inch closer until our foreheads nearly touched. "What are you going to do about it?"

The old wooden floorboards beneath our bare feet were cool, but between our bodies, the air was suffocatingly hot. From the corridors beyond, the quiet guildhall stirred with faint signs of life—the sharp click of a brass latch by the archive pantry, the whisper of silk along the staircase landing, and the distant, wet slap of bare feet skipping across the bathhouse breezeway.

Makoto’s fingers tightened in my collar, her knuckles digging into my chest as her amber eyes searched mine in the dark.
