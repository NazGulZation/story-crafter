# Island Girl

The cool night air of the sleeping corridor washed over our damp skin, a welcome relief from the suffocating steam of the bathhouse.

I carried Kaori in my arms, her head resting limp in the crook of my shoulder, her wet brunette mullet dripping onto the thick cotton bath sheet I had wrapped around her body. Her arms dangled over my chest like wet rope, completely lifeless, her fingers curling loosely into the fabric of my wrap. Every few seconds, a soft, bubbly snore fluttered from her parted lips, accompanied by a tiny string of drool that moistened my bare shoulder.

Even in dead sleep, a silly, blissful grin remained plastered across her flushed face. Her short dog tail poked out from beneath the towel, twitching in rhythmic, happy spasms against my forearm.

I slid open the shoji screen to her quarters at the end of the east wing.

Her room was a reflection of her wild, cheerful spirit. Woven palm mats covered the cedar floor, a colorful wooden sanshin hung from a peg beside the window, and dried strings of island chili peppers and sea shells dangled from the timber lintel, clicking softly in the summer breeze. In the center of the room, a thick cotton futon lay freshly aired, smelling of sweet straw and sunshine.

I knelt down and lowered her gently onto the futon.

The moment her back touched the soft cotton mattress, Kaori let out a long, luxurious groan that sounded like a contented puppy settling into a sunbeam. She rolled onto her side, hugging the oversized buckwheat pillow to her chest, her bare knees curling upward. The bath sheet slipped aside, revealing her smooth, sun-browned thighs and the sticky white streaks of my release drying like varnish across her flat stomach.

Her eyes cracked open by a mere slit, the ruby irises hazy and unfocused in the moonlight.

"Yuuki..." she slurred, her tongue still sounding too thick for her mouth. Her hand reached out blindly, her fingers clutching the sleeve of my dogi with surprising tenacity. "Don't... don't run away. Stay right here in the nest. My legs... my legs aren't attached to my hips anymore..."

I reached down, smoothed the damp bangs from her forehead, and pulled the lightweight summer quilt over her trembling shoulders.

"Good night, Kaori," I murmured.

She let out a soft, contented sigh, nuzzled her nose deep into the pillow, and was snoring soundly within three heartbeats.

I lay down beside her on the outer edge of the futon. The night was quiet, cool, and peaceful, filled only with the rhythmic rustle of the bamboo outside and the steady, warm hum of Kaori’s breathing beside me.

When morning light broke over the eastern ridge, it flooded the room with brilliant, golden sunshine.

A chorus of cicadas had already begun their rhythmic buzz in the garden, and the rich, savory aroma of toasted sesame oil and simmering white radish drifted down the hallway from the galley kitchen, indicating that Makoto had already commenced her morning culinary duties.

Beside me, the futon rustled violently.

Kaori threw both arms out in an enormous, dramatic morning yawn that sounded like a waking bear.

"WA-A-AH! Sun's up! Time to hit the—"

She threw the quilt back, planted both feet firmly onto the woven palm mats, and pushed herself upward with her usual boundless energy.

The instant her weight settled onto her feet, her knees turned entirely to jelly.

"Whoa—whoa—WHOA!"

Her legs buckled outward with a comical wobble. She swayed like a palm tree in a typhoon, flailed her arms in frantic circles trying to find balance, and promptly collapsed straight back onto the mattress with a loud, undignified *WHUMP*.

She sat there on the futon, staring down at her own thighs in complete, bewildered astonishment. She poked her left kneecap with a forefinger, then her right. Her muscular legs simply quivered, completely refusing to obey any neural command.

"What in the world...?" she blinked, her twin ahoge cowlicks drooping sideways in utter confusion. Then her gaze traveled down between her spread thighs, spotting the faint dried pearlescent traces clinging to her inner knees, and her memory seemed to violently reconnect.

A brilliant, burning crimson blush exploded across her sun-kissed cheeks, turning her whole face the color of a ripe island chili. Her canine ears stood straight up, trembling with shock, before flattening in mortified, ecstatic recollection.

"Holy sea turtles," she whispered, her hands flying to her cheeks, her jaw dropping open. "The bathhouse... the wall... the... oh, wow."

She turned her head, seeing me sitting propped on one elbow watching her from the pillow.

Instead of shrinking in shame, a massive, uninhibited, toothy grin spread across her face, lighting up her scarlet eyes with dazzling warmth. She burst out laughing—a loud, ringing, joyful sound that echoed through the entire east wing.

"Hahaha! Yuuki, you absolute monster! You completely melted my legs! I literally cannot stand up!" She reached out, grabbing my wrist and tugging with cheerful urgency. "Come here, you've gotta carry me! If I try to crawl to the kitchen, Makoto's gonna laugh me right out of Landosol!"

I smiled, swung my legs off the futon, and stood up. I pulled a loose orange tunic and her favorite red shorts over her limbs, though she giggled and squirmed the entire time like an unruly child, her legs dangling uselessly. When she was clothed, I swept her into my arms, hoisting her up in a bridal carry.

Kaori wrapped both arms securely around my neck, resting her chin on my shoulder with comfortable, unvarnished affection.

"Hehe. My personal Princess Knight carriage," she chirped, her short tail wagging against my ribs in rapid, happy thumps. "Nankurunaisa, right? Who needs working legs when you've got the strongest guy in the guild to carry you around?"

I carried her down the cedar corridor toward the central dining hall.

The sliding screen to the dining room was already wide open to the morning courtyard. Makoto was standing at the low table, setting down a heavy cast-iron skillet filled with sizzling pork strips and wild mountain greens. She was wearing her usual training tank and shorts, her violet hair tied in a neat ponytail, looking remarkably refreshed after her own nocturnal rest.

As I stepped through the threshold carrying Kaori, Makoto looked up.

Her golden amber eyes tracked our entrance, taking in Kaori clinging happily to my neck with her legs dangling limp, the suspicious redness of Kaori’s glowing cheeks, and the unmistakable, lingering aroma of pine soap and female musk hovering around us.

The wooden serving spoon in Makoto’s hand slipped, clattering loudly against the iron skillet.

"What... what happened to her?!" Makoto demanded, her voice cracking as her ears shot upright in pure alarm. "Did we get attacked by a Shadow in the night?! Is she injured?!"

"I'm not injured, Captain!" Kaori laughed cheerfully from my arms, waving a hand with sunny nonchalance. "Yuuki just gave me the most intense sparring session of my entire life in the bathhouse! My legs are out of commission for the next twenty-four hours!"

Makoto froze.

Her gaze darted from Kaori’s beaming face to mine, then down to Kaori’s floppy, useless legs. The realization hit the wolf-girl like a sledgehammer. Her face turned an incandescent, furious scarlet, her ears pinning flat against her violet hair as she gripped the handle of her skillet with white-knuckle intensity.

"You... you two... in the bath?!" Makoto sputtered, her voice rising into an indignant shriek. "Kaori, you shameless idiot! You let him... you let him scramble your brains in the communal tub?!"

"Hey, it was amazing!" Kaori shot back proudly, snuggling closer into the crook of my neck. "I don't think I've ever had my head feel that completely empty! You should try it sometime, Makoto, it really clears out the stress!"

Makoto choked on air, her mouth opening and closing like a landed trout as her blush deepened until it looked ready to burst through her skin.

"I am... I am going to throw this hot skillet at both of your heads," she growled under her breath, turning her back to the table with violent haste and aggressively scrubbing a clean ceramic bowl with a dry rag.

"A truly extraordinary clinical manifestation."

Kasumi’s cool, rhythmic voice drifted from the veranda doorway. The petite detective stood framed against the morning bamboo, holding her leather-bound case file and a freshly trimmed charcoal stylus. Her purple hair was immaculately combed, her oval spectacles polished to a pristine shine.

She walked slowly toward the table, her sharp purple-pink eyes assessing Kaori’s limp posture with devastating, surgical focus.

"According to my preliminary observation," Kasumi stated calmly, tapping her notebook with the stylus, "Subject Kaori presents acute flaccid paralysis localized strictly to the lower extremities, accompanied by pronounced facial euphoria, pupil dilation, and an inability to maintain cognitive inhibition. Furthermore, the duration of the nocturnal disturbance in the western wing exceeded fifty-two minutes. The correlation between my Assistant's physical conditioning and Subject Kaori's current neurological deficit is ninety-eight point six percent."

She pushed her glasses up the bridge of her small nose, a tiny, exquisitely subtle smirk curving her lips as she looked directly at me.

"A remarkably thorough investigation, Assistant. Though I must remind you that rendering fifty percent of our frontline combat capability physically immobile prior to afternoon patrol constitutes a severe logistical hazard."

"Oh, Kasumi, don't be so gloomy!" Kaori grinned, completely unfazed as I carefully lowered her onto a plush straw cushion beside the low table. She leaned back against my knees with a happy sigh, wiggling her bare toes. "Nankurunaisa! If a monster attacks Landosol today, Yuuki can just carry me on his back while I punch it with both hands! It'll be a new combo technique!"

Kasumi let out a long, theatrical sigh, adjusting her notebook under her arm, though her pointed Doberman ears twitched with amused fondness.

"Your tactical reasoning continues to defy all known principles of military science, Kaori-san."

"Now, now, my lovely companions. Such joyful harmony to greet the morning sun."

The shoji screen from the central stairs parted, and Maho stepped into the dining hall with graceful, gliding strides. She carried a steaming clay pot of roasted barley tea and a broad woven basket filled with sweet steamed island potatoes. Her pale green yukata swayed elegantly around her ankles, her massive, plush golden fox tail sweeping the floorboards in slow, contented arcs.

She set the basket in the center of the table, her emerald eyes sparkling with serene, courtly delight as she looked down at Kaori leaning contentedly against my legs.

"Our gallant Prince has truly blessed our humble kingdom with boundless vitality," Maho sang in her refined Kyoto lilt, kneeling gracefully onto a cushion opposite us. "To see our bright island swallow so delightfully tamed and nurtured... it brings such warmth to a princess's heart. Come, Kaori-han. Eat of the sweet mountain tubers. You must restore your strength if you are to dance under the evening lanterns once again."

"Yay! Steamed potatoes!" Kaori cheered, immediately snatching a steaming tuber from the basket, breaking it open with her fingers, and blowing on the golden flesh before taking a massive, happy bite. "Mmm! Sweet and hot! Hey, Yuuki, open wide!"

She held the other half of the steaming potato directly to my lips, her scarlet eyes shining with pure, innocent adoration. Her fingers were still warm from the steam, smelling of earth and toasted tubers.

I leaned forward and took a bite from her hand. The sweet, earthy potato was steaming hot, rich with natural sugar, a perfect antidote to the lingering fatigue deep in my muscles.

Kaori giggled, her thumb gently brushing away a stray crumb from the corner of my lip.

"Look at that, he ate it right out of your hand," Makoto grunted, setting a massive bowl of steaming white rice between us and slapping down a pair of lacquered chopsticks. "You're feeding him like a pet bear, Kaori. If you keep spoiling him, he's never going to hold a practice sword properly again."

"A well-fed warrior is a loyal champion, Makoto-san," Kasumi pointed out, adjusting her glasses with a subtle, knowing smirk as she dipped a pickled plum into her tea. "The nutritional caloric expenditure of yesterday's engagement was extraordinary. If my Assistant requires supplementary carbohydrates to sustain his motor functions, it is our collective duty to supply them."

"You two are impossible," Makoto sighed, though the red flush in her cheeks had softened into an easy, fond warmth. She sat down cross-legged beside Maho, took up her own chopsticks, and dug into a generous bowl of rice with ravenous, tomboyish enthusiasm. "Fine, eat up. But don't expect me to carry you around when we do perimeter sweeps this afternoon, Kaori."

"Yuuki's already my carriage, Captain!" Kaori laughed, leaning back against my knee with a bright, sunny grin that could have melted an iceberg. "I'm set for the whole day!"

Around the table, the morning sun bathed the Caon guildhall in golden light. The garden bamboo rustled in the cool breeze, and the air was filled with the savory aroma of sizzling pork, toasted sesame oil, and fragrant green tea. Kasumi quietly reviewed her ledger, making small notes with her stylus between sips of broth, while Maho hummed a courtly song from her Maho-Maho Kingdom, her golden fox tail resting peacefully across the woven straw mats.

Under the table, Kaori’s bare foot slid across the straw mat until her toes nudged gently against my ankle. Her toes curled lightly around my calf, warm and playful.

She leaned her shoulder back against my knee, looking up at me with an unhurried, peaceful smile that belonged entirely to the sun-drenched reefs of her island home. The lingering exhaustion in her eyes had softened into pure, unshakeable contentment.

"Thanks, Yuuki," she whispered softly, her voice warm and sincere beneath the cheerful morning chatter of our guildmates. "For taking care of me. For everything. As long as you're with me... nankurunaisa. Everything's gonna be just fine."

I reached down, resting my hand over her warm, sun-kissed shoulder, and smiled as the morning breeze carried the sweet scent of tea and pine through the open doors.
