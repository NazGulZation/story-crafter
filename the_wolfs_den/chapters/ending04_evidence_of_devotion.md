# Evidence of Devotion

I dipped a clean linen cloth into the lukewarm spring water and carefully wiped the cloudy residue from the brass-rimmed frames of her spectacles.

Beside me on the wooden changing bench, Kasumi sat swaddled in a thick, dry cotton robe of deep plum, her damp purple hair falling in smooth, glossy waves across her shoulders. Her knees were drawn up to her chest, her chin resting on her forearms, her pointed Doberman ears swiveling in rhythm with the soft scrape of my cloth against the glass.

The fierce, incandescent blush on her cheeks had settled into a soft, rose-tinted glow that made her pale skin look radiant in the dim lantern light.

I held the polished spectacles up to the flame, verifying that both oval lenses were clear of any pearlescent smudges, and gently slid the temples over her ears.

Kasumi blinked twice, her purple-pink eyes coming back into sharp focus behind the glass. She looked up at my face, her eyelashes fluttering as she observed the dark bruises her small hands had left on my collarbone and the faint scratches along my forearms.

"An acceptable restoration," she murmured softly, her voice still husky from screaming into the cedar rafters. Her fingers reached out, touching the brass bridge of her spectacles with habitual precision, though the tip of her forefinger trembled like an autumn leaf. "Though the chemical composition of the adhesive required considerable hydraulic solvent to neutralize completely."

I smiled, stepped forward, and gathered her into my arms.

Kasumi let out a tiny, contented squeak as I lifted her off the bench, her small arms looping around my neck with instinctive trust. Her body felt light as a sparrow against my chest, wonderfully soft and warm through the cotton robe, her slender tail curling securely around my wrist.

I carried her down the quiet hallway to her private quarters adjoining the guild archives.

Her room was a quiet sanctuary of intellectual order. Towering pine bookshelves held centuries of Landosol municipal registers, handwritten criminal codices, and botanical compendiums. On her low writing desk by the rear veranda, a brass reading lantern sat beside neat stacks of vellum parchment, horsehair brushes, and a heavy stone inkwell. Rows of neatly labeled pigeonholes held case files on recent Shadow incursions, classified by district, threat level, and magical anomalies.

I set her down gently onto the wide straw cushion before the desk.

Kasumi immediately reached for her leather-bound casebook and dipped a slender stylus into the dark ink, determined to assert her investigative routine.

"A thorough investigator," she murmured, adjusting the parchment with a trembling thumb, "must log all critical developments before memory degradation occurs. The physical sensations... the neurological response times... they must be codified into the permanent record."

She brought the metal tip to the creamy paper. But the moment the stylus touched the surface, her hand seized with a violent tremor.

The ink line skittered across the margin in a jagged, erratic zigzag, resembling the seismograph of a violent earthquake rather than her customary, immaculate calligraphy.

She stared down at the ruined page in horrified silence. She tried a second stroke, her teeth biting hard into her bottom lip, but her fingers simply lacked the neurological control to draw a straight line. The metal nib scratched the dry vellum, leaving an illegible blot of black ink that slowly seeped into the fibers.

"My fine motor coordination," she whispered, her voice cracking in pure, mortified disbelief. Her Doberman ears drooped sideways, their tips twitching with helpless frustration. "It is completely non-functional. The kinetic tremors in my radial nerve are... catastrophic. I cannot even author my own signature."

I knelt beside her, reached out, and gently took the stylus from her shaking fingers, laying it flat on the wooden desk. Then I took both of her small, cold hands into my warm palms, rubbing her knuckles with slow, circular strokes.

Kasumi looked at our joined hands, her Doberman ears flattening in defeat. A soft, self-deprecating chuckle escaped her throat, followed by a long, shuddering sigh that released the last lingering tension from her shoulders.

"Ever since I was a child," she said softly, her purple-pink eyes looking up to meet mine through the clean glass of her spectacles, "people found my deductions exhausting. My tutors called me precocious, my peers called me cold, and the guild administrators treated me like an eccentric oddity who preferred dust and parchment to flesh and blood. I spent years believing that if I simply observed the world through strict logical deduction, nothing could ever truly hurt me. Adolescents in Landosol play, bicker, and fall in love with reckless abandon. But I hid behind my ledgers and magnifying glasses because the prospect of emotional vulnerability terrified me."

Her fingers tightened around mine, her small nails pressing lightly into my skin.

"Yet tonight, when you entered the bathhouse... when you held me on the stone and looked through my defenses... you didn't treat me like an annoying prodigy. You saw the woman beneath the detective. You saw my fear, my loneliness, and my desperate hunger to be held."

She leaned her head against my shoulder, her breath warm against my neck.

"You took every hypothesis I had built about my own detachment and burned it to ashes, Yuuki. And for the first time in my life... I am grateful that my deductions were completely, hopelessly wrong."

I raised her hand to my lips, kissing the soft skin above her wrist, and held her close as the cool pre-dawn breeze rustled the bamboo shades along the veranda.

We rested together on the straw mat, wrapped in the warmth of the plum robe, listening to the soft whisper of the garden bamboo until the silver twilight of the sky dissolved into brilliant, golden morning.

The peaceful silence of the archives was broken by the sound of thunderous footsteps jogging down the breezeway.

"Good morning, world! Time to stretch out the legs—or at least pretend to!"

Kaori’s sunny, booming voice preceded her by three strides. She slid open the archive shoji screen with an energetic snap, wearing her bright orange tank top and red brawling shorts, her mullet bouncing over her shoulders. She was carrying a wooden tray bearing a steaming pot of roasted grain tea and four ceramic cups, her short dog tail wagging happily.

When her bright scarlet eyes landed on the two of us sitting close together on the cushion, Kasumi’s head still resting on my shoulder, Kaori pulled up with a delighted, toothy grin.

"Oho! Look at the scholars in their natural habitat!" Kaori teased, walking into the room with wide, swinging steps. Her nose twitched, catching the faint, undeniable scent of mineral bath soap, dried sweat, and female musk lingering on Kasumi’s robe. "Nankurunaisa! The case is officially solved, filed, and stamped with royal approval!"

Kasumi stiffened like an alarmed deer. She sat bolt upright, desperately attempting to smooth her wild purple hair with both hands, her pointed Doberman ears burning a ferocious, glowing crimson.

"Kaori-san!" Kasumi squeaked, her voice cracking as she adjusted her spectacles with frantic haste. "Your... your unannounced intrusion into private research chambers constitutes an egregious violation of guild boundaries! We were... we were merely conducting a post-incident administrative debriefing!"

"Yeah, right! An administrative debriefing that left your face redder than an island tomato!" Kaori laughed, setting the tea tray down on the desk with a friendly clatter. She leaned down, squinting at the trembling, zigzagged ink lines on the open ledger, and burst out in an affectionate cackle. "And look at your handwriting! It looks like a chicken walked across the page while having a seizure! Did Yuuki shake your brains out, too?"

"That is... that is localized radial fatigue due to sudden barometric pressure shifts!" Kasumi argued, her voice climbing an entire octave as she slammed the notebook shut with trembling hands.

"Tell that to your ears, Kasumi! They're twitching so fast they look like butterfly wings!"

"Will you two keep the racket down?! Some of us are trying to keep the soup from burning!"

Makoto marched into the archive room, wiping her hands on a coarse white apron tied over her training clothes. She was carrying a broad wooden platter bearing four steaming bowls of thick, fragrant miso soup loaded with sweet tofu and charred spring onions. Her violet hair was pulled into a high ponytail, her wolf ears perked in sharp vigilance.

As she entered, her golden amber eyes swept over Kasumi’s disheveled robe, the rumpled futon in the corner, and the conspicuous intimacy between the petite detective and me.

Makoto stopped dead in her tracks.

Her jaw dropped by a fraction of an inch, her golden pupils narrowing as the realization dawned. She cleared her throat loudly—a rough, defensive sound—and looked anywhere except my face, a deep, flustered pink staining her sun-browned cheeks.

"Right," Makoto muttered, shifting her weight from foot to foot as she set the soup bowls down on the corner of the low table. "So... you took care of the detective too, huh?"

"Makoto-san!" Kasumi cried out, covering her face with her sleeves in complete, mortified defeat. "Please, I implore you, do not adopt Kaori-san’s vulgar terminology—"

"I'm not adopting anything!" Makoto barked defensively, though she reached over and shoved a steaming bowl of soup directly in front of Kasumi with rough, sisterly concern. "I'm just saying... you look like you haven't slept a wink, Kasumi. Eat your damn breakfast before your hands shake right off your wrists. You need the salt."

Kasumi lowered her sleeves by an inch, peeking out through her glasses at the rich, fragrant soup. A soft, embarrassed warmth touched her eyes.

"Thank you, Makoto-san," she whispered politely.

"Yeah, yeah. Just don't expect me to read your messy reports today."

"What a truly delightful assembly of scholars and warriors to brighten the morning light."

The shoji screen parted once more, and Maho stepped into the archives with graceful, tranquil poise. She wore her elegant morning robes of soft lavender and white, holding a lacquered plate filled with golden honey-glazed rice cakes. Her massive, plush golden fox tail swayed behind her in rhythmic, courtly sweeps, brushing against the bookshelves with gentle whispers of fur.

Her emerald eyes took in the entire scene—Kaori grinning over the tea, Makoto fuming with flustered pride at the soup pot, Kasumi blushing behind her sleeves, and me sitting steadily beside the desk.

A serene, radiant smile curved Maho’s lips.

"Our gallant Prince has brought such peaceful illumination to our kingdom's cleverest mind," Maho said, her Kyoto accent singing with warm, melodious grace as she set the honey cakes beside the tea. "To see our sharp detective's heart so thoroughly captured and so tenderly cherished... it is a tale worthy of the grandest royal chronicles. Come, drink the roasted tea, my dear companions. A day begun in such joyful fellowship is a blessing to the entire realm."

"Maho-san, please, this is a municipal guildhall, not a romantic chronicle..." Kasumi protested feebly, though she accepted a cup of hot tea from Maho’s elegant hands, holding the warm ceramic between her trembling palms. "And furthermore, my cognitive faculties remain entirely uncompromised."

"Is that why your teacup is currently vibrating against the saucer, Kasumi-chan?" Kaori teased gently, nudging her shoulder with an affectionate grin. "Drink the soup! Makoto put extra scallions in it just for you! She acting all tough, but she was worried sick about you!"

"I was not worried!" Makoto snapped from the other side of the desk, aggressively biting into a pickled radish with a loud crunch, though her wolf ears drooped slightly in transparent fluster. "I just don't want our chief investigator fainting from hypoglycemia while we're reviewing tax vouchers! That's all!"

"Such fiery passion in defense of the realm," Maho giggled behind her wide sleeve, her fox tail swaying in gentle, soothing circles that brushed against Kasumi's knees. "Eat well, little scholar. Our brave Prince has proven his devotion with unyielding strength. It is only fitting that his brilliant lady be sustained by the bounty of our hearth."

The guild gathered around the wide writing desk, kneeling on the woven straw cushions as the golden morning sun flooded through the open veranda screens.

Kaori happily slurped her miso broth, humming an Okinawan melody between mouthfuls of tofu. Makoto sat cross-legged beside the desk, devouring her rice while offering sharp, practical critiques of yesterday's perimeter sweeps, though her wolf tail thumped the floorboards in relaxed contentment. Beside her, Maho poured tea with effortless grace, occasionally adjusting Kasumi’s collar with gentle, maternal care to conceal the faint red marks left on her pale collarbone.

Under the wooden edge of the writing desk, away from the teasing eyes of our guildmates, Kasumi’s small, pale hand reached out and found mine.

Her fingers slid between my knuckles, cold and delicate at first, before warming against my skin. She didn't let go. She held on with quiet, resolute strength, her thumb tracing the line of my knuckles with rhythmic, unspoken devotion.

She turned her head to look at me, pushing her spectacles up the bridge of her small nose with her free hand.

Her purple-pink eyes were bright, clear, and completely devoid of fear.

"Assistant," she whispered softly, her voice barely carrying above Kaori’s laughter and Makoto’s complaints. "The formal investigation into physical intimacy may be concluded. But the longitudinal observation of our partnership... has only just begun. I expect you to report for duty every single evening."

I smiled, squeezed her small fingers within my palm, and lifted my teacup into the warm, golden light of the new day.
