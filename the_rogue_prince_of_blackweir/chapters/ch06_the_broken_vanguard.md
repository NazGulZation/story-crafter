# Chapter 6: The Broken Vanguard

Forty wagons of smelted star-iron ingots make a sound unlike anything else in the world.

As my convoy rolled through the South Gate of Blackweir, the iron axles groaned under the weight of deep-earth treasure. Each ingot was stamped with my new mark—a single crimson drop upon an anvil face. Beside each wagon marched four of my men, their black-tempered breastplates gleaming dully beneath the overcast sky, their heavy arbalests resting across their arms.

I rode at the head of the column on a dark gelding bought from an imperial post-master, wearing a high-collared coat of dark charcoal wool over black chainmail, with *Bloodthorn* strapped securely to my hip. At my left rode Master Korgan; at my right, Orrin Pike.

The citizens of the Middle Flumes lined the street in stunned silence. Guild smiths poured out of their workshops, their eyes wide as they reached out to touch the cold, oily surface of the star-metal. 

I looked at the men marching beside my wagons. Ditch-diggers, runaway serfs, dock-scum who had spent their entire lives staring at the mud whenever a nobleman rode past. Now they marched four abreast in black star-iron plate, shoulders square, boots striking the wet flagstones in thunderous unison. 

When a wealthy silk-factor stepped off the curb to get a closer look, young Kellan leveled the dark steel point of his billhook with a flat, murderous stare. The merchant choked on his breath, blanching, stepping hurriedly backward into the gutter slurry to let the wagon pass. 

I watched the exchange from my saddle, and a slow, savage grin touched my lips. Three weeks ago, that merchant would have paid the watch five coppers to whip Kellan bloody for splashing his cloak. Now the silk-belly was cowering in the muck before the boy’s iron. There was no vintage in the world sweeter than watching the beaten dregs of the earth learn how to bare their fangs at their betters.

We halted in the Grand Plaza of the High Crescent, before the fountain of the Luminescent Mother.

The Duke’s Chief Quartermaster descended the marble steps of the Guildhall, accompanied by three magistrates and a detachment of halberdiers. The old man took one look at the open wagon-beds, drew a small brass hammer from his pocket, and struck an ingot.

*TONG.*

The sound was pure, deep, and resonated through the plaza like a temple bell. The steel showed not a scratch, not a dent.

"Forty wagons," the quartermaster whispered, his spectacles sliding down his nose as he looked up at me. "Lord Thorne... the capital armorers will pay sixty gold crowns a ton for this grade. The Duke has already approved your title as High Warden of the Gorge."

"I know what my iron is worth, Quartermaster," I replied smoothly, leaning down from the saddle. "And I expect the city's grain quotas delivered to my gatehouse before the new moon, as agreed."

Before he could offer his parchment, the brass horns at the outer gate blew—not the crisp fanfare of triumph, but the ragged, hollow sequence that signaled a broken company.

"Make way!" a watchman shouted from the southern colonnade. "Survivors of the Wyrm-Tooth expedition!"

The crowd parted. 

I stayed in my saddle, turning my gelding to watch the spectacle unfold.

It was eight men out of forty.

At the head of the miserable procession rode Lysander of House Morn. His purebred white courser was lame in the left hindleg, spattered with dried feces and freezing gray mire. His golden curls were matted with mud; his gilded armor was scratched, dented, and caked with dried vomit. The radiant aura of his goddess was entirely absent—drowned beneath the stench of sheer, unvarnished panic.

Behind him creaked a single flat-bed mule cart.

In the bed lay Briony. Her left arm was bound tight to her ribs with filthy, pus-stained splints, her broad face gray with wound-fever, moaning deliriously as the iron wheels jolted over the cobbles. Beside her sat Morwenna, shivering in a torn wool cowl, clutching the splintered stump of her focus staff, her charcoal-rimmed eyes now bandaged and weeping yellow drainage where the backblast of her shattered crystal had seared her face.

And walking beside the cart, trailing in the mud, was Althea.

Her fancy elven-suede tunic was shredded at the elbows and hem. Her boots had lost their leather soles; her bare toes, blue with frostbite, peeked through rags of stained linen. Her ash-brown hair hung in greasy, tangled hanks around her hollow, mud-smeared cheeks.

The proud Dawn Vanguard had returned.

Lysander pulled his limping horse up before the fountain, dismounting with a clumsy, stumbling stagger. He looked up at the quartermaster, his chest heaving, his face flushing crimson as he tried desperately to pull himself into an aristocratic posture.

"Quartermaster," Lysander stammered, his voice thin, cracked, and cracking at the edges. "We... we were beset by unnatural forces in the high pass. The *Gore-Horn Behemoth* possessed... demonic resistance to holy light. My company fought with valor, but the terrain collapsed beneath us—"

"You lost thirty-two men, Lord Lysander," the quartermaster said, his voice dropping like an iron portcullis. "You abandoned four wagons of the Duke’s siege equipment. And Factor Osric has already filed a lien against your noble family's name for three thousand crowns in unpaid mercenary bonds."

Lysander swallowed hard, his throat bobbing. "A temporary setback! When my father sends the gold from the capital—"

"Your father sent a dispatch by imperial raven this morning," the magistrate interrupted coldly, holding up a wax-sealed scroll. "He has formally disowned your debts and forbidden you from using the seal of House Morn in commercial transactions. You are bankrupt, boy."

Lysander swayed on his feet, his jaw dropping open.

"And while you were fleeing from Wyrm-Tooth," the quartermaster continued, gesturing toward my convoy, "someone else has paid the Duke's tribute in full."

Lysander turned his head.

His bloodshot, hollow eyes followed the quartermaster's arm, traveling across the forty wagons of gleaming star-iron, past the line of disciplined guards in black plate, until his gaze struck me.

He froze.

I sat tall in the saddle, looking down at him from beneath the dark brim of my riding hood. The cold wind from the chasm whipped through my cloak, parting the wool to reveal *Bloodthorn* at my hip and the silver signet of the High Warden on my breast.

"Thorne...?" Lysander choked out, his voice a pathetic squeak. "You... you're dead. You were supposed to be dead in the ditch..."

Beside the mule cart, Althea stopped.

She looked up at me. Her swollen, sunken hazel eyes went wide, reflecting the dark iron, the armed men, and the absolute sovereign authority radiating from my form. 

I saw the memory of *The Salt & Grackle* flash across her face like a branding iron. She remembered the booth. She remembered telling me I made her sick. She remembered laughing as she boasted of sweating beneath Lysander's furs while I froze on watch.

And now, here stood the clerk she had spat upon—honored by the Duke’s council, master of forty wagons of star-iron, while she stood shivering in rags with frostbitten toes, tethered to a disgraced coward whose father had discarded him like trash.

She took a trembling step toward my stirrup, her cracked lips parting. "Caspar...?"

I did not speak to her. 

I did not even give her the dignity of a sneer.

I merely looked straight through her, as if she were a pile of horse dung drying in the street, and turned my horse toward the Guildhall entrance.

"Quartermaster," I said, my voice carrying clearly across the silent plaza, "have your men unload the fourth wagon first. The Duke's personal armorer is waiting."

"Right away, Lord High Warden," the old man bowed deeply, stepping aside to let me pass.

I pulled my gelding up beside the portly figure of Factor Osric, who stood clutching his debt rolls beneath the marble arcade, staring hungrily at my wagons of star-iron.

"Factor Osric," I said, leaning down from the stirrup, tossing a heavy ingot of refined star-iron onto his ledger-table with a resounding *clack*. "I understand House Morn owes you three thousand gold crowns in defaulted bonds and retainer collateral."

The debt-broker swallowed hard, his plump fingers caressing the dark, unblemished metal. "They do, Lord High Warden. Three thousand, secured by personal chattel and indenture notes."

"Consider the debt settled in star-iron," I murmured, resting my gloved hand on the pommel of *Bloodthorn*. "Hand over Lysander's signed indenture deeds and debt-rolls to Marshal Orrin. Every single scrap of parchment."

Osric hastily pulled the lead-sealed deeds from his velvet satchel, bowing until his double chin wobbled. "The contracts are yours, my lord. Every lien, every signature."

I signaled Orrin to pocket the papers, then turned my horse toward the citadel gates.

Behind me, in the freezing mud of the plaza, I heard the sound of Althea collapsing to her knees, her ragged sobs swallowed by the cold wind of the chasm.
