# Chapter 8: The Gods Eye in the Arena

![Prince Daemon Targaryen in Black Star-Iron Armor, Wielding Bloodthorn](../assets/daemon_caspar_black_plate.jpg)

The damp yellow sand of the arena pit smelled of old salt, limestone dust, and slaughter.

I stood in the shadow of the southern portcullis, listening to the muffled roar of five thousand voices echoing off the tiered basalt walls of the Grand Amphitheater. Outside, high above the rim of the Cormorant Chasm, an autumn mist clung to the stone battlements. 

I checked the straps of my armor with practiced calm.

There was no gold on me. No decorative heraldry. Master Korgan had hammered this plate out of deep-earth star-iron, quenched in sulfur-pitch until the steel possessed a matte, non-reflective black finish that drank the morning light. My gorget was high and thick, shielding the hollow of my throat; my pauldrons hugged the contour of my shoulders, angled to shed downward blows. 

In my right hand, held loosely at my thigh, was *Bloodthorn*. 

The dark bastard blade hummed faintly against my pulse, its crimson veins sluggish with cold anticipation.

Across the arena, the brass war-horns of the cathedral blared with deafening, theatrical splendor.

"Behold!" the herald’s voice boomed across the stands through an enchantment of resonating bronze. "Lord Lysander of House Morn! Champion of the Radiant Sun, Favored Son of the Luminescent Mother!"

The northern iron gate rose.

Lysander strode onto the sand, and the crowd in the high balconies erupted in wild, adoring applause. 

He looked like an icon stepped down from an altar. His armor was freshly burnished plate inlaid with filigree of pure gold, catching the gray morning light and throwing warm halos across the sand. A pristine white plume cascaded from his crested helm; across his shoulders billowed a mantle of immaculate white silk. In his right hand, his gilded broadsword thrummed with a brilliant, pulsing golden aura—the radiant blessing of his goddess, dazzling the eyes of the fools who cheered his name.

He smiled his dazzling, porcelain smile, raising his glowing blade to salute Duke Aldous in the royal box, then turned to the crowd, basking in their worship.

"Let the light scour the dark!" Lysander’s melodic voice rang out. "Let the insolent worm who crawls in ash learn what it means to face the chosen of the heavens!"

Then my portcullis creaked open.

There were no horns for the southern archway. There was only the slow, rhythmic grind of iron winch-chains against wet granite.

I walked out onto the sand.

I did not swagger. I did not raise my blade to salute the Duke. I walked with the measured, predatory pacing of a panther stepping into a clearing—weight balanced on the balls of my feet, shoulders loose, my violet-tinged eyes locked dead upon the hollow of Lysander's throat.

The roaring in the stands began to falter.

A hush spread through the amphitheater, moving outward from the common benches like frost over water. The veterans of the border legions, the old sellswords who had fought in the marsh wars—they looked at the black-armored shadow advancing across the sand and went silent. They knew the difference between a knight performing for a crowd and a butcher walking to the block.

In the front row of the common tier, I saw the three ghosts of the Dawn Vanguard.

Briony leaned against the balustrade, her broken arm strapped tight to her ribs with dirty linen, her hollow, fevered eyes staring in paralyzed terror. She remembered Gallow-Gate Lane. She remembered Joram’s pulverized jaw and Skeg’s severed spine. She knew what Lysander refused to see: a butcher had come for his meat. Beside her sat Morwenna, shivering in a dark cowl that hid the burned, bandaged ruin of her face.

And between them sat Althea.

She was clutching the single copper coin in her fist so hard her knuckles were bloodless. Her lips were parted, her chest hitching as she watched me step onto the sand. She knew what was coming. Her soul already knew.

In the center of the ring, the Duke’s Chief Inquisitor raised a white staff.

"By the laws of the March," the Inquisitor chanted, "an Honor Trial to total submission or death! May the true sovereign prevail! Begin!"

The staff dropped.

"Die in the dirt, peasant!" Lysander roared.

He relied on the only tactic his pampered mind knew: overwhelming, blinding force. 

With a roar that shook the rafters, he invoked his divine blessing. The golden aura around his broadsword detonated into a blinding sunburst of radiant aether—a dazzling flash meant to scorch my retinas and leave me helpless in the opening second.

Under cover of the brilliant flash, he charged, sweeping his broadsword in a sweeping, decapitating arc.

I didn't blink.

I had fought beneath the roaring skies of the Gods Eye, amid torrents of fire that could melt stone towers. A burst of church light was nothing.

The instant the light peaked, I merely dipped the brow of my blackened bascinet a fraction of an inch, letting the narrow visor-slit shield my eyes from the glare. I did not leap backward. I did not raise my blade to catch the heavy blow.

I stepped *forward*.

Inside the blinding arc of his swing, I shifted my left foot three inches to the flank. The golden blade hissed through empty air, singing the hairs on my left shoulder.

Before Lysander could arrest his forward momentum, I moved.

It was not a knight’s technique; it was the brutal, economic infighting of Flea Bottom and the blood-soaked beaches of Bloodstone.

I brought the solid star-iron pommel of *Bloodthorn* up in a short, vicious rising arc.

*CRACK.*

The heavy steel ball struck Lysander’s right elbow from the outside hinge.

The sound of shattering bone and tearing sinew echoed off the amphitheater walls like a dry branch snapping in winter. The golden aura flickered and died instantly as Lysander’s right arm inverted, bending outward at a sickening ninety-degree angle.

The gilded broadsword slipped from his fingers, clattering into the sand.

A high, piercing shriek of pure, unadulterated agony ripped from Lysander’s throat. He stumbled backward, clutching his ruined, dangling arm against his belly, tears instantly flooding his eyes.

"My arm!" he shrieked, his voice jumping two octaves into a terrified wail. "My arm! You... you broke my arm!"

Five thousand people held their breath in petrified, suffocating silence. 

In the royal box, Duke Aldous gripped the stone balustrade so hard his knuckles turned white.

Lysander looked around frantically, expecting the referee to call a halt, expecting his priests to demand a pause for holy healing.

I did not pause.

I closed the distance with a single, fluid step.

Before he could back away, my iron-shod sabaton drove straight into his solar plexus. The hardened star-iron caved in his gilded breastplate with a dull *crunch*, driving every atom of air from his lungs.

Lysander folded like a wet parchment, wheezing, gasping, collapsing hard onto his knees in the damp sand.

"G-Goddess..." he gagged, his left hand fumbling desperately at his belt for an aether-vial. "Mother of Light... cleanse this—"

I kicked the vial from his shaking fingers. It shattered against the arena wall in a shower of useless blue mist.

Then, with my left gauntlet, I seized Lysander by the gilded throat of his gorget. 

With a surge of wiry strength honed over weeks at the volcanic anvil, I hauled the golden boy to his feet.

His pristine white plume fell into the muck. His golden curls were plastered to his forehead with cold sweat and vomit.

"You speak of the Mother," I whispered into his face, my voice quiet, conversational, and carrying with terrifying clarity through the dead silence of the amphitheater. "Where is she, boy?"

"M-Mercy..." Lysander blubbered, saliva and bile stringing from his lips. "I am Lysander of House Morn! You cannot—my father will pay—"

"Who are you?" I murmured, tilting my head with genuine boredom. "A child in borrowed gold. Your father disowned you yesterday."

My right gauntlet, clad in blackened plate, slammed across his jaw in a short, brutal cross.

*SMACK.*

Three white, symmetrical teeth exploded from his mouth in a spray of bright crimson. The jawbone splintered with a sound like crushing seashells.

Highborn ladies in the balconies screamed, several fainting into their seats.

I didn't let him fall. I caught him by his golden hair, yanking his head backward to expose his face to the sky.

"You called me ballast," I whispered, my violet eyes burning into his swollen, terror-struck face. "You laughed in the tavern while your whores mocked my work. You told me I was dead weight."

*SMACK.*

The backhand gauntlet hammered across the other side of his face.

The straight, aristocratic nose flattened into a bloody smear of cartilage. His right eye swelled shut, purple and black ballooning beneath the broken skin. The angelic symmetry that had bought him unearned glory, women, and titles was systematically pulverized into raw, unrecognizable meat.

"Please!" Lysander wailed, his voice a muffled, wet gargle through a mouthful of broken enamel and blood. He wet himself. A dark, steaming stain spread across the crotch of his white silk breeches, visible to every spectator in the stands. "Caspar! Please! Master Thorne! I beg you! Have mercy! I'll give you everything! The title, the gold, the women—take Althea, take them all! Just let me live!"

In the front row, Althea heard every word.

*Take Althea. Take them all.*

I saw her go completely rigid. Every drop of blood left her face. The horrific truth drove into her soul like a poisoned dagger: she had betrayed the boy who loved her for a coward who pissed himself in the dirt and offered her up like a sack of oats to save his own skin.

Beside her, Morwenna wept silently behind her cowl, and Briony buried her face in her good hand, shaking with unbearable shame.

In the center of the pit, I looked down at the weeping, soiling wreck in my grip.

A deep, familiar disgust coiled in my chest.

"You are not even fit to die on Dark Sister's steel," I murmured, so low only he could hear. "A prince of Old Valyria does not execute swine. He merely butchers them."

I released my grip.

Lysander collapsed face-first into the damp sand, sobbing, choking, his body trembling in pathetic, convulsing spasms.

I raised *Bloodthorn* with both hands, the dark point hovering an inch above his exposed neck.

"Hold!" 

Duke Aldous’s voice boomed from the balcony, trembling with barely concealed terror. "The challenger has yielded! The duel is ended!"

I didn't immediately lower the blade. 

I stood over the ruined golden boy for three long, agonizing seconds, letting the silence of the arena wash over me. Five thousand people sat in frozen, paralyzed awe, staring at the black-armored ghost who had dismantled the capital's golden prodigy in less than forty seconds without suffering a single scratch.

Slowly, deliberately, I reversed the blade.

I slid *Bloodthorn* back into its scabbard with a sharp, metallic *clack*.

I turned my back on the weeping ruin in the sand.

I didn't look at the Duke. I didn't look at the High Priest, whose golden robes seemed suddenly ridiculous and faded.

Instead, I turned my eyes toward the front row of the common benches.

My gaze locked onto Althea Reed.

She was shaking so hard she could barely remain seated. She looked down at me with wide, horrified eyes—eyes overflowing with agony, terror, and the devastating realization of what she had thrown into the gutter.

I gave her no sneer. I gave her no smile.

I looked straight through her, as if she were nothing more than a patch of dead grass beside a forgotten road, and turned away.

I raised my blackened gauntlet toward the sky.

And Blackweir bowed its head to its new sovereign.
