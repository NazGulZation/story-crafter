# Chapter 1: The Logistics of Felonious Teamwork

Rain tapped a steady, drumming rhythm against the narrow window casement of Room 204. Inside, the modest wooden study table—usually occupied by a porcelain mug of roasted mugicha, two striped notebooks, and a neatly folded pile of athletic wraps—groaned under an astonishing invasion of high-performance consumer electronics.

Nice Nature stood by the threshold of her kitchenette, holding an enamel kettle in one hand and a box of dried barley tea bags in the other. Her ears twitched back, flattened against her auburn hair, as she stared at her living quarters.

Four identical matte-black gaming notebooks sat arranged in a precise quadrant across the low table. Beneath each chassis, an industrial cooling pad whirred with a low, turbine-like thrum, glowing with electric cyan LED strips that cast watery reflections against her tatami mats. Coiled braided cables snaked across the floorboards like tarred dock ropes, disappearing into a pair of heavy-duty surge protectors that Dia had personally carried up the dormitory stairs in a velvet-lined shopping tote.

"Nature-san," Satono Diamond called out, her voice melodious and composed. She knelt upon a square floor cushion, smoothing the pleats of her pleated skirt with both palms. Her emerald eyes sparkled with academic pride as she gestured toward the humming array. "The local network bridge has achieved synchronization. The ping between our local nodes is registering at precisely four milliseconds. We are fully prepared to initiate the curriculum."

"Curriculum," Nature repeated slowly. She set the kettle onto the small gas burner with a dull clink, clicked the igniter, and walked over to the table. She knelt down, pulled her knit cardigan tighter around her shoulders, and leaned over to inspect the nearest screen. "Dia-chan... when you sent that message to the group chat about a 'weekend seminar on collaborative resource allocation and risk mitigation,' I assumed we were going to play that train-scheduling board game. Or maybe balance a mock training budget."

"This is far more comprehensive than railway logistics," Dia said smoothly, reaching into a gilded paper box from an upscale Ginza patisserie and setting a three-tiered ceramic tower of pastel macarons on the edge of the table. The sweet, buttery scent of almond flour and raspberry ganache immediately drifted up through the dry plastic heat of the laptop vents. "Modern competitive enterprise requires acute crisis coordination, split-second triage, and absolute faith in one's peers under simulated distress. After extensive research into overseas cooperative software, I procured four enterprise licenses for our practical training."

Nature squinted at the desktop monitor. In the center of the dark wallpaper sat a stark red-and-white icon shaped like a grinning clown mask. Below it, the label read: *PAYDAY 2: CRIMEWAVE EDITION (LEGACY BUNDLE)*.

"Wait a second," Nature murmured, her eyebrows knitting together as she dragged the mouse cursor over the store page thumbnail. "The user tags say... 'First-Person Shooter.' 'Stealth.' 'Violent Felonies.' 'Armed Robbery.' Dia-chan, this isn't a business simulator. This is an international bank robbery game."

"It is an applied logistics exercise, Nature-san," Dia corrected gently, tilting her head with an earnest, unshakeable smile. "The objective requires four specialists to infiltrate a guarded financial repository, circumvent surveillance networks, neutralize mechanical barriers under severe temporal constraints, and extract high-value assets to an external logistics vehicle. Is that not the very definition of tactical teamwork?"

"You're robbing a bank!"

"We are reallocating liquid municipal capital," Dia replied without missing a beat, picking up a raspberry macaron with the tips of her fingers. "Under the strategic direction of an independent coordinator named Bain."

"Yahoo! Look at this, Nature-san! Look at my guy!"

Across the table, Kitasan Black was practically bouncing on her knees, her tail swishing so vigorously it ruffled the edge of the low curtains. She was already wearing a bulky over-ear headset with glowing red earcups, her face split into a radiant grin. She spun her laptop around so Nature could see the character customization screen.

Standing in the virtual inventory menu was a burly man in an immaculate two-piece charcoal suit. Over his head sat a terrifying, lacquered crimson mask shaped like a traditional samurai oni, complete with gleaming brass tusks, flared nostrils, and painted gold eyebrows. In his hands, the character hefted a twelve-gauge pump-action shotgun with an extended magazine tube.

"I picked the heavy armor guy!" Kitasan announced, her voice booming through the small room. "The description said 'Enforcer'! It says here I can carry extra shotgun shells, knock down wooden doors with my shoulder, and yell really loud to scare people! It feels just like carrying the festival shrine through the shopping district!"

"Kita-chan, you don't carry shotguns to the summer festival," Nature said, pressing two fingers to the bridge of her nose. An involuntary sigh leaked through her teeth. "And please don't yell so loudly. Tachyon-san is sleeping in the room directly below us, and if you wake her up during an experiment, she'll pump strange green vapor through the radiator vents again."

"Understood! Stealth mode engaged!" Kitasan whispered loudly, grinning as she clicked the mouse button, causing her virtual avatar to pump the shotgun with a loud, mechanical *clack-clack*. "Wasshoi of silence!"

"U-Um... Nature-san?"

A timid, hesitant voice floated from the corner of the low table. Special Week was hunched over her notebook like a sprinter in the starting blocks, her knees pressed tightly together. Her brown ears stood straight up, trembling slightly with every hum of the cooling fan. Her fingers hovered over the keyboard as if the keycaps were made of thin, brittle ice.

"What is it, Spe-chan?" Nature asked, turning toward her with an instinctual soften in her expression.

"I... I think I did something strange to my person," Spe said, her cheeks flushing bright pink. She timidly angled her screen toward Nature. "I was looking through the mask workshop like Dia-chan showed us. And there were all these scary skull faces and terrifying demon faces that looked like bad omens from the mountains. But then I found this one!"

Nature leaned in to inspect Special Week’s monitor.

On screen, Spe’s avatar—a tall, slender heister named Houston wearing a slim grey suit—was currently sporting a colossal, bulbous clown mask with enormous painted eyelashes, a gaping red smile, bright canary-yellow star decals on both temples, and two radiant, fluorescent-pink circles dabbed onto its porcelain cheeks. It looked less like an elite international criminal and more like a children's party performer who had wandered into an active tactical breach after eating too much cotton candy.

"It has rosy cheeks," Spe explained earnestly, looking up with wide, hopeful purple eyes. "Back home in Hokkaido, my mother always told me that if you have to meet important people for business, you should always present a bright, friendly face so they know you have good intentions. Do you think Bain-san will think I look polite?"

Nature stared at the horrific, cheerful porcelain grin on the screen, then at Spe’s pure, beaming face. 

"Spe-chan," Nature said carefully, feeling a headache blossoming behind her left temple. "Bain is an anonymous voice on an encrypted radio channel who hires armed mercenaries to drill into bank vaults. I don't think he's going to grade us on our hospitality."

"Ah! Really?" Spe’s ears drooped by two inches. "Should I take the rosy cheeks off?"

"No," Nature sighed, reaching over to pat Spe’s shoulder. "Leave them on. Honestly, if a teller saw that coming through the lobby, they'd probably give you the money just to make you go away. It has... an aura."

"Hehe! Thank you, Nature-san!" Spe immediately perked up, her tail giving a cheerful thump against the floor.

"Now then," Dia said, setting her macaron down onto a porcelain saucer and adjusting her microphone boom. "I have established our private operational lobby on the Crime.net tactical interface. Please enter the contract room. The network password is *SatonoVictory77*."

Nature clicked her trackpad, navigating the menus on her own machine. Her avatar was Dallas, the default squad leader. While the others had spent ten minutes browsing masks, skins, and color palettes, Nature had left her avatar entirely untouched. Her character stood clad in an ordinary brown two-piece suit with an unpainted, cracked clown face, holding a basic black CAR-4 assault rifle.

"Nature-san," Dia observed, her brow furrowing slightly as she inspected Nature’s lobby profile. "Have you not customized your tactical visage? I unlocked seven tiers of Continental mask modifications using my offshore reserve."

"I'm fine with the stock model," Nature said, settling back onto her heels and taking a sip of the freshly poured roasted barley tea. The toasty, earthy warmth chased away the damp chill from the window. "A sensible girl doesn't waste virtual money on golden paint before she even knows how to walk down the street without bumping into fire hydrants. What role am I supposed to be doing, anyway?"

"I have designated you as our Combat Medic, Nature-san," Dia said with absolute, unblinking authority. "You are equipped with the Mastermind tactical tree and a deployable Doctor Bag. According to the international mechanics documentation I analyzed last night, novice squads suffer from an eighty-four percent probability of acute physical trauma during their initial extraction attempts. Your presence will ensure biological continuity."

"In plain Japanese, you expect us to get shot full of holes, and you want me to carry the bandages," Nature said dryly.

"I prefer to describe it as health asset portfolio management," Dia replied serenely.

"Right. Portfolio management." Nature rolled her eyes, but she quietly checked her loadout screen to ensure the white medical kit with the red cross was assigned to her deployable slot. "And what about you, Dia-chan? What are you bringing?"

Dia’s lips curved into a confident, aristocratic smile. She clicked her mouse twice.

On Dia’s screen, her avatar was Hoxton, dressed in an ultra-lightweight, bespoke two-piece suit without a single plate of ballistic vest. Over his face rested an exquisite Venetian masquerade mask wrought with filigreed gold vines and diamond dust. In his holsters rested a pair of sleek, black Italian pistols fitted with long cylindrical silencers.

"I am operating under the Ghost archetype," Dia announced, her fingers dancing lightly across her mechanical keyboard. "I possess maximum mobility, silent lock-picking credentials, and two deployable Electronic Countermeasure jammers capable of scrambling civilian cellular transmissions and disabling electronic security barriers. Furthermore, using my personal offshore account from my introductory tutorial drills, I have pre-funded every available intelligence asset on the pre-planning schematic."

Dia clicked a tab labeled *Pre-Planning*.

A detailed architectural blueprint of a two-story urban bank branch materialized on all four monitors. Across the floor plan, bright green icons marked every purchase Dia had made:
- *Insider Asset: Keycard placed on manager’s desk.*
- *Surveillance Asset: Camera feed unlocked in back alley.*
- *Logistics Asset: Dead drop body bag case in trash dumpster.*
- *Extraction Asset: Expert Driver contracted for rapid getaway.*

"You spent four hundred and fifty thousand dollars on pre-planning for a beginner map?" Nature stared at the asset ledger in disbelief.

"Preparation is the greatest antidote to misfortune, Nature-san," Dia said smoothly, straightening her posture. "My family has always believed that adequate capitalization prevents structural embarrassment. We shall execute this operation with absolute surgical elegance. We shall case the perimeter, identify the camera feed, neutralize the security personnel without alerting the municipal authorities, and harvest the depository’s cash reserves before a single civilian realizes their funds have departed."

"Yeah!" Kitasan cheered, pumping both fists into the air. "Stealth! Like festival ninjas!"

"I will try my best not to trip over the furniture!" Spe declared with solemn, wide-eyed resolve, gripping her mouse with both hands until her knuckles turned white.

Nature looked from Dia's polished smile to Kitasan's enthusiastic fist-pumping, and finally to Spe's deadly serious clown face with its bright pink cheeks. A cool draft whispered around the window sash, carrying the faint scent of rain-soaked asphalt from the track outside.

"Alright," Nature murmured, setting her tea cup safely out of elbow range and resting her fingers across the `W-A-S-D` keys. "Bain-san, whoever you are... please have mercy on us."

Dia pressed the primary execution button. 

The screens flashed dark, and the low, tense rumble of an acoustic bassline began pulsing through their headsets.
