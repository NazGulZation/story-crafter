# Chapter 3: The Broke-Dick Thermal Drill

Bass exploded through the small confines of Room 204 with the ferocity of a detonating transformer. 

The casing music vanished, swept away by the crushing, syncopated electronic synthesizers of Simon Viklund’s *Razormind*. The bassline hammered through the laptop speakers, sending micro-vibrations rattling through the porcelain saucers, the cooling pads, and the floorboards beneath their knees. Outside the rain-streaked window, an authentic gust of wind rattled the glass, but inside the screen, the entire world dissolved into screaming sirens and flashing red muzzle flare.

"Guys! The drill!" Bain’s voice bellowed through their headsets, competing with the thumping four-on-the-floor kick drum. *"Twitch dropped the thermal drill in the rear parking lot! You need that drill to burn through the vault door! Move your asses!"*

"The parking lot?!" Nature shouted over the roar of the music, leaning toward her screen with her ears pinned flat. "Why did he drop a two-hundred-pound industrial drill in the open parking lot?! Why didn't he leave it in the lobby before we walked in?!"

"Logistical optimization under hostile municipal constraints, Nature-san!" Dia shouted back, her fingers clattering across her keys in a frantic blur. On her monitor, Hoxton took cover behind the manager's mahogany filing cabinet. "Twitch is an independent extraction contractor! He avoids proximity to initial point-of-entry skirmishes to preserve the integrity of his getaway vehicle!"

"He's a coward who threw our power tools over a chain-link fence!" Nature yelled. She panned Dallas’s camera toward the lobby entrance. 

Through the shattered front glass, four black-and-white police cruisers screeched onto the sidewalks, tires smoking against the concrete curbs. Car doors flew open. Blue-shirted patrol officers poured out, ducking behind engine blocks and unholstering service pistols. Muzzle flashes flickered through the rain. 9mm rounds began snapping through the lobby, chewing divots out of the plaster pillars and shredding the burgundy curtains into floating rags.

*Snap! Crack! Ping!*

Dallas’s blue armor bar flashed once, twice, and vanished with a dull electronic tone.

"Aaaah! Bullets!" Spe shrieked, her avatar cowering behind a ruined teller counter with both hands clutching her AMCAR rifle. Plaster dust rained down upon her giant smiling clown mask with its fluorescent-pink cheeks. "Nature-san, they are shooting real bullets! The nice men in the blue uniforms are trying to hit my head!"

"Spe-chan! Listen to me!" Nature barked, adopting the sharp, unyielding tone of an elder training partner at the final furlongs. "Your avatar has full armor and an extra stamina bar! I need you to sprint out the side door, grab the drill bag next to the yellow sedan, and haul it straight to the vault! Do you hear me?!"

"F-Fueh?! Me?!" Spe gasped, her purple eyes wide with terror. "Into the rain?!"

"You're from Hokkaido!" Nature yelled. "You grew up running up snow drifts in rubber boots! A couple of patrol cops with pistols can't catch you in a straight line! Press `Shift` and run!"

Spe’s brown ears shot straight up. Something ancestral and stubborn flared behind her eyes. 

"Yes! Hokkaido stamina! Understood, Nature-san!"

Spe slammed her thumb onto the Shift key. 

On screen, Houston vaulted over the shattered teller counter, hit the carpet in a low, driving stride, and burst through the side emergency exit into the wet parking lot. Three patrol officers immediately opened fire from behind a dumpster. Pistol rounds pinged off Spe’s heavy vest, throwing up digital sparks, but Spe ignored the impacts entirely. Her avatar charged down the asphalt like an unbroken filly chasing the rail at Sapporo, scooped up the colossal yellow duffel bag marked *BEAST DRILL* in a single fluid motion, and turned on a dime.

"I have secured the heavy bag!" Spe yelled over the comms, hauling the massive weight back through the doorway as bullets chewed the brick lintel above her helmet. "Returning to the paddock!"

"Good girl! Vault corridor, right now!" Nature ordered, leaning around a concrete pillar to fire three controlled bursts from her CAR-4. 

The sharp *crack-crack-crack* of the 5.56mm carbine echoed through the bank. Two patrol officers dropped behind their cruisers. Nature ducked back behind cover as a hail of return fire blew a framed oil painting of the bank founder off the wall, smashing the glass into fine powder.

"Setting up the machinery!" Dia announced. 

Hoxton stood before the colossal circular steel vault door in the rear hallway. The door was a four-ton slab of reinforced alloy, secured with twelve hydraulic deadbolts. Dia held down the interact key for six agonizing seconds. 

With a heavy, mechanical clang, the thermal drill locked into the vault face. A massive tripod anchored itself into the floor tiles. Twin diamond-tipped rotary bits pressed against the steel, surrounded by coiled hydraulic hoses and an external diesel engine.

A glowing yellow HUD element materialized above the apparatus:

**THERMAL DRILL: 300 SECONDS**

"Three hundred seconds?!" Nature shrieked, staring at the five-minute timer in disbelief. "We have to hold this hallway for five entire minutes?!"

"The thermal core requires sustained thermodynamic application to liquefy the internal tumblers," Dia explained, her voice remarkably serene despite the heavy synthesizer bass vibrating her desk. "Provided we maintain defensive perimeters along the three cardinal entry corridors, five minutes represents an acceptable operational window."

The drill engine coughed, spat a plume of blue exhaust into the ceiling vents, and engaged.

*CHUG-CHUG-CHUG-CHUG-CLANK!*

The diamond drill bits spun up to maximum velocity, biting into the vault face with an ear-splitting, pneumatic scream: *SKRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRR!* Bright white sparks cascaded across the hallway tiles like molten waterfalls.

At that exact moment, the glass skylight in the center of the lobby shattered with a thunderous crash.

Heavy black tactical ropes tumbled through the opening. Four heavily armored SWAT operatives clad in dark blue ballistic gear, tactical helmets, and gas masks rappelled into the room, boots hitting the carpet in perfect unison. Behind them, through the shattered front windows, another six officers breached the entry, tossing flashbangs into the lobby.

*BANG!*

A blinding white flash illuminated the room. Dallas’s HUD turned pure, stark white, accompanied by an ear-piercing electronic ringing.

"I am blinded!" Nature cried out, waving her hand instinctively in front of her face in the real world. "I can't see the screen! My eyes are white!"

"HAVE NO FEAR, NATURE-SAN!" Kitasan’s voice boomed through the headset with the acoustic force of a temple bell. "THE FESTIVAL VANGUARD HAS ARRIVED! WASSHOI!"

Kitasan’s heavy Enforcer, clad in thick combined tactical armor and sporting the fiery crimson oni kabuto, strode out from behind the copy room copier like a walking iron fortress. 

She racked her Reinfeld 880 pump shotgun. The deep, heavy *clack-boom* shook the headphones.

A cloud of heavy twelve-gauge lead shot ripped through the center of the SWAT formation. The point man lifted off his boots, his ballistic vest shredded, and crashed into a potted ficus plant. Kitasan pumped the slide with ruthless mechanical rhythm, stepping forward into the smoke without taking a single step of retreat.

*BOOM!* Another SWAT officer was blasted through a cubicle divider.

*BOOM!* A blue helmet spun into the air like a flipped coin.

"Eat lead, municipal peacekeepers!" Kitasan shouted, her face glowing with radiant, joyful battle-lust. "Festival drum beat, one-two-three! Step into the ring! Who's next?!"

"Kita-chan, you're standing completely out in the open!" Nature screamed, blinking her vision back as the flashbang effect faded. "Get behind the pillar! Your armor is taking hits from three angles!"

"Pain is merely the spirit of the festival, Nature-san!" Kitasan cheered, though her shield bar was already flashing red under concentrated submachine gun fire. "Look, they're retreating! The blue men are falling back to regroup!"

"They aren't retreating," Dia observed with chilling precision, peering around the corner of the vault archway. "They are deploying ballistic shields to neutralize our kinetic advantage. Observe the corridor."

Two SWAT officers advanced down the carpeted hallway, crouching behind colossal, rectangular steel riot shields with narrow vision slits. Behind them trailed four assault carbines, laying down a disciplined, suppressive wall of fire that chipped concrete off the vault pillars.

Kitasan fired twice at the lead shield. The heavy lead pellets flattened harmlessly against the steel plate with dull, metallic thuds: *thwack-thwack*.

"Hey! That's cheating!" Kitasan yelled indignantly. "You can't bring a portable wall to a shotgun fight!"

"Spe-chan, flank them through the breakroom!" Nature commanded, ducking behind the water cooler as a burst of 5.56mm fire shattered the plastic jug, splashing virtual water across Dallas’s boots. "Aim for their exposed boots or the backs of their necks!"

"I... I will try!" Spe squeaked. 

Houston crept through the employee breakroom, stepped out behind the advancing shield officers, and opened fire with her AMCAR. Her shots were wild and erratic, peppering the ceiling, the carpet, and a framed corporate motivational poster before catching the rear officer in the shoulder. The formation collapsed under the unexpected crossfire. Kitasan seized the opening, lunging forward with a flying shoulder tackle that shoved the lead shield aside, finishing the officer with a point-blank blast to the ribs.

"Corridor cleared!" Kitasan yelled, raising her shotgun in triumph.

In Room 204, a collective breath of relief escaped their chests. Spe wiped a bead of sweat from her nose. Dia smiled and reached for another macaron. Nature leaned back against her bolster, exhaling a long, shuddering breath.

"Okay," Nature muttered, watching the HUD timer. "Two hundred and ten seconds remaining. If we can just keep them bottle-necked at the front door, we might actually pull this—"

*CLONK.*

The deafening pneumatic roar of the thermal drill abruptly died.

A plume of thick, acrid black smoke puffed from the diesel generator mounted on the tripod. The twin diamond bits froze mid-cut against the glowing vault steel, emitting a dying, mournful whine: *whirrrr-errr-uhhhh*.

Above the machine, the yellow timer vanished, replaced by an enormous, flashing crimson warning:

**DRILL JAMMED /// RESTART REQUIRED**

Bain’s voice tore through the comms, dripping with acid exasperation:

*"The drill is jammed! That broke-dick piece of shit drill! Someone go kick it back to life, right now!"*

Nature stared at the screen, her mouth hanging open.

"Broke-dick... what did he just call the drill?!" Nature gasped, her face flushing crimson with scandalized dorm-leader shock. "Bain-san! There are junior students present in this voice channel! Mind your language!"

"Nature-san, the mechanics do not register complaints regarding coarse American colloquialisms!" Dia warned, her voice tightening as red warning markers multiplied on the exterior radar. "The thermal drill possesses a procedural malfunction rate of approximately forty percent per cycle! If an operative does not depress and sustain the interact key upon the motor housing for ten contiguous seconds, the drilling progression will remain completely arrested!"

"Ten seconds?!" Nature looked at the drill.

The drill was situated in the center of the rear corridor. Directly above it was an exposed glass skylight, through which two police snipers on the opposite office building had already established laser sightlines. Red targeting beams danced across the floor tiles right beside the smoking motor.

"Someone has to stand completely still for ten seconds while snipers are aiming at that spot?!" Nature shrieked.

"I will go!" Kitasan volunteered immediately. "My armor is thick!"

"You have zero armor remaining, Kita-chan!" Nature yelled, pointing at Kitasan's HUD profile. "Your shield is broken and you have forty health points left! If a sniper sneezes on you, you're going into bleedout!"

"I... I can do it!" Spe offered, her voice small and trembling. "I can run very fast!"

"Spe-chan, the last time you pressed a button near that hallway, you blew up the customer service desk!" Nature snapped. 

She gripped her mouse, swallowed hard, and felt her heart hammering against her ribs. Her auburn horse ears flicked back against her hair. 

"Stay behind the counter and lay down suppressive fire," Nature commanded, her voice dropping into a low, steady growl. "I'm the Combat Medic. It’s my job to fix the broken things."

Nature pressed the `W` key. 

Dallas broke from cover, sprinting through the shattered glass of the copy room. The red sniper laser instantly locked onto Dallas’s chest.

*CRACK!*

A high-caliber sniper round tore through the skylight, punching through Dallas’s shoulder armor with an ear-splitting crack. Nature’s screen violently lurched upward, rimmed in pulsing crimson blood splatter. Her armor shattered instantly. Her health bar plunged by seventy percent in a single fraction of a second.

"Nature-san!" Spe and Kitasan screamed in unison.

"I'm fine! Keep shooting!" Nature gritted her teeth in real life, her knuckles whitening against the plastic mouse. 

She threw Dallas into a crouch-slide, sliding directly beneath the tripod of the smoking thermal drill. The red sniper beam swept across the concrete wall an inch above her head.

Nature pressed and held the `F` key.

A small circular progress wheel appeared in the center of the display, filling clockwise: *10%... 20%... 30%...*

Ten seconds had never felt so long in Nature’s entire life. 

Every tick of the timer was an eternity. Through the lobby, she could hear Kitasan roaring as she fired her secondary shotgun into an advancing squad of SWAT officers. She could hear Spe shrieking as she emptied magazine after magazine into the ceiling, the floor, and occasionally into the enemies. She could hear Dia’s calm, aristocratic voice calling out tactical reload intervals.

*60%... 70%...*

A stray pistol round skipped off the floor tile beside Nature’s boot, sending digital sparks into her face.

*80%... 90%...*

*100%.*

On screen, Dallas’s heavy leather dress shoe delivered a savage, cathartic kick directly into the side of the diesel generator.

*THUMP!*

The engine sputtered, backfired with a sharp *pop*, and roared back to life. The diamond drill bits spun into a blur of blinding speed, biting back into the vault steel with a triumphant screech of sparks: *SKRRRRRRRRRRRRRRRR!*

**THERMAL DRILL: 140 SECONDS**

"It’s running!" Nature gasped, scrambling backward on her hands and knees into the safety of the copy room. Her hands were shaking against the keyboard. Her mouth felt bone-dry. "The drill is running!"

"Splendid work, Nature-san!" Dia cheered, her serene smile returning.

"Don't celebrate yet," Nature panted, dropping her deployable Doctor Bag onto the copy room carpet with a heavy thump. The white canvas kit unfolded, revealing two gleaming red first-aid packages. "Everyone, gather here and take medical supplies. Kita-chan, patch your wounds right now before the next wave hits."

Kitasan’s avatar dashed into the room, knelt beside the kit, and replenished her health bar to maximum. "Yahoo! Full stamina! Thank you for the meal, Nature-san!"

"It's not a meal, it's medical gauze," Nature groaned, leaning her head against her hand. 

Through the bank intercom, a sudden, eerie silence descended. The heavy gunfire outside tapered off. The smoke in the lobby began to clear, drifting lazily out the shattered plate glass windows into the grey Washington drizzle.

Spe peeked around the corner of the teller desk. "Did... did we win? Did the police go home?"

"Negative," Dia whispered, her voice dropping into an ominous, chilling register. Her eyes narrowed as she stared at the flashing red text at the top of the screen.

The assault banner had not disappeared. It was pulsing faster.

From deep within the bank’s ventilation system, an unsettling, rhythmic mechanical hum began to rise—a high-pitched, metallic squeal that sounded like a cross between a dental drill and an angry hornet.

Nature reloaded her CAR-4, hearing the bolt snap forward with a cold metallic click. She checked the HUD. 

Her ammunition reserve stood at forty-eight rounds. Dallas had two medical charges remaining in the bag. And the thermal drill was still grinding through the final layer of hardened steel.
